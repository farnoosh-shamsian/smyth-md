# VERBS: ACCENT

<!-- page 143 -->

##### <a id="s423"></a> §423

Simple or compound verbs usually throw the accent as far back as the quantity of the last syllable permits (recessive accent, [159](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s159)).

<span lang="grc">λύ̄ω, λύ̄ομεν, ἐλῡόμην; παιδεύω, παιδεύουσι, ἐπαιδευέτην; ἀποβάλλω, ἀπόβαλλε; ἀπολύ̄ω, ἀπέλῡον; ἄπειμι, σύνεσμεν, σύμφημι, πάρεστι</span>.

##### <a id="s424"></a> §424

To this general rule there are exceptions.

**a. Enclitics**.—All the forms of <span lang="grc">φημί</span> _say_, and <span lang="grc">εἰμί</span> _am_, except <span lang="grc">φῄς</span> and <span lang="grc">εἶ</span>.

**b. Imperatives**.—(1) The second person sing. of the second aorist active imperative of five verbs is oxytone: <span lang="grc">εἰπέ</span> _say_, <span lang="grc">ἐλθέ</span> _come_, <span lang="grc">εὑρέ</span> _find_, <span lang="grc">ἰδέ</span> _see_, <span lang="grc">λαβέ</span> _take_. Their plurals are accented <span lang="grc">εἰπέτε, ἐλθέτε</span>, etc.; compounds have recessive accent: <span lang="grc">κάτειπε, ἄπελθε, ἔφευρε, παράλαβε</span>.

(2) The second aorist middle (2 sing.) is perispomenon, as <span lang="grc">λαβοῦ, παραβαλοῦ, καθελοῦ</span>.

**c. Contracted verbs** are only apparent exceptions: thus, e.g., <span lang="grc">τῑμᾷ</span> for <span lang="grc">τῑμάει, δηλοῦσι</span> for <span lang="grc">δηλόουσι, φιλεῖν</span> for <span lang="grc">φιλέειν</span>. So the subjunctive of the first and second aorist passive <span lang="grc">λυθῶ</span> for <span lang="grc">λυθέω, φανῶ</span> for <span lang="grc">φανέω</span>; the optatives <span lang="grc">λυθεῖμεν</span> from <span lang="grc">λυθέ-ῑ-μεν, διδοῖμεν</span> from <span lang="grc">διδό-ῑ-μεν</span>; the futures <span lang="grc">φανῶ</span> for <span lang="grc">φανέω, φανοῖμι</span> for <span lang="grc">φανέοιμι, φανεῖν</span> for <span lang="grc">φανέειν, φανῶν</span> for <span lang="grc">φανέων; λιπεῖν</span> for <span lang="grc">λιπέεν</span>; and the present and second aorist active and middle subjunctive of most <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verbs, as <span lang="grc">τιθῶ</span> for <span lang="grc">τιθέω, ἱστῶμαι, θῶμαι</span>, perf. <span lang="grc">κεκτῶμαι</span>. On <span lang="grc">διδοῦσι, τιθεῖσι</span>, see [463 d](08-endings-of-the-verb-personal-endings.md#s463).

N. 1.—In athematic optatives the accent does not recede beyond the diphthong containing <span lang="grc">-ῑ-</span>, the sign of the optative mood: <span lang="grc">ἱσταῖο, ἱσταῖμεν, ἱσταῖτο, διδοῖτο</span>; and so in <span lang="grc">λυθεῖμεν, λυθεῖεν</span>.

N. 2.—<span lang="grc">δύναμαι</span> _am able_, <span lang="grc">ἐπίσταμαι</span> _understand_, <span lang="grc">κρέμαμαι</span> _hang_, <span lang="grc">ὀνίνημι</span> _profit_, and <span lang="grc">ἐπριάμην</span> _bought_ ([749 b](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/15-present-system.md#s749), [750](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/15-present-system.md#s750) b, 757 a) have recessive accent in the subjunctive and optative (<span lang="grc">δύνωμαι, ἐπίστωμαι, δύναιτο, κρέμαιτο</span>).

**d.** Poetic forms sometimes fail to follow the rule, as <span lang="grc">ἐών</span> _being_.

##### <a id="s425"></a> §425

Infinitives, participles, and verbal adjectives are verbal nouns ([358](01-inflection-preliminary-remarks-355-380.md#s358)), and hence do not regularly show recessive accent.

**a. Infinitives**.—The following infinitives accent the penult: all infinitives in <span lang="grc">-ναι</span>, as <span lang="grc">λελυκέναι, λυθῆναι, ἱστάναι, στῆναι</span> (except Epic <span lang="grc">-μεναι</span>, as <span lang="grc">στήμεναι</span>); in verbs in <span lang="grc">ω</span> the first aorist active, as <span lang="grc">λῦσαι, παιδεῦσαι</span>, the second aorist middle, as <span lang="grc">λιπέσθαι</span>, the perfect (middle) passive, as <span lang="grc">λελύσθαι, πεπαιδεῦσθαι, πεποιῆσθαι</span>.

##### <a id="s425aD"></a> §425 a. D

The 2. aor. mid. inf. in Hom. is recessive in <span lang="grc">ἀγέρεσθαι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀγείρω</span> _assemble_); so the perf. <span lang="grc">ἀλάλησθαι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀλάομαι</span> _wander_), <span lang="grc">ἀκάχησθαι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἄχνυμαι</span> _am distressed_).

N.—The present inf. of contracted verbs and the second aorist active inf. of <span lang="grc">ω-</span>verbs have the perispomenon by 424 c.

**b. Participles**.—(1) Oxytone: the masculine and neuter sing. of the second aorist active, as <span lang="grc">λιπών, λιπόν</span>; and of all participles of the third declension ending in <span lang="grc">-ς</span> in the masculine (except the first aorist active), as <span lang="grc">λυθείς λυθέν, λελυκώς λελυκός, ἑστώς ἑστός, τιθείς τιθέν, διδούς διδόν, ἱστά̄ς ἱστάν, δεικνύ̄ς δεικνύν</span> (but <span lang="grc">λύ̄σᾱς, ποιήσᾱς</span>). Also <span lang="grc">ἰών</span> _going_ from <span lang="grc">εἶμι</span>. <!-- page 144 -->

(2) Paroxytone: the perfect middle (passive): <span lang="grc">λελυμένος</span>.

N.—Participles are accented like adjectives, not like verbs. The fem. and neuter nom. accent the same syllable as the masc. nom. if the quantity of the ultima permits, thus <span lang="grc">παιδεύων, παιδεύουσα, παιδεῦον</span> (not <span lang="grc">παίδευον</span>); <span lang="grc">ποιήσᾱς, ποιήσᾱσα, ποιῆσαν</span> (not <span lang="grc">ποίησαν</span>); <span lang="grc">φιλῶν, φιλοῦσα, φιλοῦν</span> (from <span lang="grc">φιλέον</span>).

##### <a id="s425b2D"></a> §425 b (2) D

But Hom. has <span lang="grc">ἀλαλήμενος</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀλάομαι</span> _wander_), <span lang="grc">ἀκαχήμενος</span> or <span lang="grc">ἀκηχέμενος</span> (<span lang="grc">ἄχνυμαι</span> _am distressed_), <span lang="grc">ἐσσύμενος</span> (<span lang="grc">σεύω</span> _drive_).

**c. Verbal Adjectives**.—The verbal adjective in <span lang="grc">-τος</span> is accented on the ultima (<span lang="grc">λυτός</span>); that in <span lang="grc">-τεος</span> on the penult (<span lang="grc">λυτέος</span>).

N.—Prepositional compounds in <span lang="grc">-τος</span> denoting possibility generally accent the last syllable and have three endings ([286](../04-substantives/07-adjectives.md#s286)), as <span lang="grc">διαλυτός</span> _dissoluble_, <span lang="grc">ἐξαιρετός</span> _removable_. Such compounds as have the force of a perfect passive participle accent the antepenult and have two endings, as <span lang="grc">διάλυτος</span> _dissolved_, <span lang="grc">ἐξαίρετος</span> _chosen_. All other compounds in <span lang="grc">-τος</span> accent the antepenult and have two endings, as <span lang="grc">ἄβατος</span> _impassable_, <span lang="grc">χειροποίητος</span> _artificial_.

##### <a id="s426"></a> §426

**Exceptions to the recessive accent of compound verbs.—a.** The accent cannot precede the augment or reduplication: <span lang="grc">ἄπειμι</span> _am absent_, <span lang="grc">ἀπῆν</span> _was absent_, <span lang="grc">εἰσ-ῆλθον</span> _they entered_, <span lang="grc">ἀπ-ῆσαν</span> _they were absent;_ <span lang="grc">ἀφ-ῖκται</span> _arrived_ (cp. <span lang="grc">ἷκται</span>).

N.—A long vowel or diphthong not changed by the augment receives the accent: <span lang="grc">ὑπ-εῖκε</span> _was yielding_ (indic. <span lang="grc">ὑπ-είκω</span>, imper. <span lang="grc">ὕπ-εικε</span>).

**b.** The accent cannot precede the last syllable of the preposition before the simple verb nor move back to the first of two prepositions: <span lang="grc">περίθες</span> _put around_, <span lang="grc">συνέκδος</span> _give up together_ (not <span lang="grc">σύνεκδος</span>), <span lang="grc">συγκάθες</span> _put down together_ (not <span lang="grc">σύγκαθες</span>). Compounds of the second aorist active imperatives <span lang="grc">δός, ἕς, θές</span>, and <span lang="grc">σχές</span> are thus paroxytone: <span lang="grc">ἐπίθες</span> _set on_, <span lang="grc">περίθες</span> _put around_, <span lang="grc">ἐπίσχες</span> _hold on_.

**c.** When compounded with a monosyllabic preposition, monosyllabic second aorist middle imperatives in <span lang="grc">-οῦ</span> from <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verbs retain the circumflex: <span lang="grc">προδοῦ</span> _betray_, <span lang="grc">ἐνθοῦ</span> _put in_. But the accent recedes when these imperatives prefix a dissyllabic preposition: <span lang="grc">ἀπόδου</span> _sell_, <span lang="grc">κατάθου</span> _put down_. The open forms always have recessive accent, as <span lang="grc">ἔνθεο, κατάθεο</span>.

**d.** The accent of uncompounded infinitives, participles, aorist passive, perfect passive, and of the second aorist middle imperative (2. p. sing., but see [426](#s426) c) is retained in composition.

**e.** <span lang="grc">ἀπέσται</span> _will be far from_, <span lang="grc">ἐπέσται</span> _will be upon_ do not have recessive accent.

**f.** Compound subjunctives are differently accentuated in the Mss.: <span lang="grc">ἀποδῶμαι</span> and <span lang="grc">ἀπόδωμαι, ἐπιθῆται</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐπίθηται</span>; the aorist of <span lang="grc">ἵ̄ημι</span> has <span lang="grc">προῶμαι</span> and <span lang="grc">πρόωμαι. ἀπέχω</span> has <span lang="grc">ἀπόσχωμαι</span>. Compound optatives retain the accent of the primitives: <span lang="grc">ἀποδοῖτο</span>, as <span lang="grc">δοῖτο</span>. For <span lang="grc">συνθοῖτο, προσθοῖσθε</span> ([746 c](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/15-present-system.md#s746)) the Mss. occasionally have <span lang="grc">σύνθοιτο, πρόσθοισθε</span>; and so <span lang="grc">πρόοιτο</span>.

##### <a id="s427"></a> §427

Final <span lang="grc">-αι</span> (and <span lang="grc">-οι</span>) are regarded as long in the optative ([169](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s169)), elsewhere as short. Hence distinguish the forms of the first aorist.
