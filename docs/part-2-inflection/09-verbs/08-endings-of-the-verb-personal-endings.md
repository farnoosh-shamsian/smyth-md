# VERBS: ENDINGS OF THE VERB: PERSONAL ENDINGS

<!-- page 152 -->

##### <a id="s462"></a> §462

To make the complete verbal forms, to the tense-stems in the various moods are attached the personal endings in the finite moods and other endings in the infinitives, participles, and verbal adjectives. See [366](01-inflection-preliminary-remarks-355-380.md#s366). The personal endings of the four finite moods are given below. In many forms only the <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verbs preserve distinct endings. Some of the endings are due to analogy of others and many are still unexplained. The first person dual, when it is used, has the form of the first person plural.

##### <a id="s463"></a> §463

**PRIMARY ENDINGS OF THE ACTIVE (IND. AND SUBJ.)**

**a. 1 Sing**.—<span lang="grc">-μι</span> is found only in <span lang="grc">μι</span>-verbs. Verbs in <span lang="grc">-ω</span> have no ending and simply lengthen the thematic vowel (<span lang="grc">λύ̄ω, λείπω</span>). The perfect has no personal ending, <span lang="grc">-α</span> taking the place of a thematic vowel.

##### <a id="s463aD"></a> §463 a. D

The Hom. subj. <span lang="grc">ἐθέλωμι, τύχωμι, ἀγάγωμι</span>, are new formations. Aeolic has <span lang="grc">φίλημι, δοκίμωμι</span> (indic.).

**b. 2 Sing**.—(1) <span lang="grc">-σι</span> is found in Hom. <span lang="grc">ἐσσί</span> _thou art_ from the <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verb <span lang="grc">εἰμί</span> _**I** am;_ possibly also in <span lang="grc">φῄς</span> _thou sayest_. Attic <span lang="grc">εἶ</span> _thou art_ is derived from <span lang="grc">ἐ-σι. τίθη-ς</span> is obscure. <span lang="grc">λύ̄εις</span> is probably for <span lang="grc">λῡε-σι, λῡεϊ, λῡει</span>, to which <span lang="grc">ς</span> has been added. Subj. <span lang="grc">λύ̄ῃ-ς</span> follows the analogy of the indicative, but with long thematic vowel. <span lang="grc">τιθῇς</span> for <span lang="grc">τιθέ-ῃς</span>. In the perfect <span lang="grc">-ς</span> (_not_ for <span lang="grc">-σι</span>) has been added.

**b.** (1) <span lang="grc">εἶς</span> or <span lang="grc">εἴς</span> in Hom. and Hdt. is derived from <span lang="grc">εἶ ¨ ς</span>. For this form <span lang="grc">ἐσσ</span>(<span lang="grc">ί</span>) may be read in Hom. Theocr. has <span lang="grc">-ες</span> for <span lang="grc">-εις</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀμέλγες</span>, etc.) and perf. <span lang="grc">πεπόνθεις</span> ([557](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/06-v-first-k-perfect-system.md#s557). 2. D.).

(2) <span lang="grc">-θα</span> is a perfect ending, as in <span lang="grc">οἶσθα</span> _knowest_ for <span lang="grc">οἰδ ¨ θα</span> ([83](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s83)). From the perfect it spread to the imperfects <span lang="grc">ἦσθα</span> _wast_, <span lang="grc">ἤεισθα</span> _wentst_, <span lang="grc">ἔφησθα</span> _saidst_, and to <span lang="grc">ᾔδησθα</span> or <span lang="grc">ᾔδεισθα</span> _knewest_. The perfect has commonly <span lang="grc">-α-ς. οἶσθας</span> and <span lang="grc">ἦσθας</span> are late.

**b.** (2) <span lang="grc">-σθα</span> in Hom. indic. <span lang="grc">φῆσθα, τίθησθα, ᾔδησθα</span>; subj. <span lang="grc">ἐθέλῃσθα</span> also written <span lang="grc">ἐθέλησθα</span>; opt. (rarely) <span lang="grc">κλαίοισθα, βάλοισθα. -σθα</span> occurs also occasionally in Doric (<span lang="grc">ποθορῆσθα</span>) and Aeolic (<span lang="grc">ἔχεισθα, φίλησθα</span>).

**c. 3 Sing**.—<span lang="grc">-τι</span> is found in <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verbs: <span lang="grc">ἐσ-τί, τίθησι</span> for <span lang="grc">τίθη-τι</span> (Doric) by 115. <span lang="grc">λύ̄ει</span> is obscure, but it cannot be derived from <span lang="grc">λῡε-σι</span> for <span lang="grc">λῡε-τι. λύ̄ῃ, τιθῇ</span> (for <span lang="grc">τιθέῃ</span>) follow <span lang="grc">λύ̄ει</span>, but with long thematic vowel. In the perfect, <span lang="grc">-ε</span> with no personal ending.

**c.** Aeolic has <span lang="grc">τίθη, ποίη, στεφάνοι</span>, but <span lang="grc">ἦσι</span> _says_. Subj.: Hom. <span lang="grc">ἐθέλῃσι</span> (also written <span lang="grc">ἐθέλησι</span>; cp. Arcad. <span lang="grc">ἔχη</span>), <span lang="grc">φορέῃσι, θέῃσι</span>.

**d. 3 Pl**.—Original <span lang="grc">-ντι</span> is retained in Doric <span lang="grc">λύ̄οντι</span>, whence Attic <span lang="grc">λύ̄ουσι</span> ([115 a](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s115)); <span lang="grc">ἐντί</span>, Attic <span lang="grc">εἰσί</span>. Subj. <span lang="grc">λύ̄ωσι</span> from <span lang="grc">λύ̄ω-ντι, τιθῶσι</span> from <span lang="grc">τιθέω-ντι, ποιῶσι</span> from <span lang="grc">ποιῶντι</span> (Dor.). Many <span lang="grc">μι</span> forms are derived from <span lang="grc">-αντι</span>, as <span lang="grc">τιθέᾱσι</span> (<span lang="grc">τιθέ-αντι</span>), <span lang="grc">διδόᾱσι</span> (<span lang="grc">διδό-αντι</span>), <span lang="grc">ἑστᾶσι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἑστά-αντι</span>), <span lang="grc">ἱστᾶσι</span> (from <span lang="grc">ἱστά-αντι</span>), the accent of which has been transferred to <span lang="grc">τιθεῖσι</span> ([747 D.](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/15-present-system.md#s747) 1), <span lang="grc">διδοῦσι</span> from (Dor.) <span lang="grc">τίθε-ντι, δίδο-ντι. -ᾰτι</span> from <span lang="grc">-ṇτι</span> ([35 b](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s35)), properly the ending of the perfect after a consonant, appears as <span lang="grc">-ᾰσι</span> in Hom. <span lang="grc">πεφύκᾰσι</span>; but it has been replaced by <span lang="grc">-ᾱσι</span> out of <span lang="grc">-αντι</span>, as in <span lang="grc">τετράφ-ᾱσι</span>.

**d.** Hom. has <span lang="grc">-ᾱσι</span> in <span lang="grc">ἴᾱσι</span> _they go_, <span lang="grc">ἔᾱσι</span> _they are_, and in <span lang="grc">βεβάᾱσι, γεγάᾱσι</span>. Aeolic has <span lang="grc">λύ̄οισι, φίλεισι, τί̄μαισι</span>.

##### <a id="s464"></a> §464

**SECONDARY ENDINGS OF THE ACTIVE (IND. AND OPT.)**

The optative usually has the endings of the secondary tenses of the indicative. <!-- page 154 -->

**a. 1 Sing**.—<span lang="grc">-ν</span> stands for <span lang="grc">μ</span> ([133 c](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/04-final-consonants.md#s133)), cp. <span lang="grc">ἔφερο-ν</span>, Skt. ábhara-m. After a consonant <span lang="grc">μ</span> (sonant nasal, 20 b, 35 c) became <span lang="grc">α</span>: <span lang="grc">ἔλῡσα</span> for <span lang="grc">ἐλῡσṃ</span>, Epic <span lang="grc">ἦα</span> _was_ for <span lang="grc">η᾽</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">α</span> from <span lang="grc">ἠσṃ</span>. In the pluperfect <span lang="grc">-η</span> is from <span lang="grc">ε-α</span> ([467](#s467)). <span lang="grc">-ν</span> is found in the optative when the mood suffix is <span lang="grc">-ιη-</span>; elsewhere the optative has <span lang="grc">-μι</span>.

##### <a id="s464aD"></a> §464 a. D

<span lang="grc">-ν</span> for <span lang="grc">-μι</span> is very rare (<span lang="grc">τρέφοιν</span> in Eur., <span lang="grc">ἁμάρτοιν</span> in Cratinus).

**b. 2 Sing**.—On <span lang="grc">-σθα</span> see [463](#s463) b (2).

**c. 3 Sing**.—<span lang="grc">-τ</span> dropped ([133 b](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/04-final-consonants.md#s133)) in <span lang="grc">ἔλῡε, ἐτίθη</span>, and in the opt. <span lang="grc">λύ̄οι, εἴη</span> (cp. Old Lat. _sied_). <span lang="grc">ἔλῡσε</span> has its <span lang="grc">-ε</span> from the perfect (cp. <span lang="grc">οἶδε</span>) and shows no personal ending.

**c.** Doric <span lang="grc">ἦς</span> _was_ for <span lang="grc">ἠσ</span>(<span lang="grc">τ</span>).

**d. Dual**.—<span lang="grc">-την</span> is rarely found for <span lang="grc">-τον</span> in the 2 dual (<span lang="grc">εὑρέτην</span> in Plato). Hom. has <span lang="grc">ἐτεύχετον</span> as 3 dual.

**e. 3 Pl**.—<span lang="grc">-ν</span> for <span lang="grc">-ντ</span> by 133 b. <span lang="grc">-σαν</span> (taken from the 1 aorist) is used (1) in the imperf. and 2 aor. of <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verbs, as <span lang="grc">ἐτίθε-σαν, ἔθε-σαν</span>; (2) in the aor. pass. <span lang="grc">ἐλύθη-σαν, ἐφάνη-σαν</span> (here <span lang="grc">-ν</span> preceded by a short vowel occurs in poetry, 585 a. D.); (3) in the pluperf. <span lang="grc">ἐλελύκε-σαν</span>; (4) in the opt. when <span lang="grc">-ιη-</span> is the modal suffix ([460](07-tense-suffixes-thematic-vowel-mood-suffixes.md#s460)). In the opt. <span lang="grc">-σαν</span> is rare.

**e.** <span lang="grc">-ν</span> is regular in Doric and common in Hom. and later poetry; as <span lang="grc">ἔστᾰ-ν</span> (<span lang="grc">ἔστη-σαν</span>), <span lang="grc">ἔδιδο-ν</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐδίδο-σαν</span>), <span lang="grc">φίληθεν</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐφιλήθη-σαν</span>), <span lang="grc">τράφεν</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐτράφη-σαν</span>). The short vowel before <span lang="grc">ν</span>(<span lang="grc">τ</span>) is explained by 40. Hom. <span lang="grc">ἦε-ν</span> _were_ became <span lang="grc">ἦν</span>, used in Dor. as 3 pl.; in Attic it was used as 3 sing.

##### <a id="s465"></a> §465

**ENDINGS OF THE MIDDLE (INDIC., SUBJ., OPT.)**

**a. 2 Sing**.—Primary <span lang="grc">-σαι</span> retains its <span lang="grc">ς</span> in the perfect of all verbs (<span lang="grc">λέλυ-σαι</span>), and in the pres. of <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verbs (<span lang="grc">τίθε-σαι</span>). Elsewhere <span lang="grc">ς</span> drops between vowels, as in <span lang="grc">λύ̄ῃ</span> or <span lang="grc">λύ̄ει</span> from <span lang="grc">λύ̄ε-σαι, λυθήσῃ</span> or <span lang="grc">-ει, φανῇ</span> from <span lang="grc">φανέε-σαι, τῑμᾷ</span> from <span lang="grc">τῑμάε-σαι</span>; subj. <span lang="grc">λύ̄ῃ</span> from <span lang="grc">λύη-σαι, φήνῃ</span> from <span lang="grc">φήνη-σαι, θῇ</span> from <span lang="grc">θήε-σαι, δῷ</span> from <span lang="grc">δώη-σαι, ᾗ</span> from <span lang="grc">ἕη-σαι, φιλῇ</span> from <span lang="grc">φιλέη-σαι, δηλοῖ</span> from <span lang="grc">δηλόῃ ῀ δηλόη-σαι</span>.

N. 1.—The forms <span lang="grc">-ῃ</span> and <span lang="grc">-ει</span> are found in the present, future, and future perfect. See [628](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/13-inflection-of-omega-verbs.md#s628).

N. 2.—<span lang="grc">δύνᾳ</span> and <span lang="grc">δύνῃ</span> for <span lang="grc">δύνασαι, ἐπίστᾳ</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐπίστῃ</span> for <span lang="grc">ἐπίστασαι, ἐφί̄ει</span> for <span lang="grc">ἐφί̄εσαι</span>, are poetic and dialectic or late.

##### <a id="s465aD"></a> §465 a. D

Hom. has <span lang="grc">βούλεαι</span>, perf. <span lang="grc">μέμνηαι</span>, but pres. <span lang="grc">δύνασαι, παρίστασαι; ὄψει</span> is unique (for <span lang="grc">ὄψεαι</span>); subj. <span lang="grc">δύνηαι</span>. Doric often contracts, as <span lang="grc">οἴῃ</span> for <span lang="grc">οἴε-αι</span>. Aeolic generally leaves <span lang="grc">εαι</span> open (<span lang="grc">κείσε-αι</span>). Hdt. has open <span lang="grc">-εαι, -ηαι</span>.

**b. 2 Sing**.—<span lang="grc">-σο</span> stays in all plups. and in the imperf. of <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verbs. Elsewhere it loses its <span lang="grc">ς</span>, as in <span lang="grc">ἐλύ̄ου</span> from <span lang="grc">ἐλύ̄ε-σο, ἐλύ̄σω</span> from <span lang="grc">ἐλύ̄σα-σο, ἐφήνω</span> from <span lang="grc">ἐφήνα-σο, ἐλίπου</span> from <span lang="grc">ἐλίπε-σο, ἔθου</span> from <span lang="grc">ἔθε-σο, ἐπρίω</span> from <span lang="grc">ἐπρία-σο, ἐτῑμῶ</span> from <span lang="grc">ἐτῑμάε-σο, ἐφιλοῦ</span> from <span lang="grc">ἐφιλέε-σο</span>. In the optative, <span lang="grc">λύ̄οιο, λίποιο, τιθεῖο, εἷο, λύ̄σαιο</span>, from <span lang="grc">λύ̄οι-σο</span>, etc.; <span lang="grc">τῑμῷο</span> from <span lang="grc">τῑμάοι-σο</span>.

N. 1.—<span lang="grc">ἐδύνω</span> or <span lang="grc">ἠδύνω</span> and <span lang="grc">ἠπίστω</span> are commoner than <span lang="grc">ἐδύνασο</span> and <span lang="grc">ἠπίστασο</span> from <span lang="grc">δύναμαι</span> _am able_ and <span lang="grc">ἐπίσταμαι</span> _understand_.

N. 2.—After a diphthong or a long vowel in the 2 aor. indic. mid. <span lang="grc">-σο</span> is retained, as <span lang="grc">εἷσο</span> (<span lang="grc">ἵ̄ημι</span> _send_), <span lang="grc">ὤνησο</span> (<span lang="grc">ὀνίνημι</span> _benefit_).

**b.** Hom., Doric, and Aeolic have generally open forms, as Hom. <span lang="grc">βάλλε-ο</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">βάλλευ</span>), <span lang="grc">ὠδύσα-ο. ἔρειο, σπεῖο</span> are from <span lang="grc">-εεο</span>. Hom. has <span lang="grc">ἐμάρναο</span> for Attic <span lang="grc">ἐμάρνασο</span>, and may drop <span lang="grc">ς</span> even in the pluperfect (<span lang="grc">ἔσσυο</span>). When Doric contracts <span lang="grc">αο</span> we have <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span>. In Hdt. <span lang="grc">αο, εο</span> are open, but the writing <span lang="grc">ευ</span> for <span lang="grc">εο</span> is found. <!-- page 155 -->

**c. Dual**.—The 1 pl. is used for the 1 dual except in the three poetic forms <span lang="grc">περιδώμεθον, λελείμμεθον, ὁρμώμεθον</span>. Hom. has <span lang="grc">-σθον</span> for <span lang="grc">-σθην</span> in <span lang="grc">θωρήσσεσθον</span>.

**d. 1 Pl**.—In epic and dramatic poetry <span lang="grc">-μεσθα</span> is often used for <span lang="grc">-μεθα</span> for metrical reasons (<span lang="grc">βουλόμεσθα, ἐπιστάμεσθα</span>).

**e. 2 Pl**.—On the loss of <span lang="grc">ς</span> in <span lang="grc">σθε</span> (<span lang="grc">ἔσταλθε</span>), see [103](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s103).

**f. 3 Pl**.—After vowel stems <span lang="grc">-νται, -ντο</span> are preserved. After stems ending in a consonant <span lang="grc">-νται, -ντο</span> became <span lang="grc">-αται, -ατο</span> by 35 b. These forms were retained in prose till about 400 B.C. (e.g. <span lang="grc">τετάχαται, ἐτετάχατο</span>).

**f.** <span lang="grc">-αται, -ατο</span> occur in Hom. regularly in the perfect and pluperfect of consonant stems, as <span lang="grc">τετράφαται, ἕαται</span> for <span lang="grc">ἑσ-νται, ἥατο</span> for <span lang="grc">ἡσ-ντο</span> from <span lang="grc">ἧμαι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἡσμαι</span>); also in stems ending in <span lang="grc">-ι</span>, as <span lang="grc">ἐφθίατο. -αται, -ατο</span> were transferred to vocalic stems, as <span lang="grc">βεβλήαται, βεβλήατο</span>, Hdt. <span lang="grc">δυνέαται</span>. Hom. has <span lang="grc">-δ-αται</span> in <span lang="grc">ἐληλάδαται</span> from <span lang="grc">ἐλαύνω</span> _drive_. In the opt. <span lang="grc">-ατο</span> always (<span lang="grc">γενοίατο</span> for <span lang="grc">γένοιντο</span>). In Hdt. <span lang="grc">η</span> before <span lang="grc">-αται, -ατο</span> is shortened, as perf. <span lang="grc">ἡγέαται</span> for <span lang="grc">ἡγή-αται ῀ ἥγηνται, ἐβεβλέατο</span> for <span lang="grc">-ηατο</span>. For <span lang="grc">κεῖνται</span>, Hom. <span lang="grc">κείαται</span> and <span lang="grc">κέαται</span>, Hdt. has <span lang="grc">κέαται</span>. In the opt. Hdt. has <span lang="grc">-ατο</span>: <span lang="grc">βουλοίατο, δεξαίατο</span>. In Hdt. <span lang="grc">-αται, -ατο</span> occur even in the present system, <span lang="grc">τιθέαται, δυνέαται, ἱ̄στέατο</span>.

##### <a id="s466"></a> §466

**ENDINGS OF THE IMPERATIVE**

\1. Active.

**a. 2 Sing**.—<span lang="grc">λῦε, λίπε, τίθει</span> (for <span lang="grc">τίθε-ε</span>) have not lost <span lang="grc">-θι. -θι</span> is found in 2 aor. pass. <span lang="grc">φάνη-θι</span>; in <span lang="grc">στῆ-θι</span> and <span lang="grc">ἕστα-θι</span>; in some 2 aorists, like <span lang="grc">γνῶ-θι, τλῆ-θι, πῖ-θι</span>, which are <span lang="grc">μι</span> forms though they have presents of the <span lang="grc">ω</span> form ([687](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/13-inflection-of-omega-verbs.md#s687)). Also in <span lang="grc">ἴσ-θι</span> _be_ or _know_, <span lang="grc">ἴθι</span> _go_, <span lang="grc">φάθι</span> or <span lang="grc">φαθί</span> _say_. <span lang="grc">λύθητι</span> is for <span lang="grc">λυθηθι</span> by 125 b.

##### <a id="s466aD"></a> §466 a. D

<span lang="grc">-θι</span> is not rare in Hom., pres. <span lang="grc">δίδωθι ῀ δίδου, ὄρνυθι</span>, aor. <span lang="grc">κλῦθι</span>, perf. <span lang="grc">τέτλαθι</span>. Aeolic has <span lang="grc">ἴστᾱ, φίλη. πίει, δέχοι, δίδοι</span> (Pindar) are very rare.

**b.** <span lang="grc">-ς</span> occurs in <span lang="grc">θές, ἕς, δός, σχές</span> (and in the rare <span lang="grc">θίγες, πίεις</span>). This <span lang="grc">-ς</span> is _not_ derived from <span lang="grc">-θι</span>.

**c.** <span lang="grc">λῦσ-ον</span> aor. act. and <span lang="grc">λῦσ-αι</span> aor. mid. are obscure in origin.

\2. Middle.

**a. 2 Sing**.—<span lang="grc">-σο</span> retains its <span lang="grc">ς</span> in the (rare) perf. of all verbs and in the pres. of <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verbs (<span lang="grc">λέλυσο, τίθεσο, ἵστασο</span>). Elsewhere <span lang="grc">ς</span> is dropped, as in <span lang="grc">λύ̄ου</span> from <span lang="grc">λύ̄ε-σο, λιποῦ</span> from <span lang="grc">λιπέ-σο, θοῦ</span> from <span lang="grc">θέ-σο, οὗ</span> from <span lang="grc">ἕ-σο, πρίω</span> from <span lang="grc">πρία-σο, τῑμῶ</span> from <span lang="grc">τῑμάε-σο</span>.

N.—<span lang="grc">τίθου, ἵστω, δίδου</span> are poetic or late.

\3. **3 Pl**.—For <span lang="grc">-ντων</span> and <span lang="grc">-σθων</span> we find <span lang="grc">-τωσαν</span> and <span lang="grc">-σθωσαν</span> in prose after Thucydides, in Euripides, and in inscriptions after 300 B.C. Thus, <span lang="grc">λῡέτωσαν, λῡσάτωσαν, λῡέσθωσαν, λῡσάσθωσαν, λυθήτωσαν, λιπέτωσαν, λιπέσθωσαν, φηνάσθωσαν, φανήτωσαν, τῑμάσθωσαν, φιλείσθωσαν, γεγράφθωσαν, πεπείσθωσαν, τιθέτωσαν, διδότωσαν, θέτωσαν, τιθέσθωσαν, θέσθωσαν, -ἕτωσαν, -ἕσθωσαν</span>.

N.—<span lang="grc">ἔστων</span> for <span lang="grc">ὄντων</span> is rare. Attic inscriptions have (very rarely) <span lang="grc">-ντωσαν</span>.

\3. Doric has also <span lang="grc">-ντω</span>, as in <span lang="grc">παρεχόντω</span>; Aeolic <span lang="grc">-ντον</span>, as <span lang="grc">φέροντον</span>. Doric has <span lang="grc">-σθω</span> (pl.) and <span lang="grc">-σθων</span>.

## ENDINGS OF THE PLUPERFECT, ENDINGS IN <span lang="grc">σθ</span>

<!-- page 156 -->

##### <a id="s467"></a> §467

**Endings of the Pluperfect Active**.—<span lang="grc">-η, -ης, -ει</span>(<span lang="grc">ν</span>) are derived from <span lang="grc">-ε</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">α, -ε</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">ας, -ε</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">ε</span>. In later Greek the endings are <span lang="grc">-ειν, -εις, -ει</span>(<span lang="grc">ν</span>), <span lang="grc">-ειτον, -ειτην, -ειμεν, -ειτε</span>, and very late <span lang="grc">-εισαν</span>.

##### <a id="s467D"></a> §467 D

Hom. has <span lang="grc">-εα, -ης, -ει</span> or <span lang="grc">ει-ν</span> (<span lang="grc">-εε</span> only in <span lang="grc">ᾔδεε</span>), <span lang="grc">-εσαν</span>, and rarely <span lang="grc">-ον, -ες, -ε</span>; Hdt. has <span lang="grc">-εα, -εας, -εε</span> (<span lang="grc">-ει</span>?), <span lang="grc">-εατε, -εσαν</span>.

##### <a id="s468"></a> §468

**The Endings <span lang="grc">-σθε</span>, etc.**—The <span lang="grc">ς</span> of the endings <span lang="grc">-σθε, -σθω, -σθον, -σθων, -σθαι</span> ([409](00-introduction.md#s409) N.) has no exact parallel in cognate languages, and seems to have spread in Greek from forms like <span lang="grc">τετέλεσ-θε, ἔζωσ-θε</span>, etc., where a sigma-stem was followed by original <span lang="grc">-θε</span>.

## ENDINGS OF THE INFINITIVE, PARTICIPLE, AND VERBAL ADJECTIVE

##### <a id="s469"></a> §469

**Infinitive**.—The following are the endings added to the tense-stem to make the infinitive.

**a. <span lang="grc">-εν</span>:** in present and 2 aorist active of <span lang="grc">ω-</span>verbs, all futures active. Thus, <span lang="grc">λύ̄ειν, τῑμᾶν, λιπεῖν, λύ̄σειν, φανεῖν</span> from <span lang="grc">λύ̄ε-εν, τῑμάε-εν, λιπέ-εν, λύ̄σε-εν, φανέε-εν</span>.

**b. <span lang="grc">-αι</span>:** in 1 aor. active, as <span lang="grc">λῦσαι, παιδεῦσαι, δεῖξαι</span>.

**c. <span lang="grc">-ναι</span>:** (1) present, 2 perf. of <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verbs, the two passive aorists, as <span lang="grc">τιθέ-ναι, ἑστάναι, λυθῆ-ναι, φανῆ-ναι</span>; (2) perfect active, <span lang="grc">λελυκέ-ναι</span>, and <span lang="grc">εἰδέ-ναι</span> from <span lang="grc">εἰδ-ε</span> (<span lang="grc">οἶδα</span>).

N. 1.—The ending <span lang="grc">εναι</span> appears in the 2 aor. of <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verbs, as <span lang="grc">δοῦναι</span> from <span lang="grc">δό-εναι, θεῖναι</span> from <span lang="grc">θέ-εναι</span>.

**d. <span lang="grc">-σθαι</span>**: in other cases.

N. 2.—The infinitives are old cases of substantives, those in <span lang="grc">-αι</span> being datives, the others locatives.

##### <a id="s469D"></a> §469 D

<span lang="grc">-εν</span> appears also in Hom. <span lang="grc">ἰδέεν</span> (miswritten <span lang="grc">ἰδέειν</span>). Hom. has no case of <span lang="grc">-εναι</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἰέναι</span> write <span lang="grc">ἴμεναι</span>). For <span lang="grc">-εν</span> or <span lang="grc">-ναι</span> Hom. often uses <span lang="grc"> **-μεναι** </span> (also Aeolic) and <span lang="grc"> **-μεν** </span> (which is also Doric); both endings show the accent on the preceding syllable, as <span lang="grc">ζευγνύμεναι, ἔμμεναι</span> ( = <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span>), <span lang="grc">φιλήμεναι, στήμεναι, ἑστάμεναι, ἀξέμεναι, ὁμοιωθήμεναι, δαήμεναι; τιθέμεν, ἔμμεν, ἴμεν, θέμεν, ἐλθέμεν, ἀξέμεν</span>. Doric has <span lang="grc">-μεν</span> in the aorist passive, as <span lang="grc">αἰσχυνθῆμεν. -μεν</span> is preceded by a short syllable and generally stands before a vowel. <span lang="grc">-ναι</span> always follows a long vowel. Doric has <span lang="grc">-ην</span> and <span lang="grc">-εν</span> in the present. Aeolic has <span lang="grc">-ην</span> in the present and 2 aorist.

##### <a id="s470"></a> §470

**Participles**.—The stem of the participle is formed by adding the following endings to the tense stem.

**a. <span lang="grc">-ντ-</span>**: in all active tenses except the perfect, and in 1 and 2 aor. passive ([301](../04-substantives/08-declension-of-participles.md#s301)).

**b. <span lang="grc">-οτ-</span>**: in the perfect active (for <span lang="grc">-ϝοτ-</span>); masc. <span lang="grc">-ώς</span>, fem. <span lang="grc">-υῖα</span>, neut. <span lang="grc">-ός</span> ([301 c](../04-substantives/08-declension-of-participles.md#s301)).

**c. <span lang="grc">-μενο-</span>**: in the middle, and in the passive except in the aorist.

##### <a id="s471"></a> §471

**Verbal Adjectives**.—Most of the verbals in <span lang="grc">-τός</span> and <span lang="grc">-τέος</span> are formed by adding these suffixes to the verbal stem of the aorist passive (first or second). Thus, <span lang="grc">φιλητός, -τέος</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐ-φιλή-θην</span>); <span lang="grc">πειστός, -τέος</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐ-πείσ-θην</span>); <span lang="grc">τελεστός, -τέος</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐ-τελέσ-θην</span>); <span lang="grc">σταλτός, -τέος</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐ-στάλ-ην</span>); <span lang="grc">βλητός, -τέος</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐ-βλή-θην</span>). On the accent of compound verbals, see [425 c](04-accent.md#s425). <!-- page 157 -->

**a.** Some are derived from other stem forms (pres. and fut.), as <span lang="grc">φερ-τός, ί-τέον, δυνα-τός; μενετός</span> (cp. <span lang="grc">μενέ-ω ῀ μενῶ</span> fut.).

##### <a id="s472"></a> §472

Verbals in <span lang="grc">-τός, -τή, -τόν</span> either (1) have the meaning of a perfect passive participle, as <span lang="grc">κρυπτός</span> _hidden_, <span lang="grc">παιδευτός</span> _educated_, or (2) express _possibility_, as <span lang="grc">νοητός</span> _thinkable_, <span lang="grc">ὁρᾱτός</span> _visible_. Many have either signification, but some are passive only, as <span lang="grc">ποιητός</span> _done_. See [425 c](04-accent.md#s425). N.

**a.** Usually passive in meaning are verbals from deponent verbs, as <span lang="grc">μῑμητός</span> _imitated_.

**b.** Usually active in meaning are compounds derived from transitive active verbs; but some intransitive verbs make active verbals, as <span lang="grc">ῥυτός</span> _flowing_.

**c.** Many are active or passive, others only active: <span lang="grc">μεμπτός</span> _blamed_, _blamable_, _blaming_, <span lang="grc">πιστός</span> _trusting in_ (rare), _trusted_, <span lang="grc">ἄπρᾱκτος</span> _doing nothing, not done_, <span lang="grc">φθεγκτός</span> _sounding_.

##### <a id="s473"></a> §473

Verbals in <span lang="grc">-τέος, -τέᾱ, -τέον</span> express _necessity_ (cp. the Lat. gerundive in _-ndus_), as <span lang="grc">δοτέος</span> _that must be given_, <span lang="grc">παιδευτέος</span> _educandus_.
