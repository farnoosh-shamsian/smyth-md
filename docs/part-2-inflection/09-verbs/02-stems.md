# VERBS: STEMS

##### <a id="s367"></a> §367

A Greek verb has two kinds of stems: (1) the _tense-stem_, to which the endings are attached, and (2) a common _verb-stem_ (also called _theme_) from which all the tense-stems are derived. The tense-stem is usually made from the verb-stem by prefixing a _reduplication-syllable_ ([439](06-reduplication.md#s439)), and by affixing signs for _mood_ ([457](07-tense-suffixes-thematic-vowel-mood-suffixes.md#s457), [459](07-tense-suffixes-thematic-vowel-mood-suffixes.md#s459)) and _tense_ ([455](07-tense-suffixes-thematic-vowel-mood-suffixes.md#s455)). A tense-stem may be identical with a verbstem.

##### <a id="s368"></a> §368

**The Tense-stems**.—The tenses fall into nine classes called _tense-systems_. Each tense-system has its own separate tense-stem. <!-- page 109 -->

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | **SYSTEMS.** |  | **TENSES.** |
| I. | _Present_, | including | _present_ and _imperfect_. |
| II. | _Future_, | “ | _future active_ and _middle_. |
| III. | _First aorist_, | “ | _first aorist active_ and _middle_. |
| IV. | _Second aorist_, | “ | _second aorist active_ and _middle_. |
| V. | _First perfect_, | “ | _first perfect, first pluperfect_, and _fut. perf._, _active_. |
| VI. | _Second perfect_, | “ | _second perfect_ and _second pluperfect active_. |
| VII. | _Perfect middle_, | “ | _perfect_ and _pluperfect middle_ (_pass._), _future perfect_. |
| VIII. | _First passive_, | “ | _first aorist_ and _first future passive_. |
| IX. | _Second passive_, | “ | _second aorist_ and _second future passive_. |

The tense-stems are explained in detail in 497-597.

**a.** Since few verbs have both the _first_ and _second_ form of the same tense ([361](01-inflection-preliminary-remarks-355-380.md#s361)), most verbs have only six of these nine systems; many verbs do not even have six. Scarcely any verb shows all nine systems.

**b.** There are also secondary tense-stems for the future passive, the pluperfect, and the future perfect.

**c.** The tense-stems assume separate forms in the different moods.

##### <a id="s369"></a> §369

The _principal parts_ of a verb are the first person singular indicative of the tense-systems occurring in it. These are generally six: the present, future, first aorist, first (or second) perfect active, the perfect middle, and the first (or second) aorist passive. The future middle is given if there is no future active. The second aorist (active or middle) is added if it occurs. Thus:

<span lang="grc">λύ̄ω</span> _loose_, <span lang="grc">λύ̄σω, ἔλῡσα, λέλυκα, λέλυμαι, ἐλύθην</span>.

<span lang="grc">λείπω</span> _leave_, <span lang="grc">λείψω, λέλοιπα, λέλειμμαι, ἐλείφθην</span>, 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔλιπον</span>.

<span lang="grc">γράφω</span> _write_, <span lang="grc">γράψω, ἔγραψα, γέγραφα, γέγραμμαι</span>, 2 aor. pass. <span lang="grc">ἐγράφην</span>.

<span lang="grc">σκώπτω</span> _jeer_, <span lang="grc">σκώψομαι, ἔσκωψα, ἐσκώφθην</span>.

##### <a id="s370"></a> §370

The principal parts of deponent verbs ([356 c](01-inflection-preliminary-remarks-355-380.md#s356)) are the present, future, perfect, and aorist indicative. Both first and second aorists are given if they occur.

<span lang="grc">βούλομαι</span> _wish_, <span lang="grc">βουλήσομαι, βεβούλημαι, ἐβουλήθην</span> (passive deponent).

<span lang="grc">γ́γνομαι</span> _become_, <span lang="grc">γενήσομαι, γεγένημαι</span>, 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἐγενόμην</span> (middle deponent).

<span lang="grc">ἐργάζομαι</span> _work_, <span lang="grc">ἐργάσομαι, εἰργασάμην, εἴργασμαι, εἰργάσθην</span>.

##### <a id="s371"></a> §371

**Verb-stem** (or Theme).—The tense-stems are made from one fundamental stem called the verb-stem (or theme).

This verb-stem may be a root ([193](../01-parts-of-speech.md#s193)) as in <span lang="grc">τί̄-ω</span> _honour_, or a root to which a derivative suffix has been appended, as in <span lang="grc">τῑ-μά-ω</span> _honour_.

##### <a id="s372"></a> §372

A verb forming its tense-stems directly from a root is called a _primitive_ verb. A _denominative_ verb forms its tense-stems from a longer verb-stem, originally a noun-stem; as <span lang="grc">δουλόω</span> _enslave_ from <span lang="grc">δοῦλος</span> _slave_. Verbs in <span lang="grc">μι</span> ([379](#s379)), and verbs in <span lang="grc">ω</span> of two syllables (in the present indicative active, as <span lang="grc">λέγ-ω</span> _speak_) or of three syllables <!-- page 110 --> (in the middle, as <span lang="grc">δέχομαι</span> _receive_) are _generally_ primitive. Others are denominative.

##### <a id="s373"></a> §373

The verb-stem may show numerous modifications in form.

Thus, corresponding to the gradations in _sing_, _sang_, _sung_ ([35](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s35)), the verb <span lang="grc">λείπ-ω</span> _leave_ shows the stems <span lang="grc">λειπ-, λοιπ-</span> (2 perf. <span lang="grc">λέ-λοιπ-α</span>), <span lang="grc">λιπ-</span> (2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔ-λιπ-ο-ν</span>); the verb <span lang="grc">φεύγ-ω</span> _flee_ shows <span lang="grc">φευγ-</span> and <span lang="grc">φυγ-</span> (2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔ-φυγ-ο-ν</span>). In <span lang="grc">ῥήγνῡμι</span> _break_ we find the three stems <span lang="grc">ῥηγ, ῥωγ</span> (2 perf. <span lang="grc">ἔρρωγα</span>), <span lang="grc">ῥαγ</span> (2 aor. pass. <span lang="grc">ἐρράγην</span>). <span lang="grc">στέλλ-ω</span> _send_ has the stems <span lang="grc">στελ-</span> and <span lang="grc">σταλ-</span> (perf. <span lang="grc">ἔ-σταλ-κα</span>, 2 fut. pass. <span lang="grc">σταλ-ήσομαι</span>).

**a.** When the fundamental stem shows modifications, it is customary for convenience to call its shorter (or shortest) form the verb-stem, and to derive the other forms from it. The student must, however, beware of assuming that the short forms are _older_ than the other forms.

##### <a id="s374"></a> §374

The verb-stem may also show modifications in quantity, as present <span lang="grc">λύ̄-ω</span> _loose_, perfect <span lang="grc">λέ-λῠ-κα</span>.

N.—Various causes produce this variation. <span lang="grc">λύ̄ω</span> has <span lang="grc">ῡ</span> from analogy to <span lang="grc">λύ̄-σω, ἔ-λῡ-σα</span> where the verb-stem <span lang="grc">λῠ</span> has been regularly lengthened ([534](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/03-ii-future-system.md#s534), [543](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/04-iii-first-sigmatic-aorist-system.md#s543)). For Attic <span lang="grc">φθάνω</span> _anticipate_ Hom. has <span lang="grc">φθά̄νω</span> for <span lang="grc">φθανϝω</span> ([28](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s28), [147](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/06-syllables-and-their-quantity.md#s147) D.).

##### <a id="s375"></a> §375

**<span lang="grc">ω</span> Inflection and <span lang="grc">μι</span> Inflection**.—There are two slightly different methods of inflecting verbs, the first according to the _common_, the second according to the <span lang="grc"> **μι** </span> system. The names <span lang="grc">ω</span>-_verbs_ and <span lang="grc">μι</span> _verbs_ (a small class) refer to the ending of the first person singular active of the present tense indicative only: <span lang="grc">λύ̄-ω</span> _loose_, <span lang="grc">τίθη-μι</span> _place_.

**a.** In the <span lang="grc">ω</span> inflection the tense-stem ends in the thematic vowel. To this form belong all futures, and the presents, imperfects, and second aorists _showing the thematic vowel_.

##### <a id="s376"></a> §376

According to the ending of the verb-stem, <span lang="grc">ω</span>-verbs are termed:

\1. Vowel (or pure) verbs:

a. Not contracted: those that end in <span lang="grc">υ</span> or <span lang="grc">ι</span>, as <span lang="grc">λύ̄-ω</span> _loose_, <span lang="grc">παιδεύ-ω</span> _educate_, <span lang="grc">χρί̄-ω</span> _anoint_. Such verbs retain the final vowel of the stem unchanged in all their forms.

b. Contracted: those that end in <span lang="grc">α, ε, ο</span>, as <span lang="grc">τῑμῶ</span> _honour_ from <span lang="grc">τῑμά-ω, ποιῶ</span> _make_ from <span lang="grc">ποιέ-ω, δηλῶ</span> _manifest_ from <span lang="grc">δηλό-ω</span>.

\2. Consonant verbs, as:

Liquid or nasal verbs: <span lang="grc">δέρ-ω</span> _flay_, <span lang="grc">μέν-ω</span> _remain_.

Verbs ending in a stop (or mute), as <span lang="grc">ἄγ-ω</span> _lead_, <span lang="grc">πείθ-ω</span> _persuade_.

N.—Verbs ending in a stop consonant are called labial, dental, or palatal verbs. Consonant verbs do not retain the final consonant of the stem unchanged in all their forms. The final consonant may be assimilated to a following consonant, or may form with it a double consonant.

##### <a id="s377"></a> §377

**Thematic Vowel**.—Some tense-stems end in a vowel which varies between <span lang="grc">ο</span> and <span lang="grc">ε</span> (or <span lang="grc">ω</span> and <span lang="grc">η</span>) in certain forms. This is called the _thematic_ (or _variable_) vowel. Thus <span lang="grc">λύ̄ο-μεν λύ̄ε-τε, λύ̄ω-μεν λύ̄η-τε</span>, <!-- page 111 --> <span lang="grc">λύ̄σο-μεν λύ̄σε-τε</span>. The thematic vowel is written <span lang="grc">όε</span> or <span lang="grc">ώη</span>, as <span lang="grc">λῡόε-, γραφώη-</span>. See [456](07-tense-suffixes-thematic-vowel-mood-suffixes.md#s456).

##### <a id="s378"></a> §378

<span lang="grc">ο</span> is used before <span lang="grc">μ</span> or <span lang="grc">ν</span> in the indicative, and in the optative, <span lang="grc">ω</span> before <span lang="grc">μ</span> or <span lang="grc">ν</span> in the subjunctive, elsewhere <span lang="grc">ε</span> is used in the indicative (<span lang="grc">η</span> in the subjunctive).

##### <a id="s379"></a> §379

In the <span lang="grc">μι</span> inflection no thematic vowel is employed, and the endings are attached directly to the tense-stem. The <span lang="grc">μι</span> form is used only in the present, imperfect, and second aorist. In the other tenses, verbs in <span lang="grc">μι</span> generally show the same inflection as <span lang="grc">ω</span>-verbs. For further explanation of the <span lang="grc">ω</span> and the <span lang="grc">μι</span> inflection see [602](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/12-first-conjugation-or-verbs-in-omega.md#s602)ff., 717ff.

##### <a id="s380"></a> §380

**Meanings of the Tenses and Moods**.—In the synopsis ([382](00-introduction.md#s382)) meanings are given wherever these are not dependent on the use of the various forms in the sentence. The meanings of the subjunctive and optative forms and the difference between the tenses can be learned satisfactorily only from the syntax. Some of these meanings may here be given:

**a.** Subjunctive: <span lang="grc">λύ̄ωμεν</span> or <span lang="grc">λύ̄σωμεν</span> _let us loose_, (<span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν</span>) <span lang="grc">λύ̄ω</span> or <span lang="grc">λύ̄σω</span> (if) _I loose_, (<span lang="grc">ἵνα</span>) <span lang="grc">γράφω</span> (that) _I may write_.

**b.** Optative: (<span lang="grc">εἴθε</span>) <span lang="grc">λύ̄οιμι</span> or <span lang="grc">λύ̄σαιμι</span> (would) _that I may loose!_ (<span lang="grc">εἰ</span>) <span lang="grc">λύ̄οιμεν</span> or <span lang="grc">λύ̄σαιμεν</span> (if) _we should loose_.
