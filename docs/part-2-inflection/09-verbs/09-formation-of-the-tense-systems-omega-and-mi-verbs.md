# VERBS: FORMATION OF THE TENSE-SYSTEMS (Ω AND MI-VERBS)

## CHANGES IN THE VERB-STEM

##### <a id="s474"></a> §474

From the verb-stem (or theme) each tense-stem is formed by the addition of a tense-suffix ([455](07-tense-suffixes-thematic-vowel-mood-suffixes.md#s455)) or of a prefix, or of both. In 475-495 certain modifications of the verb-stem are considered.

##### <a id="s475"></a> §475

**Variation in Quantity**.—Many verbs of the first class ([498 f](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/02-1-present-system.md#s498)f.) show variation in the quantity of the vowel of the verb-stem, which is commonly long in the present but fluctuates in other tenses, as <span lang="grc">λύ̄-ω, λύ̄-σω, ἔλῡ-σα</span>, but <span lang="grc">λέλῠ-κα, λέλῠ-μαι, ἐλύ̆-θην</span>. (Other examples, 500.)

**a.** Some verbs of the Fourth Class ([523 c](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/02-1-present-system.md#s523)) lengthen a short vowel of the present in some other tenses. Thus, <span lang="grc">λαμβάνω</span> (<span lang="grc">λαβ-</span>) _take_, <span lang="grc">λήψομαι, εἴληφα, εἴλημμαι, ἐλήφθην</span>, but 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔλαβον</span>.

##### <a id="s476"></a> §476

**Vowel Gradation** (**35**, **36**).—Verbs of the first class show a variation between a strong grade (or two strong grades) and a weak grade. The weak grades, <span lang="grc">ῐ, ῠ, ᾰ</span>, appear especially in the second aorist and second passive systems; the corresponding strong grades, <span lang="grc">ει</span> (<span lang="grc">οι</span>), <span lang="grc">ευ</span> (<span lang="grc">ου</span>), <span lang="grc">η</span> (<span lang="grc">ω</span>), appear usually in the other systems (<span lang="grc">οι, ου, ω</span>, in the second perfect).

**a.** Expulsion of a short vowel between consonants (so-called syncope [493](#s493)) produces a weak form of the stem of the same grade as <span lang="grc">ι, υ, α</span> ([36](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s36)). Cp. <span lang="grc">γί-γν-ομαι</span> _become_ (aor. <span lang="grc">ἐ-γεν-ό-μην</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐ-πτ-ό-μην</span> (pres. <span lang="grc">πέτ-ο-μαι</span> _fly_) with <span lang="grc">ἔ-λιπ-ο-ν, ἔ-φυγ-ο-ν, ἐ-τάκ-η-ν</span> ([477 c](#s477)). So <span lang="grc">ἔ-σχ-ο-ν</span> _got_ from <span lang="grc">ἔχ-ω</span> _have_.

**b. <span lang="grc">α</span>** is the weak form of <span lang="grc"> **η** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **ᾱ** </span>), as in <span lang="grc">τήκω ἐτάκην</span>; and of <span lang="grc"> **ε** </span>, when <span lang="grc">ε</span> has <span lang="grc">λ, μ, ν, ρ</span> before or after it, as in <span lang="grc">τρέπω, ἐτράπην</span> ([479](#s479)).

##### <a id="s477"></a> §477

The following examples illustrate the principles of 476.

**a. <span lang="grc">ει οι ι</span>**: <span lang="grc">λείπω</span> _leave_, <span lang="grc">λείψω</span>, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">λέλ**οι**πα, λέλειμμαι, ἐλείφθην</span>, 2 aor. <span lang="grc"> **ἔλιπομ** </span> <!-- page 158 -->

N.—The weak form appears when the verb undergoes Attic reduplication ([446](06-reduplication.md#s446)); as in <span lang="grc">ἀλείφω</span> _anoint_, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">ἀλήλιφα, ἀλήλιμμαι; ἐρείκω</span> _tear_ (Ionic and poetic), 2 perf. <span lang="grc">ἐρήριγμαι</span>, 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἤρικον; ἐρείπω</span> _overthrow_, Epic <span lang="grc">ἐρήριπα</span>; but <span lang="grc">ἐρείδω</span> _prop_, <span lang="grc">ἐρήρεισμαι</span>.

**b. <span lang="grc">ευ ου υ</span>**: <span lang="grc">ἐλ**εύ**</span>(<span lang="grc">θ</span>)<span lang="grc">σομαι</span> _I shall go_, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">ἐλήλ**υ**θα</span> (Epic <span lang="grc">ἐλήλ**ου**θα</span>), 2 aor. (Epic <span lang="grc">ἤλ**υ**θον</span>); <span lang="grc">φ**εύ**γω</span> _flee_, <span lang="grc">φεύξομαι</span> or <span lang="grc">φευξοῦμαι</span>, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">πέφευγα</span>, 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔφυγον; ῥέω</span> _flow_ (for <span lang="grc">ῥευ-ω</span>, [43](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s43)), <span lang="grc">ῥ**εύ**σομαι, ἐρρ**ύ**ηκα</span> (<span lang="grc">ῥυε-</span>), 2 aor. pass. <span lang="grc">ἐρρ**ύ**ην</span>.

N.—<span lang="grc">χέω</span> _pour_ (for <span lang="grc">χευ-ω</span>, [43](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s43)), <span lang="grc">ἔχεα</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἔχευα</span>), has <span lang="grc">υ</span> in <span lang="grc">κέχυκα, κέχυμαι, ἐχύθην; σεύω</span> (poetic) _urge_, <span lang="grc">ἔσσευα, ἔσσυμαι, ἐσσύθην</span> or <span lang="grc">ἐσύθην</span> _rushed_. See also <span lang="grc">τεύχω</span> in the List of Verbs.

**c. <span lang="grc">η ω α</span>**: <span lang="grc">ῥ**ή**γ-νῡμι</span> _break_, <span lang="grc">ῥήξω, ἔρρηξα</span>, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">ἔρρ**ω**γα</span>, 2 aor. pass. <span lang="grc">ἐρρ**ά**γην; τ**ή**κ-ω</span> _melt_, <span lang="grc">τήξω, ἔτηξα, τέτηκα, ἐτήχθην</span>, 2 aor. pass. <span lang="grc">ἐτ**ά**κην</span>.

N.—Verbs of class c usually have <span lang="grc"> **ᾰ** </span> in the 2 aorist, <span lang="grc"> **ω** </span> in the 2 perfect (if there is one), elsewhere <span lang="grc">**η**. ω</span> occurs in the present in <span lang="grc">τρώγω</span> _gnaw_, 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔτραγον</span>.

##### <a id="s478"></a> §478

**Change of <span lang="grc">ε</span> to <span lang="grc">ο</span> in the Second Perfect**.—In the second perfect <span lang="grc"> **ε** </span> of the verb-stem is changed to <span lang="grc">ο</span>.

<span lang="grc">κλέπ-τ-ω</span> _steal_ <span lang="grc">κέκλοφα</span>, (<span lang="grc">ἀπο-</span>) <span lang="grc">κτείνω</span> _kill_ (<span lang="grc">κτεν-</span>, [519](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/02-1-present-system.md#s519)) <span lang="grc">-έκτονα, λέγ-ω</span> _collect_ <span lang="grc">εἴλοχα, πάσχω</span>, fut. <span lang="grc">πείσομαι</span> (from <span lang="grc">πενθσομαι</span>, [100](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s100)) <span lang="grc">πέπονθα, πέμπ-ω</span> _send_ <span lang="grc">πέπομφα, στέργ-ω</span> _love_ <span lang="grc">ἔστοργα, τίκτω</span> _beget_ <span lang="grc">τέτοκα, τρέπ-ω</span> _turn_ <span lang="grc">τέτροφα, τρέφ-ω</span> _nourish_ <span lang="grc">τέτροφα, φθείρ-ω</span> _corrupt_ <span lang="grc">ἔφθορα</span>. So in <span lang="grc">γίγ</span>(<span lang="grc">ε</span>)<span lang="grc">νομαι</span> _become_ <span lang="grc">ἐγενόμην, γέγονα; ἐγείρω</span> _awaken_ <span lang="grc">ἐγρήγορα</span> ([446](06-reduplication.md#s446)). This change corresponds to that of <span lang="grc">ει</span> to <span lang="grc">οι</span> ([477 a](#s477)).

##### <a id="s479"></a> §479

**Change of <span lang="grc">ε</span> to <span lang="grc">α</span>**.—In verb-stems containing <span lang="grc"> **λ, μ, ν, ρ** </span>, an <span lang="grc"> **ε** </span> is usually changed to <span lang="grc">α</span> in the first perfect, perfect middle, and second passive systems.

<span lang="grc">τρέπ-ω</span> _turn_, <span lang="grc">τέτραμμαι, ἐτράπην</span> (1 aor. <span lang="grc">ἐτρέφθην</span>); <span lang="grc">τρέφ-ω</span> _feed_, <span lang="grc">τέθραμμαι, ἐτράφην</span> (1 aor. <span lang="grc">ἐθρέφθην</span>); <span lang="grc">σπείρω</span> (<span lang="grc">σπερ-</span>) _sow_, <span lang="grc">ἔσπαρμαι, ἐσπάρην; φθείρω</span> (<span lang="grc">φθερ-</span>) _destroy_, <span lang="grc">ἔφθαρμαι, ἐφθάρην; στέλλω</span> (<span lang="grc">στελ-</span>) _send_, <span lang="grc">ἔσταλκα, ἔσταλμαι, ἐστάλην; τείνω</span> (<span lang="grc">τεν-</span>) _stretch_, <span lang="grc">τέτακα, τέταμαι, ἐτάθην</span> (1 aor.).

**a.** Also in the 2 aor. pass. of <span lang="grc">κλέπτω</span> _steal_ (<span lang="grc">ἐκλάπην</span>), <span lang="grc">πλέκω</span> _weave_ (<span lang="grc">ἐπλάκην</span>), <span lang="grc">τέρπω</span> _gladden_ (Epic <span lang="grc">ἐτάρπην</span>). Many of these verbs also show <span lang="grc">ο</span> in the second perfect ([478](#s478)).

##### <a id="s480"></a> §480

This <span lang="grc"> **ᾰ** </span> is also found in the second aorist active and middle of <span lang="grc">κτείνω</span> _kill_ (<span lang="grc">ἔκτανον</span> poetic), <span lang="grc">τέμνω</span> _cut_ (dialectal <span lang="grc">ἔταμον</span>), <span lang="grc">τρέπω</span> _turn_ (<span lang="grc">ἔτραπον</span> poetic), <span lang="grc">τέρπω</span> _gladden_ (<span lang="grc">ἐταρπόμην</span> poetic), poetic <span lang="grc">δέρκομαι</span> _see_ (<span lang="grc">ἔδρακον</span>). Also <span lang="grc">πέρθω, πτήσσω</span>.

##### <a id="s481"></a> §481

<span lang="grc"> **ε** </span> in the perfect middle in <span lang="grc">κέκλεμμαι</span> (<span lang="grc">κλέπτω</span> _steal_), <span lang="grc">πέπλεγμαι</span> (<span lang="grc">πλέκω</span> _weave_) is introduced from the present.

##### <a id="s482"></a> §482

The <span lang="grc"> **ᾰ** </span> in 479, 480 is developed from a liquid or nasal brought between two consonants ([35 b](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s35)). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔσταλμαι, τέταμαι</span> from <span lang="grc">ἐστḷμαι, τετṇμαι, ἐτάθην</span> from <span lang="grc">ἐτṇθην</span> ([20 b](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s20)). Here <span lang="grc">στλ, τν</span> represent weak grades of the stem.

##### <a id="s483"></a> §483

**a.** The variations <span lang="grc">**ε**, **ο**, **α**, **ω**</span> appear in <span lang="grc">τρ**έ**πω</span> _turn_, <span lang="grc">τρέψω, ἔτρεψα</span>, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">τέτρ**ο**φα, τέτρ**α**μμαι, ἐτρέφθην</span>, 2 aor. pass. <span lang="grc">ἐτράπην</span>; frequentative <span lang="grc">τρ**ω**πάω</span> ([867](../../part-3-formation-of-words/05-denominative-verbs.md#s867)).

**b.** The variations <span lang="grc">**ε**, **ο**, **ω**</span> appear in <span lang="grc">π**έ**τομαι</span> _fly_, <span lang="grc">π**ο**τέομαι</span> (poet.) and frequentative <span lang="grc">π**ω**τάομαι</span> (poet., [867](../../part-3-formation-of-words/05-denominative-verbs.md#s867)) _fly about_. <!-- page 159 -->

##### <a id="s484"></a> §484

<span lang="grc">**η**, **α**</span> **in the Second Perfect**.—In the second perfect <span lang="grc">ᾰ</span> of the verb-stem is lengthened to <span lang="grc">η</span> (<span lang="grc">ᾱ</span>): <span lang="grc">θάλλω</span> (<span lang="grc">θαλ-</span>) _bloom_, <span lang="grc">τέθηλα; φαίνω</span> (<span lang="grc">φαν-</span>) _show_, <span lang="grc">πέφηνα; μαίνω</span> (<span lang="grc">μαν-</span>) _madden_, <span lang="grc">μέμηνα; κρά̄ζω</span> (<span lang="grc">κραγ-</span>) _cry out_, <span lang="grc">κέκρᾱγα</span>.

##### <a id="s485"></a> §485

**Addition of <span lang="grc">ε</span>**.—a. To the verb-stem <span lang="grc">ε</span> is added to make the present stem in <span lang="grc">δοκέω</span> _seem_, fut. <span lang="grc">δόξω</span>, aor. <span lang="grc">ἔδοξα</span> (<span lang="grc">δοκ-</span>); so in <span lang="grc">γαμέω</span> _marry_, <span lang="grc">ὠθέω</span> _push_. Usually <span lang="grc">ε</span> is added in some stem other than the present.

**b.** In many verbs <span lang="grc">ε</span> is added to the verb-stem to form the tense-stems other than present, second aorist, and second perfect, e.g. <span lang="grc">μάχομαι</span> (<span lang="grc">μαχ-</span>) _fight_, <span lang="grc">μαχοῦμαι</span> ( = <span lang="grc">μαχε</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">ομαι</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐμαχεσάμην, μεμάχημαι</span>. So <span lang="grc">ἄχθομαι</span> _am grieved_, <span lang="grc">βούλουαι</span> _wish_, <span lang="grc">γίγνομαι</span> _become_, <span lang="grc">δέω</span> _want_, (<span lang="grc">ε᾽</span>)<span lang="grc">θέλω</span> _wish_, <span lang="grc">μέλλω</span> _intend_, <span lang="grc">μέλει</span> _is a care_, <span lang="grc">οἴομαι</span> _think_.

**c.** In some verbs <span lang="grc">ε</span> is added to form one or more tense-stems, as <span lang="grc">μένω</span> (<span lang="grc">μεν-</span>) _remain_, <span lang="grc">μεμένηκα</span> (<span lang="grc">μενε-</span>) to avoid <span lang="grc">-ν-κα</span> in the perfect. So, <span lang="grc">νέμω</span> _distribute_, <span lang="grc">ἔχω</span> _have_, <span lang="grc">οἴχομαι</span> _am gone_. So also <span lang="grc">δαρθάνω, ὀσφαίνομαι, ῥέω, στείβω</span> (poetic), <span lang="grc">τυγχάνω</span>.

**d.** Some verbs have alternative presents with or without <span lang="grc">ε</span>. Here sometimes one is used in prose, the other in poetry, sometimes both are poetic or both used in prose. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἕλκω</span> _draw_ (Hom. also <span lang="grc">ἑλκέω</span>), <span lang="grc">ἰάχω ἰαχέω</span> _sound_ (both poetic), <span lang="grc">μέδω μεδέω</span> (both poetic), <span lang="grc">ῥί̄πτω</span> and <span lang="grc">ῥῑπτέω</span> _throw_ (both in prose).

##### <a id="s485D"></a> §485 D

Some Ionic and poetic verbs adding <span lang="grc">ε</span> are <span lang="grc">ἀλέξω, ἄλθομαι, γεγωνέω, γηθέω, δουπέω, εἴρομαι, εἰλέω, ἐπαυρέω, κελαδέω, κέλομαι, κεντέω, κήδω, κτυπέω, κυρέω, λάσκω, μέδομαι, μύζω, πατέομαι, ῥῑγέω, στυγέω, τορέω, χάζω, φιλέω</span> (poetic forms), <span lang="grc">χρα&lt;\*&gt; σμέω; ἀμπλακίσκω, ἀπαφίσκω</span>; Epic <span lang="grc">ἐδιδάσκησα</span> (<span lang="grc">διδάσκω</span>), <span lang="grc">πιθήσω, πεπιθήσω&lt;\*&gt; πιθήσᾱς</span> (<span lang="grc">πείθω</span>), <span lang="grc">πεφιδήσομαι</span> (<span lang="grc">φείδομαι</span>).

##### <a id="s486"></a> §486

**Addition of <span lang="grc">α</span> and <span lang="grc">ο</span>**.—<span lang="grc">α</span> or <span lang="grc">ο</span> is added to the verb-stem in some verbs. Thus, <span lang="grc">μῡκάομαι</span> _bellow_ (Epic 2 aor. <span lang="grc">μύκον</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐμῡκησάμην; ἁλίσκομαι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἁλ-</span>) _be captured_, <span lang="grc">ἁλώσομαι</span> from <span lang="grc">ἁλο-; ὄμνῡ-μι</span> _swear_ (<span lang="grc">ὀμ-</span>) <span lang="grc">ὤμοσα, ὀμώμοκα</span> etc. (<span lang="grc">ὀμο-</span>); <span lang="grc">οἴχομαι</span> _am gone_, Epic <span lang="grc">οἴχωκα</span> or <span lang="grc">ᾤχωκα</span>.

##### <a id="s486D"></a> §486 D

<span lang="grc">α</span> is added also in <span lang="grc">βρῡχάομαι, γοάω, δηριάομαι, λιχμάω, μηκάομαι, μητιάω</span>. All these are mainly poetic.

##### <a id="s487"></a> §487

**Lengthening of Short Final Vowel**.—Verb-stems ending in a short vowel generally lengthen that vowel before the tense-suffix in all tenses (except the present and imperfect) formed from them. Here <span lang="grc">α</span> (except after <span lang="grc">**ε**, **ι**</span>, and <span lang="grc"> **ρ** </span>) and <span lang="grc"> **ε** </span> become <span lang="grc">**η**, **ο**</span> become <span lang="grc"> **ω** </span>.

<span lang="grc">τῑμά-ω</span> (<span lang="grc">τῑμα-</span>) _honour_, <span lang="grc">τῑμή-σω, ἐτί̄μη-σα, τετί̄μη-κα, τετί̄μη-μαι, ἐτῑμή-θην; θηρά-ω</span> (<span lang="grc">θηρα-</span>) _hunt_, <span lang="grc">θηρά̄-σω, ἐθήρᾱ-σα</span>, etc. ([389](00-introduction.md#s389)); <span lang="grc">ποιέω</span> (<span lang="grc">ποιε-</span>) _make_, <span lang="grc">ποιή-σω, ἐποίη-σα, πεποίη-κα, πεποίη-μαι, ἐποιή-θην; δηλόω</span> (<span lang="grc">δηλο-</span>) _manifest_, <span lang="grc">δηλώ-σω, ἐδήλω-σα</span>, etc.; <span lang="grc">ἐάω</span> _permit_, <span lang="grc">ἐά̄σω</span>, etc.

**a.** Note <span lang="grc">ἀκροά̄σομαι, ἠκροᾱσάμην</span>, etc., from <span lang="grc">ἀκροάομαι</span> _hear;_ <span lang="grc">χρήσω, ἔχρησα</span> from <span lang="grc">χράω</span> _give oracles;_ <span lang="grc">χρήσομαι, ἐχρησάμην</span> from <span lang="grc">χράομαι</span> _use;_ <span lang="grc">τρήσω</span> and <span lang="grc">ἔτρησα</span> from <span lang="grc">τετραίνω</span> _bore_ are from <span lang="grc">τρε-</span>.

**b.** Verb-stems adding <span lang="grc">ε</span> or <span lang="grc">ο</span> ([486](#s486)), and stems apparently receiving a short final vowel by metathesis ([128](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s128)), lengthen the short final vowel, as <span lang="grc">βούλομαι</span> (<span lang="grc">βουλ-</span>) _wish_, <span lang="grc">βουλή-σομαι</span> (<span lang="grc">βουλε-</span>, [485](#s485)), <span lang="grc">κάμνω</span> (<span lang="grc">καμ-</span>) _am weary_, <span lang="grc">κέκμη-κα</span> (<span lang="grc">κμα-</span>). <!-- page 160 -->

##### <a id="s488"></a> §488

**Retention of Short Final Vowel**.—Many verb-stems ending apparently in a short vowel retain the short vowel, contrary to 487, in some or all the tenses.

<span lang="grc">γελά̆-ω</span> _laugh_, <span lang="grc">γελά̆σομαι, ἐγέλᾰσα, ἐγελά̆σθην; τελέω</span> _finish_, <span lang="grc">τελῶ</span> from <span lang="grc">τελέ-ω, ἐτέλεσα, τετέλεκα, τετέλεσμαι, ἐτελέσθην; ἀνύω</span> _accomplish_, <span lang="grc">ἀνύ̆σω, ἤνῠσα, ἤνῠσμαι</span>.

**a.** The following verbs retain the final short vowel of the verb-stem in all tenses: <span lang="grc">ἄγα-μαι, αἰδέ-ομαι, ἀκέ-ομαι, ἀλέ-ω, ἀνύ-ω, ἀρέσκω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀρε-</span>), <span lang="grc">ἀρκέ-ω, ἀρό-ω, ἀρύ-ω, γελά-ω, ἐλαύνω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐλα-</span>), <span lang="grc">ἑλκύ-ω</span>, and <span lang="grc">ἕλκ-ω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἑλκ-ε-</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐμέ-ω, ἐρά-ω, ἔρα-μαι</span> (poet.), <span lang="grc">ἐσθίω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐσθι-, ἐδ-ε-, ἐδο-</span>), <span lang="grc">ζέ-ω, θλά-ω, ἱ̄λάσκομαι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἱ̄λα-</span>), <span lang="grc">κλά-ω</span> _break_, <span lang="grc">μεθύσκω</span> (<span lang="grc">μεθυ-</span>), <span lang="grc">ξέ-ω, πτύ̄-ω</span> (<span lang="grc">πτῡ-, πτῠ-</span>), <span lang="grc">σπά-ω, τελέ-ω, τρέ-ω, φθίνω</span> (<span lang="grc">φθι-</span>), <span lang="grc">φλά-ω, χαλά-ω, χέ-ω</span> (<span lang="grc">χυ-</span>). Also all verbs in <span lang="grc">-αννῡμι</span> and <span lang="grc">-εννῡμι</span> (except <span lang="grc">ἔσβηκα</span> from <span lang="grc">σβέννῡμι</span> _extinguish_), and <span lang="grc">ὄλλῡμι</span> (<span lang="grc">ὀλ-ε-</span>), <span lang="grc">ὄμνῡμι</span> (<span lang="grc">ὀμ-, ὀμε-, ὀμο-</span>), <span lang="grc">στόρνῡμι</span> (<span lang="grc">στορ-ε</span>).

**b.** The following verbs keep short the final vowel in the future, but lengthen it in one or more other tense-systems, or have double future forms, one with the short vowel, the other with the long vowel: <span lang="grc">αἰνέω</span> (<span lang="grc">αἰνέσω, ᾔνεσα, ᾔνεκα, ᾐνέθην, ᾔνημαι</span>), <span lang="grc">ἄχθομαι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀχθ-, ἀχθε-</span>), <span lang="grc">καλέ-ω, μάχομαι</span> (<span lang="grc">μαχ-ε-</span>), <span lang="grc">μύ̄ω, πί̄νω</span> (<span lang="grc">πι-, πο-</span>), <span lang="grc">ποθέ-ω, πονέ-ω, ἐρύ-ω</span> (Epic), <span lang="grc">φθάνω</span> (<span lang="grc">φθα-</span>).

**c.** In some verbs the final short vowel of the verb-stem remains short in one or more tense-stems, but is lengthened in the future, as <span lang="grc">δέ-ω</span> _bind_, <span lang="grc">δήσω, ἔδησα, δέδεκα, δέδεμαι, ἐδέθην</span>. So <span lang="grc">αἱρέω, βαίνω</span> (<span lang="grc">βα-</span>), <span lang="grc">βῡνέω</span> (<span lang="grc">βυ-</span>), <span lang="grc">δίδωμι</span> (<span lang="grc">δο-, δω-</span>), <span lang="grc">δύνα-μαι, δύ̄ω</span> (<span lang="grc">δῠ-, δῡ-</span>), <span lang="grc">εὑρίσκω</span> (<span lang="grc">εὑρ-ε-</span>), <span lang="grc">ἔχω</span> (<span lang="grc">σεχ-, σχε-</span>), <span lang="grc">θύ̄ω</span> (<span lang="grc">θῠ-, θῡ-</span>), <span lang="grc">ἵημι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἑ-, ἡ-</span>), <span lang="grc">ἵστημι</span> (<span lang="grc">στᾰ-, στη-</span>), <span lang="grc">λύ̄ω</span> (<span lang="grc">λῠ-, λῡ-</span>), <span lang="grc">τίθημι</span> (<span lang="grc">θε-, θη-</span>), <span lang="grc">τίνω</span> (<span lang="grc">τι-</span>), <span lang="grc">φύ̄ω</span> (<span lang="grc">φῠ-, φῡ-</span>), and the root <span lang="grc">ἐρ-, ῥε-</span> (<span lang="grc">εἶπον</span>).

**d.** Most of the verbs refusing to lengthen a final short vowel have verb-stems originally ending in <span lang="grc">ς</span> ([624](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/12-first-conjugation-or-verbs-in-omega.md#s624)); as <span lang="grc">τελέω</span> from <span lang="grc">τελεσ-\[ιγλιδε\]ω</span> (cp. <span lang="grc">τὸ τέλος</span>). By analogy to these, other verbs retain their short final vowel.

##### <a id="s488D"></a> §488 D

Here belong Epic <span lang="grc">ἀκηδέω, κοτέω, λοέω, νεικέω</span>, and the forms <span lang="grc">ἄασα, -άμην, ἄεσα. ἐρύω</span> shows <span lang="grc">ἐρῡ-</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐρῠ-</span>.

##### <a id="s489"></a> §489

**Insertion of <span lang="grc">ς</span>**.—In the perfect middle and first aorist passive systems, verbs which retain a short final vowel and some others usually insert <span lang="grc">ς</span> before the personal ending.

Thus, <span lang="grc">τελέω</span> ([488 d](#s488)), <span lang="grc">τετέλεσμαι, ἐτελέσθην; σπάω</span> _draw_, <span lang="grc">ἔσπασμαι, ἐσπάσθην; κελεύω</span> _order_, <span lang="grc">κεκέλευσμαι, ἐκελεύσθην; γιγνώσκω</span> _know_, <span lang="grc">ἔγνωσμαι, ἐγνώσθην</span>.

**a.** If the aorist passive ends in <span lang="grc">-θην</span> and not in <span lang="grc">-σθην</span>, the perfect middle does not insert <span lang="grc">ς</span>. Thus <span lang="grc">-θην</span>, not <span lang="grc">-σθην</span>, occurs in all verbs in <span lang="grc">-ευω</span> except <span lang="grc">λεύω</span> _stone to death_, in all verbs in <span lang="grc">-εω</span> which have <span lang="grc">-θην</span> preceded by <span lang="grc">η</span>, in all verbs in <span lang="grc">-οω</span> except <span lang="grc">χόω</span> _heap up_, and in all verbs in <span lang="grc">-αω</span> except those that retain <span lang="grc">ᾰ</span>. Stems originally ending in <span lang="grc">ς</span> ([624](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/12-first-conjugation-or-verbs-in-omega.md#s624)) properly show <span lang="grc">ς</span>.

**b.** If the aorist passive ends in <span lang="grc">-σθην</span>, the perfect middle may or may not insert <span lang="grc">ς</span>. Verbs in <span lang="grc">-αζω</span> and <span lang="grc">-ιζω</span> (stems <span lang="grc">-αδ, -ιδ</span>) regularly have <span lang="grc">ς</span> by 83, 587. In the case of other verbs some always show <span lang="grc">ς</span>, some never show <span lang="grc">ς</span>, and some are doubtful. In many cases the later usage with <span lang="grc">ς</span> has crept into the Mss. of <!-- page 161 --> the classical authors (so with the perfect of <span lang="grc">ἀλέω, βαίνω, δράω, ζώννῡμι, κλείω</span> (<span lang="grc">κλῄω</span>), <span lang="grc">σῴζω, χρί̄ω</span>, and with the aorist of <span lang="grc">παύω</span>).

**c.** The following verbs show an inserted <span lang="grc">ς</span> both in the perfect middle and the aorist passive in classical Greek: <span lang="grc">αἰδέομαι, γιγνώσκω, ἑλκύω, θλάω, θραύω, κελεύω, κλάω, κνα</span>(<span lang="grc">ί</span>)<span lang="grc">ω, κορέννῡμι, κυλί̄ω, ξύ̄ω, πίμπλημι, πρί̄ω, πτίττω, σβέννῡμι, σείω, σκεδάννῡμι, σπάω, τανύω, τελέω, τίνω, ὕ̄ω, φλάω, χόω, χρῴζω</span>.

**d.** The following form only the perfect middle with <span lang="grc">ς</span> in classical Greek: <span lang="grc">βῡνέω, ἕννῡμι</span> (<span lang="grc">εἷμαι</span>, but <span lang="grc">ἕστο</span> Hom.), <span lang="grc">ἐρύω, ζώννῡμι, ξέω, \*ὀδύσσομαι, πλέω, φλεύω</span> (Hdt.).

**e.** The following form only the aorist passive with <span lang="grc">ς</span> in classical Greek: <span lang="grc">ἄγαμαι, ἀκούω, ἀνύω, ἀρέσκω, ἄχθομαι, γελάω, δαίνῡμι, δράω, ἐλύω, ἔραμαι, ἐράω, ἱ̄λάσκομαι, κλείω</span> (<span lang="grc">κλῄω</span>), <span lang="grc">λεύω, μεθύσκω, μιμνῄσκω, ὀί̄ω, ὄνομαι</span> (Hdt.), <span lang="grc">παίω, παλαίω, πετάννῡμι, πίμπρημι, ῥαίω, ῥώννῡμι, στόρνῡμι, χαλάω, χράομαι, χράω, χρἱ̄ω</span>.

**f.** Only in post-classical Greek is <span lang="grc">ς</span> attested both in the perfect middle and aorist passive in <span lang="grc">ἀρκέω, ζέω, κλαίω</span>, (<span lang="grc">ἀπο</span>) <span lang="grc">λαύω, λόω, ὄλλῡμι, πνέω, πταίω, σάω, ψαύω</span>. —Only in the **perfect middle**: <span lang="grc">ἄγαμαι, ἀκούω, ἀνύω, γελάω, δράω, ἐμέω, ἔραμαι, κεράννῡμι, κολούω, μεθύσκω, ναίω, νάω</span> _spin_, <span lang="grc">ὀπυίω, παίω, παλαίω, πετάννῡμι</span> (and in Ionic), <span lang="grc">πίμπρημι</span> (Aristotle; earlier perf. <span lang="grc">πέπρημαι</span>), <span lang="grc">στόρνῡμι, χαλάω, ψαύω</span>. When the perfect middle is not attested in classical Greek some at least of the <span lang="grc">ς</span> forms from the above verbs may represent classical usage, provided the aorist passive has <span lang="grc">-σθην</span>.—Only in the **aorist passive**: <span lang="grc">ἀκέομαι, ἀλέω, ἀρύω, βαίνω, βῡνέω, ψεύω, εἰλύω, ἐλαύνω, ἔρυμαι, ἐρύω, ζώννῡμι, καίω, ξέω, μάχομαι, νέω</span> _heap up_, <span lang="grc">\*ὀδύσσομαι, πλέω, πτύω, σῴζω, φθάνω</span>.

**g.** Some verbs have double forms (one of which may be disputed) in the classical period: <span lang="grc"> **δύναμαι** </span>: <span lang="grc">ἐδυνήθην</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐδυνάσθην</span> (chiefly Ionic and poetic); <span lang="grc"> **κε&lt;\*&gt;άννῡμι** </span>: <span lang="grc">ἐκρά̄θην</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐκεράσθην; **κρούω**</span>: <span lang="grc">κέκρουμαι</span> better than <span lang="grc">κέκρουσμαι; **νέω**</span>: <span lang="grc">νἑνημαι</span> and <span lang="grc">νένησμαι; **ὄμνῡμι**</span>: <span lang="grc">ὀμώμομαι</span> (and <span lang="grc">ὀμώμοσται</span>), <span lang="grc">ὠμόθην</span> and <span lang="grc">ὠμόσθην</span>.— Dialectal or dialectal and late are <span lang="grc">ἐβώσθην</span> for <span lang="grc">ἐβοήθην</span> (<span lang="grc">βοάω</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐλήλασμαι ἠλάσθην</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐλαύνω</span>), <span lang="grc">κεκόρημαι</span> for <span lang="grc">κεκόρεσμαι</span> (<span lang="grc">κορέννῡμι</span>), <span lang="grc">πεπέτασμαι</span> (<span lang="grc">πετάννῡμι</span>).

**h.** Some verb-stems ending in <span lang="grc">ν</span> show <span lang="grc">-σ-μαι</span> in the perfect middle: <span lang="grc">ἡδύ̄νω, μιαίνω, παχύ̄νω, περαίνω, ὑφαίνω, φαίνω</span>. Thus <span lang="grc">πέφασμαι, ἥδυσμαι, μεμίασμαι</span>. Dialectal or late: <span lang="grc">θηλύ̄νω, κοιλαίνω, λεπτύ̄νω, λῡμαίνομαι, ξαίνω, ξημαίνω, σημαίνω</span>. On <span lang="grc">-μμαι</span> see [579](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/08-vii-perfect-middle-system.md#s579).

**i.** Observe that some vowel verbs inserting <span lang="grc">ς</span> do not lengthen the final vowel of the verb-stem in any tense (<span lang="grc">γελάω, τελέω</span>); and that some not inserting <span lang="grc">ς</span> (<span lang="grc">δέω, θύ̄ω, λύ̄ω</span>) do not lengthen the final vowel in some tenses. <span lang="grc">ἐπ-αινέω</span> _commend_ and <span lang="grc">παρ-αινέω</span> _exhort_ do not insert <span lang="grc">ς</span> and have the short vowel in _all_ tenses.

**j.** The insertion of <span lang="grc">ς</span> in the perfect middle started in the 3 sing. and 2 pl. Before the endings <span lang="grc">-ται</span> and <span lang="grc">-σθε, ς</span> was retained in the case of verbs with stems originally ending in <span lang="grc">ς</span> (as <span lang="grc">τελέω</span>), or where <span lang="grc">ς</span> developed from <span lang="grc">τ, δ, θ</span> ([98](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s98)) before <span lang="grc">-ται, -σθε</span> (<span lang="grc">πέπεισται</span> from <span lang="grc">πεπειθται</span>). See [409 b](00-introduction.md#s409), [624](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/12-first-conjugation-or-verbs-in-omega.md#s624). In all cases where the verb-stem did not originally end in <span lang="grc">ς</span>, the sigma forms are due to analogy; as in <span lang="grc">κεκέλευσμαι</span> (<span lang="grc">κελεύω</span>), <span lang="grc">πέπλησμαι</span> (<span lang="grc">πίμπλημι</span>), <span lang="grc">ἔγνωσμαι</span> (<span lang="grc">γιγνώσκω</span>).

##### <a id="s489D"></a> §489 D

Hom. has original forms in <span lang="grc">πεφραδμένος</span> (<span lang="grc">φραζω</span>), <span lang="grc">κεκορυθμένος</span> (<span lang="grc">κορύττω</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐπέπιθμεν</span> (<span lang="grc">πείθω</span>).

##### <a id="s490"></a> §490

**Addition of <span lang="grc">θ</span>**.—The present stems of some poetical verbs are made by the addition of <span lang="grc">θ</span>; as <span lang="grc">νή-θ-ω</span> _spin_, <span lang="grc">πλή-θ-ω</span> _am full_ (<span lang="grc">πίμ-πλη-μι</span>). Cp. [832](../../part-3-formation-of-words/01-word-formation-introductory.md#s832). <!-- page 162 -->

**a.** Most of the indicative forms seem to be imperfects, but since some have the force of aorists (e.g., Soph. _O. C._ 862, 1334, _O. T._ [650](../10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/13-inflection-of-omega-verbs.md#s650)), in certain editions they are regarded as second aorists, and the infinitives and participles are accented (against the Mss.) on the ultima (<span lang="grc">διωκαθεῖν, εἰκαθών</span>).

##### <a id="s490D"></a> §490 D

A few verbs make poetic forms by adding <span lang="grc">-θόε-</span> to the present or the 2 aorist tense-stem, in which <span lang="grc">α</span> or <span lang="grc">ε</span> (<span lang="grc">υ</span> once) takes the place of the thematic vowel of the simple verb. Such forms are chiefly Homeric, but occur sometimes in Attic poetry, very rarely in prose. Thus, <span lang="grc">φλεγέθω</span> (<span lang="grc">φλέγω</span> _burn_), <span lang="grc">ἐδιώκαθοι</span> (<span lang="grc">διώκω</span> _pursue_), <span lang="grc">ἔσχεθον</span> (<span lang="grc">ἔχω</span> _have_). <span lang="grc">θ-</span>forms are found in moods other than the indicative (<span lang="grc">εἰκάθω, εἰκάθοιμι, ἀμῡνάθατε, διωκάθειν, εἰκάθων</span>).

##### <a id="s491"></a> §491

**Omission of <span lang="grc">ν</span>**.—Some verbs in <span lang="grc">-νω</span> drop the <span lang="grc">ν</span> of the verbal stem in the first perfect, perfect middle, and first passive systems.

<span lang="grc">κρί̄νω</span> (<span lang="grc">κριν-</span>), _judge_, <span lang="grc">κέκρι-κα, κέκρι-μαι, ἐκρί-θην</span>. So also <span lang="grc">κλί̄νω</span> _incline_, <span lang="grc">πλύ̄νω</span> _wash_.

##### <a id="s492"></a> §492

**Metathesis**.—The verbal stem may suffer metathesis ([128](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s128)).

**a.** In the present: <span lang="grc">θνῄσκω</span> _die_, 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔθανον</span>, perf. <span lang="grc">τέθνηκα</span>.

**b.** In other tenses: <span lang="grc">βάλλω</span> _throw_ (<span lang="grc">βαλ-</span>), perf. <span lang="grc">βέβληκα, ἐβλήθην</span> (<span lang="grc">βλη-</span>); <span lang="grc">τέμνω</span> _cut_ (<span lang="grc">τεμ-ν-</span>), 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔτεμον</span>, perf. <span lang="grc">τέτμηκα; δέρκομαι</span> (<span lang="grc">δερκ-</span>) _see_, 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔδρακον; τέρπω</span> _delight_, 2 aor. pass. <span lang="grc">ἐτάρπην</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐτράπην</span> (both poetical).

##### <a id="s492D"></a> §492 D

See the List of Verbs for poetical forms of <span lang="grc">ἁμαρτάνω, δαρθάνω, θρά̄ττω, βλώσκω, δαμάζω, δέμω, πορ-</span>.

##### <a id="s493"></a> §493

**Syncope**.—Some verbs suffer syncope ([44 b](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s44)).

**a.** In the present: <span lang="grc">πί̄πτω</span> _fall_ for <span lang="grc">πι-π</span>(<span lang="grc">ε</span>)<span lang="grc">τ-ω, ἴσχω</span> _hold_ for (<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">ι-σ</span>(<span lang="grc">ε</span>)<span lang="grc">χ-ω</span> ([125 e](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s125)), <span lang="grc">μίμνω</span> for <span lang="grc">μι-μεν-ω</span>.

**b.** In the future: <span lang="grc">πτήσομαι</span> from <span lang="grc">πέτομαι</span> _fly_.

**c.** In the second aorist: <span lang="grc">ἔσχον</span> for <span lang="grc">ἐ-σεχ-ον</span> from <span lang="grc">ἔχω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐχ-</span> for <span lang="grc">σεχ-</span>, 125 e).

**d.** In the perfect: <span lang="grc">πέ-πτα-μαι</span> _have expanded_ from <span lang="grc">πετά-ννῡμι</span>.

N.—Syncopated forms are properly _weak_ stems ([476 a](#s476)).

##### <a id="s493D"></a> §493 D

See the List of Verbs for poetical forms of <span lang="grc">πέλω, πελάζω, μέλω, μέλομαι</span>; also <span lang="grc">ἔτετμον</span> _found_, <span lang="grc">ἔπεφνον</span> _slew_.

##### <a id="s494"></a> §494

**Reduplication**.—The verb-stem may be reduplicated.

**a.** In the present with <span lang="grc">ι</span>: <span lang="grc">γι-γνώ-σκω</span> (<span lang="grc">γνω-</span>) _know_, <span lang="grc">τί-θη-μι</span> _place_, <span lang="grc">ἵ-στη-μι</span> _set_, <span lang="grc">δί-δω-μι</span> _give_. The present reduplication may be carried over to other tenses: <span lang="grc">διδά</span> (<span lang="grc">κ</span>) <span lang="grc">σκω</span> _teach_ ([99](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s99)), <span lang="grc">διδάξω</span>. With <span lang="grc">ε</span>: <span lang="grc">τε-τραίνω</span> _bore_.

**b.** In the second aorist: <span lang="grc">ἄγω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀγ-</span>) _lead_, <span lang="grc">ἤγ-αγ-ον; ἕπομαι</span> _follow_, <span lang="grc">ἑσπόμην</span> (for <span lang="grc">σε-σπ-ομην</span>).

**c.** Regularly with <span lang="grc">ε</span> in the perfect.

##### <a id="s494D"></a> §494 D

Poetic <span lang="grc">ἀραρίσκω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀρ</span>) _fit_, and the intensives ([867](../../part-3-formation-of-words/05-denominative-verbs.md#s867)) <span lang="grc">μαρ-μαίρω</span> (<span lang="grc">μαρ-</span>) _flash_, <span lang="grc">πορ-φύ̄ρω</span> (<span lang="grc">φυρ-</span>) _grow red_, <span lang="grc">παμ-φαίνω</span> (<span lang="grc">φαν-</span>) _shine brightly_, <span lang="grc">ποι-πνύω</span> (<span lang="grc">πνυ-</span>) _puff_. Also with <span lang="grc">η</span> in <span lang="grc">δη-δέκ-το</span> _greeted_ (Mss. <span lang="grc">δείδεκτο</span>).

##### <a id="s495"></a> §495

**Iterative Imperfects and Aorists in <span lang="grc">-σκ</span>**<span lang="grc">όε-</span>.—Homer and Herodotus have iterative imperfects and aorists in <span lang="grc">-σκον</span> and <span lang="grc">-σκομην</span> denoting a customary or repeated past action. Homer has iterative forms in the imperfect and 1 and 2 aorist active and middle. Herodotus has no iteratives in the 1 aorist and few <!-- page 163 --> in the 2 aorist; and only from <span lang="grc">ω-</span>verbs. Herodotus regularly and Homer usually omit the augment. <span lang="grc">-αω</span> verbs have <span lang="grc">-αα-σκον</span> or <span lang="grc">-α-σκον; -εω</span> verbs <span lang="grc">-εε-σκον</span>, in Hom. also <span lang="grc">-ε-σκον. -α-σκον</span> is rare in other verbs than those in <span lang="grc">-αω</span>. The vowel preceding the suffix is always short.

**a.** The suffix <span lang="grc">-σκόε-</span> is added to the tense-stem. _Imperf.:_ <span lang="grc">φεύγε-σκε</span> (<span lang="grc">φεύγω</span> _flee_), <span lang="grc">ἔχε-σκον</span> (<span lang="grc">ἔχω</span> _have_), <span lang="grc">νῑκά-σκομεν</span> (<span lang="grc">νῑκάω</span> _conquer_), <span lang="grc">γοάα-σκε</span> (<span lang="grc">γοάω</span> _bewail_), <span lang="grc">κρύπτα-σκε</span> (<span lang="grc">κρύπτω</span> _hide_), <span lang="grc">καλέε-σκον</span> (<span lang="grc">καλέω</span> _call_), <span lang="grc">ζωννύσκετο</span> (<span lang="grc">ζώννῡμι</span> _gird_) _; 1 aor.:_ <span lang="grc">ἀπο-τρέψα-σκε</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀποτρέπω</span> _turn away_)_; 2 aor.:_ <span lang="grc">φύγε-σκε, στά-σκε</span> _stood_.
