##### <a id="s769"></a> §769

The optative forms <span lang="grc">εἴημεν, εἴητε, εἴησαν</span> are found only in prose writers. <span lang="grc">εἶμεν</span> occurs in poetry and Plato, <span lang="grc">εἶτε</span> only in poetry, <span lang="grc">εἶεν</span> in poetry and prose and more frequently than <span lang="grc">εἴησαν</span>.

##### <a id="s770"></a> §770

The indicative <span lang="grc">εἰμί</span> is for \*<span lang="grc">ἐσ-μι</span> ([37](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s37)); <span lang="grc">εἶ</span> is for \*<span lang="grc">ἐσι</span> (originally <span lang="grc">ἐσ-σί</span>, 463 b); <span lang="grc">ἐσ-τί</span> retains the original ending <span lang="grc">τι; εἰσί</span> is for (<span lang="grc">σ-</span>)<span lang="grc">εντι</span>, cp. Lat. _sunt;_ <span lang="grc">ἐσμέν</span>, with <span lang="grc">ς</span> before <span lang="grc">μ</span> despite 105; the <span lang="grc">ς</span> is due to the influence of <span lang="grc">ἐστέ</span>. The subjunctive <span lang="grc">ὦ</span> is for <span lang="grc">ἔω</span>, from <span lang="grc">ἐσ-ω</span>; the optative <span lang="grc">εἴην</span> is for <span lang="grc">ἐσ-ιη-ν; εἶμεν</span> for <span lang="grc">ἐσ-ῑμεν</span>, cp. Lat. _sĩmus_. The infinitive <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span> is for <span lang="grc">ἐσ-ναι</span>; the participle <span lang="grc">ὤν</span> is for <span lang="grc">ἐών</span>, from <span lang="grc">ἐσ-ων</span>. <!-- page 212 -->

##### <a id="s771"></a> §771

Old Attic <span lang="grc">ἦ</span> is from <span lang="grc">ἦα</span> (Hom.) = <span lang="grc">ἠσṃ</span>, i.e. <span lang="grc">ἐσ-</span> augmented + the secondary ending <span lang="grc">μ</span>, which becomes <span lang="grc">α</span> by 35 c. <span lang="grc">ἦς</span> for <span lang="grc">ἦσθα</span> is rare. The 3 pl. was originally <span lang="grc">ἦν</span>, contracted from <span lang="grc">ἦεν</span> (Hom.); this <span lang="grc">ἦν</span> came to be used as 3 sing. By analogy to <span lang="grc">ἦμεν ἦστε</span> the 1 sing. <span lang="grc">ἦν</span> was formed.

##### <a id="s774"></a> §774

<span lang="grc">εἶμι</span> in the indicative present means _I shall go_, _I am going_. See [1880](../../part-4-syntax/20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1880). For _I go_ <span lang="grc">ἔρχομαι</span> is used in the present indicative, but not (in prose) in the imperfect, or in the other moods. The scheme of moods and tenses is as follows: **Present**: indic. <span lang="grc">ἔρχομαι</span>, subj. <span lang="grc">ἴω</span>, opt. <span lang="grc">ἴοιμι</span> or <span lang="grc">ἰοίην</span>, imper. <span lang="grc">ἴθι</span>, inf. <span lang="grc">ἰέναι</span>, part. <span lang="grc">ἰών</span>. **Imperfect**: <span lang="grc">ᾖα</span>. **Future**: <span lang="grc">εἶμι, ἐλευσοίμην, ἐλεύσεσθαι, ἐλευσόμενος</span>.

##### <a id="s775"></a> §775

In the imperfect the older prose writers usually have <span lang="grc">ᾖα, ᾔεισθα, ᾔει-ν</span>, the later have <span lang="grc">ᾔειν, ᾔεις, ᾔει</span>. The plural forms <span lang="grc">ᾔειμεν</span> and <span lang="grc">ᾔειτε</span> are not classical. Prose writers seem to prefer <span lang="grc">ᾔεσαν</span> to <span lang="grc">ᾖσαν</span>. The <span lang="grc">ῃ</span> here is the stem <span lang="grc">ει</span> augmented.

\1. In the subjunctive <span lang="grc">μεθείω, μεθήῃ, ἀφέῃ, μεθῶμεν</span>.

\2. \3. Dor. has perf. <span lang="grc">ἕωκα, ἕωμαι</span>.

##### <a id="s778"></a> §778

Since <span lang="grc">ἵημι</span> is reduplicated (probably for <span lang="grc">σι-ση-μι</span>) the initial <span lang="grc">ι</span> should be short, as it is in Hom. (rarely in Attic poetry). <span lang="grc">ῑ</span> is probably due to confusion with the <span lang="grc">ῑ</span> of Hom. <span lang="grc">ἵ̄εμαι</span> (<span lang="grc">ϝί̄εμαι</span>) _strive_, a meaning that <span lang="grc">ἵεμαι</span> occasionally shows in Attic. <span lang="grc">ἵεμαι</span> meaning _hasten_ occurs only in the present and imperfect.

##### <a id="s779"></a> §779

<span lang="grc">ει</span> is for <span lang="grc">ε ¨ ε</span> in the second aorist active (<span lang="grc">ἐ-ἑ-μεν ῀ εἷμεν</span>), perfect active (<span lang="grc">ἑ-ἑ-κα ῀ εἷκα</span>), perfect middle (<span lang="grc">ἑ-ἑ-μαι ῀ εἷμαι</span>), second aorist passive (<span lang="grc">ε᾽ ¨ ἑ-θην ῀ εἵθην</span>). In the aorists <span lang="grc">ε᾽</span> is the augment, in the perfects the first <span lang="grc">ἑ</span> is the reduplication of the weak stem <span lang="grc">ἑ-</span>. The first aorist <span lang="grc">ἧ-κα</span> has the strong stem form. Present subj. <span lang="grc">ἱ̄ῶ, ἱ̄ῇς</span>, etc., are for <span lang="grc">ἱ̄ἑω, ἱ̄ἑῃς</span>, etc.; aor. subj. <span lang="grc">-ὧ, -ᾗς</span>, etc., are for <span lang="grc">-ἕ-ω, -ἕ-ῃς</span>, etc.

##### <a id="s780"></a> §780

Much confusion exists in the Mss. as regards the accentuation. Thus for <span lang="grc">ἱ̄εῖς</span> we find <span lang="grc">ἵεις</span>, and in Hom. <span lang="grc">προΐει</span> (present), as if from <span lang="grc">ἵω</span>. See [746 c](15-present-system.md#s746).

##### <a id="s784"></a> §784

All the forms of the present indicative except <span lang="grc">φῄς</span> are enclitic ([181 c](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s181)). —In composition <span lang="grc">σύμφημι, σύμφῃς</span> (but the Mss. often have <span lang="grc">συμφῄς</span> and <span lang="grc">συμφῇς</span>), <span lang="grc">συμφῶ, σύμφαθι</span>.

##### <a id="s785"></a> §785

In the optative <span lang="grc">φαῖτε</span> does not occur, perhaps by chance ([461](../09-verbs/07-tense-suffixes-thematic-vowel-mood-suffixes.md#s461), [683](13-inflection-of-omega-verbs.md#s683) a). <span lang="grc">φαῖμεν, φαῖεν</span> are ordinary Attic; <span lang="grc">φαίημεν, φαίησαν</span> are rare.

##### <a id="s786"></a> §786

Middle forms in present, imperfect, and future are dialectic.

##### <a id="s786D"></a> §786 D

Middle forms of <span lang="grc">φημί</span> are rare or unknown in Attic (Plato has perf. imper. <span lang="grc">πεφάσθω</span>), but common in other dialects; yet the pres. indicative middle is rare. Hom. has imperf. <span lang="grc">ἐφάμην, ἔφατο</span> or <span lang="grc">φάτο</span>, etc., imper. <span lang="grc">φάο, φάσθω</span>, etc., inf. <span lang="grc">φάσθαι</span> (and in choral poetry), part. <span lang="grc">φάμενος</span> (also in Hdt.). These middle forms are active in meaning.

##### <a id="s787"></a> §787

<span lang="grc">οὔ φημι</span> means _refuse_ (Lat. _nego_). In the meaning _assert_, <span lang="grc">φάσκω</span> is commonly used outside of the indicative. In the meaning _say often_, <span lang="grc">φάσκω</span> is used. <span lang="grc">ἔφησα</span> and <span lang="grc">φήσω</span> are aor. and fut. in the meanings _say yes_ and _assent_. <span lang="grc">ἔφην, ἔφη</span> (and <span lang="grc">φάναι</span>) often correspond to Lat. _inquam_, _inquit_.

## PECULIARITIES IN THE USE OF THE VOICE-FORMS, ETC.

##### <a id="s800"></a> §800

Some verbs in the present appear in classical Greek in the active voice only, as <span lang="grc">βαίνω</span> _go_, <span lang="grc">ἕρπω</span> _creep_, <span lang="grc">τρέω</span> _tremble;_ others in the middle only, as <span lang="grc">ἅλλομαι</span> _leap_, <span lang="grc">βούλομαι</span> _wish_, <span lang="grc">κάθημαι</span> _sit_, <span lang="grc">κεῖμαι</span> _lie_.

##### <a id="s801"></a> §801

Outside of the present some active verbs show middle forms especially in the future, as <span lang="grc">βήσομαι</span> _shall go_, <span lang="grc">ἀκούσομαι</span> _shall hear_ ([805](#s805)); and some verbs exclusively or chiefly deponent show active forms especially in the perfect, as <span lang="grc">γίγνομαι</span> _become_ <span lang="grc">γέγονα, μαίνομαι</span> _rage_ <span lang="grc">μέμηνα, δέρκομαι</span> poet., 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔδρακον</span>, perf. <span lang="grc">δ&lt;\*&gt;δορκα</span>.

##### <a id="s802"></a> §802

For the passive voice the middle forms sufficed in most cases; many middle futures are still used passively ([807](#s807), as <span lang="grc">ἀδικήσο-</span> <!-- page 219 --> <span lang="grc">μαι</span> _shall be wronged;_ and traces of the passive use of the aorist middle appear in Hom., as <span lang="grc">ἔβλητο</span> _was hit_. This use was largely abandoned when <span lang="grc">-ην</span> and <span lang="grc">-θην</span> came to be used as special marks of the passive. Originally neither <span lang="grc">-ην</span> nor <span lang="grc">-θην</span> was passive in meaning.

##### <a id="s802D"></a> §802 D

Hom. has <span lang="grc">ἐκτάμην</span> _was killed_, <span lang="grc">ἐσχόμην</span> _was stayed_. Cp. also <span lang="grc">ᾐδεσάμην</span> and <span lang="grc">αἴδεσθεν</span> (<span lang="grc">αἰδέομαι</span> _respect_), <span lang="grc">ὀἱ̄σατο</span> and <span lang="grc">ὠίσθην</span> (<span lang="grc">οἴομαι</span> _think_), <span lang="grc">ἐχολωσάμην</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐχολώθην</span> (<span lang="grc">χολόω</span> _enrage_).

##### <a id="s803"></a> §803

The second aorist in <span lang="grc">-ην</span> is primarily intransitive and shows active inflection (as <span lang="grc">ἔστην</span> _stood_). Many so-called passive forms are in fact merely intransitive aorists of active verbs, as <span lang="grc">ἐρρύην</span> from <span lang="grc">ῥέω</span> _flow_, <span lang="grc">κατεκλίνην</span> from <span lang="grc">κατακλί̄νω</span> _lie down_, and do not differ in meaning from the aorists of deponent verbs, as <span lang="grc">ἐμάνην</span> from <span lang="grc">μαίνομαι</span> _rage_.

##### <a id="s804"></a> §804

The aorists in <span lang="grc">-θην</span> that are called passive are often active or middle in meaning, as <span lang="grc">ἥσθην</span> _took pleasure in_ from <span lang="grc">ἥδομαι, ᾐσχύνθην</span> _felt ashamed_ from <span lang="grc">αἰσχύ̄νω</span> _disgrace_, <span lang="grc">αἰσχύ̄νομαι</span> _am ashamed;_ <span lang="grc">ὠργίσθην</span> _became angry_ from <span lang="grc">ὀργίζω</span> _anger_.

##### <a id="s810"></a> §810

**Middle Deponents**.—Deponent verbs whose aorists have an active or middle meaning with middle forms are called _middle deponents_. The aorist passive of such verbs, when it occurs, has a passive force. Thus <span lang="grc">αἰτιάομαι</span> _accuse_, <span lang="grc">ᾐτιᾱσάμην</span> _accused_, <span lang="grc">ᾐτιά̄θην</span> _was accused_. Others 813 c.

##### <a id="s811"></a> §811

**Passive Deponents**.—Deponent verbs whose aorists have the passive form but the active or middle meaning are called _passive deponents;_ as <span lang="grc">βούλομαι</span> _wish_, aor. <span lang="grc">ἐβουλήθην</span>. The future is usually middle in form. Most passive deponents express mental action of some sort.

##### <a id="s813"></a> §813

**Deponents with Passive Meaning**.—Some deponent verbs have a passive meaning. This is avoided by good writers in the present and imperfect or future passive, is not frequent in the aorist, but is common in the perfect and pluperfect passive. Thus <span lang="grc">ἀπεκρίνεται</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀπεκρίθη</span>) <span lang="grc">ταῦτα</span> _this answer is_ (_was_) _made_ is not good Greek. Few verbs show the passive meaning in most of these tenses; as <span lang="grc">ὠνέομαι</span> _buy_, _am bought_, <span lang="grc">ἐωνήθην</span> _was bought_, <span lang="grc">ἐώνημαι</span> _have bought, have been bought_.

**a.** Present and Imperfect: <span lang="grc">ἀγωνίζομαι</span> _contend_, _am contended for_, <span lang="grc">βιάζομαι</span> _force_, _am forced_, <span lang="grc">λῡμαίνομαι</span> _maltreat_, _am maltreated_, <span lang="grc">ὠνέομαι</span> _buy_, _am bought_.

**b.** Future Passive: <span lang="grc">ἀπαρνέομαι</span> _deny_, <span lang="grc">ἀπαρνηθήσομαι, ἐργάζομαι</span> _work_, _do_, <span lang="grc">ἐργασθήσομαι</span>.

**c.** Aorist Passive: These verbs (_middle deponents_, [810](#s810)) have also an aorist middle; the aorist passive is used in a passive sense: <span lang="grc">ἀγωνίζομαι</span> _contend_, <span lang="grc">αἰκίζομαι</span> _harass_, <span lang="grc">αἰνίττομαι</span> _speak darkly_, <span lang="grc">αἰτιάομαι</span> _accuse_, <span lang="grc">ἀκέομαι</span> _heal_, <span lang="grc">βιάζομαι</span> _force_, <span lang="grc">δέχομαι</span> _receive_, <span lang="grc">δωρέομαι</span> _present_, <span lang="grc">ἐργάζομαι</span> _work_, _do_, <span lang="grc">ἡγέομαι</span> _lead_, <span lang="grc">θεάομαι</span> _behold_, <span lang="grc">ἰ̄άομαι</span> _heal_, <span lang="grc">κτάομαι</span> _acquire_, <span lang="grc">λῡμαίνομαι</span> _maltreat_, <span lang="grc">λωβάομαι</span> _abuse_, <span lang="grc">μῑμέομαι</span> _imitate_, <span lang="grc">ὀλοφύ̄ρομαι</span> _lament_, <span lang="grc">προφασίζομαι</span> _feign an excuse_, <span lang="grc">χράομαι</span> _use_, <span lang="grc">ὠνέομαι</span> _buy_. <span lang="grc">ἀποκρί̄νομαι</span> has <span lang="grc">ἀπεκρί̄νατο</span> _answered_, <span lang="grc">ἀπεκρίθην</span> usu. means _was separated_.

**d.** Perfect and Pluperfect: These verbs use the perfect middle in the middle or the passive sense: <span lang="grc">ἀγωνίζομαι</span> _contend_, <span lang="grc">αἰνίττομαι</span> _speak darkly_, <span lang="grc">αἰτιάομαι</span> _accuse_, <span lang="grc">ἀποκρί̄νομαι</span> _answer_, <span lang="grc">ἀπολογέομαι</span> _make a defence_, <span lang="grc">βιάζομαι</span> _force_, <span lang="grc">ἐνθῡμέομαι</span> _consider_, <span lang="grc">ἐργάζομαι</span> _work_, _do_, <span lang="grc">εὔχομαι</span> _pray_, <span lang="grc">ἡγέομαι</span> _lead_, <span lang="grc">κτάομαι</span> <!-- page 222 --> _acquire_, <span lang="grc">λωβάομαι</span> _abuse_, <span lang="grc">μηχανάομαι</span> _devise_, <span lang="grc">μῑμέομαι</span> _imitate_, <span lang="grc">παρρησιάζομαι</span> _speak boldly_, <span lang="grc">πολῑτεύομαι</span> _act as_ (_discharge the duties of_) _a citizen_, <span lang="grc">πρᾱγματεύομαι</span> _am engaged in_, <span lang="grc">σκέπτομαι</span> _view_, <span lang="grc">χράομαι</span> _use_, <span lang="grc">ὠνέομαι</span> _buy_.

##### <a id="s814"></a> §814

**Active Verbs with Aorist Passive in a Middle Sense**.—The aorist passive of some active verbs has a reflexive or middle sense, either sometimes or always. Thus <span lang="grc">εὐφραίνω</span> _gladden_, <span lang="grc">ηὐφράνθην</span> _rejoiced_, <span lang="grc">κῑνέω</span> _move_, <span lang="grc">ἐκῑνήθην</span> _was moved_ or _moved myself_, <span lang="grc">φαίνω</span> _show_, <span lang="grc">ἐφάνην</span> _showed myself, appeared_ (<span lang="grc">ἐφάνθην</span> usually _was shown_).

**a.** These verbs are often called _middle passives_.

**b.** The middle and the passive form of the future of such verbs is often found, the middle being frequently preferred.

##### <a id="s818"></a> §818

Some verbs have a passive aorist rarely in a middle sense; with the middle aorist in a different meaning.

†<span lang="grc">μίζω</span> _bring_, <span lang="grc">ἐκομίσθην</span> _betook myself_, <span lang="grc">ἐκομισάμην</span> _carried off_.

<span lang="grc">ῴζω</span> _save_, <span lang="grc">ἐσώθην</span> _saved myself_ (_was saved_), <span lang="grc">ἐσωσάμην</span> _saved for myself_.

<span lang="grc">ψεύδω</span> _deceive_, <span lang="grc">ἐψεύσθην</span> _deceived myself_ (_was deceived_), <span lang="grc">ἐψευσάμην</span> _lied_.

##### <a id="s819"></a> §819

In some verbs showing 1st and 2nd aorist, or 1st and 2nd perfect, the first tenses are generally transitive, the second tenses generally intransitive. The future active of these verbs is transitive. In some transitive verbs the perfect (usually the 2nd perf.) is intransitive. <!-- page 223 -->

<span lang="grc"> **ἄγνῡμι** </span>: trans. <span lang="grc">κατά̄γνῡμι</span> _break_, <span lang="grc">-έᾱξα</span>; intrans. <span lang="grc">κατά̄γνυμαι</span> _break_, 2 aor. <span lang="grc">-εά̄γην</span>; 2 perf. <span lang="grc">-έᾱγα</span> _am broken_.

<span lang="grc"> **βαίνω** </span> _go:_ trans. <span lang="grc">βήσω</span> _shall cause to go_, 1 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔβησα</span>, Ion. and poet.; intrans. 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔβην</span> _went_, pf. <span lang="grc">βέβηκα</span> _have gone, stand fast_.

<span lang="grc"> **δύ̄ω** </span>: trans. _cause to enter, sink_, _put on_, <span lang="grc">δύ̄σω, ἔδῡσα, δέδῠκα</span>; intrans. _enter_, _pass under_, <span lang="grc">δύ̄ομαι, δύ̄νω</span>, 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔδῡν</span> _dived_, _went down_, <span lang="grc">δέδῡκα</span> _have entered, gone down_. In prose usually <span lang="grc">καταδύ̄ω</span> _make sink_, <span lang="grc">κατέδῡσα, καταδύ̄σω; καταδύ̄ομαι</span> _sink_, <span lang="grc">καταδύ̄σομαι, κατέδῡν</span>.—Of _another's_ clothes, <span lang="grc">ἐνδύ̄ω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐνέδῡσα</span>) means _put on_, <span lang="grc">ἀποδύ̄ω ἐκδύ̄ω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀπέδῡσα ἐξέδῡσα</span>) mean _take off;_ of _one's own_ clothes, <span lang="grc">ἐνδύ̄ομαι</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐνέδῡν</span> mean _put on_, <span lang="grc">ἀποδύ̄ομαι ἐκδύ̄ομαι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀπέδῡν ἐξέδῡν</span>) mean _take off_.

<span lang="grc"> **ἐγείρω** </span>: trans. _rouse_, _wake up_, <span lang="grc">ἐγερῶ, ἤγειρα</span>, etc.; intrans. <span lang="grc">ἐγείρομαι</span> _wake_, _am awake_, <span lang="grc">ἐγερθήσομαι, ἠγέρθην</span>, 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἠγρόμην</span> _awoke_, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">ἐγρήγορα</span> _am awake_.

<span lang="grc"> **ἵστημι** </span> _set:_ trans. <span lang="grc">στήσω</span> _shall set_, 1 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔστησα</span> _set_, <span lang="grc">ἐστάθην</span> _was set_, <span lang="grc">ἵσταμαι</span> _set for myself_, <span lang="grc">στήσομαι, ἐστησάμην</span>. Four active tenses are intrans.: 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔστην</span> (set myself) _stood_, pf. <span lang="grc">ἕστηκα</span> (have set myself) _stand_, _am standing_, <span lang="grc">εἱστήκη</span> _stood_, _was standing_, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">ἕστατον</span> _stand_, fut. pf. <span lang="grc">ἑστήξω</span> _shall stand_. So also <span lang="grc">ἵσταμαι</span> _set myself, stand_, <span lang="grc">στήσομαι</span>.

N.—The same distinction prevails in the compounds: <span lang="grc">ἀνίστημι</span> _raise up_, <span lang="grc">ἀνέστην</span> _stood up_, <span lang="grc">ἀφίστημι</span> _set off, cause to revolt_, <span lang="grc">ἀπέστην</span> _stood off, revolted_, <span lang="grc">ἀφέστηκα</span> _am distant, am in revolt;_ <span lang="grc">ἐφίστημι</span> _set over_, <span lang="grc">ἐπέστην</span> _set myself over_, <span lang="grc">ἐφέστηκα</span> _am set over;_ <span lang="grc">καθίστημι</span> _set down, establish_, <span lang="grc">κατέστην</span> _established myself_, _became established_, <span lang="grc">καθέστηκα</span> _am established_. The aorist middle has a different meaning: <span lang="grc">κατεστήσατο</span> _established for himself;_ <span lang="grc">συνίστημι</span> _introduce_, _unite_, <span lang="grc">συνέστημεν</span> _banded together_.

<span lang="grc"> **λείπω** </span> _leave:_ trans. <span lang="grc">λείψω, ἔλιπον, λέλοιπα</span> _have left, have failed, am wanting_. <span lang="grc">λείπομαι</span> mid. = _remain_ (_leave myself_), pass. = _am left, am left behind, am inferior;_ 2 aor. mid. <span lang="grc">ἐλιπόμην</span> _left for myself_ (in Hom. _was left, am inferior_), <span lang="grc">λείψομαι</span> _will leave for myself, will remain, be left_.

<span lang="grc"> **μαίνω** </span>: trans. _madden_, <span lang="grc">ἐκμαίνω, -μανῶ, -έμηνα</span>; intrans. _rage_, <span lang="grc">μαίνομαι, μανοῦμαι, ἐμάνην</span>, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">μέμηνα</span> _am raging_.

<span lang="grc"> **ὄλλῡμι** </span>: trans. _destroy_ (_perdo_), <span lang="grc">ἀπόλλῡμι, -ολῶ, -ώλεσα, -ολώλεκα</span> _have ruined_ (_perdidi_); intrans. _perish_ (_pereo_), <span lang="grc">ἀπόλλυμαι, -ολοῦμαι</span>, 2 aor. <span lang="grc">-ωλόμην</span>, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">-όλωλα</span> _am ruined_ (_perii_).

<span lang="grc"> **πείθω** </span>: trans. _persuade_, <span lang="grc">πείσω, ἔπεισα, πέπεικα</span> _have persuaded_, <span lang="grc">ἐπείσθην, πεισθήσομαι</span>; intrans. (_persuade myself_) _obey_, _believe_, <span lang="grc">πείθομαι, πείσομαι, ἐπείσθην, πέπεισμαι</span> _am convinced;_ 2 perf. <span lang="grc">πέποιθα</span> _I trust_ ( = <span lang="grc">πιστεύω</span>) is rare in prose.

<span lang="grc"> **πήγνῡμι** </span>: trans. _fix_, _make fast_, <span lang="grc">πήξω, ἔπηξα, ἐπήχθην</span>; intrans. _am fixed_, _freeze_, <span lang="grc">πήγνυμαι, παγήσομαι, ἐπάγην</span>, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">πέπηγα</span> _am fixed, frozen_.

<span lang="grc"> **πί̄νω** </span> _drink:_ 2 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔπιον</span> _drank_, 1 aor. <span lang="grc">ἔπῑσα</span> _caused to drink_.

<span lang="grc"> **πλήττω** </span>: trans. _terrify_, <span lang="grc">ἐκπλήττω, καταπλήττω, -έπληξα</span>; intrans. _am affrighted_, <span lang="grc">ἐκπλήττομαι, -επλάγην</span>.

<span lang="grc"> **πρά̄ττω** </span> _do:_ <span lang="grc">πέπρᾱχα</span> (probably late) _have done_, <span lang="grc">πέπρᾱγα</span> _have fared_ (_well_ or _ill_) and _have done_.

<span lang="grc"> **ῥήγνῡμι** </span>: trans. _break_, <span lang="grc">-ῥήξω, ἔρρηξα</span>; intrans. _break_, _burst_, <span lang="grc">ῥήγνυμαι, -ῥαγήσομαι, ἐρράγην</span>, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">ἔρρωγα</span> _am broken_.

<span lang="grc"> **σβέννῡμι** </span>: trans. _extinguish_, _put out_, <span lang="grc">ἀποσβέννῡμι, ἀπέσβεσα, ἀπεσβέσθην</span>; intrans. <!-- page 224 --> _be extinguished, go out_, <span lang="grc">ἀποσβέννυμαι, ἀποσβήσομαι, ἀπέσβην</span> _went out_, <span lang="grc">ἀπέσβηκα</span> _am extinguished_.

<span lang="grc"> **σήπω** </span>: trans. _make rot;_ intrans. _rot_, <span lang="grc">σήπομαι, ἐσάπην</span> _rotted_, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">σέσηπα</span> _am rotten_.

<span lang="grc"> **τήκω** </span>: trans. _cause to melt;_ intrans. _melt_, <span lang="grc">τήκομαι, ἐτάκην</span>, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">τέτηκα</span> _am melted_.

<span lang="grc"> **φαίνω** </span>: trans. _show_, <span lang="grc">φανῶ, ἔφηνα, πέφαγκα</span> _have shown_, <span lang="grc">πέφασμαι, ἐφάνθην</span> _was shown_, _made known;_ trans. also _show_, _declare_, <span lang="grc">φαίνομαι, φανοῦμαι, ἐφηνάμην</span> _showed_ (rare and poetic in the simple form; <span lang="grc">ἀπεφηνάμην</span> _declared_ is common); intrans. _show oneself, appear_, <span lang="grc">φαίνομαι, φανήσομαι</span> and <span lang="grc">φανοῦμαι, ἐφάνην</span> _appeared_, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">πέφηνα</span> _have shown myself, appeared_. The middle means _show oneself, appear;_ the passive, _am shown, am made evident_. <span lang="grc">φανήσομαι</span> means _shall appear_ or _shall be shown_, and is not very different in sense from <span lang="grc">φανοῦμαι</span> (but see [1738](../../part-4-syntax/20-the-verb-voices/04-passive-voice.md#s1738), [1911](../../part-4-syntax/20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1911)).

<span lang="grc"> **φθείρω** </span>: trans. _destroy_, <span lang="grc">διαφθείρω, -φθερῶ, -έφθειρα, -έφθαρκα</span>; intrans. _am ruined_, <span lang="grc">διαφθείρομαι, -εφθάρην, -φθαρήσομαι</span>, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">διέφθορα</span> _am ruined_ in Hom., _have destroyed_ in Attic poetry.

<span lang="grc"> **φύ̄ω** </span>: trans. _bring forth, produce_, <span lang="grc">φύ̄σω, ἔφῡσα</span>; intrans. _am produced, come into being_, <span lang="grc">φύ̄ομαι, φύ̄σομαι, ἔφῡν</span>, 2 perf. <span lang="grc">πέφῡκα</span> _am by nature_.
