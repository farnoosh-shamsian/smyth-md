# VERB-STEM AND PRESENT STEM: INFLECTION OF Ω-VERBS

##### <a id="s625"></a> §625

Verbs which end in <span lang="grc">ω</span> in the first person present indicative active, and deponent verbs in which the personal endings are preceded by the thematic vowel, have the following peculiarities of inflection:

**a.** The thematic vowel usually appears in all tenses except the perfect and pluperfect middle (passive) and the aorist passive (except in the subjunctive). These three tenses are inflected like <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verbs.

**b.** The present and future singular active end in <span lang="grc">-ω, -εις, -ει</span> ([463](../09-verbs/08-endings-of-the-verb-personal-endings.md#s463)). The ending <span lang="grc">-μι</span> appears only in the optative.

**c.** The thematic vowel <span lang="grc">ο</span> unites in the indicative with the ending <span lang="grc">-ντι</span>, and forms <span lang="grc">-ουσι</span> ([463 d](../09-verbs/08-endings-of-the-verb-personal-endings.md#s463)).

**d.** The third plural active of past tenses ends in <span lang="grc">-ν</span>.

**e.** The imperative active has no personal ending in the second person singular except <span lang="grc">-ο-ν</span> in the first aorist.

**f.** Except in the perfect and pluperfect the middle endings <span lang="grc">-σαι</span> and <span lang="grc">-σο</span> lose <span lang="grc">ς</span> and contract with the final vowel of the tense-stem ([465 a](../09-verbs/08-endings-of-the-verb-personal-endings.md#s465), b). In the optative contraction cannot take place (<span lang="grc">λύ̄οι-</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">ο</span>, <span lang="grc">λύ̄σαι-</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">ο</span>).

**g.** The infinitive active has <span lang="grc">-ειν</span> (for <span lang="grc">-ε-εν</span>) in the present, future, and second aorist; <span lang="grc">-ε-ναι</span> in the perfect; and <span lang="grc">-αι</span> in the aorist.

**h.** Active participles with stems in <span lang="grc">-οντ-</span> have the nominative masculine in <span lang="grc">-ων</span>.

##### <a id="s626"></a> §626

In 627-716 the method of inflection of all <span lang="grc">ω-</span>verbs, both vowel and consonant, is described. The examples are generally taken from vowel verbs, but the statements hold true of consonant verbs.

Forms of <span lang="grc">ω-</span>verbs which are inflected according to the non-thematic conjugation are included under the <span lang="grc">ω-</span>verbs.

## PRESENT AND IMPERFECT ACTIVE AND MIDDLE (PASSIVE)

For the formation of the present stem see [497](02-1-present-system.md#s497)-531.

##### <a id="s627"></a> §627

**Indicative**.—Vowel and consonant verbs in <span lang="grc">-ω</span> inflect the present by attaching the primary endings (when there are any) to the present stem in <span lang="grc">-όε</span> <!-- page 189 --> (<span lang="grc">-ώη-</span>). <span lang="grc">λύ̄ω, τῑμῶ</span> (<span lang="grc">τῑμά-ω</span>), <span lang="grc">φαίνω, λείπω</span>. The imperfect attaches the secondary endings to the present stem with the augment. See the paradigms, pp. 114, 120. For the active forms <span lang="grc">-ω, -εις, -ει</span>, see [463](../09-verbs/08-endings-of-the-verb-personal-endings.md#s463).

##### <a id="s628"></a> §628

<span lang="grc">-ῃ</span> and <span lang="grc">-ει</span> are found in the pres. fut. mid. and pass., fut. perf. pass. <span lang="grc">ε-</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">αι</span> yields <span lang="grc">ῃ</span> (written EI in the Old Attic alphabet, 2 a), which is usually given as the proper spelling in the texts of the tragic poets, whereas <span lang="grc">ει</span> is printed in the texts of prose and comedy. <span lang="grc">ει</span> was often written for <span lang="grc">ηι</span> (<span lang="grc">ῃ</span>) after 400 B.C., as in <span lang="grc">ἀγαθεῖ τύχει</span>, since both had the sound of a close long _e._ It is often impossible to settle the spelling; but <span lang="grc">βούλει</span> _wishest_, <span lang="grc">οἴει</span> _thinkest_, and <span lang="grc">ὄψει</span> _shalt see_ (from <span lang="grc">ὁράω</span>) have only the <span lang="grc">-ει</span> forms. <span lang="grc">-ει</span> is sometimes called Attic and Ionic in contrast to <span lang="grc">-ῃ</span> of the other dialects, including the Koiné.

##### <a id="s629"></a> §629

**Subjunctive**.—The present subjunctive adds the primary endings to the tense-stem with the long thematic vowel. For the endings <span lang="grc">-ῃς, -ῃ</span> see [463](../09-verbs/08-endings-of-the-verb-personal-endings.md#s463). Thus, <span lang="grc">λύ̄ω, -ῃς, -ῃ, τῑμᾷς</span> ( = <span lang="grc">τῑμά-ῃς</span>), <span lang="grc">τῑμᾷ</span> ( = <span lang="grc">τῑμά-ῃ</span>), <span lang="grc">φαίνωμεν, -ητε, -ωσι</span> (from <span lang="grc">-ωντι</span>). Middle <span lang="grc">λύ̄ω-μαι, λύ̄ῃ</span> ( = <span lang="grc">λύ̄η-σαι</span>), <span lang="grc">λύ̄η-ται; τῑμᾶ-σθον</span> ( = <span lang="grc">τῑμάη-σθον</span>); <span lang="grc">φαινώ-μεθα, φαίνη-σθε, φαίνω-νται</span>.

##### <a id="s630"></a> §630

**Optative**.—To the tense-stem ending in the thematic vowel (always <span lang="grc">ο</span>) are added the mood-sign <span lang="grc">-ῑ-</span> (<span lang="grc">-ιε-</span>) or <span lang="grc">-ιη-</span> ([459](../09-verbs/07-tense-suffixes-thematic-vowel-mood-suffixes.md#s459), [460](../09-verbs/07-tense-suffixes-thematic-vowel-mood-suffixes.md#s460)) and the secondary personal endings (except <span lang="grc">-μι</span> for <span lang="grc">-ν</span>, where the mood sign is <span lang="grc">-ῑ-</span>, [459](../09-verbs/07-tense-suffixes-thematic-vowel-mood-suffixes.md#s459)). In the 3 pl. we have <span lang="grc">-ιε-ν</span>.

**a.** The final vowel of the tense-stem (<span lang="grc">ο</span>) contracts with the mood suffix (<span lang="grc">ῑ</span>), <span lang="grc">ο-ῑ</span> becoming <span lang="grc">οι</span>. Thus <span lang="grc">λύ̄οιμι</span> (<span lang="grc">λύ̄ο-ῑ-μι</span>), <span lang="grc">λύ̄οις</span> (<span lang="grc">λύ̄ο-ῑ-ς</span>), <span lang="grc">λύ̄οιεν</span> (<span lang="grc">λύ̄ο-ιε-ν</span>), <span lang="grc">λῡοίμην</span> (<span lang="grc">λῡο-ί̄-μην</span>), <span lang="grc">λύ̄οιο</span> (<span lang="grc">λύ̄ο-ῑ-σο</span>).

##### <a id="s631"></a> §631

**Imperative**.—The present imperative endings are added to the tensestem with the thematic vowel <span lang="grc">ε</span> (<span lang="grc">ο</span> before <span lang="grc">-ντων</span>). The 2 pers. sing. active has no ending, but uses the tense-stem instead (<span lang="grc">παίδευε, φαῖνε</span>). In the middle <span lang="grc">-σο</span> loses its <span lang="grc">ς</span> ([466](../09-verbs/08-endings-of-the-verb-personal-endings.md#s466), [2](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s2) a); <span lang="grc">λύ̄ου</span> from <span lang="grc">λύ̄ε-σο, φαίνου</span> from <span lang="grc">φαίνε-σο</span>. On the forms in <span lang="grc">-ετωσαν</span> and <span lang="grc">-εσθωσαν</span> for <span lang="grc">-οντων</span> and <span lang="grc">-εσθων</span>, see [466](../09-verbs/08-endings-of-the-verb-personal-endings.md#s466), [2](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s2) b.

##### <a id="s632"></a> §632

**Infinitive**.—The present stem unites with <span lang="grc">-εν</span>: <span lang="grc">λύ̄ε-εν ῀ λύ̄ειν, λείπε-εν ῀ λείπειν</span>. In the middle (passive) <span lang="grc">-σθαι</span> is added: <span lang="grc">λύ̄ε-σθαι, λείπε-σθαι</span>.

##### <a id="s632D"></a> §632 D

Severer Doric has <span lang="grc">ἔχην</span> and <span lang="grc">ἔχεν</span>; Milder Doric has <span lang="grc">ἔχειν</span>; Aeolic has <span lang="grc">ἔχην</span>. Hom. has <span lang="grc">ἀμύ̄νειν, ἀμῡνέμεναι, ἀμῡνέμεν</span>.

##### <a id="s633"></a> §633

**Participle**.—The present participle adds <span lang="grc">-ντ-</span> to the present stem ending in the thematic vowel <span lang="grc">ο</span>. Stems in <span lang="grc">-ο-ντ</span> have the nominative singular in <span lang="grc">-ων</span>. Thus masc. <span lang="grc">λύ̄ων</span> from <span lang="grc">λῡοντ-ς</span>, fem. <span lang="grc">λύ̄ουσα</span> from <span lang="grc">λῡοντ-\[ιγλιδε\]α</span>, neut. <span lang="grc">λῦον</span> from <span lang="grc">λῡον</span>(<span lang="grc">τ</span>). See [301](../04-substantives/08-declension-of-participles.md#s301) a and N.

##### <a id="s633D"></a> §633 D

Aeolic has fem. <span lang="grc">-οισα</span> in the present and second aorist ([37 D.](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s37) 3), <span lang="grc">λύ̄οισα, λίποισα</span>.

##### <a id="s634"></a> §634

A few <span lang="grc">ω-</span>verbs in the present and imperfect show forms of the <span lang="grc">μι-</span>conjugation. These are usually Epic.

<span lang="grc">δέχομαι</span>, 3 pl. <span lang="grc">δέχαται</span> _await_ for <span lang="grc">δεχṇται</span>, part. <span lang="grc">δέγμενος</span>, imperf. <span lang="grc">ἐδέγμην</span>. But these are often regarded as perfect and pluperfect without reduplication. <span lang="grc">ἐδέγμην</span> <!-- page 190 --> in some passages is a second aorist ([688](#s688)).—<span lang="grc">ἔδω</span> _eat_ ([529](02-1-present-system.md#s529). 5), inf. <span lang="grc">ἔδμεναι.—ἐρύω</span> (or <span lang="grc">εἰρύω</span>) in <span lang="grc">εἰρύαται.—λοῦται</span> _wash_ is from <span lang="grc">λόεται</span>, not from <span lang="grc">λούω</span> (cp. [398](../09-verbs/00-introduction.md#s398) a).— <span lang="grc">οἶμαι</span> _think_ is probably a perfect to <span lang="grc">οἴομαι</span> (<span lang="grc">οἰ-ο-</span>).—<span lang="grc">οὐτάω</span> _wound_ in <span lang="grc">οὖτα, οὐτάμεναι</span> is 2 aor.—<span lang="grc">φέρω</span> _bear_, imper. <span lang="grc">φέρτε</span>.

### CONTRACT VERBS

##### <a id="s635"></a> §635

Verbs in <span lang="grc">-αω, -εω, -οω</span> contract the final <span lang="grc">α, ε, ο</span> of the verb-stem with the thematic vowel <span lang="grc">-όε</span> (<span lang="grc">-ώη</span>) in the present and imperfect tenses. Thus, <span lang="grc">τῑμάω τῑμῶ, ποιέω ποιῶ, δηλόω δηλῶ; ἐτί̄μαον ἐτί̄μων, ἐποίεον ἐποίουν, ἐδήλοον ἐδήλουν</span>. The rules of contraction are given in 4955; the paradigms, p. 120.

**a.** Open forms of <span lang="grc">-εω</span> verbs occur in the lyric parts of tragedy.

##### <a id="s636"></a> §636

**Subjunctive**.—The subjunctive adds the primary endings. For the contractions see [59](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/02-euphony-of-vowels.md#s59).

##### <a id="s637"></a> §637

**Optative**.—<span lang="grc">άοι</span> becomes <span lang="grc">ῷ, έοι</span> and <span lang="grc">όοι</span> become <span lang="grc">οῖ</span>. Thus. <span lang="grc">-άο-ῑ-μι ῀ -ῷμι, -αο-ίη-ν ῀ -ῴην, -αο-ί̄-μην ῀ -ῴμην; -έο-ῑ-μι ῀ -οῖμι, -εο-ίη-ν ῀ -οίην, -εο-ί̄-μην ῀ -οίμην; -όο-ῑ-μι ῀ -οῖμι, -ο-ίη-ν ῀ -οίην, -οο-ί̄-μην ῀ -οίμην</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">τῑμῴην</span> (<span lang="grc">τῑμαο-ίη-ν</span>), <span lang="grc">τῑμῴης</span> (<span lang="grc">τῑμαο-ίη-ς</span>), <span lang="grc">τῑμῴη</span> (<span lang="grc">τῑμαο-ίη</span>), <span lang="grc">τῑμῴμην</span> (<span lang="grc">τῑμαο-ί̄-μην</span>), <span lang="grc">ποιοῖο</span> (<span lang="grc">ποιέο-ῑ-σο</span>), <span lang="grc">ποιοῖτο</span> (<span lang="grc">ποιέο-ῑ-το</span>).

##### <a id="s638"></a> §638

In the _singular_ <span lang="grc">-αω</span> verbs usually end in <span lang="grc">-ῴην, -ῴης, -ώῃ</span>, rarely in <span lang="grc">-ῷμι, -ῷς, -ῷ. -εω</span> verbs usually end in <span lang="grc">-οίην, -οίης, -οίη</span>, rarely in <span lang="grc">-οῖμι, -οῖς, -οῖ</span> (<span lang="grc">-οῖ</span> chiefly in Plato).

##### <a id="s639"></a> §639

In the _dual_ and _plural_ <span lang="grc">-αω</span> verbs usually end in <span lang="grc">-ῷτον, -ῴτην, -ῷμεν, -ῷτε, -ῷεν</span>, rarely in <span lang="grc">-ῴητον, -ῳήτην, -ῴημεν, -ῴητε, -ῴησαν. -εω</span> verbs usually end in <span lang="grc">-οῖτον, -οίτην, -οῖμεν, -οῖτε, -οῖεν</span>, rarely in <span lang="grc">-οίητον, -οιήτην, -οίημεν, -οίητε, -οίησαν</span>.

##### <a id="s640"></a> §640

Few cases of the optative of <span lang="grc">-οω</span> verbs occur. In the sing. both <span lang="grc">-οίην</span> and <span lang="grc">-οῖμι</span> are found; in the plur. <span lang="grc">-οῖμεν, -οῖτε, -οῖεν</span>. For <span lang="grc">ῥῑγῴην</span> from <span lang="grc">ῥῑγόω</span> _shiver_ see [641](#s641).

##### <a id="s641"></a> §641

Several contract verbs have stems in <span lang="grc">-ᾱ, -η, -ω</span>.

These are the verbs of 394, 398 with apparently irregular contraction, and <span lang="grc">δρῶ</span> _do;_ with presents made from <span lang="grc">-ᾱ-\[ιγλιδε\]ω, -η-\[ιγλιδε\]ω, -ω-\[ιγλιδε\]ω</span>. Thus, from <span lang="grc">ζήω, ζήεις, ζήει</span> and <span lang="grc">χρήομαι, χρήε</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">αι, χρήεται</span> come <span lang="grc">ζῶ, ζῇς, ζῇ</span> and <span lang="grc">χρῶμαι, χρῇ, χρῆται</span>; so <span lang="grc">διψῆν, πεινῆν</span> from <span lang="grc">διψή-εν, πεινή-εν. ίδρόω, ῥῑγόω</span> ([398](../09-verbs/00-introduction.md#s398)) derive the forms in <span lang="grc">ω</span> and <span lang="grc">ῳ</span> from <span lang="grc">ἱδρω-, ῥῑγω-</span> (<span lang="grc">ἱδρώω, ῥῑγώω</span> from <span lang="grc">ἱδρωσ-\[ιγλιδε\]ω, ῥῑγωσ-\[ιγλιδε\]ω</span>). The forms in <span lang="grc">-οω</span> are from the weaker stems <span lang="grc">ἰδροσ-, ῥῑγοσ-</span>.

##### <a id="s641D"></a> §641 D

Hom. has <span lang="grc">διψά̄ων, πεινά̄ων, πεινήμεναι, μνά̄ομαι, χρήων</span> (Mss. <span lang="grc">χρείων</span>) _uttering oracles_, <span lang="grc">γελώω, ἱδρώω</span>. The verbs in 394, except <span lang="grc">διψῶ</span> and <span lang="grc">πεινῶ</span>, have stems in <span lang="grc">η</span> and <span lang="grc">ᾰ</span> ([36 e](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s36)); thus, in Hdt., <span lang="grc">χρᾶται</span> from <span lang="grc">χράεται</span>, but <span lang="grc">χρέω</span> imper., <span lang="grc">χρεώμενος</span> from <span lang="grc">χρῆο, χρηόμενος</span> by 34. Hom. and Ion. <span lang="grc">ζώω</span> has the stem <span lang="grc">ζω</span> (<span lang="grc">ζω-\[ιγλιδε\]ω</span>). Hdt. has <span lang="grc">ζῆν, διψῆν</span>, but <span lang="grc">κνᾶν, σμᾶν</span>.

##### <a id="s644"></a> §644

The assimilated forms are used only when the second vowel (in the unchanged form) stood in a syllable long by nature or position. Hence <span lang="grc">ὁροωμεν, ὁραατε, ὁραατο</span>, do not occur for <span lang="grc">ὁράομεν</span>, etc. (<span lang="grc">μνωόμενος</span> for <span lang="grc">μνᾱόμενος</span> is an exception.) The first vowel is lengthened only when the metre requires it, as in <span lang="grc">ἡβώοντες</span> for <span lang="grc">ἡβάοντες</span> ¯ ˘ ¯ ˘. Thus two long vowels do not occur in succession except to fit the form to the verse, as <span lang="grc">μενοινώω</span> for <span lang="grc">μενοινάω</span>; but <span lang="grc">ἡβώοιμι</span>, not <span lang="grc">ἡβώῳμι</span>. When the first vowel is metrically lengthened, the second vowel is not lengthened, though it may be long either in a final syllable (as in <span lang="grc">μενοινά̄ᾳ</span>) or when it represents the spurious diphthong <span lang="grc">ου</span> from <span lang="grc">-οντ-</span> (as in <span lang="grc">ἡβώωσα, δρώωσι</span> for <span lang="grc">ἡβάουσα, δρά̄ουσι</span> from <span lang="grc">-οντ\[ιγλιδε\]α, -οντι</span>).

##### <a id="s645"></a> §645

The assimilated forms include the “Attic” future in <span lang="grc">-αω</span> from <span lang="grc">-ασω</span> ([539](03-ii-future-system.md#s539)); as <span lang="grc">ἐλόωσι</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἐλάουσι</span>), <span lang="grc">κρεμόω, δαμάᾳ, δαμόωσι</span>.

##### <a id="s646"></a> §646

The assimilated forms are found only in the artificial language of Homer, Hesiod, and their imitators, and nowhere in the living speech. They are commonly explained as derived from the contracted forms by a process of ‘distraction,’ and as inserted in the text for the sake of the metre. Thus <span lang="grc">ὁρᾷς</span>, <!-- page 192 --> <span lang="grc">βοῶντες</span>, the spoken forms which had taken the place of original <span lang="grc">ὁράεις, βοάοντες</span>, in the text, were expanded into <span lang="grc">ὁράᾳς, βοόωντες</span>, by repetition of the <span lang="grc">α</span> and <span lang="grc">ο</span>. While the restoration of the original uncontracted forms is generally possible, and is adopted in several modern editions, a phonetic origin of many of the forms in question is still sought by some scholars who regard <span lang="grc">ὁρόω</span> as an intermediate stage between <span lang="grc">ὁράω</span> and <span lang="grc">ὁρῶ</span>. It will be observed, however, that the forms in 648 can be derived only from the unassimilated forms.

##### <a id="s647"></a> §647

In the imperfect contraction generally occurs, and assimilation is rare.

##### <a id="s648"></a> §648

Some verbs show <span lang="grc">εο</span> for <span lang="grc">αο</span>, as <span lang="grc">ἤντεον, τρόπεον, μενοίνεον, ποτέονται</span>. Cp. [649](#s649), [653](#s653).

##### <a id="s649"></a> §649

**<span lang="grc">-αω</span> verbs in Herodotus**.—Hdt. contracts <span lang="grc">-αω</span> verbs as they are contracted in Attic. In many cases before an <span lang="grc">ο</span> sound the Mss. substitute <span lang="grc">ε</span> for <span lang="grc">α</span> (<span lang="grc">τολμέω, ὁρέων, ἐφοίτεον</span>). This <span lang="grc">ε</span> is never found in _all_ the forms of the same verb, and the Mss. generally disagree on each occurrence of any form.—Hdt. always has <span lang="grc">-ῴην, -ῴμην</span>, in the optative.

##### <a id="s650"></a> §650

**<span lang="grc">-εω</span> verbs in Homer**.—**a.** Hom. rarely contracts <span lang="grc">εω</span> and <span lang="grc">εο</span> (except in the participle). In a few cases <span lang="grc">ευ</span> appears for <span lang="grc">εο</span>, as <span lang="grc">ποιεύμην</span>; rarely for <span lang="grc">εου</span>, as <span lang="grc">τελεῦσι</span>. When the metre allows either <span lang="grc">-εε</span> and <span lang="grc">-εει</span>, or <span lang="grc">-ει</span>, the open forms are slightly more common. <span lang="grc">ει</span> is often necessary to admit a word into the verse (as <span lang="grc">ἡγεῖσθαι, ἐφίλει</span>), and is often found at the verse-end. <span lang="grc">-έ-ε-αι, -έ-ε-ο</span>, in the 2 sing. mid. may become <span lang="grc">-εῖαι, -εῖο</span>, or <span lang="grc">-έαι, -έο</span>, by the expulsion of one <span lang="grc">ε</span>; as <span lang="grc">μῡθεῖαι</span> or <span lang="grc">μῡθέαι</span> _sayest_, <span lang="grc">αἰδεῖο</span> _show regard_.

**b.** <span lang="grc">νεικείω, τελείω</span>, from <span lang="grc">-εσ-\[ιγλιδε\]ω</span> (<span lang="grc">νεικεσ-, τελεσ-</span>) are older forms than <span lang="grc">νεικέω, τελέω</span>. See [488 d](../09-verbs/09-formation-of-the-tense-systems-omega-and-mi-verbs.md#s488), [624](12-first-conjugation-or-verbs-in-omega.md#s624). <span lang="grc">θείω, πλείω, πνείω</span> show metrical lengthening ([28 D.](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s28)).

**c.** On <span lang="grc">-ημεναι</span> in Hom. see [657](#s657).

##### <a id="s651"></a> §651

**<span lang="grc">-εω</span> verbs in Herodotus**.—**a.** Hdt. generally leaves <span lang="grc">εο, εω, εου</span>, open, except when a vowel precedes the <span lang="grc">ε</span>, in which case we find <span lang="grc">ευ</span> for <span lang="grc">εο</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀγνοεῦντες</span>). In the 3 plur. <span lang="grc">-έουσι</span> is kept except in <span lang="grc">ποιεῦσι</span>. For <span lang="grc">-έ-εο</span> in the 2 sing. mid. we find <span lang="grc">έ-ο</span> in <span lang="grc">αἰτέο. εε, εει</span>, in stems of more than one syllable, are usually uncontracted in the Mss., but this is probably an error. <span lang="grc">δεῖ</span> _it is necessary_ and <span lang="grc">δεῖν</span> are never written otherwise.—The Ion. <span lang="grc">ευ</span> for <span lang="grc">εο, εου</span>, occurs rarely in tragedy.

**b.** In the optative Hdt. has <span lang="grc">-έοι</span> after a consonant, as <span lang="grc">καλέοι</span>, but <span lang="grc">-οῖ</span> after a vowel, as <span lang="grc">ποιοῖμι, ποιοῖ</span>.

##### <a id="s652"></a> §652

**Verbs in <span lang="grc">-οω</span>**.—**a.** Hom. always uses the contracted forms except in the case of such as show assimilation like that in <span lang="grc">-αω</span> verbs.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="grc">οο</span> = (1) <span lang="grc">οω</span> : <span lang="grc">δηϊόοντο ῀ δηϊόωντο</span>. | <span lang="grc">οοι ῀ οῳ</span> : <span lang="grc">δηιόοιεν ῀ δηιόῳεν</span>. |
| (2) <span lang="grc">ωο</span> : <span lang="grc">ὑπνόοντας ῀ ὑπνώοντας</span>. | <span lang="grc">οου ῀ οω</span> : <span lang="grc">ἀρόουσι ῀ ἀρόωσι</span>. |

**b.** Hdt. contracts <span lang="grc">-οω</span> verbs as in Attic. Forms with <span lang="grc">ευ</span> for <span lang="grc">ου</span>, as <span lang="grc">δικαιεῦσι, ἐδικαίευν</span>, are incorrect.

##### <a id="s653"></a> §653

**Doric**.—Doric ([59 D.](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/02-euphony-of-vowels.md#s59)) contracts <span lang="grc">αε</span> and <span lang="grc">αη</span> to <span lang="grc">η; αει</span> and <span lang="grc">αῃ</span> to <span lang="grc">ῃ; αο, αω</span>, to <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> except in final syllables: <span lang="grc">τῑμῶ, τῑμῇς, τῑμῇ, τῑμᾶμες, τῑμῆτε, τῑμᾶντι, τί̄μη, τῑμῆν</span>. Monosyllabic stems have <span lang="grc">ω</span> from <span lang="grc">α ¨ ο</span> or <span lang="grc">α ¨ ω</span>. Some verbs in <span lang="grc">-αω</span> have alternative forms in <span lang="grc">-εω</span> ([648](#s648)), as <span lang="grc">ὁρέω, τῑμέω</span>. <!-- page 193 -->

##### <a id="s654"></a> §654

The contractions of <span lang="grc">-εω</span> verbs in Doric may be illustrated thus:

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Severer Doric | Milder Doric |
| <span lang="grc">φιλέω, φιλῶ, φιλίω</span> | <span lang="grc">φιλέω, φιλῶ</span> |
| <span lang="grc">φιλεῖς, φιλέσ</span>(?) | <span lang="grc">φιλεῖς, φιλέσ</span>(?) |
| <span lang="grc">φιλεῖ</span> | <span lang="grc">φιλεῖ</span> |
| <span lang="grc">φιλέομες, φιλίομες, φιλίωμες, φιλῶμες</span> | <span lang="grc">φιλέομες, φιλοῦμες, φιλεῦμες</span> |
| <span lang="grc">φιλῆτε</span> | <span lang="grc">φιλεῖτε</span> |
| <span lang="grc">φιλέοντι, φιλίοντι, φιλόντι</span> | <span lang="grc">φιλέοντι, φιλοῦντι, φιλεῦντι</span> |

**a.** <span lang="grc">ιω</span> for <span lang="grc">εο</span> is a diphthong. <span lang="grc">ευ</span> for <span lang="grc">εο</span> is common in Theocritus. In Cretan <span lang="grc">ι</span> ( = _y_) for <span lang="grc">ε</span> is often expelled (<span lang="grc">κοσμόντες ῀ κοσμέοντες</span>).

##### <a id="s655"></a> §655

Verbs in <span lang="grc">-οω</span> contract <span lang="grc">οο</span> and <span lang="grc">οε</span> to <span lang="grc">ω</span> in Severer Doric and to <span lang="grc">ου</span> in Milder Doric.

##### <a id="s656"></a> §656

**Aeolic**.—In Aeolic contract verbs commonly pass into the <span lang="grc">μι-</span>conjugation: <span lang="grc">τί̄μαιμι, -αις, -αι, τί̄μᾱμεν, τί̄μᾱτε, τί̄μαισι</span>, imperfect, <span lang="grc">ἐτί̄μᾱν, ἐτί̄μᾱς, ἐτί̄μᾱ</span>, etc. inf. <span lang="grc">τί̄μᾱν</span>, part. <span lang="grc">τί̄μαις, -αντος</span>, mid. <span lang="grc">τί̄μᾱμαι</span>, inf. <span lang="grc">τῑμά̄μεναι</span>. So <span lang="grc">φίλημι, φίλημεν, φίλητε, φίλεισι, ἐφίλην</span>, inf. <span lang="grc">φίλην</span>, part. <span lang="grc">φίλεις, -εντος</span>. Thus <span lang="grc">ὄρημι</span> from <span lang="grc">ὀρέω</span> = Att. <span lang="grc">ὁράω, κάλημι, αἴνημι</span>. So also <span lang="grc">δήλωμι</span>, 3 pl. <span lang="grc">δήλοισι</span>, inf. <span lang="grc">δήλων</span>. Besides these forms we find a few examples of the earlier inflection in <span lang="grc">-αω, -εω, -οω</span>, but these forms usually contract except in a few cases where <span lang="grc">ε</span> is followed by an <span lang="grc">ο</span> sound (<span lang="grc">ποτέονται</span>). From other tenses, e.g. the fut. in <span lang="grc">-ησω, η</span> has been transferred to the present in <span lang="grc">ἀδικήω, ποθήω</span>.

##### <a id="s657"></a> §657

Hom. has several cases of contract verbs inflected according to the <span lang="grc">μι-</span> conjugation in the 3 dual: <span lang="grc">σῡλή-την</span> (<span lang="grc">σῡλάω</span> _spoil_), <span lang="grc">προσαυδή-την</span> (<span lang="grc">προσαυδάω</span> _speak to_), <span lang="grc">ἀπειλή-την</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀπειλέω</span> _threaten_), <span lang="grc">ὁμαρτή-την</span> (<span lang="grc">ὁμαρτέω</span> _meet_); also <span lang="grc">σάω</span> 3 sing. imperf. (<span lang="grc">σαόω</span> _keep safe_). In the infinitive <span lang="grc">-ημεναι</span>, as <span lang="grc">γοήμεναι</span> (<span lang="grc">γοάω</span>), <span lang="grc">πεινήμεναι</span> (<span lang="grc">πεινά̄ω</span>, [641](#s641)), <span lang="grc">φιλήμεναι</span> (<span lang="grc">φιλέω</span>), <span lang="grc">φορήμεναι</span> and <span lang="grc">φορῆναι</span> (<span lang="grc">φορέω</span>). But <span lang="grc">ἀγῑνέω</span> has <span lang="grc">ἀγῑνέμεναι</span>.

## FUTURE ACTIVE AND MIDDLE ([532 f](03-ii-future-system.md#s532)f.). FUTURE PERFECT ([580 f](08-vii-perfect-middle-system.md#s580)f.)

##### <a id="s658"></a> §658

All vowel and consonant verbs in <span lang="grc">-ω</span> inflect the future alike.

##### <a id="s659"></a> §659

**Indicative**.—The future active and middle add the primary endings, and are inflected like the present; as <span lang="grc">λύ̄σω, λύ̄σομαι</span>. On the two endings of the second singular middle, see [628](#s628). Liquid verbs, Attic futures ([538](03-ii-future-system.md#s538)), Doric futures ([540](03-ii-future-system.md#s540)) are inflected like contract verbs in <span lang="grc">-εω</span>; thus <span lang="grc">φανῶ φανοῦμαι, καλῶ καλοῦμαι</span>, and <span lang="grc">πεσοῦμαι</span>, follow <span lang="grc">ποιῶ ποιοῦμαι</span> ([385](../09-verbs/00-introduction.md#s385)).

**a.** The only future perfect active from an <span lang="grc">ω-</span>verb is <span lang="grc">τεθνήξω</span> _shall be dead_ ([584](08-vii-perfect-middle-system.md#s584)), which is inflected like a future active. Ordinarily the periphrastic formation is used: <span lang="grc">λελευκὼς ἔσομαι</span> _shall have loosed_. The future perfect passive (<span lang="grc">λελύ̄σομαι</span> _shall have been loosed_) is inflected like the future middle. The periphrastic forms and the future perfect passive rarely occur outside of the indicative.

##### <a id="s660"></a> §660

**Optative**.—The inflection is like the present: <span lang="grc">λύ̄σο-ῑ-μι, λῡσο-ί̄-μην</span>. In the optative singular of liquid verbs, <span lang="grc">-ιη-ν, -ιη-ς, -ιη</span>, in the dual and plural <span lang="grc">-ῑ-τον</span>, <!-- page 194 --> <span lang="grc">-ῑ-την, -ῑ-μεν, -ῑ-τε, -ιε-ν</span>, are added to the stem ending in the thematic vowel <span lang="grc">ο</span>; thus <span lang="grc">φανεο-ίην ῀ φανοίην, φανέο-ῑ-μεν ῀ φανοῖμεν</span>. So in Attic futures in <span lang="grc">-άζω</span>, as <span lang="grc">βιβάζω</span> ([539 d](03-ii-future-system.md#s539)) _cause to go:_ <span lang="grc">βιβῴην, -ῴης, -ῴη</span>, pl. <span lang="grc">βιβῷμεν</span>.

##### <a id="s661"></a> §661

**Infinitive**.—The future infinitive active adds <span lang="grc">-εν</span>, as <span lang="grc">λύ̄σειν</span> from <span lang="grc">λύ̄σε-εν, φανεῖν</span> from <span lang="grc">φανέ</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">ε-εν</span>. The infinitive middle adds <span lang="grc">-σθαι</span>, as <span lang="grc">λύ̄σε-σθαι, φανεῖσθαι</span>, from <span lang="grc">φανέ</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">ε-σθαι</span>.

##### <a id="s661D"></a> §661 D

Hom. has <span lang="grc">ἀξέμεναι, ἀξέμεν, ἄξειν</span>. Doric has <span lang="grc">-ην, -ειν</span>; Aeolic has <span lang="grc">-ην</span>.

##### <a id="s662"></a> §662

**Participle**.—The future participle has the same endings as the present: <span lang="grc">λύ̄σων λύ̄σουσα λῦσον, φανῶν φανοῦσα φανοῦν</span>; middle, <span lang="grc">λῡσόμενος, φανούμενος</span>.

## FIRST AND SECOND FUTURE PASSIVE ([589](09-viii-first-passive-system-thetaeta-passive.md#s589), [597](10-ix-second-passive-system-h-passive.md#s597))

##### <a id="s663"></a> §663

All verbs inflect the first and second future passive alike, that is, like the future middle.

##### <a id="s664"></a> §664

The indicative adds <span lang="grc">-μαι</span> to the stem ending in <span lang="grc">-θησο-</span> or <span lang="grc">-ησο-</span>, as <span lang="grc">λυθήσο-μαι, φανή-σο-μαι</span>. For the two forms of the second person singular see [628](#s628). The optative adds <span lang="grc">-ῑ-μην</span>, as <span lang="grc">λυθησο-ί̄-μην, φανησο-ί̄-μην</span>. The infinitive adds <span lang="grc">-σθαι</span>, as <span lang="grc">λυθή-σε-σθαι, φανή-σε-σθαι</span>. The participle adds <span lang="grc">-μενος</span>, as <span lang="grc">λυθησό-μενος, φανησό-μενος</span>.

## FIRST AORIST ACTIVE AND MIDDLE ([542](04-iii-first-sigmatic-aorist-system.md#s542))

##### <a id="s665"></a> §665

All vowel and consonant <span lang="grc">ω-</span>verbs inflect the first aorist alike.

##### <a id="s666"></a> §666

**Indicative**.—The secondary endings of the first aorist active were originally added to the stem ending in <span lang="grc">-σ-</span>; thus, <span lang="grc">ἐλῡσṃ, ἐλῡσ-ς, ἐλῡσ-τ, ἐλῡσ-μεν, ἐλῡσ-τε, ἐλῡσ-ντ</span>. From <span lang="grc">ἐλῡσṃ</span> came <span lang="grc">ἔλῡσα</span> (by 35 c), the <span lang="grc">α</span> of which spread to the other forms except in the 3 sing., where <span lang="grc">ε</span> was borrowed from the perfect.

**a.** In the middle the secondary endings are added to the stem ending in <span lang="grc">-σα-</span>. For the loss of <span lang="grc">ς</span> in <span lang="grc">-σο</span>, see [465 b](../09-verbs/08-endings-of-the-verb-personal-endings.md#s465).

##### <a id="s667"></a> §667

**Subjunctive**.—In the subjunctive the long thematic vowel <span lang="grc">-ώη-</span> is substituted for the <span lang="grc">α</span> of the indicative, and these forms are inflected like the present subjunctive: <span lang="grc">λύ̄σω λύ̄σωμαι, φήνω φήνωμαι</span>. For the loss of <span lang="grc">ς</span> in <span lang="grc">-σαι</span> see [465 a](../09-verbs/08-endings-of-the-verb-personal-endings.md#s465).

##### <a id="s667D"></a> §667 D

Hom. has forms with the short thematic vowel, as <span lang="grc">ἐρύσσομεν, ἀλγήσετε, νεμεσήσετε; μῡθήσομαι, ἐφάψεαι, ἱ̄λασόμεσθα, δηλήσεται</span>. In such forms aorist subjunctive and future indicative are alike ([532](03-ii-future-system.md#s532)). Pindar has <span lang="grc">βά̄σομεν, αὐδά̄σομεν</span> ([457 D.](../09-verbs/07-tense-suffixes-thematic-vowel-mood-suffixes.md#s457)).

##### <a id="s668"></a> §668

**Optative**.—To the stem ending in <span lang="grc">α</span> the mood-suffix <span lang="grc">ῑ</span> is added, making <span lang="grc">αι</span>, to which the same endings are affixed as in the present: <span lang="grc">λύ̄σα-ῑ-μι ῀ λύ̄σαιμι, λῡσα-ί̄-μην ῀ λῡσαίμην, φήνα-ῑ-μι ῀ φήναιμι</span>. The inflection in the middle is like that of the present. For the loss of <span lang="grc">ς</span> in <span lang="grc">-σο</span> see [465 b](../09-verbs/08-endings-of-the-verb-personal-endings.md#s465).—In the active <span lang="grc">-ειας, -ειε, -ειαν</span> are more common than <span lang="grc">-αις, -αι, -αιεν</span>.

##### <a id="s668D"></a> §668 D

Hom. has both sets of endings, but that in <span lang="grc">αι</span> is rarer. In the drama <span lang="grc">-ειας</span> is very much commoner than <span lang="grc">-αις. -αις</span> is most frequent in Plato and Xenophon, less common in poetry, and very rare in the orators. Neither Thuc. nor Hdt. has <span lang="grc">-αις. -αι</span> is rare in prose, most examples being in Plato and Demosthenes. Hdt. has no case. In Aristotle <span lang="grc">-αι</span> is as common as <span lang="grc">-ειε. -αῖεν</span> is very rare in poetry, in Thuc. and Hdt., but slightly better represented in Xenophon and the orators. <span lang="grc">-ειαν</span> is probably the regular form in the drama.—The forms in <span lang="grc">-ειας, -ειε, -ειαν</span> are called “Aeolic,” but do not occur in the remains of that dialect. <!-- page 195 -->

##### <a id="s669"></a> §669

**Imperative**.—The regular endings ([462](../09-verbs/08-endings-of-the-verb-personal-endings.md#s462)) are added to the stem in <span lang="grc">-σα</span> (or <span lang="grc">-α</span> in liquid verbs) except in the active and middle 2 sing., in which <span lang="grc">-ον</span> and <span lang="grc">-αι</span> take the place of <span lang="grc">-α</span>: <span lang="grc">λῦσον λῡσάτω, λῦσαι λῡσάσθω, φῆνον φηνάτω, φῆναι φηνάσθω</span>.

##### <a id="s670"></a> §670

**Infinitive**.—The aorist active infinitive ends in <span lang="grc">-αι</span>, which is an old dative: the middle ends in <span lang="grc">-σθαι</span>: <span lang="grc">λῦσαι λύ̄σα-σθαι, φῆναι φήνα-σθαι, πλέξαι πλέξα-σθαι</span>.

##### <a id="s671"></a> §671

**Participle**.—The active participle adds <span lang="grc">-ντ</span> like the present: masc. <span lang="grc">λύ̄σᾱς</span> from <span lang="grc">λῡσαντ-ς</span>, fem. <span lang="grc">λύσᾱσα</span> from <span lang="grc">λυσᾱντ-\[ιγλιδε\]α</span>, neut. <span lang="grc">λῦσαν</span> from <span lang="grc">λῡσαν</span>(<span lang="grc">τ</span>). See [301](../04-substantives/08-declension-of-participles.md#s301). The middle ends in <span lang="grc">-μενος</span>: <span lang="grc">λῡσά-μενος, φηνά-μενος</span>.

##### <a id="s671D"></a> §671 D

Aeolic has <span lang="grc">-αις, -αισα, -αν</span> ([37 D.](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s37) 3).

## FIRST AND SECOND AORIST PASSIVE ([585](09-viii-first-passive-system-thetaeta-passive.md#s585), [590](10-ix-second-passive-system-h-passive.md#s590))

##### <a id="s672"></a> §672

All vowel and consonant verbs in <span lang="grc">-ω</span> inflect the aorists passive alike, that is, according to the <span lang="grc">μι-</span>conjugation, except in the subjunctive.

**a.** Vowel verbs rarely form second aorists that are passive in form, as <span lang="grc">ῥέω</span> _flow_, <span lang="grc">ἐρρύην</span> ([803](16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s803)). But <span lang="grc">ῥέω</span> is properly not a vowel verb (see [503](02-1-present-system.md#s503)).

##### <a id="s673"></a> §673

**Indicative**.—The indicative adds the active secondary endings directly to the tense stem ending in <span lang="grc">-θη-</span> (first aorist) or <span lang="grc">-η-</span> (second aorist). The inflection is thus like that of the imperfect of a verb in <span lang="grc">-μι</span>.

|  |  |  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="grc"> **ἐλύθη-ν** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ἐτίθη-ν** </span> |  |  | <span lang="grc"> **ἐλύθη-μεν** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ἐτίθε-μεν** </span> |
| <span lang="grc"> **ἐλύθη-ς** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ἐτίθη-ς** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ἐλύθη-τον** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ἐτίθε-τον** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ἐλύθη-τε** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ἐτίθε-τε** </span> |
| <span lang="grc"> **ἐλύθη** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ἐτίθη** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ἐλυθή-την** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ἐτιθέ-την** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ἐλύθη-σαν** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ἐτίθε-σαν** </span> |

**a.** For <span lang="grc">-σαν</span> we find <span lang="grc">-ν</span> from <span lang="grc">-ν</span>(<span lang="grc">τ</span>) in poetical and dialectic forms before which <span lang="grc">η</span> has been shortened to <span lang="grc">ε</span> ([40](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s40)), thus <span lang="grc">ὥρμηθεν</span> for <span lang="grc">ὡρμήθησαν</span> from <span lang="grc">ὁρμάω</span> _urge_.

##### <a id="s674"></a> §674

**Subjunctive**.—The subjunctive adds <span lang="grc">-ώη-</span> to the tense stem ending in <span lang="grc">-θε-</span> or <span lang="grc">-ε-</span> and contracts: <span lang="grc">λυθῶ, -ῇς, -ῇ</span>, etc., from <span lang="grc">λυθέω, -έῃς, -έῃ</span>, etc.; <span lang="grc">φανῶ, -ῇς, -ῇ</span> from <span lang="grc">φανέω, -έῃς, -έῃ</span>, etc.

##### <a id="s674D"></a> §674 D

Hdt. leaves <span lang="grc">εω</span> open (<span lang="grc">αἱρεθέω, φανέωσι</span>) but contracts <span lang="grc">εη, εῃ</span> (<span lang="grc">φανῇ</span>). Hom. has some forms like the 2 aor. subj. of <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verbs. Thus, from <span lang="grc">δαμνάω</span> (<span lang="grc">δάμνημι</span>) _subdue:_ <span lang="grc">δαμήω, -ήῃς, -ήῃ, -ήετε</span>. So also <span lang="grc">δαήω</span> (<span lang="grc">δα-</span> _learn_), <span lang="grc">σαπήῃ</span> (<span lang="grc">σήπω</span> _cause to rot_), <span lang="grc">φανήῃ</span> (<span lang="grc">φαίνω</span> _show_), <span lang="grc">τραπήομεν</span> (<span lang="grc">τέρπω</span> _amuse_). The spellings with <span lang="grc">ει</span> (e.g. <span lang="grc">δαμείω, δαείω</span>) are probably incorrect.

##### <a id="s675"></a> §675

**Optative**.—The optative adds <span lang="grc">-ῑ-</span> or <span lang="grc">-ιη-</span> to the tense-stem ending in <span lang="grc">-θε-</span> or <span lang="grc">-ε-</span>, and contracts. In the singular <span lang="grc">-ιη-</span> is regular; in the dual and plural <span lang="grc">-ῑ-</span> is generally preferred. Thus <span lang="grc">λυθείην</span> from <span lang="grc">λυθε-ίη-ν, φανείην</span> from <span lang="grc">φανε-ίη-ν</span>, <!-- page 196 --> <span lang="grc">λυθεῖτον</span> from <span lang="grc">λυθέ-ῑ-τον, φανεῖτον</span> from <span lang="grc">φανέ-ῑ-τον, λυθεῖμεν</span> from <span lang="grc">λυθέ-ῑ-μεν, φανεῖεν</span> from <span lang="grc">φανέ-ιε-ν</span>. The inflection is like that of the present optative of a <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verb.

|  |  |  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="grc"> **λυθε-ίη-ν** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **τιθε-ίη-ν** </span> |  |  | <span lang="grc"> **λυθε-ῖ-μεν** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **τιθε-ῖ-μεν** </span> |
| <span lang="grc"> **λυθε-ίη-ς** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **τιθε-ίη-ς** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **λυθε-ῖ-τον** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **τιθε-ῖ-τον** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **λυθε-ῖ-τε** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **τιθε-ῖ-τε** </span> |
| <span lang="grc"> **λυθέ-ίη** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **τιθε-ίη** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **λυθε-ί-την** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **τιθε-ί-την** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **λυθε-ῖε-ν** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **τιθε-ῖε-ν** </span> |

**a.** <span lang="grc">-είημεν</span> is used only in prose (but Plato and Isocrates have also <span lang="grc">-εῖμεν</span>). <span lang="grc">-είητε</span> is almost always found in the Mss. of prose writers; <span lang="grc">-εῖτε</span> occurs only in poetry (except from <span lang="grc">μι-</span>verbs). <span lang="grc">-εῖεν</span> is more common in prose than <span lang="grc">-είησαν</span>.

##### <a id="s676"></a> §676

**Imperative**.—The endings of the imperative are added to the tense-stem ending in <span lang="grc">-θη-</span> or <span lang="grc">-η-</span>. Before <span lang="grc">-ντων, -θη-</span> and <span lang="grc">-η-</span> become <span lang="grc">-θε-</span> and <span lang="grc">-ε-</span> (<span lang="grc">λυθέντων, φανέντων</span>). For <span lang="grc">-τι</span> instead of <span lang="grc">-θι</span> in the first aorist (<span lang="grc">λύθητι</span>) see [125 b](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s125).

##### <a id="s677"></a> §677

**Infinitive**.—<span lang="grc">-ναι</span> is added to the tense-stem in <span lang="grc">-θη-</span> or <span lang="grc">-η-</span>: <span lang="grc">λυθῆ-ναι, φανῆ-ναι</span>.

##### <a id="s677D"></a> §677 D

Hom. has <span lang="grc">-μεναι</span>, as <span lang="grc">ὁμοιωθήμεναι, δαήμεναι</span> (and <span lang="grc">δαῆναι</span>). Doric has <span lang="grc">-μεν</span>, Aeolic <span lang="grc">-ν</span> (<span lang="grc">μεθύσθην ῀ μεθυσθῆναι</span>).

##### <a id="s678"></a> §678

**Participle**.—The participle adds <span lang="grc">-ντ</span>, as masc. <span lang="grc">λυθείς</span> from <span lang="grc">λυθεντ-ς</span>, fem. <span lang="grc">λυθεῖσα</span> from <span lang="grc">λυθεντ-\[ιγλιδε\]α</span>, neut. <span lang="grc">λυθέν</span> from <span lang="grc">λυθεν</span>(<span lang="grc">τ</span>). See [301](../04-substantives/08-declension-of-participles.md#s301). So <span lang="grc">φανείς</span>, etc.

<span lang="grc">σκέλλω</span> in <span lang="grc">ἀποσκέλλω</span> (<span lang="grc">σκελ-, σκλε-</span>) _dry up_, <span lang="grc">ἀποσκλῆναι</span>.

<span lang="grc">τλα-</span> _endure_, fut. <span lang="grc">τλήσομαι</span>, poetic <span lang="grc">ἔτλην</span> (<span lang="grc">τλῶ, τλαίην, τλῆθι, τλῆναι, τλά̄ς</span>).

<span lang="grc">φθάνω</span> (<span lang="grc">φθα-</span>) _anticipate_, <span lang="grc">ἔφθην</span> (<span lang="grc">φθῶ, φθαίην, φθῆναι, φθά̄ς</span>).

<span lang="grc">φύ̄ω</span> (<span lang="grc">φῠ̄-</span>) _produce_, <span lang="grc">ἔφῡν</span> _was produced, am_ (<span lang="grc">φύω</span> subj., <span lang="grc">φῦναι, φύ̄ς</span> [308](../04-substantives/08-declension-of-participles.md#s308)).

##### <a id="s688"></a> §688

The following <span lang="grc">ω-</span>verbs have in poetry (especially in Homer) second aorists of the <span lang="grc">μι</span> form: <span lang="grc">ἅλλομαι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἆλσο, ἆλτο</span>), <span lang="grc">ἀπαυράω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀπούρᾱς</span>), <span lang="grc">ἀραρίσκω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἄρμενος</span>), <span lang="grc">ἄω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἄ̄μεναι</span>), <span lang="grc">βάλλω</span> (<span lang="grc">ξυμβλήτην, ἔβλητο</span>), <span lang="grc">βιβρώσκω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἔβρων</span>), root <span lang="grc">γεν-</span> (<span lang="grc">γέντο</span> _grasped_), <span lang="grc">δέχομαι</span> (<span lang="grc">δέκτο</span>), Epic <span lang="grc">κιχά̄νω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐκίχην, κιχήω, κιχείη, κιχῆναι</span> and <span lang="grc">κιχήμεναι, κιχείς</span> and <span lang="grc">κιχήμενος</span>; properly from <span lang="grc">κίχημι</span>), <span lang="grc">κλάω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀπόκλᾱς</span>), <span lang="grc">κλύω</span> (<span lang="grc">κλῦθι, κέκλυθι</span>), <span lang="grc">κτίζω</span> (<span lang="grc">κτίμενος</span>), root <span lang="grc">λεχ-</span> (<span lang="grc">ἔλεκτο</span> _laid himself to rest_), <span lang="grc">λύ̄ω</span> (<span lang="grc">λύτο</span>), <span lang="grc">οὐτάω</span> (<span lang="grc">οὖτα, οὐτάμενος</span>), <span lang="grc">πάλλω</span> (<span lang="grc">πάλτο</span>), <span lang="grc">πελάζω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐπλήμην</span>), <span lang="grc">πέρθω</span> (<span lang="grc">πέρθαι ῀ περθ-σθαι</span>), <span lang="grc">πλώω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἔπλων</span>), <span lang="grc">πνῡ-</span> (<span lang="grc">ἄμπνῡτο</span> _revived_), <span lang="grc">πτήσσω</span> (<span lang="grc">καταπτήτην</span>), <span lang="grc">σεύω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐσσύμην, ἔσυτο, σύμενος</span>), <span lang="grc">φθίνω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐφθίμην</span>), <span lang="grc">χέω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐχύμην, χύμενος</span>).

<span lang="grc">ἔλεκτο, πάλτο</span> are properly first aorists (for <span lang="grc">ἐλεκ-σ-το, παλ-σ-το</span>), <span lang="grc">ς</span> being lost between two consonants ([103](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s103)).

## FIRST AND SECOND PERFECT AND PLUPERFECT ACTIVE ([555](06-v-first-k-perfect-system.md#s555), [561](07-vi-second-perfect-system.md#s561))

##### <a id="s689"></a> §689

All vowel and consonant verbs in <span lang="grc">-ω</span> inflect the first perfect alike. Some verbs in <span lang="grc">-ω</span> inflect the second perfect according to the <span lang="grc">ω-</span>conjugation, others inflect it according to the <span lang="grc">μι-</span>conjugation.

##### <a id="s690"></a> §690

**Indicative**.—Originally the endings were added to the stem without any thematic vowel. Of this unthematic formation a few traces survive ([573](07-vi-second-perfect-system.md#s573)). In the 2 p. sing. the ending is <span lang="grc">-ς</span>, but originally <span lang="grc">-θα</span>; in the 3 pl. <span lang="grc">-κᾱσι</span> stands for <span lang="grc">κα-νσι</span> out of <span lang="grc">κα-ντι</span> ([100](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s100)). Thus <span lang="grc">λέλυκα, -ας, -ε, πέπομφα, -ας, -ε</span>, etc. The periphrastic combination occurs in the indicative ([599 a](11-periphrastic-forms.md#s599)).

##### <a id="s691"></a> §691

**Subjunctive**.—The perfect subjunctive is commonly formed periphrastically by the perfect active participle and <span lang="grc">ὦ, ᾖς, ᾖ</span>, etc. Thus <span lang="grc">λελυκὼς</span> (<span lang="grc">γεγραφὼς</span>) <span lang="grc">ὦ</span>, etc., <span lang="grc">λελυκότες</span> (<span lang="grc">γεγραφότες</span>) <span lang="grc">ὦμεν</span>, etc. Of the periphrastic forms only the 1 and 3 sing., 2 and 3 plur. are attested.

##### <a id="s692"></a> §692

Instances of the simple perfect subjunctive (<span lang="grc">λελύκω, γεγράφω</span>) are very rare. The simple form is made by substituting the thematic vowel <span lang="grc">ώη</span> for <span lang="grc">α</span> in the tense-stem. Only the sing. and the 3 plur. are attested from <span lang="grc">ω-</span>verbs.

##### <a id="s693"></a> §693

Besides <span lang="grc">εἰδῶ</span> (<span lang="grc">οἶδα</span>) and <span lang="grc">ἑστήκῃ</span>, etc., Attic prose has only about 16 occurrences of the simple perf. subj., and from the following verbs only: <span lang="grc">βαίνω, δέδια, ἐγείρω, ἔοικα, θνῄσκω, λαμβάνω, λανθάνω, πάσχω, ποιῶ, φύ̄ω</span>. Hippocr. has forms from <span lang="grc">βιβρώσκω, πονῶ, τεύχω</span>. There are about 30 occurrences in the <!-- page 199 --> poetry. Attic prose writers show about 25 cases of the periphrasis from all <span lang="grc">ω-</span>verbs.

##### <a id="s694"></a> §694

**Optative**.—The perfect optative is commonly formed periphrastically by the perfect active participle and <span lang="grc">εἴην, εἴης, εἴη</span>, etc. Thus <span lang="grc">λελυκὼς</span> (<span lang="grc">γεγραφὼς</span>) <span lang="grc">εἴην</span>, etc., <span lang="grc">λελυκότες</span> (<span lang="grc">γεγραφότες</span>) <span lang="grc">εἶμεν</span>, etc. The dual is exceedingly rare.

##### <a id="s695"></a> §695

Occasionally the simple forms are used (<span lang="grc">λελύκοιμι, γεγράφοιμι</span>). These are formed by adding the mood-sign <span lang="grc">ῑ</span>, and the endings, to the tense-stem with the thematic vowel (<span lang="grc">ο</span>). All the <span lang="grc">-ιη-</span>forms are attested; of the <span lang="grc">-ῑ-</span>forms only the 3 sing. and 1 and 3 plur.

##### <a id="s696"></a> §696

Of the simple optative there are about 25 occurrences in Attic prose, and from the following verbs only: <span lang="grc">ἀποχωρῶ, ἐξαπατῶ, εἰσβάλλω, παραδίδωμι, ἔοικα, -ἑστήκοι, ὑπηρετῶ, θνῄσκω, λανθάνω, καταλείπω, ποιῶ, πάσχω, προέρχομαι, ἐμπί̄πτω, φύ̄ω</span>. In the poets there are about 16 occurrences. Prose writers show about 106 occurrences of the periphrastic forms.

##### <a id="s697"></a> §697

**Imperative**.—The usual form of the first perfect imperative is periphrastic: <span lang="grc">λελυκὼς ἴσθι, ἔστω</span>, etc. No classical Attic writer uses the simple forms.

##### <a id="s698"></a> §698

The second perfect is rare, and occurs only in the case of verbs which have a present meaning. From active verbs inflected according to the <span lang="grc">ω-</span> conjugation there occur <span lang="grc">κεχήνετε</span> _gape_, Ar. _Ach._ 133 (<span lang="grc">χάσκω, χαν-</span>), and <span lang="grc">κεκρά̄γετε</span> _screech_, _Vesp._ 415 (<span lang="grc">κρά̄ζω</span>). Most second perfects show the <span lang="grc">μι</span> form and have present meaning, as <span lang="grc">τέθναθι</span> (Hom.) <span lang="grc">τεθνάτω</span> from <span lang="grc">θνῄσκω</span> _die_, <span lang="grc">δέδιθι</span> from <span lang="grc">δέδια</span> _fear_, and <span lang="grc">κέκρᾱχθι</span> from <span lang="grc">κρά̄ζω</span> in Aristophanes. Most such second perfects are poetical.

##### <a id="s699"></a> §699

**Infinitive**.—The perfect infinitive adds <span lang="grc">-έ-ναι</span>, as <span lang="grc">λελυκέναι, λελοιπέναι</span>.

##### <a id="s699D"></a> §699 D

Doric has <span lang="grc">-ην</span> and <span lang="grc">-ειν</span>, as <span lang="grc">δεδύκην ῀ δεδυκέναι, γεγά̄κειν ῀ γεγονέναι</span>. Aeolic has <span lang="grc">-ην</span>, as <span lang="grc">τεθνάκην</span>.

##### <a id="s700"></a> §700

**Participle**.—The suffixes of the perfect participle in the nominative are -(<span lang="grc">ϝ</span>)<span lang="grc">ώς, -υῖα</span>, -(<span lang="grc">ϝ</span>)<span lang="grc">ός</span>, as <span lang="grc">λελυκώς, λελοιπώς</span>. See [301 c](../04-substantives/08-declension-of-participles.md#s301), d, 309.

##### <a id="s700D"></a> §700 D

In the 2 perf. Hom. sometimes has <span lang="grc">-ῶτ-ος</span> for <span lang="grc">-ότ-ος</span>, as <span lang="grc">κεκμηώς, -ῶτος</span> (<span lang="grc">κάμνω</span> _am weary_). In the 2 perf. Hom. sometimes has <span lang="grc">α</span> for Attic <span lang="grc">η</span> in the feminine, as <span lang="grc">ἀρηρώς ἀραρυῖα</span> from <span lang="grc">ἄρηρα</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀραρίσκω</span> _fit_). See [573](07-vi-second-perfect-system.md#s573). Aeolic inflects the perfect participle as a present in <span lang="grc">-ων, -οντος</span>. Thus Hom. <span lang="grc">κεκλήγοντας</span> for <span lang="grc">κεκληγότας</span> (<span lang="grc">κλάζω</span> _scream_), Pind. <span lang="grc">πεφρί̄κοντας</span> (<span lang="grc">φρί̄ττω</span> _shudder_).

##### <a id="s701"></a> §701

**Pluperfect Active**.—The pluperfect is formed by adding <span lang="grc">-εα, -εας, -εε, -ετον, -ετην, -εμεν, -ετε, -εσαν</span> to the reduplicated stem. By contraction from <span lang="grc">ἐλελυκεα, -εας, -εε</span> come the forms <span lang="grc">ἐλελύκη, -ης -ει</span> (<span lang="grc">ν</span>). In the later language <span lang="grc">ει</span> spread from the 3 sing. and was used throughout, as <span lang="grc">ἐλελύκειν, -εις, -ει, -ειτον, -είτην, -ειμεν, -ειτε</span>, and very late <span lang="grc">-εισαν</span>. The best Mss. of Demosthenes have <span lang="grc">-ειν</span> in 1 sing. Instead of the simple pluperfect we find periphrastic forms, 599 a.

## PERFECT AND PLUPERFECT MIDDLE AND PASSIVE ([574](08-vii-perfect-middle-system.md#s574))

##### <a id="s706"></a> §706

All vowel and consonant verbs in <span lang="grc">-ω</span> inflect the perfect middle according to the <span lang="grc">μι-</span>conjugation.

##### <a id="s707"></a> §707

**Indicative**.—The perfect middle is inflected by adding the primary middle endings directly to the tense-stem, herein agreeing with the <span lang="grc">μι-</span>conjugation. The pluperfect adds the secondary middle endings. In vowel verbs the formation is simple, as in <span lang="grc">λέλυ-μαι, ἐλελύ-μην</span>. But in consonant verbs, the consonant at the end of the stem comes into collision with the consonant at the beginning of the ending; hence certain euphonic changes described in 409. The periphrastic form occurs in the 3 pl. and sometimes in the 3 sing. ([599 d](11-periphrastic-forms.md#s599), e).

**a.** Stems in <span lang="grc">ν</span> avoid the forms <span lang="grc">-ν-σαι, -ν-σο</span>; thus, from <span lang="grc">φαίνω</span>, instead of <span lang="grc">πέφανσαι, ἐπέφανσο</span> the periphrastic <span lang="grc">πεφασμένος εἶ, ἦσθα</span> were probably used.

##### <a id="s708"></a> §708

**Subjunctive**.—The perfect middle subjunctive is commonly formed by periphrasis of the perfect middle participle and <span lang="grc">ὦ, ᾖς, ᾖ</span>, etc. Thus <span lang="grc">λελυμένος ὦ</span>.

##### <a id="s708D"></a> §708 D

Hdt. has <span lang="grc">μεμνεώμεθα</span>, and this form may be read in <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 14.168"><span lang="grc">ξ</span> 168</cite>.

##### <a id="s709"></a> §709

From two verbs, whose perfect stem ends in <span lang="grc">η-</span>(<span lang="grc">α</span>), the simple forms are constructed. <span lang="grc"> **κτάομαι** </span> (<span lang="grc">κτα-</span>) _acquire_, perf. <span lang="grc">κέκτημαι</span> _possess_ ([1946](../../part-4-syntax/20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1946)), forms its subjunctive by adding the thematic vowel <span lang="grc">-ώη-</span> to <span lang="grc">κε-κτα</span>; thus <span lang="grc">κε-κτά-ω-μαι ῀ κεκτῶμαι, κε-κτά-η-σαι ῀ κεκτῇ, κε-κτά-η-ται ῀ κεκτῆται</span>, etc.—<span lang="grc"> **μιμνῄσκω** </span> (<span lang="grc">μνα-</span> _remind_, perf. <span lang="grc">μέμνημαι</span> _remember_ ([1946](../../part-4-syntax/20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1946)): <span lang="grc">με-μνά-ω-μαι ῀ μεμνῶμαι, μεμνη-ώ-μεθα ῀ μεμνώμεθα</span>. With <span lang="grc">κεκτῶμαι, μεμνῶμαι</span>, cp. <span lang="grc">ἱστῶμαι</span>, p. 137. The periphrastic <span lang="grc">κεκτημένος ὦ, μεμνημένος ὦ</span> occur.

##### <a id="s710"></a> §710

**Optative**.—The perfect middle optative is commonly formed by the periphrasis of the perfect middle participle and <span lang="grc">εἴην, εἴης, εἴη</span>, etc. Thus <span lang="grc">λελυμένος εἴην</span>, etc.

##### <a id="s711"></a> §711

Some verbs add <span lang="grc">-ί̄-μην, -ο-ί̄-μην</span> to the tense-stem ([709](#s709)).—**a.** <span lang="grc">κτάομαι</span> (<span lang="grc">κτα-</span>) _acquire_, perf. <span lang="grc">κέκτημαι</span> _possess_ ([1946](../../part-4-syntax/20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1946)): opt. <span lang="grc">κεκτη-ί̄-μην ῀ κεκτῄμην, κεκτήῑ-σο ῀ κεκτῇο, κεκτή-ῑ-το ῀ κεκτῇτο</span>. Less frequent and doubtful are <span lang="grc">κεκτῴμην, -ῷο, -ῷτο, -ῴμεθα</span> from <span lang="grc">κεκτη-ο-ί̄-μην</span>, etc.

**b.** <span lang="grc">μιμνῄσκω</span> (<span lang="grc">μνα-</span>) _remind_, perf. <span lang="grc">μέμνημαι</span> _remember;_ opt. <span lang="grc">μεμνη-ί̄-μην ῀ μεμνῄμην, μεμνή-ῑ-σο ῀ μεμνῇο, μεμνή-ῑ-το ῀ μεμνῇτο</span>, etc. The forms <span lang="grc">μεμνῴμην, -ῷο, -ῷτο</span>, etc., from <span lang="grc">μεμνη-ο-ί̄-μην</span>, etc., are uncommon and suspected.

**c.** <span lang="grc">καλέω</span> (<span lang="grc">καλε-, κλη-</span>) _call_, perf. <span lang="grc">κέκλημαι</span> _am called_ ([1946](../../part-4-syntax/20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1946)); opt. <span lang="grc">κεκλη-ί̄-μην</span>, etc. = <span lang="grc">κεκλῄμην, κεκλῇο, κεκλῇτο, κεκλῄμεθα</span>.

**d.** <span lang="grc">βάλλω</span> (<span lang="grc">βαλ-, βλη-</span>) _throw_, perf. <span lang="grc">διαβέβλημαι</span>, opt. <span lang="grc">διαβεβλῇσθε</span>.

N.—The forms in <span lang="grc">-ῄμην</span>, etc., have the <span lang="grc">μι-</span>form; the doubtful <span lang="grc">-ῴμην</span>, etc., belong to the <span lang="grc">ω-</span>conjugation.

##### <a id="s711D"></a> §711 D

##### <a id="s712"></a> §712

**Imperative**.—In the third person singular the perfect meaning is regularly retained, as <span lang="grc">εἰρήσθω</span> _let it have been said_. The 2 sing. and pl. are generally found only in the case of perfects with a present meaning, as <span lang="grc">μέμνησθε</span> _remember!_ <span lang="grc">μὴ πεφόβησθε</span> _do not be afraid!_ <span lang="grc">πέπαυσο</span> _stop!_ See [698](#s698).

a. The dual and 3 pl. are apparently wanting. The 2 sing. in <span lang="grc">-νσο</span> from stems in <span lang="grc">-ν</span> does not occur. For <span lang="grc">πέφανσο, πεφασμένος ἴσθι</span> was probably used.

##### <a id="s713"></a> §713

Attic prose writers have <span lang="grc">ἀναβεβλήσθω, ἀποκεκρίσθω, εἰρήσθω, ἐκτήσθω, ἐψεύσθω, κεῖσο, -κείσθω, κέκτησο, μέμνησθε, πεπαίσθω, πεπεράνθω, πεποίησο, πεπρά̄σθω, πεφάσθω, πεφόβησθε, τετάχθω, τετολμήσθω</span>.

##### <a id="s714"></a> §714

Instead of the simple forms of the imperative we find the periphrastic use of the perfect participle and <span lang="grc">ἴσθι, ἔστω</span>, etc. ([599 g](11-periphrastic-forms.md#s599)). Thus <span lang="grc">εἰρημένον ἔστω ῀ εἰρήσθω</span>.

##### <a id="s715"></a> §715

**Infinitive**.—The perfect infinitive adds <span lang="grc">-σθαι</span>, as <span lang="grc">λελύ-σθαι</span>. Consonant stems lose the <span lang="grc">ς</span> by 103, as <span lang="grc">λελεῖφθαι, πεπρᾶχθαι</span> ([406](../09-verbs/00-introduction.md#s406)), <span lang="grc">ἐληλέγχθαι, πεφάνθαι</span> ([407](../09-verbs/00-introduction.md#s407)).

##### <a id="s716"></a> §716

**Participle**.—The perfect participle adds <span lang="grc">-μένος</span>, as <span lang="grc">λελυμένος, λελειμμένος, πεπρᾱγμένος</span> ([406](../09-verbs/00-introduction.md#s406), [407](../09-verbs/00-introduction.md#s407)). On the <span lang="grc">ς</span> of <span lang="grc">πεφασμένος</span> see [409 d](../09-verbs/00-introduction.md#s409).
