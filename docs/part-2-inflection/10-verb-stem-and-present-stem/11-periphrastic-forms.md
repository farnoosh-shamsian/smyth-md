# VERB-STEM AND PRESENT STEM: PERIPHRASTIC FORMS

##### <a id="s599"></a> §599

**Perfect**.—For the simple perfect and pluperfect periphrastic forms are often used.

**a.** For the perfect or pluperfect active indicative the forms of the perfect active participle and <span lang="grc">εἰμί</span> or <span lang="grc">ἦν</span> may be used: as <span lang="grc">λελυκώς εἰμι</span> for <span lang="grc">λέλυκα, λελυκὼς ἦν</span> for <span lang="grc">ἐλελύκη</span>. So <span lang="grc">βεβοηθηκότες ἦσαν</span> for <span lang="grc">ἐβεβοηθήκεσαν</span> (<span lang="grc">βοηθέω</span> _come to aid_); <span lang="grc">εἰμὶ τεθηκὼς</span> for <span lang="grc">τέθηκα</span> _I have placed;_ <span lang="grc">γεγραφὼς ἦν</span> for <span lang="grc">ἐγεγράφη</span> _I had written;_ <span lang="grc">πεπονθὼς ἦν</span> _I had suffered_. Such forms are more common in the pluperfect and in general denote _state_ rather than _action_.

**b.** For the perfect active a periphrasis of the aorist participle and <span lang="grc">ἔχω</span> is sometimes used, especially when a perfect active form with transitive meaning is lacking; as <span lang="grc">στήσᾱς ἔχω</span> _I have placed_ (<span lang="grc">ἕστηκα</span>, intransitive, _stand_), <span lang="grc">ἐρασθεὶς ἔχω</span> _I have loved_. So often because the aspirated perfect is not used, as <span lang="grc">ἔχεις ταράξᾱς</span> _thou hast stirred up_. Cp. _habeo_ with the perfect participle. <!-- page 183 -->

**c.** In the perfect active subjunctive and optative the forms in <span lang="grc">-κω</span> and <span lang="grc">-κοιμι</span> are very rare. In their place the perfect active participle with <span lang="grc">ὦ</span> and <span lang="grc">εἴην</span> is usually employed: <span lang="grc">λελυκὼς</span> (<span lang="grc">λελοιπὼς</span>) <span lang="grc">ὦ, εἴην</span>. Other forms than 3 sing. and 3 pl. are rare. Cp. [691](13-inflection-of-omega-verbs.md#s691), [694](13-inflection-of-omega-verbs.md#s694).

**d.** The perfect or pluperfect passive is often paraphrased by the perfect participle and <span lang="grc">ἐστί</span> or <span lang="grc">ἦν</span>; as <span lang="grc">γεγραμμένον ἐστί</span> _it stands written_, <span lang="grc">ἐστὶ δεδογμένον</span> _it stands resolved_, <span lang="grc">παρηγγελμένον ἦν ῀ παρήγγελτο</span> (<span lang="grc">παραγγέλλω</span> _give orders_).

**e.** In the third plural of the perfect and pluperfect middle (passive) the perfect middle participle with <span lang="grc">εἰσί</span> (<span lang="grc">ἦσαν</span>) is used when a stem ending in a consonant would come in direct contact with the endings <span lang="grc">-νται, -ντο</span>. See [408](../09-verbs/00-introduction.md#s408).

**f.** The perfect subjunctive and optative middle are formed by the perfect middle participle with <span lang="grc">ὦ</span> or <span lang="grc">εἴην</span>: <span lang="grc">λελυμένος ὦ, εἴην</span>.

**g.** The perfect imperative of all voices may be expressed by combining the perfect participle with <span lang="grc">ἴσθι, ἔστω</span> ([697](13-inflection-of-omega-verbs.md#s697)). <span lang="grc">λελυκὼς ἴσθι</span> _loose_, etc., <span lang="grc">εἰρημένον ἔστω</span> _let it have been said_, <span lang="grc">γεγονὼς ἔστω</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 951c">P. L. 951c</cite>, <span lang="grc">γεγονότες ἔστωσαν</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 779d">P. L. 779d</cite>.

**h.** Periphrasis of the infinitive is rare: <span lang="grc">τεθνηκότα εἶναι</span> _to be dead_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.11">X. C. 1.4.11</cite>.

##### <a id="s600"></a> §600

**Future Perfect Active**.—The future perfect active of most verbs is formed by combining the perfect active participle with <span lang="grc">ἔσομαι</span> _shall be_. Thus, <span lang="grc">γεγραφὼς ἔσομαι</span> _I shall have written_, cp. _scriptus ero_. For the two verbs which do not use this periphrasis, see [584](08-vii-perfect-middle-system.md#s584).

**a.** The perfect middle participle is used in the case of deponent verbs: <span lang="grc">ἀπολελογημένος ἔσομαι</span> <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.72">And. 1.72</cite>.

##### <a id="s601"></a> §601

**Future Perfect Passive**.—The future perfect passive may be expressed by using the perfect middle (passive) participle with <span lang="grc">ἔσομαι</span> _shall be_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐψευσμένοι ἔσεσθε</span> _you will have been deceived_.
