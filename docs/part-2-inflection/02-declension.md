# DECLENSION

##### <a id="s194"></a> §194

Declension deals with variations of number, gender, and case.

##### <a id="s195"></a> §195

**Number**.—There are three numbers: singular, dual, and plural. The dual speaks of _two_ or _a pair_, as <span lang="grc">τὼ ὀφθαλμώ</span> _the two eyes;_ but it is not often used, and the plural (which denotes _more than one_) is frequently substituted for it (<span lang="grc">οἱ ὀφθαλμοί</span> _the eyes_).

##### <a id="s196"></a> §196

**Gender**.—There are three genders: masculine, feminine, and neuter.

**a.** Gender strictly marks sex-distinction. But in Greek, as in German and French, many inanimate objects are regarded as masculine or feminine. Such words are said to have ‘grammatical’ gender, which is determined only by their _form_. Words denoting objects without natural gender usually show their grammatical gender by the form of the adjective, as <span lang="grc">μακρὸς λόγος</span> _a long speech_, <span lang="grc">μακρὰ̄ νῆσος</span> _a long island_, <span lang="grc">μακρὸν τεῖχος</span> _a long wall_.

**b.** The gender of Greek words is usually indicated by means of the article: <span lang="grc">ὁ</span> for masculine, <span lang="grc">ἡ</span> for feminine, <span lang="grc">τό</span> for neuter.

##### <a id="s197"></a> §197

**Rule of Natural Gender**.—Nouns denoting male persons are masculine, nouns denoting female persons are feminine. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁ ναύτης</span> _seaman_, <span lang="grc">ὁ στρατιώτης</span> _soldier_, <span lang="grc">ἡ γυνή</span> _woman_, <span lang="grc">ἡ κόρη</span> _maiden_.

**a.** A whole class is designated by the masculine: <span lang="grc">οἱ ἄνθρωποι</span> _men_, i.e. _men and women_.

**b.** EXCEPTIONS TO THE RULE OF NATURAL GENDER.—Diminutives in <span lang="grc">-ιον</span> are neuter ([199 d](#s199)), as <span lang="grc">τὸ ἀνθρώπιον</span> _manikin_ (<span lang="grc">ὁ ἄνθρωπος</span> _man_), <span lang="grc">τὸ παιδίον</span> _little child_ (male or female, <span lang="grc">ὁ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἡ παῖς</span> _child_), <span lang="grc">τὸ γύναιον</span> _little woman_ (<span lang="grc">ἡ γυνή</span> _woman_). Also the words <span lang="grc">τέκνον, τέκος</span> _child_ (strictly ‘thing born’), <span lang="grc">ἀνδράποδον</span> _captive_. <!-- page 46 -->

##### <a id="s198"></a> §198

**Common Gender**.—Many nouns denoting persons are either masculine or feminine. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁ παῖς</span> _boy_, <span lang="grc">ἡ παῖς</span> _girl_, <span lang="grc">ὁ θεός</span> _god_, <span lang="grc">ἡ θεός</span> (<span lang="grc">ἡ θεά̄</span> poet.) _goddess_. So with names of animals: <span lang="grc">ὁ βοῦς</span> _ox_, <span lang="grc">ἡ βοῦς</span> _cow_, <span lang="grc">ὁ ἵππος</span> _horse_, <span lang="grc">ἡ ἵππος</span> _mare_.

**a.** Some names of animals have only one grammatical gender without regard to sex, as <span lang="grc">ὁ λαγώς</span> _he-hare_ or _she-hare_, <span lang="grc">ἡ ἀλώπηξ</span> _he-fox_ or _she-fox_.

##### <a id="s199"></a> §199

**Gender of Sexless Objects**.—The gender of most nouns denoting sexless objects has to be learned by the endings ([211](04-substantives/01-first-declension-stems-in-alpha.md#s211), [228](04-substantives/02-second-declension-stems-in-omicron.md#s228), [255](04-substantives/05-third-declension.md#s255)) and by observation. The following general rules should be noted.

**a.** Masculine are the names of _winds_, _months_, and most _rivers_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁ Βορέᾱς</span> _the North Wind_, <span lang="grc">ὁ Ἑκατομβαιών</span> _Hecatombaeon_, <span lang="grc">ὁ Κηφισσός</span> _Cephissus_.

N.—The gender of these proper names is made to correspond to <span lang="grc">ὁ ἄνεμος</span> _wind_, <span lang="grc">ὁ μήν</span> _month_, <span lang="grc">ὁ ποταμός</span> _river_. In the case of winds and rivers the gender may be due in part to personification.

**b.** Feminine are the names of almost all _countries_, _islands_, _cities_, _trees_, and _plants_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἡ Ἀττική</span> _Attica_, <span lang="grc">ἡ Δῆλος</span> _Delos_, <span lang="grc">ἡ Κόρινθος</span> _Corinth_, <span lang="grc">ἡ πίτυς</span> _pine_, <span lang="grc">ἡ ἄμπελος</span> _vine_. The gender here follows that of <span lang="grc">ἡ γῆ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἡ χώρᾱ</span> _land_, _country_, <span lang="grc">ἡ νῆσος</span> _island_, <span lang="grc">ἡ πόλις</span> _city_, <span lang="grc">ἡ δρῦς</span>, originally _tree_ in general, but later _oak_ (<span lang="grc">τὸ δένδρον</span> is the ordinary word for _tree_).

**c.** Feminine are most abstract words, that is, words denoting a _quality_ or a _condition_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἡ ἀρετή</span> _virtue_, <span lang="grc">ἡ εὔνοια</span> _good-will_, <span lang="grc">ἡ ταχύτης</span> _swiftness_, <span lang="grc">ἡ ἐλπίς</span> _hope_.

**d.** Neuter are diminutives ([197 b](#s197)), words and expressions quoted, letters of the alphabet, infinitives, and indeclinable nouns. Thus, <span lang="grc">τὸ ὑ̄μεῖς</span> _the word_ ‘_you_,’ <span lang="grc">τὸ γνῶθι σεαυτόν</span> _the saying_ ‘_learn to know thyself_,’ <span lang="grc">τὸ ἄλφα</span> _alpha_, <span lang="grc">τὸ παιδεύειν</span> _to educate_, <span lang="grc">τὸ χρεών</span> _necessity_.

N.—But some names of women end in <span lang="grc">-ιον</span> ([197 b](#s197)): <span lang="grc">ἡ Γλυκέριον</span> _Glycerium_.

##### <a id="s200"></a> §200

**Remarks**.—**a.** Most of the exceptions to 199 a-b are due to the endings; e.g. <span lang="grc">ἡ Λήθη</span> _Lethe_, <span lang="grc">ἡ Στύξ</span> _Styx_ (rivers of the Lower World), <span lang="grc">τὸ Ἄργος</span> _Argos_, <span lang="grc">ὁ Καλυδών</span> _Calydon_, <span lang="grc">τὸ Ἴ̄λιον</span> _Ilium_, <span lang="grc">οἱ Δελφοί</span> _Delphi_, <span lang="grc">ὁ λωτός</span> _lotus_.

**b.** Change in gender is often associated with change in form: <span lang="grc">ὁ λύκος</span> _he-wolf_, <span lang="grc">ἡ λύκαινα</span> _she-wolf_, <span lang="grc">ὁ ποιητής</span> _poet_, <span lang="grc">ἡ ποιήτρια</span> _poetess_, <span lang="grc">ὁ βίοτος</span> and <span lang="grc">ἡ βιοτή</span> _life_, <span lang="grc">ὁ τρόπος</span> _manner_, <span lang="grc">ἡ τροπή</span> _rout_.

**c.** The gender of one word may influence that of another word of like meaning. Thus <span lang="grc">ἡ νῆσος</span> _island_ and <span lang="grc">ἡ λίθος</span> _stone_ are feminine probably because of <span lang="grc">ἡ γῆ</span> _land_ and <span lang="grc">ἡ πέτρᾱ</span> _rock_.

##### <a id="s201"></a> §201

**Cases**.—There are five cases: nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, and vocative. The genitive denotes _from_ as well as _of_, the dative denotes _to_ or _for_ and also _by_, _with_, _on_, _in_, _at_, etc. The other cases are used as in Latin.

**a.** The genitive, dative, and accusative are called _oblique_ cases to distinguish them from the nominative and vocative.

##### <a id="s202"></a> §202

The vocative is often like the nominative in the singular; in the plural it is always the same. Nominative, vocative, and accusative have the same form in neuter words, and always have <span lang="grc">α</span> in the <!-- page 47 --> plural. In the dual there are two forms, one for nominative, accusative, and vocative, the other for genitive and dative.

##### <a id="s203"></a> §203

**Lost Cases**.—Greek has generally lost the _forms_ of the instrumental and locative cases (which have become fused with the dative) and of the ablative. The Greek dative is used to express _by_, as in <span lang="grc">βίᾳ</span>, Lat. <span lang="grc">υῑ</span> _; with_, as in <span lang="grc">λίθοις</span> _with stones;_ and _in_, _on_, as in <span lang="grc">γῇ</span> _on the earth. From_ may be expressed by the genitive: <span lang="grc">πόρρω Σπάρτης</span> _far from Sparta_. When the genitive and dative do duty for the ablative, prepositions are often used. Instances of the forms of the lost cases are given in 341.

##### <a id="s204"></a> §204

**Declensions**.—There are three declensions, which are named from the stems to which the case endings are attached.

\1. First or Â-declension, with stems in <span lang="grc"> **ᾱ** </span>) Vowel Declension.

\2. Second or O-declension, with stems in <span lang="grc"> **ο** </span>)

\3. Third or Consonant declension, with stems in a consonant or in <span lang="grc"> **ι** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **υ** </span>.

The nominative and accusative are alike in the singular and plural of all neuter nouns. The nominative and vocative are alike in the plural.
