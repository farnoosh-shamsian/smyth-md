# ADVERBS

<!-- page 99 -->

##### <a id="s341"></a> §341

**Origin**.—Adverbs, like prepositions and conjunctions, were originally case forms, made from the stems of nouns and pronouns. Some of these nominal and pronominal stems have gone out of common use, so that only petrified forms are left in the adverbs. Some of these words were still felt to be live cases; in others no consciousness of their origin survived. Many adverbs show old suffixes joined to the stem or to a case form ([342](#s342)). It is sometimes uncertain whether we should speak of _adverbs_ or of _nouns with local endings_.

_Nominative_ (rare): <span lang="grc">πύξ</span> _with clenched fist_, <span lang="grc">ἅπαξ</span> _once_, <span lang="grc">ἀναμίξ</span> _pell-mell_.

_Genitive:_ <span lang="grc">ἕνης</span> _day after to-morrow_, <span lang="grc">ἑξῆς</span> _next_, <span lang="grc">ποῦ, οὗ</span> _where_, <span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ</span> _in the very place_, <span lang="grc">ἐκποδών</span> _out of the way_ (<span lang="grc">ἐκ ¨ ποδῶν</span>); by analogy, <span lang="grc">ἐμποδών</span> _in one's way_.

_Dative:_ <span lang="grc">δημοσίᾳ</span> _at public cost_, <span lang="grc">λάθρᾳ</span> _in secret_, <span lang="grc">κοινῇ</span> _in common_, etc. ([1527 b](../part-4-syntax/18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1527)), <span lang="grc">ἄλλῃ</span> _otherwise_, <span lang="grc">πῇ</span> _how_.

_Accusative:_ very common, especially such adverbs as have the form of the accusative of neuter adjectives, as <span lang="grc">πολύ</span> _much_, <span lang="grc">μῑκρόν</span> _a little_, <span lang="grc">πρῶτον</span> _at first_, <span lang="grc">τήμερον</span> _to-day_, <span lang="grc">πολλά</span> _often_. See [1606](../part-4-syntax/18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1606)-1611.

_Locative:_ <span lang="grc">οἴκο-ι</span> _at home_ (<span lang="grc">οἶκο-ς</span> _house_), <span lang="grc">Ἰσθμο-ῖ</span> _at the Isthmus_, <span lang="grc">ποῖ</span> _whither_, and all adverbs in <span lang="grc">-οι</span>. The <span lang="grc">-ι</span> of the consonantal declension is properly the ending of the locative, as in <span lang="grc">Μαραθῶν-ι</span> _at Marathon;_ <span lang="grc">-οισι</span> ([234](04-substantives/02-second-declension-stems-in-omicron.md#s234)) in O stems, in contrast to <span lang="grc">-οις; -ᾱσι</span> (<span lang="grc">-ησι</span>) in Ā stems ([215](04-substantives/01-first-declension-stems-in-alpha.md#s215)): <span lang="grc">θύρᾱσι</span> _at the doors_, <span lang="grc">Πλαταιᾶσι</span> _at Plataea_, <span lang="grc">Ἀθήνησι</span> _at Athens;_ further in <span lang="grc">πάλαι</span> _long ago_, <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖ</span> _there_, <span lang="grc">πανδημεί</span> _in full force_.

_Instrumental:_ <span lang="grc">ἄνω</span> _above_, <span lang="grc">κάτω</span> _below_, <span lang="grc">οὔπω</span> _not yet_, <span lang="grc">ὧ-δε</span> _thus_ (but the forms in <span lang="grc">-ω</span> may be ablatives); <span lang="grc">κρυφῆ</span> and <span lang="grc">λάθρᾱ</span> _in secret_.

_Ablative:_ all adverbs in <span lang="grc">-ως</span>, as <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> _as_, <span lang="grc">οὕτως</span> _thus_, <span lang="grc">ἑτέρως</span> _otherwise_. Here, e.g. original <span lang="grc">ἑτερωδ</span> (cp. Old Lat. _altoōd_, abl. of _altus_) became <span lang="grc">ἑτερω</span> ([133](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/04-final-consonants.md#s133)), which took on <span lang="grc">-ς</span> from the analogy of such words as <span lang="grc">ἀμφίς</span> parallel to <span lang="grc">ἀμφί</span>.

##### <a id="s342"></a> §342

**Place**.—To denote place the common endings are:—

<span lang="grc">-ι, -θι, -σι</span> _at_, _in_ to denote place _where_ (locative). <span lang="grc">-ου</span>, the sign of the genitive, is also common.

<span lang="grc">-θεν</span> _from_ to denote the place _whence_ (ablative).

<span lang="grc">-δε</span> (<span lang="grc">-ζε</span>), <span lang="grc">-σε</span> _to_, _toward_ to denote place _whither_.

In the following examples poetical words are bracketed.

**a.** In <span lang="grc">-αζε, -δε</span> is added to the accusative ([1589](../part-4-syntax/18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1589)), and stands for <span lang="grc">-α</span>(<span lang="grc">ν</span>)<span lang="grc">ς</span>, the old acc. pl.,+<span lang="grc">-δε</span> (Eng. TO). Cp. [26](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s26), [106](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s106). The other endings are added to the stem. <span lang="grc">-σε</span> is usually added only to pronominal stems. <span lang="grc">-σι</span> forms a locative plural. <span lang="grc">ο</span> sometimes takes the place of <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> of the first declension (<span lang="grc">ῥίζοθεν</span> _from the root_, stem <span lang="grc">ῥιζᾱ-</span>), or is added to consonant stems. Words in <span lang="grc">-τερο-</span> lengthen <span lang="grc">ο</span> to <span lang="grc">ω</span>. Between stem and ending <span lang="grc">αχ</span> is often inserted.

**b.** <span lang="grc">-θεν</span> may take the form <span lang="grc">-θε</span> in poetry, and especially when the idea of _whence_ is lost, as <span lang="grc">πρόσθε</span> _in front_ ([134 D.](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/05-movable-consonants.md#s134)). <span lang="grc">-θα</span> is found in <span lang="grc">ἔνθα</span> in all dialects. <span lang="grc">-θα</span> for <span lang="grc">-θεν</span> occurs in Aeolic and Doric.

**c.** Some local adverbs are made from prepositions, as <span lang="grc">ἄνω</span> _above_, <span lang="grc">ἔξω</span> _outside_, <span lang="grc">ἔσω</span> _within_, <span lang="grc">κάτω</span> _below_, <span lang="grc">πρόσθεν</span> _in front_.

##### <a id="s342D"></a> §342 D

Hom. has many cases of the local endings, e.g. <span lang="grc">οὐρανό-θι</span> _in heaven_, <span lang="grc">ἀγορῆ-θεν</span> _from the assembly;_ also after prepositions as a genitive case: <span lang="grc">ἐξ ἁλόθεν</span> _out of the sea_, <span lang="grc">Ἰ̄λιό-θι πρό</span> _before Ilium_. Cp. <span lang="grc">ἐμέθεν, σέθεν, ἕθεν</span>, 325 D. 1. <span lang="grc"> **-δε** </span> in <span lang="grc">ἅλα-δε</span> _to the sea_, <span lang="grc">πόλιν-δε</span> _to the city_, <span lang="grc">πεδίον-δε</span> _to the plain_, <span lang="grc">Ἀιδόσ-δε</span> _to_ (the house of) _Hades_, <span lang="grc">ὅν-δε δόμον-δε</span> _to his house_.

##### <a id="s344"></a> §344

**Various Other Endings**.—Adverbs have many other endings, _e.g.:_— <span lang="grc"> **-α** </span>: <span lang="grc">ἅμα</span> _at the same time_, <span lang="grc">μάλα</span> _very_, <span lang="grc">τάχα</span> _quickly_ (in Attic prose _perhaps_). <span lang="grc"> **-ακις** </span>: <span lang="grc">πολλάκις</span> _many times, often_, <span lang="grc">ἑκαστάκις</span> _each time_, <span lang="grc">τοσαυτάκις</span> _so often_, <span lang="grc">ὁσάκις</span> _as often as_, <span lang="grc">πλειστάκις</span> _very often_, <span lang="grc">ὀλιγάκις</span> _seldom_, <span lang="grc">πλεονάκις</span> _more times_. The forms without <span lang="grc">-ς</span> (<span lang="grc">ὁσάκι, πολλάκι</span>) are earlier, and <span lang="grc">-ς</span> has been added by imitation of <span lang="grc">δίς, τρίς. **-δην**</span>: <span lang="grc">συλλήβδην</span> _in short_. <span lang="grc"> **-δον** </span>: <span lang="grc">ἔνδον</span> _within_, <span lang="grc">σχεδόν</span> _almost_. <span lang="grc"> **-ει** </span>: <!-- page 101 --> <span lang="grc">πανδημεί</span> _in full levy_ ([341](#s341), locative). <span lang="grc"> **-τε** </span>: <span lang="grc">ὅτε</span> _when_ (Aeolic <span lang="grc">ὄτα</span>, Dor. <span lang="grc">ὅκα</span>). <span lang="grc">**-τι**, **-στι**</span>: <span lang="grc">ἐθελοντί</span> _voluntarily_, <span lang="grc">Ἑλληνιστί</span> _in Greek_ (_fashion_).

##### <a id="s345"></a> §345

##### <a id="s346"></a> §346

**Correlative Adverbs**.—Adverbs from pronominal stems often correspond in form and meaning. In the list on p. 102 poetic or rare words are in ().

**a.** The demonstratives in () are foreign to Attic prose except in certain phrases, as <span lang="grc">καὶ ὥς</span> _even thus_, <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽</span> (<span lang="grc">μηδ᾽</span>) <span lang="grc">ὥς</span> _not even thus_ (cp. [180](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s180) c); <span lang="grc">ἔνθα μὲν . . . ἔνθα δέ</span> _here . . . there_, <span lang="grc">ἔνθεν</span> (<span lang="grc">μέν</span>) <span lang="grc">καὶ ἔνθεν</span> (<span lang="grc">δέ</span>) _from this side and that_. <span lang="grc">ἔνθα</span> and <span lang="grc">ἔνθεν</span> are usually relatives, <span lang="grc">ἔνθα</span> taking the place of <span lang="grc">οὗ</span> _where_ and <span lang="grc">οἷ</span> _whither_, and <span lang="grc">ἔνθεν</span> of <span lang="grc">ὅθεν</span> _whence_.

**b.** <span lang="grc">τοτὲ μὲν . . . τοτὲ δέ</span> is synonymous with <span lang="grc">ποτὲ μὲν . . . ποτὲ δέ</span>.

**c.** <span lang="grc">οὖν</span> ([339 e](06-pronouns.md#s339)) may be added for indefiniteness: <span lang="grc">ὁπωσοῦν</span> _in any way whatever_, <span lang="grc">ὁποθενοῦν</span> _from what place soever_. <span lang="grc">ποτέ</span> is often used after interrogatives to give an intensive force, as in <span lang="grc">τίς ποτε</span> _who in the world_ (as _qui tandem_); also with negatives, as in <span lang="grc">οὔποτε</span> _never_, <span lang="grc">οὐπώποτε</span> _never yet_. ##### <a id="s346D"></a> §346 D

\1. Hom. has (Aeolic) <span lang="grc">ππ</span> in <span lang="grc">ὅππως, ὁππότε</span>; Hdt. has <span lang="grc">κ</span> for the <span lang="grc">π-</span>forms, e.g. <span lang="grc">κοῦ, κού, ὅκου, κότε</span>, etc. Hdt. has <span lang="grc">ἐνθαῦτα, ἐνθεῦτεν</span> for <span lang="grc">ἐνταῦθα, ἐντεῦθεν</span> ([126 D.](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s126)).

\2. Poetic are <span lang="grc">πόθι</span> for <span lang="grc">ποῦ, ὅθι</span> for <span lang="grc">οὗ, ἦμος</span> _when_, <span lang="grc">ἧ</span> _which way, where_, etc.
