# SUBSTANTIVES: FIRST DECLENSION (STEMS IN ᾱ )

##### <a id="s211"></a> §211

Stems in <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> are masculine or feminine. The feminine nominative singular ends in <span lang="grc">-ᾱ, -ᾰ</span>, or <span lang="grc">-η</span>; the masculine nominative singular adds <span lang="grc">-ς</span> to the stem, and thus ends in <span lang="grc">-ᾱς</span> or <span lang="grc">-ης</span>.

Observe the shortening of the stem in vocative singular and plural, in nominative and dative plural, and genitive and dative dual. <!-- page 49 -->

##### <a id="s213"></a> §213

**Accent**.—For special rule of accent in the genitive plural, see [208](../03-general-rules-for-the-accent-of-nouns.md#s208). The genitive plural is always perispomenon since <span lang="grc">-ῶν</span> is contracted from <span lang="grc">-έ-ων</span> derived from original (and Hom.) <span lang="grc">-ά̄-ων</span> ([51](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/02-euphony-of-vowels.md#s51)). Final <span lang="grc">-αι</span> is treated as short ([169](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s169)).

**a.** The form of the gen. pl. is taken from the pronominal adjective, i.e. (Hom.) <span lang="grc">θεά̄ων</span> _goddesses_ follows the analogy of (Hom.) <span lang="grc">τά̄ων</span> ([332 D.](../06-pronouns.md#s332)) for <span lang="grc">τᾱ-</span> (<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">ων</span>, cf. Lat. _istā-rum deā-rum_.

##### <a id="s214"></a> §214

The dialects show various forms.

##### <a id="s214D"></a> §214 D

\1. For <span lang="grc">η</span>, Doric and Aeolic have original <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span>; thus, <span lang="grc">νί̄κᾱ, ϝί̄κᾱς, ϝί̄κᾳ, νί̄κᾱν; πολί̄τᾱς, κριτά̄ς, Ἀτρείδᾱς</span>.

\2. Ionic has <span lang="grc">η</span> for the <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> of Attic even after <span lang="grc">ε, ι</span>, and <span lang="grc">ρ</span>; thus, <span lang="grc">γενεή, οἰκίη, ἀγορή, μοίρης, μοίρῃ</span> (nom. <span lang="grc">μοῖρᾰ</span>), <span lang="grc">νεηνίης</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀγορή, -ῆς, -ῇ, -ήν; νεηνίης, -ου, -ῃ, -ην</span>. But Hom. has <span lang="grc">θεά̄</span> _goddess_, <span lang="grc">Ἑρμείᾱς</span> _Hermes_.

\3. The dialects admit <span lang="grc">-ᾰ</span> in the nom. sing. less often than does Attic. Thus, Ionic <span lang="grc">πρύμνη</span> _stern_, <span lang="grc">κνί̄ση</span> _savour_ (Att. <span lang="grc">πρύμνα, κνῖσα</span>), Dor. <span lang="grc">τόλμᾱ</span> _daring_. Ionic has <span lang="grc">η</span> for <span lang="grc">ᾰ</span> in the abstracts in <span lang="grc">-είη, -οίη</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀληθείη</span> _truth_, <span lang="grc">εὐνοίη</span> _good-will_). Hom. has <span lang="grc">νύμφᾰ</span> _oh maiden_ from <span lang="grc">νύμφη</span>.

\4. **Nom. sing. masc**.—Hom. has <span lang="grc">-τα</span> for <span lang="grc">-της</span> in <span lang="grc">ἱππότα</span> _horseman_, <span lang="grc">ἱππηλάτα</span> _driver of horses_, <span lang="grc">νεφεληγερέτα</span> _cloud-collector_, <span lang="grc">κῡανοχαῖτα</span> _dark-haired;_ and, with recessive accent, <span lang="grc">μητίετα</span> _counsellor_. So in the adj. <span lang="grc">εὐρύοπα</span> _far-sounding_. Cp. Lat. _poeta_, _scriba_.

\5. **Gen. sing. masc**.—(a) <span lang="grc"> **-ᾱο** </span>, the original form from <span lang="grc">ᾱ-</span>(<span lang="grc">ι</span>)<span lang="grc">ο</span>, is used by Hom. (<span lang="grc">Ἀτρείδᾱο</span>). It contracts in Aeolic and Doric to <span lang="grc"> **-ᾱ** </span> (<span lang="grc">Ἀτρείδᾱ</span>).

(b) <span lang="grc"> **-εω** </span>, from <span lang="grc">ηο</span> (= <span lang="grc">ᾱο</span>) by 34, is also used by Hom., who makes it a single syllable by synizesis ([60](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/02-euphony-of-vowels.md#s60)), as in <span lang="grc">Ἀτρείδεω</span>. Hdt. has <span lang="grc">-εω</span>, as <span lang="grc">πολί̄τεω</span> ([163 a](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s163)).

(c) <span lang="grc"> **-ω** </span> in Hom. after a vowel, <span lang="grc">Βορέω</span> (nom. <span lang="grc">Βορέης</span>).

\6. **Accus. sing. masc**.—In proper names Hdt. often has <span lang="grc">-εα</span> borrowed from <span lang="grc"> **ς** </span> stems ([264](05-third-declension.md#s264)), as <span lang="grc">Μιλτιάδεα</span> for <span lang="grc">Μιλτιάδη-ν</span>.

\7. **Dual**.—Hom. has the nom. dual of masculines only. In the gen. and dat. Hom. has <span lang="grc">-αιν</span> and also <span lang="grc">-αιιν</span>.

\8. **Gen. plur**.—(a) <span lang="grc"> **-ά̄ων** </span>, the original form, occurs in Hom. (<span lang="grc">μουσά̄ων, ἀγορά̄ων</span>). In Aeolic and Doric <span lang="grc">-ά̄ων</span> contracts to (b) <span lang="grc"> **-ᾶν** </span> (<span lang="grc">ἀγορᾶν</span>). The Doric <span lang="grc">-ᾶν</span> is found also in the choral songs of the drama (<span lang="grc">πετρᾶν</span> _rocks_). (c) <span lang="grc"> **-έων** </span>, the Ionic form, appears in Homer, who usually makes it a single syllable by synizesis ([60](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/02-euphony-of-vowels.md#s60)) as in <span lang="grc">βουλέωνν</span>, from <span lang="grc">βουλή</span> _plan_. <span lang="grc">-έων</span> is from <span lang="grc">-ήων</span>, Ionic for <span lang="grc">-ά̄ων</span>. (d) <span lang="grc"> **-ῶν** </span> in Hom. generally after vowels (<span lang="grc">κλισιῶν</span>, from <span lang="grc">κλισίη</span> _hut_).

\9. **Dat. plur**.: <span lang="grc">-ῃσι</span>(<span lang="grc">ν</span>), <span lang="grc">-ῃς</span>, generally before vowels, and (rarely) <span lang="grc">-αις</span> in Hom. Ionic has <span lang="grc">-ῃσι</span>, Aeolic <span lang="grc">-αισι</span>(<span lang="grc">ν</span>), <span lang="grc">-αις</span>, Doric <span lang="grc">-αις</span>.

\10. **Accus. plur**.: <span lang="grc">-ανς, -ᾰς, ᾱς</span> in various Doric dialects, <span lang="grc">-αις</span> in Aeolic.

##### <a id="s215"></a> §215

**Dative Plural**.—The ending <span lang="grc">-αισι</span>(<span lang="grc">ν</span>) occurs in Attic poetry (<span lang="grc">δίκαισι</span> from <span lang="grc">δίκη</span> _right_, <span lang="grc">δεσπόταισι</span> from <span lang="grc">δεσπότης</span> _lord_).

**a.** Attic inscriptions to 420 B. C. have <span lang="grc">-ῃσι</span> (written <span lang="grc">-ηισι</span>), <span lang="grc">-ησι</span>, and (after <span lang="grc">ε, ι, ρ</span>) <span lang="grc">-ᾳσι</span> (written <span lang="grc">-αισι</span>) and <span lang="grc">-ᾱσι</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">δραχμῇσι</span> and <span lang="grc">δραχμῆσι</span> _drachmas_, <span lang="grc">ταμίᾳσι</span> and <span lang="grc">ταμίᾱσι</span> _stewards_. <span lang="grc">-ησι</span> and <span lang="grc">-ᾱσι</span> are properly endings of the locative case ([341](../07-adverbs.md#s341)). <!-- page 50 -->

##### <a id="s217"></a> §217

RULES.—**a.** If the nominative singular ends in _alpha_ preceded by a vowel (<span lang="grc">σκιά̄</span> _shadow_) or <span lang="grc">ρ</span> (<span lang="grc">μοῖρα</span>), _alpha_ is kept throughout the singular.

**b.** If the nominative singular ends in _alpha_ preceded by a consonant not <span lang="grc">ρ</span>, _alpha_ is changed to <span lang="grc">η</span> in the genitive and dative singular.

**c.** If the nominative singular ends in <span lang="grc">η, η</span> is kept in all the cases of the singular.

**d.** When the genitive singular has <span lang="grc">-ης</span>, final <span lang="grc">α</span> of the nominative singular is _always_ short; when the genitive singular has <span lang="grc">-ᾱς</span>, the final <span lang="grc">α</span> is _generally_ long.

Feminines fall into two classes:

##### <a id="s218"></a> §218

(I) Feminines with <span lang="grc"> **ᾱ** </span> or <span lang="grc"> **η** </span> in all the cases of the singular.

After <span lang="grc">ε, ι</span>, or <span lang="grc">ρ, **ᾱ**</span> appears in all the cases of the singular, as in <span lang="grc">γενεά̄</span> _race_, <span lang="grc">οἰκίᾱ</span> _house_, <span lang="grc">χώρᾱ</span> _land_. Otherwise, <span lang="grc">η</span> throughout the singular, as <span lang="grc">νί̄κη</span> _victory_.

**a.** After <span lang="grc">ο</span>, we find both <span lang="grc"> **ᾱ** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **η** </span>, as <span lang="grc">στοά̄</span> _porch_, <span lang="grc">βοή</span> _shout_, <span lang="grc">ἀκοή</span> _hearing_, <span lang="grc">ὁͅοή</span> _current_, <span lang="grc">ῥόᾱ</span> _pomegranate_. After <span lang="grc">ρ</span> we have <span lang="grc">η</span> in <span lang="grc">κόρη</span> _girl_, <span lang="grc">δέρη</span> _neck_ ([31](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s31)).

##### <a id="s219"></a> §219

(II) Feminines with <span lang="grc"> **ᾰ** </span> in the nominative, accusative, and vocative singular. The quantity of the vowel is generally shown by the accent ([163](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s163), [164](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s164)).

In this class are included:

\1. Substantives having <span lang="grc"> **ς** </span> (<span lang="grc">ξ, ψ, ττ</span>, or <span lang="grc">σς</span>), <span lang="grc"> **ζ, λλ** </span>, or <span lang="grc"> **αιν** </span> before the final <span lang="grc">α</span> show <span lang="grc"> **ᾰ** </span> in nom., accus., and voc. sing., and <span lang="grc">η</span> in gen. and dat. sing. Thus, <!-- page 51 --> <span lang="grc">μοῦσα</span> _muse_, <span lang="grc">μούσης, μούσῃ, ἅμαξα</span> _wagon_, <span lang="grc">τράπεζα</span> _table_, <span lang="grc">γλῶττα</span> _tongue_, <span lang="grc">ῥίζα</span> _root_, <span lang="grc">ἅμιλλα</span> _contest_, <span lang="grc">λέαινα</span> _lioness_. Others are <span lang="grc">τόλμα</span> _daring_, <span lang="grc">δίαιτα</span> _mode of life_, <span lang="grc">ἄκανθα</span> _thorn_, <span lang="grc">μυῖα</span> _fly_.

\2. Substantives in <span lang="grc">ᾰ</span> in nom., accus., and voc. sing., and <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> in gen. and dat. sing.

**a.** Substantives in <span lang="grc">-εια</span> and <span lang="grc">-τρια</span> denoting females, as <span lang="grc">βασίλεια</span> _queen_ (but <span lang="grc">βασιλείᾱ</span> _kingdom_), <span lang="grc">ψάλτρια</span> _female harper;_ so the fem. of adj. in <span lang="grc">-υς</span>, as <span lang="grc">γλυκύς, γλυκεῖα</span> _sweet_.

**b.** Abstracts in <span lang="grc">-εια</span> and <span lang="grc">-οια</span> from adjectives in <span lang="grc">-ης</span> and <span lang="grc">-οος</span>, as <span lang="grc">ἀλήθεια</span> _truth_ (from <span lang="grc">ἀληθής</span> _true_), <span lang="grc">εὔνοια</span> _good will_ (from <span lang="grc">εὔνους, εὔνοος</span> _kind_, [290](07-adjectives.md#s290)).

**c.** Most substantives in <span lang="grc">-ρα</span> after a diphthong or <span lang="grc">ῡ</span>, as <span lang="grc">μοῖρα</span> _fate_, <span lang="grc">γέφῡρα</span> _bridge_.

##### <a id="s220"></a> §220

Exceptions to 219, 1: <span lang="grc">κόρση</span> _temple_ (later <span lang="grc">κόρρη</span>), <span lang="grc">ἕρση</span> _dew;_ to 2 b: in Attic poetry, <span lang="grc">ἀληθείᾱ, εὐνοίᾱ, ἀγνοίᾱ</span> _ignorance_, which owe their <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> to the influence of the genitive and dative <span lang="grc">ἀληθείᾱς, ἀληθείᾳ</span>, etc.

##### <a id="s221"></a> §221

Most, if not all, of the substantives in <span lang="grc">ᾰ</span> are formed by the addition of the suffix <span lang="grc">\[ιγλιδε\]α</span> or <span lang="grc">ια</span> ([20](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s20)); thus, <span lang="grc">γλῶττα</span> from <span lang="grc">γλωχ-\[ιγλιδε\]α</span> (cp. <span lang="grc">γλωχῖν-ες</span> _points_), <span lang="grc">γέφῡρα</span> from <span lang="grc">γεφυρ-\[ιγλιδε\]α, δότειρα</span> _giver_ from <span lang="grc">δοτερ-\[ιγλιδε\]α</span> (and so <span lang="grc">φέρουσα</span> _bearing_ from <span lang="grc">φεροντ\[ιγλιδε\]α</span>), <span lang="grc">μοῖρα</span> from <span lang="grc">μορ-\[ιγλιδε\]α, ψάλτρ-ια</span>.

##### <a id="s223"></a> §223

**Accent**.—The vocative of <span lang="grc">δεσπότης</span> _lord_ is <span lang="grc">δέσποτα</span>. <!-- page 52 -->

##### <a id="s224"></a> §224

<span lang="grc"> **ᾱ** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **η** </span>.—In the final syllable of the singular <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> appears after <span lang="grc">ε, ι</span>, and <span lang="grc">ρ</span>; otherwise we find <span lang="grc">η</span>. Cp. [218](#s218).

**a.** Exceptions are compounds in <span lang="grc">-μέτρης</span>: <span lang="grc">γεω-μέτρης</span> _measurer of land_.

##### <a id="s225"></a> §225

**Genitive singular**.—The form in <span lang="grc">-ου</span> is borrowed from the genitive singular of the second declension. A few words in <span lang="grc">-ᾱς</span>, generally names of persons not Greeks, have <span lang="grc">-ᾱ</span>, the Doric genitive ([214 D.](#s214) 5): <span lang="grc">Ἀννίβᾱς</span> _Hannibal_, gen. <span lang="grc">Ἀννίβᾱ</span>.

##### <a id="s226"></a> §226

**Vocative singular**.—Masculines in <span lang="grc">-ᾱς</span> have the vocative in <span lang="grc">-ᾱ</span> (<span lang="grc">νεᾱνίᾱ</span>); those in <span lang="grc">-της</span> have <span lang="grc">-ᾰ</span> (<span lang="grc">πολῖτα</span>), all others in <span lang="grc">-ης</span> have <span lang="grc">-η</span> (<span lang="grc">Ἀτρείδη, Κρονίδη</span> _son of Kronos_) except names of nations and compounds: <span lang="grc">Πέρσᾰ</span> _Persian_, <span lang="grc">Σκύθᾰ</span> _Scythian_, <span lang="grc">γεω-μέτρᾰ</span> (nom. <span lang="grc">γεω-μέτρης</span> _measurer of land_), <span lang="grc">παιδο-τρίβᾰ</span> _gymnastic master_.
