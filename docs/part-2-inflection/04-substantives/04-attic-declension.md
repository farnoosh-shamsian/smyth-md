# SUBSTANTIVES: ATTIC DECLENSION

##### <a id="s237"></a> §237

Some substantives ending in <span lang="grc">-εως</span> are placed under the Second Declension because they are derived from earlier <span lang="grc">ο</span> stems preceded by a long vowel (<span lang="grc">-εως</span> from <span lang="grc">-ηος</span>, [34](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s34)). A few others have a consonant before <span lang="grc">-ως</span>. The vocative has no special form.

N.—This declension is called “Attic” because the words in question generally show <span lang="grc">-ως</span> in Attic and <span lang="grc">-ος</span> in the Koinè dialect (p. 3, F).

##### <a id="s238"></a> §238

<!-- page 56 -->

**a.** So <span lang="grc">ὁ λεώς</span> _people_, <span lang="grc">ὁ Μενέλεως</span> _Menelaus_, <span lang="grc">ὁ λαγώς</span> _hare_. Observe that <span lang="grc">ω</span> is found in every form, and that it takes <span lang="grc">ι</span> _subscript_ in the dative of all numbers where an ordinary <span lang="grc">ο</span> stem has <span lang="grc">ι</span>.

**b.** There are no neuter substantives belonging to the Attic declension in standard classical literature; but neuter adjectives ([289](07-adjectives.md#s289)) end in <span lang="grc">-ων</span>.

**c.** <span lang="grc">νεώς</span> and most words of this declension owe their forms to transfer of quantity ([34](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s34)) or to shortening ([39](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s39)). Thus, <span lang="grc">νεώς</span> is from <span lang="grc">νηός</span> ( = Doric <span lang="grc">νᾱός</span>), <span lang="grc">νεών</span> from <span lang="grc">νηόν; νεῴ</span> is from <span lang="grc">νηῷ. λαγώς</span> is contracted from <span lang="grc">λαγωός</span>.

**d.** In the accusative singular some words end in <span lang="grc">-ω</span> or <span lang="grc">-ων</span>, as <span lang="grc">λαγώ</span> or <span lang="grc">λαγών</span> _hare_. So <span lang="grc">ὁ Ἄθως, ἡ Κέως, ἡ Τέως, ἡ Κῶς, ὁ Μί̄νως. ἡ ἕως</span> _dawn_ always has <span lang="grc">ἕω</span>.

##### <a id="s238D"></a> §238 D

Hom. has <span lang="grc">νηός</span> _temple_, <span lang="grc">λᾱός</span> _people_, <span lang="grc">κάλος</span> _cable_, <span lang="grc">λαγωός</span> _hare_, <span lang="grc">γάλοως</span> _sister-in-law_, <span lang="grc">Ἀθόως, Κόως</span>; Hdt. has <span lang="grc">λεώς, λαγός, Κέος</span>. Hom. and Hdt. have <span lang="grc">ἠώς</span>, gen. <span lang="grc">ἠοῦς</span>, _dawn_, whence Att. <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> by 39. Hom. has <span lang="grc">Πετεῶ-ο</span>, the original form of the genitive, from <span lang="grc">Πετεώς. νεώ</span> is from <span lang="grc">νεωο</span> out of <span lang="grc">νηοο</span>.

##### <a id="s239"></a> §239

**Accent**.—**a.** The accent of the nominative is kept in all cases. <span lang="grc">Μενέ0εως</span> ([163 a](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s163)) retains the accent of the earlier <span lang="grc">Μενέλᾱος</span>.

**b.** The genitive and dative are oxytone when the final syllable is accented.

N.—The accentuation of the words of this declension is doubtful. Some of the ancients accented <span lang="grc">λαγώς, λαγών</span>, others <span lang="grc">λαγῶς, λαγῶν</span>, etc.
