# SUBSTANTIVES: ADJECTIVES

## ADJECTIVES OF THE FIRST AND SECOND DECLENSIONS

##### <a id="s286"></a> §286

**Adjectives of Three Endings**.—Most adjectives of the vowel declension have three endings: <span lang="grc">-ος, -η</span> (or <span lang="grc">-ᾱ</span>), <span lang="grc">-ον</span>. The masculine and neuter are declined according to the second declension, the feminine according to the first.

**a.** When <span lang="grc">ε, ι,</span> or <span lang="grc">ρ</span> ([30](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s30), [218](01-first-declension-stems-in-alpha.md#s218)) precedes <span lang="grc">-ος</span> the feminine ends in <span lang="grc">-ᾱ</span>, not in <span lang="grc">-η</span>. But adjectives in <span lang="grc">-οος</span> (not preceded by <span lang="grc">ρ</span>) have <span lang="grc">η</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὄγδοος, ὀγδόη, ὄγδοον</span> _eighth_, <span lang="grc">ἀθρόος, ἀθρόᾱ, ἀθρόον</span> _crowded_. See [290 e](#s290).

<span lang="grc">ἐσθλός</span> _good_, <span lang="grc">κακός</span> _bad_, <span lang="grc">σοφός</span> _wise_, <span lang="grc">κοῦφος, κούφη, κοῦφον</span> _light_, <span lang="grc">δῆλος</span> _clear;_ <span lang="grc">ἀνδρεῖος, ἀνδρείᾱ, ἀνδρεῖον</span> _courageous_, <span lang="grc">δίκαιος</span> _just_, <span lang="grc">ὅμοιος</span> _like_, <span lang="grc">αἰσχρός, αἰσχρά̄, αἰσχρόν</span> _base_, <span lang="grc">ἐλεύθερος</span> _free;_ all participles in <span lang="grc">-ος</span> and all superlatives.

**a.** The accent in the feminine nominative and genitive plural follows that of the masculine: <span lang="grc">ἄξιαι, ἀξίων</span>, not <span lang="grc">ἀξίαι, ἀξιῶν</span>, as would be expected according to the rule for substantives ([205](../03-general-rules-for-the-accent-of-nouns.md#s205)), e.g. as in <span lang="grc">αἰτίᾱ</span> _cause_, <span lang="grc">αἰτίαι, αἰτιῶν</span>.

**b.** All adjectives and participles may use the mas uline instead of the feminine dual forms: <span lang="grc">τὼ ἀγαθὼ μητέρε</span> _the two good mothers_.

##### <a id="s287D"></a> §287 D

In the fem. nom. sing. Ionic has <span lang="grc">-η</span>, never <span lang="grc">-ᾱ</span>; in the fem. gen. pl. Hom. has <span lang="grc">-ά̄ων</span> (less often <span lang="grc">-έων</span>); Hdt. has <span lang="grc">-έων</span> in oxytone adjectives and participles, and so probably in barytones.

##### <a id="s288"></a> §288

**Adjectives of Two Endings**.—Adjectives using the masculine for the feminine are called adjectives of two endings. Most such adjectives are compounds.

**a.** Like <span lang="grc">ἄδικος</span> are declined the compounded <span lang="grc">ἄ-λογος</span> _irrational_, <span lang="grc">ἄ-τῑμος</span> _dishonoured_, <span lang="grc">ἀ-χρεῖος</span> _useless_, <span lang="grc">ἔμ-πειρος</span> _experienced_, <span lang="grc">ἐπί-φθονος</span> _envious_, <span lang="grc">εὔ-ξενος</span> _hospitable_, <span lang="grc">ὑπ-ήκοος</span> _obedient_. Like <span lang="grc">φρόνιμος</span> are declined the uncompounded <span lang="grc">βάρβαρος</span> _barbarian_, <span lang="grc">ἥσυχος</span> _quiet_, <span lang="grc">ἥμερος</span> _tame_, <span lang="grc">λάλος</span> _talkative_.

**b.** Like <span lang="grc">ἵ̄λεως</span> are declined other adjectives of the Attic declension ([237](04-attic-declension.md#s237)), as <span lang="grc">ἄκερως</span> _without horns_, <span lang="grc">ἀξιόχρεως</span> _serviceable_. For the accent, see [163 a](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s163). Adjectives in <span lang="grc">-ως, -ων</span> have <span lang="grc">-α</span> in the neut. pl., but <span lang="grc">ἔκπλεω</span> occurs in Xenophon.

**c.** <span lang="grc"> **πλέως** </span> _full_ has three endings: <span lang="grc">πλέως, πλέᾱ, πλέων</span>, pl. <span lang="grc">πλέῳ, πλέαι, πλέα</span>, but most compounds, such as <span lang="grc">ἔμπλεως</span> _quite full_, have the fem. like the masc. <span lang="grc"> **σῶς** </span> _safe_ has usually sing. N. <span lang="grc">σῶς</span> masc., fem. (rarely <span lang="grc">σᾶ</span>), <span lang="grc">σῶν</span> neut., A. <span lang="grc">σῶν</span>; plur. N. <span lang="grc">σῷ</span> masc., fem., <span lang="grc">σᾶ</span> neut., A. <span lang="grc">σῶς</span> masc., fem., <span lang="grc">σᾶ</span> neut. Other cases are supplied by <span lang="grc">σῶος, σώᾱ, σῶον. σῶον</span> also occurs in the accusative.

**d.** In poetry, and sometimes in prose, adjectives commonly of two endings have a feminine form, as <span lang="grc">πάτριος</span> _paternal_, <span lang="grc">βίαιος</span> _violent;_ and those commonly of three endings have no feminine, as <span lang="grc">ἀναγκαῖος</span> _necessary_, <span lang="grc">φίλιος</span> _friendly_.

## ADJECTIVES OF THE CONSONANT DECLENSION

##### <a id="s291"></a> §291

Such adjectives as belong only to the consonant declension have two endings. Most such adjectives have stems in <span lang="grc">ες</span> (nominative <span lang="grc">-ης</span> and <span lang="grc">-ες</span>) and <span lang="grc">ον</span> (nominative <span lang="grc">-ων</span> and <span lang="grc">-ον</span>). Under <span lang="grc">ον</span> stems fall comparative adjectives, as <span lang="grc">βελτί̄ων, βέλτῑον</span> _better_.

**a.** There are some compounds with other stems: M. F. <span lang="grc">ἀπάτωρ</span>, N. <span lang="grc">ἄπατορ</span> _fatherless_, G. <span lang="grc">ἀπάτορος; ἄπολις ἄπολι</span> _without a country_, <span lang="grc">ἀπόλιδος; αὐτοκράτωρ αὐτοκράτορ</span> _independent_, <span lang="grc">αὐτοκράτορος; ἄρρην</span> (older <span lang="grc">ἄρσην</span>) <span lang="grc">ἄρρεν</span> _male_, <span lang="grc">ἄρρενος; εὔχαρις εὔχαρι</span> _agreeable_, <span lang="grc">εὐχάριτος; εὔελπις εὔελπι</span> _hopeful_, <span lang="grc">εὐέλπιδος</span>. For the acc. of stems in <span lang="grc">ιτ</span> and <span lang="grc">ιδ</span> see [247](05-third-declension.md#s247). Neut. <span lang="grc">εὔχαρι</span> and <span lang="grc">εὔελπι</span> for <span lang="grc">εὐχαριτ, εὐελπιδ</span> ([133](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/04-final-consonants.md#s133)).

##### <a id="s294"></a> §294

Adjectives of the consonant declension having a separate form for the feminine inflect the feminine like a substantive of the first declension ending in <span lang="grc">-ᾰ</span> ([216](01-first-declension-stems-in-alpha.md#s216)).

##### <a id="s295"></a> §295

The feminine is made from the stem of the masculine (and neuter) by adding the suffix <span lang="grc">-\[ιγλιδε\]α</span> (_y_<span lang="grc">α</span>), which is combined with the preceding syllable in different ways. The genitive plural feminine is always perispomenon (cp. [208](../03-general-rules-for-the-accent-of-nouns.md#s208)). For the feminine dual, see [287 b](#s287).

##### <a id="s296"></a> §296

**Stems in <span lang="grc">υ</span>** (<span lang="grc">**-υς**, **-εια**, **-υ**</span>).—The masculine and neuter have the inflection of <span lang="grc">πῆχυς</span> and <span lang="grc">ἄστυ</span>, except that the genitive singular masculine and neuter ends in <span lang="grc">-ος</span> (not <span lang="grc">-ως</span>) and <span lang="grc">-εα</span> in the neuter plural remains uncontracted.

##### <a id="s296D"></a> §296 D

Hom. has usually <span lang="grc">-εῖα, -είης, -είῃ</span>, etc.; sometimes <span lang="grc">-έα, -έης, -έῃ</span>, etc. The forms without <span lang="grc">ι</span> ([43](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s43)) are regular in Hdt. For <span lang="grc">-ύν</span> Hom. has <span lang="grc">-έα</span> in <span lang="grc">εὐρέα πόντον</span> _the wide sea_. <span lang="grc">ἡδύς</span> and <span lang="grc">θῆλυς</span> are sometimes feminine in Hom.

Like <span lang="grc">χαρίεις</span> are inflected <span lang="grc">πτερόεις</span> _winged_, <span lang="grc">φωνήεις</span> _voiced_, <span lang="grc">δακρυόεις</span> _tearful_. Adjectives in <span lang="grc">-όεις</span> and <span lang="grc">-ήεις</span> are generally poetical or Ionic. <span lang="grc">φωνήεντα</span> meaning _vowels_ is always open.

**a.** <span lang="grc">χαρίεις, πᾶς</span> are derived from <span lang="grc">χαριεντ-ς, παντ-ς</span> by 100; <span lang="grc">χαρίεν</span> from <span lang="grc">χαριεντ-</span> by 133. The <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> of <span lang="grc">πᾶν</span> (for <span lang="grc">πᾰν</span>(<span lang="grc">τ</span>)-) is irregular and borrowed from <span lang="grc">πᾶς</span>. Compounds have <span lang="grc">ᾰ</span>: <span lang="grc">ἅπαν, σύμπαν</span>.

**b.** From <span lang="grc">χαριετ-</span> is derived <span lang="grc">χαρίεσσα</span> with <span lang="grc">σς</span>, not <span lang="grc">ττ</span>, by 114 a. <span lang="grc">χαριετ-</span> is a weak form of the stem <span lang="grc">χαριεντ-</span>; it appears also in <span lang="grc">χαρίεσι</span> for <span lang="grc">χαριετ-σι</span> ([98](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s98)). Participles in <span lang="grc">-εις</span> ([307](08-declension-of-participles.md#s307)) form the feminine from the strong stem <span lang="grc">-εντ ¨ \[ιγλιδε\]α. πᾶσα</span> stands for <span lang="grc">παντσα</span> out of <span lang="grc">παντ-\[ιγλιδε\]α</span> ([113 a](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s113)). <span lang="grc">πάντων, πᾶσι</span> are accented contrary to 252; but <span lang="grc">παντός, παντί, πᾱσῶν</span> are regular.

**c.** Adjectives in <span lang="grc">-όεις</span> contract, as <span lang="grc">μελιτοῦς, μελιτοῦττα, μελιτοῦν</span>, G. <span lang="grc">μελιτοῦντος, μελιτούττης</span>, etc. (<span lang="grc">μελιτόεις</span> _honied_). <span lang="grc">πτερόεις</span> has <span lang="grc">πτεροῦντα, πτεροῦσσα</span>. So in names of places: <span lang="grc">Ἀργεννοῦσσαι</span> _Argennusae_ for <span lang="grc">-όεσσαι; Ῥαμνοῦς, -οῦντος</span>, for <span lang="grc">Ῥαμνόεις, -όεντος</span>.

##### <a id="s299D"></a> §299 D

Hom. has <span lang="grc">αἱματόεσσα</span> _bloody_, <span lang="grc">σκιόεντα</span> _shadowy_, but <span lang="grc">τῑμῆς</span> and <span lang="grc">τῑμήεις</span> _valuable_, <span lang="grc">τῑμῆντα</span> and <span lang="grc">τῑμήεντα</span>. Doric has sometimes <span lang="grc">-ᾶς, -ᾶντος</span> for <span lang="grc">-ά̄εις, -ά̄εντος</span>, as <span lang="grc">φωνᾶντα</span>. Attic poetry often has the open forms <span lang="grc">-όεις, -όεσσα</span>.
