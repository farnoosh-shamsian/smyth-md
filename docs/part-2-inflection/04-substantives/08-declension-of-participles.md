# SUBSTANTIVES: DECLENSION OF PARTICIPLES

##### <a id="s300"></a> §300

Like <span lang="grc">ἀγαθός, -ή, -όν</span> are inflected all the participles of the middle, and the future passive participle.

##### <a id="s301"></a> §301

Participles of the active voice (except the perfect, [309](#s309)), and the aorist passive participle have stems in <span lang="grc">ντ</span>. The masculine and neuter follow the third declension, the feminine follows the first declension.

**a.** Most stems in <span lang="grc">οντ</span> make the nom. sing. masc. without <span lang="grc">ς</span>, like <span lang="grc">γέρων</span> ([243](05-third-declension.md#s243)). But stems in <span lang="grc">οντ</span> in the present and second aorist of <span lang="grc">μι</span>-verbs (<span lang="grc">διδούς, δούς</span>), and all stems in <span lang="grc">αντ, εντ, υντ</span>, add <span lang="grc">ς</span>, lose <span lang="grc">ντ</span> ([100](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s100)), and lengthen the preceding vowel (<span lang="grc">-ους, -ᾱς, -εις, -ῡς</span>, [37](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s37)). In like manner the dat. pl. is formed: <span lang="grc">-οντ-σι ῀ -ουσι</span>, etc.

N.—The stem of participles in <span lang="grc">-ων, -οντος</span> was originally <span lang="grc">ωντ. γέρων</span> was orignally a participle.

**b.** The nominative neuter of all participles drops final <span lang="grc">τ</span> of the stem ([133](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/04-final-consonants.md#s133)).

**c.** The perfect active participle (stem <span lang="grc">οτ</span>) has <span lang="grc">-ως</span> in the masculine, <span lang="grc">-ος</span> in the neuter. <span lang="grc">-ως</span> and <span lang="grc">-ος</span> are for <span lang="grc">-ϝωτ-ς, -ϝοτ-ς</span>.

**d.** The feminine singular is made by adding <span lang="grc">\[ιγλιδε\]α</span> to the stem. Thus, <span lang="grc">λύ̄ουσα</span> (<span lang="grc">λῡοντ-\[ιγλιδε\]α</span>), <span lang="grc">οὗσα</span> (<span lang="grc">ὀντ-\[ιγλιδε\]α</span>), <span lang="grc">ἱστᾶσα</span> (<span lang="grc">ἱσταντ-\[ιγλιδε\]α</span>), <span lang="grc">τιθεῖσα</span> (<span lang="grc">τιθεντ-\[ιγλιδε\]α</span>). The perfect adds <span lang="grc">-υ</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">-\[ιγλιδε\]α</span>, as in <span lang="grc">εἰδ-υῖα</span>.

##### <a id="s302"></a> §302

The vocative of all participles is the same as the nominative.

##### <a id="s303"></a> §303

Participles in <span lang="grc">-ων, -ᾱς, -εις, -ους, -ῡς</span> frequently use the masculine for the feminine in the dual.

##### <a id="s304"></a> §304

The accent of monosyllabic participles is an exception to 252: <span lang="grc">ὤν, ὄντος</span> (not <span lang="grc">ὀντός</span>), <span lang="grc">στά̄ς, στάντος</span>.

**a.** All participles in <span lang="grc">-ων</span> are inflected like <span lang="grc">λύ̄ων</span>, those in <span lang="grc">-ών</span> having the accent of <span lang="grc">ὤν, ὄντος</span>, etc.; as <span lang="grc">λιπών, λιποῦσα, λιπόν</span> _having left_. Such participles are from <span lang="grc">ω-</span>verbs, in which <span lang="grc">ο</span> is a part of the tense suffix.

**b.** Like participles are declined the adjectives <span lang="grc">ἑκών, ἑκοῦσα, ἑκόν</span> _willing_, <span lang="grc">ἄ̄κων, ἄ̄κουσα, ἀ̂̄κον</span> _unwilling_ (for <span lang="grc">ἀέκων</span>, etc.), G. <span lang="grc">ἄ̄κοντος, ἀ̄κούσης, ἄ̄κοντος</span>.

##### <a id="s305D"></a> §305 D

In the feminine of participles from stems in <span lang="grc">οντ, αντ</span> ([306](#s306)), Aeolic has <span lang="grc">-οισα, -αισα</span> (<span lang="grc">λύ̄οισα. λύ̄σαισα</span>), and <span lang="grc">-αις</span> in the masculine (<span lang="grc">λύ̄σαις</span>).
