# SUBSTANTIVES: SECOND DECLENSION (STEMS IN ο )

<!-- page 53 -->

##### <a id="s228"></a> §228

O stems in the nominative add <span lang="grc">-ς</span> to the stem in masculines and feminines; <span lang="grc">-ν</span> in neuters. The feminines, of which there are few, are declined like the masculines. In the neuters, nominative, vocative, and accusative singular have the same form (in <span lang="grc">-ο-ν</span>); in the plural these cases end in <span lang="grc">-α</span>.

**a.** Final <span lang="grc">-οι</span> is treated as short ([169](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s169)).

**b.** The dat. sing. in <span lang="grc">-ῳ</span> represents the union of the stem vowel <span lang="grc">-ο</span> and _ai_, the original case ending in the I. E. languages. Forms in <span lang="grc">-οι</span>, as <span lang="grc">οἴκοι</span> _at home_, may be locatives (<span lang="grc">-ο ¨ ι</span>, the locative ending).—The stem vowel <span lang="grc">ο</span> varies with <span lang="grc">ε</span>, which appears in the vocative sing., and in <span lang="grc">πανδημεί</span> (locative) _in full force_.— N. A. V. dual <span lang="grc">-ω</span> is for I. E. _ōu_.—The genitive pl. <span lang="grc">-ων</span> is due to the union of <span lang="grc">-ο ¨ ων</span>, which contracted to <span lang="grc">-ων</span> in the earliest period of the language.—The neuter plural is probably the relic of a feminine collective ending in <span lang="grc">-ᾱ</span>, which was shortened to <span lang="grc">-ᾰ</span>.

##### <a id="s230"></a> §230

The dialects show various forms.

##### <a id="s230D"></a> §230 D

\1. **Gen. sing**.—<span lang="grc"> **-οιο** </span>, the original form, appears in Hom. <span lang="grc">πολέμοιο</span>. By loss of <span lang="grc">ι</span> ([43](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s43)) comes <span lang="grc"> **-οο** </span>, which is sometimes read in Hom. (<span lang="grc">Αἰόλοο</span> for <span lang="grc">Αἰόλου κ</span> [36](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s36)). By contraction of <span lang="grc">οο</span> comes <span lang="grc"> **-ου** </span> found in Hom., Ionic, Milder Doric. <span lang="grc">οο</span> yields <span lang="grc"> **ω** </span> in Aeolic and Severer Doric (<span lang="grc">ἵππω</span>).

\2. **Dual**.—<span lang="grc">-οιιν</span> in Hom. (<span lang="grc">ἵπποιιν</span>).

\3. **Dat. pl**.—<span lang="grc">-οισι</span>(<span lang="grc">ν</span>) Hom., Aeolic, Ionic.

\4. **Acc. pl**.—<span lang="grc">-ους</span> is from <span lang="grc">-ον-ς</span> (found in Cretan), that is, the accus. sing. + <span lang="grc">ς</span>. From <span lang="grc">-ονς</span> comes <span lang="grc">-ως</span> Severer Doric, <span lang="grc">-οις</span> Aeolic, <span lang="grc">-ος</span> Cretan and in Dor. poetry. <span lang="grc">-ους</span> is Hom., Ionic, and Milder Doric.

##### <a id="s233"></a> §233

**Vocative**.—The nominative <span lang="grc">θεός</span> is used instead of the vocative. <span lang="grc">ἀδελφός</span> _brother_ retracts the accent (<span lang="grc">ἄδελφε</span>).

##### <a id="s234"></a> §234

**Dative Plural**.—The ending <span lang="grc">-οισι</span>(<span lang="grc">ν</span>) often appears in poetry, rarely in Attic prose (Plato).
