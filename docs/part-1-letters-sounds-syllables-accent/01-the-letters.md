##### <a id="s2"></a> §2

The Greek alphabet as given above originated in Ionia, and was adopted at Athens in 403 B.C. The letters from A to T are derived from Phoenician and have Semitic names. The signs <span lang="grc">Υ</span> to <span lang="grc">Ω</span> were invented by the Greeks. From the Greek alphabet are derived the alphabets of most European countries. The ancients used only the large letters, called _majuscules_ (capitals as <span lang="grc">Ε</span>, uncials as \[Eunc \]); the small letters (_minuscules_), which were used as a literary hand in the ninth century, are cursive forms of the uncials.

**a.** Before 403 B.C. in the official Attic alphabet E stood for <span lang="grc">ε, η</span>, spurious <span lang="grc">ει</span> (6), O for <span lang="grc">ο, ω</span>, spurious <span lang="grc">ου</span> (6), H for the rough breathing, <span lang="grc">ΧΣ</span> for <span lang="grc">Ξ, ΦΣ</span> for <span lang="grc">Ψ. Λ</span> was written for <span lang="grc">γ</span>, and \[lins \] for <span lang="grc">λ</span>. Thus:

##### <a id="s3"></a> §3

In the older period there were two other letters: (1) <span lang="grc">Ϝ</span>: <span lang="grc">ϝαῦ</span>, _uau_, called digamma (i.e. _double-gamma_) from its shape. It stood after <span lang="grc">ε</span> and was pronounced like <span lang="grc">ω. ϝ</span> was written in Boeotian as late as 200 B.C. (2) ϟ: <span lang="grc">κόππα</span>, _koppa_, which stood after <span lang="grc">π</span>. Another <span lang="grc">ς</span>, called _san_, is found in the sign \[sampi \], called _sampi_, i.e. _san_ + _pi_. On these signs as numerals, see [348](../part-2-inflection/08-numerals.md#s348).

##### <a id="s3D"></a> §3 D

Vau was in use as a genuine sound at the time the Homeric poems were composed, though it is found in no Mss. of Homer. Many apparent irregularities of epic verse (such as _hiatus_, 47 D.) can be explained only by supposing that <span lang="grc">ϝ</span> was actually sounded. Examples of words containing <span lang="grc">ϝ</span> are: <span lang="grc">ἄστυ</span> _town_, <span lang="grc">ἄναξ</span> _lord_, <span lang="grc">ἁνδάνω</span> _please_, <span lang="grc">εἴκω</span> _give way_ (cp. _weak_), <span lang="grc">εἴκοσι</span> _twenty_ (cp. _viginti_), <span lang="grc">ἕκαστος</span> _each_, <span lang="grc">ἑκών</span> _willing_, <span lang="grc">ἔλπομαι</span> _hope_ (cp. _voluptas_), <span lang="grc">ἔοικα</span> _am like_, <span lang="grc">ἕο, οἷ, ἕ</span> _him_, <span lang="grc">ἕξ</span> _six_, <span lang="grc">ἔπος</span> _word_, <span lang="grc">εἶπον</span> _said_, <span lang="grc">ἔργον, ἔρδω</span> _work_, <span lang="grc">ἕννῡμι</span> _clothe_, fr. <span lang="grc">ϝεσ-νῡμι</span> (cp. _vestis_), <span lang="grc">ἐρέω</span> _will say_ (cp. _verbum_), <span lang="grc">ἕσπερος</span> _evening_ (cp. _vesper_), <span lang="grc">ἴον</span> _violet_ (cp. _viola_), <span lang="grc">ἔτος</span> _year_ (cp. _vetus_), <span lang="grc">ἡδύς</span> _sweet_ (cp. _suavis_), <span lang="grc">ἰδεῖν</span> (<span lang="grc">οἶδα</span>) _know_ (cp. _videre_, _wit_), <span lang="grc">ἴ̄ς</span> _strength_ (cp. _vis_), <span lang="grc">ἰ̄τέα</span> _willow_ (cp. _vitis_, _withy_), <span lang="grc">οἶκος</span> _house_ (cp. _vicus_), <span lang="grc">οἶνος</span> _wine_ (cp. _vinum_), <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> _his_ ([123](03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s123)), <span lang="grc">ὄχος</span> _carriage_ (cp. _veho_, _wain_). Vau was lost first before <span lang="grc">ο-</span>sounds (<span lang="grc">ὁράω</span> _see_, cp. be-_ware_). <span lang="grc">ϝ</span> occurred also in the middle of words: <span lang="grc">κλέϝος</span> _glory_, <span lang="grc">αἰϝεί</span> _always_, <span lang="grc">ὄϝις</span> _sheep_ (cp. _ovis_), <span lang="grc">κληϝίς</span> _key_ (Dor. <span lang="grc">κλᾱΐς</span>, cp. _clavis_), <span lang="grc">ξένϝος</span> _stranger_, <span lang="grc">Διϝί</span> _to Zeus_, <span lang="grc">καλϝός</span> _beautiful_. Cp. [20](#s20), [31](#s31), 37 D., 122, 123.

## Vowels and Diphthongs

##### <a id="s4"></a> §4

There are seven vowels: <span lang="grc">α, ε, η, ι, ο, υ, ω</span>. Of these <span lang="grc">ε</span> and <span lang="grc">ο</span> are always short, and take about half the time to pronounce as <span lang="grc">η</span> and <span lang="grc">ω</span>, which are always long; <span lang="grc">α, ι, υ</span> are short in some syllables, long in others. In this Grammar, when <span lang="grc">α, ι, υ</span> are not marked as long (<span lang="grc">ᾱ, ῑ, ῡ</span>) they are understood to be short. All vowels with the circumflex ([149](07-accent-general-principles.md#s149)) are long. On length by position, see [144](06-syllables-and-their-quantity.md#s144).

**a.** Vowels are said to be _open_ or _close_ according as the mouth is more open <!-- page 9 --> or less open in pronouncing them, the tongue and lips assuming different positions in the case of each.

##### <a id="s5"></a> §5

A diphthong (<span lang="grc">δίφθογγος</span> _having two sounds_) combines two vowels in one syllable. The second vowel is <span lang="grc">ι</span> or <span lang="grc">υ</span>. The diphthongs are: <span lang="grc">αι, ει, οι, ᾱͅ, ῃ, ῳ; αυ, ευ, ου, ηυ</span>, and <span lang="grc">υι</span>. The <span lang="grc">ι</span> of the so-called _improper_ diphthongs, <span lang="grc">ᾱͅ, ῃ, ῳ</span>, is written below the line and is called _iota subscript_. But with capital letters, <span lang="grc">ι</span> is written on the line (_adscript_), as <span lang="grc">ΤΗΙ ΩΙΔΗΙ ῀ τῇ ᾠδῇ</span> or <span lang="grc">Ὠιδῇ</span> _to the song_. All diphthongs are long.

**a.** In <span lang="grc">ᾳ, ῃ, ῳ</span> the <span lang="grc">ι</span> ceased to be written about 100 B.C. The custom of writing <span lang="grc">ι</span> under the line is as late as about the eleventh century.

##### <a id="s5D"></a> §5 D

A diphthong <span lang="grc">ωυ</span> occurs in New Ionic (<span lang="grc">ὡυτός</span> _the same_ from <span lang="grc">ὁ αὐτός</span> 68 D., <span lang="grc">ἐμωυτοῦ</span> _of myself_ = <span lang="grc">ἐμαυτοῦ</span> 329 D., <span lang="grc">θωῦμα ῀ θαῦμα</span> _wonder_). Ionic has <span lang="grc">ηυ</span> for Attic <span lang="grc">αυ</span> in some words (Hom. <span lang="grc">νηῦς</span> _ship_).

##### <a id="s6"></a> §6

<span lang="grc">ει, ου</span> are either _genuine_ or _spurious_ (apparent) diphthongs ([25](#s25)). Genuine <span lang="grc">ει, ου</span> are a combination of <span lang="grc">ε ¨ ι, ο ¨ υ</span>, as in <span lang="grc">λείπω</span> _I leave_ (cp. <span lang="grc">λέλοιπα</span> _I have left_, 35 a), <span lang="grc">γένει</span> _to a race_ ([49](02-euphony-of-vowels.md#s49)), <span lang="grc">ἀκόλουθος</span> _follower_ (cp. <span lang="grc">κέλευθος</span> _way_). Spurious <span lang="grc">ει, ου</span> arise from contraction ([50](02-euphony-of-vowels.md#s50)) or compensatory lengthening ([37](#s37)). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐφίλει</span> _he loved_, from <span lang="grc">ἐφίλεε, θείς</span> _placing_ from <span lang="grc">θεντ-ς; ἐφίλουν</span> _they loved_ from <span lang="grc">ἐφίλεον, πλοῦς</span> _voyage_ from <span lang="grc">πλόος, δούς</span> _giving_ from <span lang="grc">δοντ-ς</span>.

##### <a id="s7"></a> §7

The figure of a triangle represents the relations of the vowels and spurious diphthongs to one another. <!-- figure: no image in source -->

From <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> to <span lang="grc">ι</span> and from <span lang="grc">ᾰ</span> to <span lang="grc">ου</span> the elevation of the tongue gradually increases. <span lang="grc">ω, ο, ου, υ</span> are accompanied by rounding of the lips.

##### <a id="s8"></a> §8

**Diaeresis**.—A double dot, the mark of diaeresis (<span lang="grc">διαίρεσις</span> _separation_), may be written over <span lang="grc">ι</span> or <span lang="grc">υ</span> when these do not form a diphthong with the preceding vowel: <span lang="grc">προΐστημι</span> _I set before_, <span lang="grc">νηΐ</span> _to a ship_.

##### <a id="s8D"></a> §8 D

In poetry and in certain dialects vowels are often written apart which later formed diphthongs: <span lang="grc">πάις</span> (or <span lang="grc">πάϊς</span>) _boy_ or _girl_, <span lang="grc">Πηλεΐδης</span> _son of Peleus_, <span lang="grc">ἐύ</span> (or <span lang="grc">ἐΰ</span>) _well_, <span lang="grc">Ἀίδης</span> (or <span lang="grc">Ἀΐδης</span>) _Hades_, <span lang="grc">γένεϊ</span> _to a race_.

## Breathings

##### <a id="s9"></a> §9

Every initial vowel or diphthong has either the rough (‘) or the smooth (’) breathing. The rough breathing (_spiritus asper_) is pronounced as _h_, which is sounded before the vowel; the smooth <!-- page 10 --> breathing (_spiritus lenis_) is not sounded. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὅρος</span> hóros _boundary_, <span lang="grc">ὄρος</span> óros _mountain_.

##### <a id="s9D"></a> §9 D

The Ionic of Asia Minor lost the rough breathing at an early date. So also before <span lang="grc">ρ</span> ([13](#s13)). Its occurrence in compounds ([124 D.](03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s124)) is a relic of the period when it was still sounded in the simple word. Hom. sometimes has the smooth where Attic has the rough breathing in forms that are not Attic: <span lang="grc">Ἀΐδης</span> (<span lang="grc">Ἅ̄ιδης</span>), the god _Hades_, <span lang="grc">ἆλτο</span> _sprang_ (<span lang="grc">ἅλλομαι</span>), <span lang="grc">ἄμυδις</span> _together_ (cp. <span lang="grc">ἅμα</span>), <span lang="grc">ἠέλιος</span> _sun_ (<span lang="grc">ἥλιος</span>), <span lang="grc">ἠώς</span> _dawn_ (<span lang="grc">ἕως</span>), <span lang="grc">ἴ̄ρηξ</span> _hawk_ (<span lang="grc">ἱέρᾱξ</span>), <span lang="grc">οὖρος</span> _boundary_ (<span lang="grc">ὅρος</span>). But also in <span lang="grc">ἄμαξα</span> _wagon_ (Attic <span lang="grc">ἅμαξα</span>). In Laconian medial <span lang="grc">ς</span> became ( (_h_): <span lang="grc">ἐνί̄κᾱἑ ῀ ἐνί̄κησε</span> _he conquered_.

##### <a id="s10"></a> §10

Initial <span lang="grc">υ</span> (<span lang="grc">ῠ</span> and <span lang="grc">ῡ</span>) always has the rough breathing.

##### <a id="s10D"></a> §10 D

In Aeolic, <span lang="grc">υ</span>, like all the other vowels (and the diphthongs), always has the smooth breathing. The epic forms <span lang="grc">ὔμμες</span> _you_, <span lang="grc">ὔμμι, ὔμμε</span> ([325 D.](../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s325)) are Aeolic.

##### <a id="s11"></a> §11

Diphthongs take the breathing, as the accent ([152](07-accent-general-principles.md#s152)), over the second vowel: <span lang="grc">αἱρέω</span> hairéo _I seize_, <span lang="grc">αἴρω</span> aíro _I lift_. But <span lang="grc">ᾳ, ῃ, ῳ</span> take both the breathing and the accent on the first vowel, even when <span lang="grc">ι</span> is written in the line (5): <span lang="grc">ᾁδω ῀ Ἄιδω</span> _I sing_, <span lang="grc">ᾄδης ῀ Ἅ̄ιδης</span> _Hades_, but <span lang="grc">Αἰνείᾱς</span> _Aeneas_. The writing <span lang="grc">ἀίδηλος</span> (<span lang="grc">Ἀίδηλος</span>) _destroying_ shows that <span lang="grc">αι</span> does not here form a diphthong; and hence is sometimes written <span lang="grc">αϊ</span> (8).

##### <a id="s12"></a> §12

In compound words (as in <span lang="grc">προορᾶν</span> _to foresee_, from <span lang="grc">πρό ¨ ὁρᾶν</span>) the rough breathing is not written, though it must often have been pronounced: cp. <span lang="grc">ἐξέδρᾱ</span> _a hall with seats_, Lat. _exhedra_, _exedra_, <span lang="grc">πολυίστωρ</span> _very learned_, Lat. _polyhistor_. On Attic inscriptions in the old alphabet (2 a) we find <span lang="grc">ΕΥΗΟΡΚΟΝ εὐὅρκον</span> _faithful to one's oath_.

##### <a id="s13"></a> §13

Every initial <span lang="grc">ρ</span> has the rough breathing: <span lang="grc">ῥήτωρ</span> _orator_ (Lat. _rhetor_). Medial <span lang="grc">ρρ</span> is written <span lang="grc">ῤῥ</span> in some texts: <span lang="grc">Πύῤῥος</span> _Pyrrhus_.

##### <a id="s14"></a> §14

The sign for the rough breathing is derived from H, which in the Old Attic alphabet (2 a) was used to denote _h_. Thus, HO <span lang="grc">ὁ</span> _the_. After H was used to denote <span lang="grc">η</span>, one half (\[rough \]) was used for _h_ (about 300 B.C.), and, later, the other half (\[smooth\]) for the smooth breathing. From \[rough \] and \[smooth\] come the forms ‘and’.

## CONSONANTS

##### <a id="s15"></a> §15

The seventeen consonants are divided into stops (or mutes), spirants, liquids, nasals, and double consonants. They may be arranged according to the degree of tension or slackness of the vocal chords in sounding them, as follows:

**a.** Voiced (sonant, i.e. sounding) consonants are produced when the vocal chords vibrate. The sounds are represented by the letters <span lang="grc">β, δ, γ</span> (stops), <span lang="grc">λ, ρ</span> (liquids), <span lang="grc">μ, ν, γ-</span>nasal ([19 a](#s19)) (nasals), and <span lang="grc">ζ</span>. (All the vowels are voiced.) <span lang="grc">ρ</span> with the rough breathing is voiceless.

**b.** Voiceless (surd, i.e. hushed) consonants require no exertion of the vocal chords. These are <span lang="grc">π, τ, κ, φ, θ, χ</span> (stops), <span lang="grc">ς</span> (spirant or sibilant), and <span lang="grc">ψ</span> and <span lang="grc">ξ</span>.

**c.** Arranged according to the increasing degree of noise, nearest to the vowels are the nasals, in sounding which the air escapes without friction through the nose; next come the semivowels <span lang="grc">\[υγλιδε\]</span> and <span lang="grc">\[ιγλιδε\]</span> ([20 a](#s20)), the liquids, and the spirant <span lang="grc">ς</span>, in <!-- page 11 --> sounding which the air escapes with friction through the cavity of the mouth; next come the stops, which are produced by a removal of an obstruction; and finally the double consonants.

##### <a id="s16"></a> §16

**Stops** (or mutes). Stopped consonants are so called because in sounding them the breath passage is for a moment completely closed. The stops are divided into three _classes_ (according to the part of the mouth chiefly active in sounding them) and into three _orders_ (according to the degree of force in the expiratory effort).

| Labial (lip sounds) | <span lang="grc">π</span> | <span lang="grc">β</span> | <span lang="grc">φ</span> |
| Dental (teeth sounds) | <span lang="grc">τ</span> | <span lang="grc">δ</span> | <span lang="grc">θ</span> |
| Palatal (palate sounds) | <span lang="grc">κ</span> | <span lang="grc">γ</span> | <span lang="grc">χ</span> |
|  | Orders |  |  |
| Smooth | <span lang="grc">π</span> | <span lang="grc">τ</span> | <span lang="grc">κ</span> |

| Rough | <span lang="grc">φ</span> | <span lang="grc">θ</span> | <span lang="grc">χ</span> |

**a.** The dentals are sometimes called _linguals_. The rough stops are also called _aspirates_ (lit. breathed sounds) because they were sounded with a strong emission of breath ([26](#s26)). The smooth stops are thus distinguished from the rough stops by the absence of breathing. ( (_h_) is also an aspirate. The middle stops owe their name to their position in the above grouping, which is that of the Greek grammarians.

##### <a id="s17"></a> §17

**Spirants**.—There is one spirant: <span lang="grc">ς</span> (also called a _sibilant_).

**a.** A spirant is heard when the breath passage of the oral cavity is so narrowed that a rubbing noise is produced by an expiration.

##### <a id="s18"></a> §18

**Liquids**.—There are two liquids: <span lang="grc">λ</span> and <span lang="grc">ρ</span>. Initial <span lang="grc">ρ</span> always has the rough breathing ([13](#s13)).

##### <a id="s19"></a> §19

**Nasals**.—There are three nasals: <span lang="grc">μ</span> (labial), <span lang="grc">ν</span> (dental), and <span lang="grc">γ-</span>nasal (palatal).

**a.** Gamma before <span lang="grc">κ, γ, χ, ξ</span> is called <span lang="grc">γ-</span>nasal. It had the sound of _n_ in _think_, and was represented by _n_ in Latin. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἄγκῡρα</span> (Lat. _ancora_) _anchor_, <span lang="grc">ἄγγελος</span> (Lat. _angelus_) _messenger_, <span lang="grc">σφίγξ</span> _sphinx_.

**b.** The name _liquids_ is often used to include both liquids and nasals.

##### <a id="s20"></a> §20

**Semivowels**.—<span lang="grc">ι, υ</span>, the liquids, nasals, and the spirant <span lang="grc">ς</span> are often called _semivowels_. (<span lang="grc">\[ιγλιδε\]</span> becoming <span lang="grc">ζ</span>, and <span lang="grc">ϝ</span> are also called spirants.)

**a.** When <span lang="grc">ι</span> and <span lang="grc">υ</span> correspond to _y_ and _w_ (cp. _minion_, _persuade_) they are said to be unsyllabic; and, with a following vowel, make one syllable out of two. Semivocalic <span lang="grc">ι</span> and <span lang="grc">υ</span> are written <span lang="grc">\[ιγλιδε\]</span> and <span lang="grc">\[υγλιδε\]</span>. Initial <span lang="grc">\[ιγλιδε\]</span> passed into ( (_h_), as in <span lang="grc">ἧπαρ</span> _liver_, Lat. _jecur;_ and into <span lang="grc">ζ</span> in <span lang="grc">ζυγόν</span> _yoke_, Lat. _jugum_ (here it is often called the spirant _yod_). Initial <span lang="grc">\[υγλιδε\]</span> was written <span lang="grc">ϝ</span> (3). Medial <span lang="grc">\[ιγλιδε\], \[υγλιδε\]</span> before vowels were often lost, as in <span lang="grc">τῑμά-</span>(<span lang="grc">\[ιγλιδε\]</span>) <span lang="grc">ω</span> _I honour_, <span lang="grc">βο</span>(<span lang="grc">\[υγλιδε\]</span>)<span lang="grc">-ός</span>, gen. of <span lang="grc">βοῦ-ς</span> _ox_, _cow_ ([43](#s43)).

**b.** The form of many words is due to the fact that the liquids, nasals, and <span lang="grc">ς</span> may fulfil the office of a vowel to form syllables (cp. _bridle_, _even_, _pst_). This is expressed by <span lang="grc">λ̣ο̣, μ̣ο̣, ν̣ο̣, ρ̣ο̣, σ̣ο̣</span>, to be read ‘syllabic <span lang="grc">λ</span>,’ etc., or ‘sonant <span lang="grc">λ</span>’ (see [35 b](#s35), c).

##### <a id="s21"></a> §21

**Double Consonants**.—These are <span lang="grc">ζ, ξ</span>, and <span lang="grc">ψ. ζ</span> is a combination of <span lang="grc">σδ</span> (or <span lang="grc">δς</span>) or <span lang="grc">δι</span> ([26](#s26)). <span lang="grc">ξ</span> is written for <span lang="grc">κς, γτ, χτ; ψ</span> for <span lang="grc">πς, βς, φς</span>. <!-- page 12 -->

## ANCIENT GREEK PRONUNCIATION

##### <a id="s23"></a> §23

The pronunciation of Ancient Greek varied much according to time and place, and differed in many important respects from that of the modern language. While in general Greek of the classical period was a phonetic language, i.e. its letters represented the sounds, and no heard sound was unexpressed in writing (but see [108](03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s108)), in course of time many words were retained in their old form though their pronunciation had changed. The tendency of the language was thus to become more and more unphonetic. Our current pronunciation of Ancient Greek is only in part even approximately correct for the period from the death of Pericles ([429](../part-2-inflection/09-verbs/05-augment.md#s429) B.C.) to that of Demosthenes ([322](../part-2-inflection/05-comparison-of-adjectives.md#s322)); and in the case of several sounds, e.g. <span lang="grc">ζ, φ, χ, θ</span>, it is certainly erroneous for that period. But ignorance of the exact pronunciation, as well as long-established usage, must render any reform pedantical, if not impossible. In addition to, and in further qualification of, the list of sound equivalents in 1 we may note the following:

##### <a id="s24"></a> §24

**Vowels**.—Short <span lang="grc">α, ι, υ</span> differed in sound from the corresponding long voweis only in being less prolonged; <span lang="grc">ε</span> and <span lang="grc">ο</span> probably differed from <span lang="grc">η</span> and <span lang="grc">ω</span> also in being less open, a difference that is impossible to parallel in English as our short vowels are more open than the long vowels. <span lang="grc"> **ᾰ** </span>: as _a_ in Germ. _hat_. There is no true _ă_ in accented syllables in English; the _a_ of _idea_, _aha_ is a neutral vowel. <span lang="grc"> **ε** </span>: as _é_ in _bonté;_ somewhat similar is _a_ in _bakery_. <span lang="grc"> **η** </span>: as _ê_ in _fête_, or <!-- page 13 --> nearly as _e_ in _where_. <span lang="grc"> **ῐ** </span>: nearly as the first _e_ in _meteor_, _eternal_. <span lang="grc"> **ο** </span>: as _o_ in Fr. _mot_, somewhat like unaccented _ŏ_ in _obey_ or _phonetic_ (as often sounded). <span lang="grc"> **ω** </span>: as _o_ in Fr. _encore_. Eng. _ō_ is prevailingly diphthongal (_o^{u}_). <span lang="grc"> **υ** </span> was originally sounded as _u_ in _prune_, but by the fifth century had become like that of Fr. _tu_, Germ. _thür_. It never had in Attic the sound of _u_ in _mute_. After <span lang="grc">υ</span> had become like Germ. _ü_, the only means to represent the sound of the old <span lang="grc">υ</span> (_oo_ in _moon_) was <span lang="grc">ου</span> ([25](#s25)). Observe, however, that, in diphthongs, final <span lang="grc">υ</span> retained the old <span lang="grc">υ</span> sound.

##### <a id="s24D"></a> §24 D

In Lesbos, Boeotia, Laconia, possibly in Ionia, and in some other places, <span lang="grc">υ</span> was still sounded <span lang="grc">οο</span> after it became like Germ. _ü_ in Attic.

##### <a id="s25"></a> §25

**Diphthongs**.—The diphthongs were sounded nearly as follows:

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="grc">αι</span> as in _Cairo_ | <span lang="grc">αυ</span> as _ou_ in _out_ | <span lang="grc">ηυ</span> as _ēh’-oo_ |
| <span lang="grc">ει</span> as in _vein_ | <span lang="grc">ευ</span> as _e_ (m*e*t) + _oo_ (m*oo*n) | <span lang="grc">ωυ</span> as _ōh’-oo_ |
| <span lang="grc">οι</span> as in _soil_ | <span lang="grc">ου</span> as in _ourang_ | <span lang="grc">υι</span> as in Fr. _huit_ |

In <span lang="grc">**ᾳ**, **ῃ**, **ῳ**</span> the long open vowels had completely overpowered the <span lang="grc">ι</span> by 100 B.C., so that <span lang="grc">ι</span> ceased to be written (5 a). The <span lang="grc">ι</span> is now generally neglected in pronunciation though it may have still been sounded to some extent in the fourth century B.C.—The genuine diphthongs <span lang="grc"> **ει** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **ου** </span> (6) were originally distinct double sounds (_ĕh’-i_, _ŏh’-oo_), and as such were written <span lang="grc">ΕΙ, ΟΥ</span> in the Old Attic alphabet (2 a): <span lang="grc">ΕΓΕΙΔΕ ἐπειδή, ΤΟΥΤΟΝ τούτων</span>. The spurious diphthongs <span lang="grc"> **ει** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **ου** </span> (6) are digraphs representing the long sounds of simple <span lang="grc">ε</span> (French _é_) and original <span lang="grc">υ</span>. By 400 B.C. genuine <span lang="grc">ει</span> and <span lang="grc">ου</span> had become simple single sounds pronounced as _ei_ in _vein_ and _ou_ in _ourang;_ and spurious <span lang="grc">ει</span> and <span lang="grc">ου</span>, which had been written E and O (2 a), were now often written <span lang="grc">ΕΙ</span> and <span lang="grc">ΟΥ</span>. After 300 B.C. <span lang="grc">ει</span> gradually acquired the sound of _ei_ in _seize_. <span lang="grc"> **ευ** </span> was sounded like _eh’-oo_, <span lang="grc"> **ηυ** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **ωυ** </span> like _ēh’-oo_, _ōh’-oo_, pronounced rapidly but smoothly. <span lang="grc"> **υι** </span> is now commonly sounded as _ui_ in _quit_. It occurred only before vowels, and the loss of the <span lang="grc">ι</span> in <span lang="grc">ὑός</span> _son_ ([43](#s43)) shows that the diphthongal sound was disliked.

##### <a id="s26"></a> §26

**Consonants**.—Most of the consonants were sounded as in English (1). Before <span lang="grc">**ι**, **κ**, **γ**, **τ**, **ς**</span> never had a _sh_ (or _zh_) sound heard in _Lycia_ (<span lang="grc">Λυκίᾱ</span>), _Asia_ (<span lang="grc">Ἀσίᾱ</span>). <span lang="grc"> **ς** </span> was usually like our sharp _s_; but before voiced consonants ([15 a](#s15)) it probably was soft, like _z_; thus we find both <span lang="grc">κόζμος</span> and <span lang="grc">κόσμος</span> on inscriptions. —<span lang="grc"> **ζ** </span> was probably = _zd_, whether it arose from an original <span lang="grc">σδ</span> (as in <span lang="grc">Ἀθήναζε</span>, from <span lang="grc">Ἀθηνα</span>(<span lang="grc">ν</span>)<span lang="grc">σ-δε</span> _Athens-wards_), or from _dz_, developed from _dy_ (as in <span lang="grc">ζυγόν</span>, from (_d_)_y_<span lang="grc">υγόν</span>, cp. _jugum_). The _z_ in _zd_ gradually extinguished the _d_, until in the Hellenistic period (p. 4) <span lang="grc">ζ</span> sank to _z_ (as in _zeal_), which is the sound in Modern Greek.—The aspirates <span lang="grc">**φ**, **θ**, **χ**</span> were voiceless stops ([15 b](#s15), [16](#s16) a) followed by a strong expiration: <span lang="grc">π</span>^{h}, <span lang="grc">τ</span>^{h}, <span lang="grc">κ</span>^{h} as in _upheaval_, _hothouse_, _backhand_ (though here _h_ is in a different syllable from the stop). Thus, <span lang="grc">φεύγω</span> was <span lang="grc">π̔εύγω, θέλω</span> was <span lang="grc">τ̔έλω, ἔχω</span> was <span lang="grc">ἔ-κ̔ω</span>. Cp. <span lang="grc">ἐφ᾽ ᾧ</span> for <span lang="grc">ἐπ</span>(<span lang="grc">ὶ</span>) <span lang="grc">῾ῷ</span>, etc. Probably only one _h_ was heard when two aspirates came together, as in <span lang="grc">ἐχθρός</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐκτ̔ρός</span>). After 300 A.D. (probably) <span lang="grc">φ, θ</span>, and <span lang="grc">χ</span> became spirants, <span lang="grc">φ</span> being sounded as _f_ (as in <span lang="grc">Φίλιππος</span> _Philip_), <span lang="grc">θ</span> as _th_ in _theatre_, <span lang="grc">χ</span> as _ch_ in German _ich_ or _loch_. The stage between aspirates and spirants is sometimes represented by the writing <span lang="grc">πφ</span> (= _pf_), <span lang="grc">τθ, κχ</span>, <!-- page 14 --> which are affricata.—The neglect of the _h_ in Latin representations of <span lang="grc">φ, θ, χ</span> possibly shows that these sounds consisted of a stop + _h_. Thus, _Pilipus_ = <span lang="grc">Φίλιππος</span>, _tus_ = <span lang="grc">θύος</span>, _Aciles_ = <span lang="grc">Ἀχιλλεύς</span>. Modern Greek has the spirantic sounds, and these, though at variance with classical pronunciation, are now usually adopted. See also [108](03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s108).

##### <a id="s26D"></a> §26 D

Aeolic has <span lang="grc">σδ</span> for <span lang="grc">ζ</span> in <span lang="grc">ὔσδος</span> (<span lang="grc">ὄζος</span> _branch_). In late Laconian <span lang="grc">θ</span> passed into <span lang="grc">ς</span> (<span lang="grc">σηρίον ῀ θηρίον</span> _wild beast_). In Laconian and some other dialects <span lang="grc">β</span> became a spirant and was written for <span lang="grc">ϝ. δ</span> became a spirant in Attic after Christ.

##### <a id="s27"></a> §27

| LONG <span lang="grc"> **η** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **ᾱ** </span> after <span lang="grc">ε, ι, ρ</span>, [31](#s31)) |  | <span lang="grc"> **η** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ῑ** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ω** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ῡ** </span> |

##### <a id="s28"></a> §28

Difference in quantity between Attic and Epic words is due chiefly either to (1) metrical lengthening, or to (2) different phonetic treatment, as <span lang="grc">καλϝός, τινϝω</span> become Epic <span lang="grc">κᾱλός</span> _fair_, <span lang="grc">τί̄νω</span> _I pay_ ([37 D.](#s37) 1), Attic <span lang="grc">κᾰλός, τί̆νω</span>.

##### <a id="s28D"></a> §28 D

Metrical lengthening.—Many words, which would otherwise not fit into the verse, show in the Epic <span lang="grc">ει</span> for <span lang="grc">ε, ου</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">οι</span>) for <span lang="grc">ο</span>, and <span lang="grc">ᾱ, ῑ, ῡ</span> for <span lang="grc">α, ι, υ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰνάλιος</span> _in the sea_ for <span lang="grc">ἐνάλιος, εἰαρινός</span> _vernal_ for <span lang="grc">ἐαρινός, ὑπείροχος</span> _eminent_ for <span lang="grc">ὑπέροχος, εἰλήλουθα</span> _have come_ for <span lang="grc">ἐλήλουθα, οὐλόμενος</span> _destructive_, _accursed_ for <span lang="grc">ὀλόμενος, οὔρεα</span> _mountains_ from <span lang="grc">ὄρος, Οὐλύμποιο</span> _of Olympus_ from <span lang="grc">Ὄλυμπος. ο</span> before a vowel appears as <span lang="grc">οι</span> in <span lang="grc">πνοιή</span> _breath_. Similarly, <span lang="grc">ἠγάθεος</span> _very holy_ for <span lang="grc">ἀγάθεος</span>; but <span lang="grc">ἠνεμόεις</span> _windy_ (from <span lang="grc">ἄνεμος</span>) has the <span lang="grc">η</span> of <span lang="grc">ὑπήνεμος</span> _under the wind_ ([29](#s29)), and <span lang="grc">τιθήμενος</span> _placing_ (for <span lang="grc">τιθέμενος</span>) borrows <span lang="grc">η</span> from <span lang="grc">τίθημι</span>.

A short syllable under the rhythmic accent (‘ictus’) is lengthened metrically: (1) in words having three or more short syllables: the first of three shorts (<span lang="grc">οὐλόμενος</span>), the second of four shorts (<span lang="grc">ὑπείροχος</span>), the third of five shorts (<span lang="grc">ἀπερείσια</span> _boundless_); (2) in words in which the short ictus syllable is followed by two longs and a short (<span lang="grc">Οὐλύμποιο</span>). A short syllable not under the rhythmic accent is lengthened when it is preceded and followed by a long; thus, any vowel preceded by <span lang="grc">ϝ</span> (<span lang="grc">πνείω</span> _breathe_ = <span lang="grc">πνεϝω</span>), <span lang="grc">ι</span> or <span lang="grc">υ</span> before a vowel (<span lang="grc">προθῡμί̄ῃσι</span> _zeal_).

##### <a id="s29"></a> §29

The initial short vowel of a word forming the second part of a compound is often lengthened: <span lang="grc">στρατηγός</span> _general_ (<span lang="grc">στρατός</span> _army_ + <span lang="grc">ἄγειν</span> _to lead_ 887 d).

##### <a id="s30"></a> §30

**Attic <span lang="grc">η</span>**, <span lang="grc"> **ᾱ** </span>.—Attic has <span lang="grc">η</span> for original <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> of the earlier period, as <span lang="grc">φήμη</span> _report_ (Lat. _fāma_). Ionic also has <span lang="grc">η</span> for original <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span>. Doric and Aeolic retain original <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> (<span lang="grc">φά̄μᾱ</span>). <!-- page 15 -->

**a.** This is true also of the <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> which is the result of early compensative lengthening, by which <span lang="grc">-ανσ-, -ασλ-, -ασμ-</span>, and <span lang="grc">-ασν-</span> changed to <span lang="grc">-ᾱσ-, -ᾱλ-, -ᾱμ-</span>, and <span lang="grc">-ᾱν-</span>. (See [37 b](#s37).) But in a few cases like <span lang="grc">τά̄ς</span> for <span lang="grc">τάνς</span>, and in <span lang="grc">πᾶσα</span> for <span lang="grc">πάνσα</span> ([113](03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s113)) where the combination <span lang="grc">ανς</span> arose at a later period, <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> was not changed to <span lang="grc">η. ὑφᾶναι</span> for <span lang="grc">ὑφῆναι</span> _to weave_ follows <span lang="grc">τετρᾶναι</span> _to pierce_.

**b.** Original <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> became <span lang="grc">η</span> after <span lang="grc">υ</span>, as <span lang="grc">φυή</span> _growth_. In some words, however, we find <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span>.

##### <a id="s30D1"></a> §30 D. 1

Doric and Aeolic retain original <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span>, as in <span lang="grc">μᾶλον</span> _apple_ (cp. Lat. _mālum_, Att. <span lang="grc">μῆλον</span>), <span lang="grc">κᾶρυξ</span> _herald_ (Att. <span lang="grc">κῆρυξ</span>). But Doric and Aeolic have original <span lang="grc">η</span> when <span lang="grc">η</span> interchanges with <span lang="grc">ε</span>, as in <span lang="grc">τίθημι</span> _I place_, <span lang="grc">τίθεμεν</span> _we place_, <span lang="grc"> **μά̄τηρ μᾱτέρα** </span> _mother_, <span lang="grc">ποιμήν ποιμένι</span> _shepherd_.

\2. Ionic has <span lang="grc">η</span> after <span lang="grc">ε, ι</span>, and <span lang="grc">ρ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">γενεή, σκιή, ἡμέρη</span>.

##### <a id="s31"></a> §31

In Attic alone this <span lang="grc">η</span> was changed back to <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span>:

\1. When preceded by a <span lang="grc">ρ</span>; as <span lang="grc">ἡμέρᾱ</span> _day_, <span lang="grc">χώρᾱ</span> _country_. This appears to have taken place even though an <span lang="grc">ο</span> intervened: as <span lang="grc">ἀκρόᾱμα</span> _a musical piece_, <span lang="grc">ἀθρόᾱ</span> _collected_.

EXCEPTIONS: (a) But <span lang="grc">ρϝη</span> was changed to <span lang="grc">ρη</span>: as <span lang="grc">κόρη</span> for <span lang="grc">κορϝη</span> _maiden_. (b) Likewise <span lang="grc">ρη</span>, when the result of contraction of <span lang="grc">ρεα</span>, remained: as <span lang="grc">ὄρη</span> from <span lang="grc">ὄρεα</span> _mountains_. (c) And <span lang="grc">ρση</span> was changed to <span lang="grc">ρρη</span>: as <span lang="grc">κόρρη</span> for <span lang="grc">κόρση</span> ([79](03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s79)) _one of the temples_.

\2. When preceded by <span lang="grc">ε</span> or <span lang="grc">ι</span>: as <span lang="grc">γενεά̄</span> _generation_, <span lang="grc">σκιά̄</span> _shadow_.

This change takes place even when the <span lang="grc">η</span> is the result of the contraction of <span lang="grc">εα</span>: as <span lang="grc">ὑγιᾶ</span> _healthy_, <span lang="grc">ἐνδεᾶ</span> _lacking_, for <span lang="grc">ὑγιῆ</span> from <span lang="grc">ὑγιε</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">α, ἐνδεῆ</span> from <span lang="grc">ἐνδεε</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">α</span>; also, if originally a <span lang="grc">ϝ</span> intervened, as <span lang="grc">νέᾱ</span> for <span lang="grc">νεϝᾱ</span> _young_ (Lat. _nova_).

EXCEPTIONS: Some exceptions are due to analogy: <span lang="grc">ὑγιῆ</span> _healthy_, <span lang="grc">εὐφυῆ</span> _shapely_ ([292 d](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/07-adjectives.md#s292)) follow <span lang="grc">σαφῆ</span> _clear_.

##### <a id="s32"></a> §32

In the choruses of tragedy Doric <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> is often used for <span lang="grc">η</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">μά̄τηρ</span> _mother_, <span lang="grc">ψῡχά̄</span> _soul_, <span lang="grc">γᾶ</span> _earth_, <span lang="grc">δύστᾱνος</span> _wretched_, <span lang="grc">ἔβᾱν</span> _went_.

##### <a id="s33"></a> §33

The dialects frequently show vowel sounds that do not occur in the corresponding Attic words.

##### <a id="s33D"></a> §33 D

<span lang="grc"> **α** </span> for <span lang="grc"> **ε** </span>: <span lang="grc">ἱαρός</span> _sacred_, <span lang="grc">Ἄρταμις</span> (for <span lang="grc">Ἄρτεμις</span>), <span lang="grc">τράπω</span> _turn_ Dor.; <span lang="grc"> **ε** </span> for <span lang="grc"> **α** </span>: <span lang="grc">θέρσος</span> _courage_ Aeol., <span lang="grc">ἔρσην</span> _male_, <span lang="grc">ὁρέω</span> _see_, <span lang="grc">τέσσερες</span> _four_ ( = <span lang="grc">τέτταρες</span>) Ion.; <span lang="grc"> **α** </span> for <span lang="grc"> **ο** </span>: <span lang="grc">διᾱκατίοι</span> (for <span lang="grc">διᾱκόσιοι</span>) 200 Dor., <span lang="grc">ὐπά</span> _under_ Aeol.; <span lang="grc"> **ο** </span> for <span lang="grc"> **α** </span>: <span lang="grc">στρότος</span> (<span lang="grc">στρατός</span>) _army_, <span lang="grc">ὄν</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀνά</span>) _up_ Aeol., <span lang="grc">τέτορες</span> (<span lang="grc">τέτταρες</span>) _four_ Dor.; <span lang="grc"> **ε** </span> for <span lang="grc"> **η** </span>: <span lang="grc">ἕσσων</span> _inferior_ (<span lang="grc">ἥττων</span>) Ion.; <span lang="grc"> **ε** </span> for <span lang="grc"> **ο** </span>: <span lang="grc">Ἀπέλλων</span> Dor. (also <span lang="grc">Ἀπόλλων</span>); <span lang="grc"> **ε** </span> for <span lang="grc"> **ει** </span>: <span lang="grc">μέζων</span> _greater_ Ion.; <span lang="grc"> **ε** </span> for <span lang="grc"> **ι** </span>: <span lang="grc">κέρνᾱν</span> _mix_ ( = <span lang="grc">κιρνάναι</span> for <span lang="grc">κεραννύναι</span>) Aeol.; <span lang="grc"> **ι** </span> for <span lang="grc"> **ε** </span>: <span lang="grc">ἱστίη</span> _hearth_ Ion., <span lang="grc">ἱστίᾱ</span> Dor. (for <span lang="grc">ἑστίᾱ</span>), <span lang="grc">χρύ̄σιος</span> (<span lang="grc">χρύ̄σεος</span>) _golden_ Aeol., <span lang="grc">θιός</span> _god_ Boeot., <span lang="grc">κοσμίω</span> _arrange_ Dor.; <span lang="grc"> **υ** </span> for <span lang="grc"> **α** </span>: <span lang="grc">πίσυρες</span> _four_ (<span lang="grc">τέτταρες</span>) Hom.; <span lang="grc"> **υ** </span> for <span lang="grc"> **ο** </span>: <span lang="grc">ὄνυμα</span> _name_ Dor., Aeol., <span lang="grc">ἀπύ</span> _from_ Aeol.; <span lang="grc"> **ω** </span> for <span lang="grc"> **ου** </span>: <span lang="grc">ὦν</span> _accordingly_ Ion., Dor.

##### <a id="s34"></a> §34

**Transfer of Quantity**.—<span lang="grc">ηο, ηα</span> often exchange quantities, be coming <span lang="grc">εω, εᾱ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ληός</span> (Epic <span lang="grc">λᾱός</span> _folk_) becomes <span lang="grc">λεώς</span>, as <span lang="grc">πόληος</span> becomes <span lang="grc">πόλεως</span> _of a city;_ <span lang="grc">τεθνηότος τεθνεῶτος</span> _dead;_ <span lang="grc">βασιλῆα βασιλέᾱ</span> _king_.

##### <a id="s34D"></a> §34 D

Often in Ionic: <span lang="grc">Ἀτρεΐδεω</span> from earlier <span lang="grc">Ἀτρεΐδᾱο</span> _son of Atreus_, <span lang="grc">ἱκέτεω</span> from <span lang="grc">ἱκέτᾱο</span> _suppliant_. This <span lang="grc">εω</span> generally makes a single syllable in poetry ([60](02-euphony-of-vowels.md#s60)). The <span lang="grc">ηο</span> intermediate between <span lang="grc">ᾱο</span> and <span lang="grc">εω</span> is rarely found. <!-- page 16 -->

##### <a id="s35"></a> §35

**Qualitative Vowel Gradation**.—In the same root or suffix we find an interchange among different vowels (and diphthongs) similar to the interchange in _sing_, _sang_, _sung_.

**a.** This variation appears in _strong_ grades and in a _weak_ grade (including actual expulsion of a vowel—in diphthongs, of the first vowel). Thus, <span lang="grc">φέρ-ω</span> _I carry_, <span lang="grc">φόρ-ο-ς</span> _tribute_, <span lang="grc">φώρ</span> _thief_, <span lang="grc">φαρ-έ-τρᾱ</span> _quiver_, <span lang="grc">δί-φ ρ-ο-ς</span> _chariot_ (_twocarrier_), <span lang="grc">λείπ-ω</span> _I leave_, <span lang="grc">λέ-λοιπ-α</span> _I have left_, <span lang="grc">λιπ-εῖν</span> _to leave_. The interchange is quantitative in <span lang="grc">φόρ-ο-ς φώρ</span> (cp. [27](#s27)).

**b.** When, by the expulsion of a vowel in the weak grade, an unpronounceable combination of consonants resulted, a vowel sound was developed to render pronunciation possible. Thus, <span lang="grc">ρα</span> or <span lang="grc">αρ</span> was developed from <span lang="grc">ρ</span> between consonants, as in <span lang="grc">πα-τρ**ά**-σι</span> from <span lang="grc">ṛατρ-σι</span> ([262](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/05-third-declension.md#s262)); and <span lang="grc">α</span> from <span lang="grc">ν</span>, as in <span lang="grc">αὐτό-μ**α**-το-ν</span> for <span lang="grc">αὐτο-μṇ-τον</span> _automaton_ (_acting of its own will_), cp. <span lang="grc">μέν-ο-ς</span> _rage_, <span lang="grc">μέ-μον-α</span> _I yearn_. So in <span lang="grc">ὀνομαίνω</span> _name_ for <span lang="grc">ὀνομṇ-\[ιγλιδε\]ω</span>; cp. <span lang="grc">ὄνομα</span>.

**c.** A vowel may also take the place of an original liquid or nasal after a consonant; as <span lang="grc">ἔλῡσα</span> for <span lang="grc">ἐλῡσṃ</span>. This <span lang="grc">ρ, λ, μ, ν</span> in **b** and **c** is called _sonant liquid_ or _sonant nasal_.

## COMPENSATORY LENGTHENING

##### <a id="s37"></a> §37

Compensatory lengthening is the lengthening of a short vowel to make up for the omission of a consonant. <!-- page 17 -->

|  |  |  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The short vowels | <span lang="grc"> **α** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ε** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ι** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ο** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **υ** </span> |
| are lengthened to | <span lang="grc"> **ᾱ** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ει** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ῑ** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ου** </span> | <span lang="grc"> **ῡ** </span> |
| Thus the forms | <span lang="grc">τάν-ς</span> | <span lang="grc">ἐ-μεν-σα</span> | <span lang="grc">ἐκλιν-σα</span> | <span lang="grc">τόνς</span> | <span lang="grc">δεικνυντ-ς</span> |
| become | <span lang="grc">τά̄ς</span> | <span lang="grc">ἔμεινα</span> | <span lang="grc">ἔκλῑνα</span> | <span lang="grc">τούς</span> | <span lang="grc">δεικνύ̄ς</span> |
|  | _the_ | _I remained_ | _I leaned_ | _the_ | _showing_ |

**a.** Thus are formed <span lang="grc">κτείνω</span> _I kill_ for <span lang="grc">κτεν-\[ιγλιδε\]ω, φθείρω</span> _I destroy_ for <span lang="grc">φθερ-\[ιγλιδε\]ω, δότειρα</span> _giver_ for <span lang="grc">δοτερ-\[ιγλιδε\]α, κλί̄νω</span> _I lean_ for <span lang="grc">κλιν-\[ιγλιδε\]ω, ὀλοφύ̄ρω</span> _I lament_ for <span lang="grc">ὀλοφυρ-\[ιγλιδε\]ω</span>.

**b.** <span lang="grc">α</span> becomes <span lang="grc">η</span> in the <span lang="grc">σ-</span>aorist of verbs whose stems end in <span lang="grc">λ, ρ</span>, or <span lang="grc">ν</span>, when not preceded by <span lang="grc">ι</span> or <span lang="grc">ρ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐφαν-σα</span> becomes <span lang="grc">ἔ-φηνα</span> _I showed_, but <span lang="grc">ἐπεραν-σα</span> becomes <span lang="grc">ἐπέρᾱνα</span> _I finished_. So <span lang="grc">σελήϝη</span> _moon_ for <span lang="grc">σελασ-νη</span> (<span lang="grc">σέλας</span> _gleam_).

**c.** The diphthongs <span lang="grc">ει</span> and <span lang="grc">ου</span> due to this lengthening are _spurious_ (6).

##### <a id="s37D"></a> §37 D

\1. Ionic agrees with Attic except where the omitted consonant was <span lang="grc">ϝ</span>, which in Attic disappeared after a consonant without causing lengthening. Thus, <span lang="grc">ξεῖνος</span> for <span lang="grc">ξένος</span> _stranger_, <span lang="grc">εἵνεκα</span> _on account of_ (also in Dem.) for <span lang="grc">ἕνεκα, οὖρος</span> _boundary_ for <span lang="grc">ὅρος, κοῦρος</span> _boy_ for <span lang="grc">κόρος, μοῦνος</span> _alone_ for <span lang="grc">μόνος</span>. These forms are also used generally in poetry.

\2. Doric generally lengthens <span lang="grc">ε</span> and <span lang="grc">ο</span> to <span lang="grc">η</span> and <span lang="grc">ω</span>: <span lang="grc">ξῆνος, ὧρος, κῶρος, μῶνος</span>. So <span lang="grc">μῶσα</span> _muse_ from <span lang="grc">μονσα</span> for <span lang="grc">μοντ\[ιγλιδε\]α, τώς</span> for <span lang="grc">τόνς</span> _the_, <span lang="grc">ἠμί</span> _am_ for <span lang="grc">ἐσμι, χηλίοι</span> 1000 for <span lang="grc">χεσλιοι</span>, Ionic <span lang="grc">χείλιοι</span>. (In some Doric dialects <span lang="grc">ϝ</span> drops as in Attic (<span lang="grc">ξένος, ὅρος</span>); and <span lang="grc">ανς, ονς</span> may become <span lang="grc">ᾰς, ος</span>: <span lang="grc">δεσπότᾰς</span> _lords_, <span lang="grc">τός</span> _the_.)

\3. Aeolic has <span lang="grc">αις, εις</span> (a _genuine_ diphth.), <span lang="grc">οις</span> from <span lang="grc">ανς, ενς, ονς</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">παῖσα</span> _all_ (Cretan <span lang="grc">πάνσα</span>, Att. <span lang="grc">πᾶσα</span>), <span lang="grc">λύ̄οισι</span> _they loose_ from <span lang="grc">λύ̄οντι</span>. Elsewhere Aeol. prefers assimilated forms (<span lang="grc">ἔμεννα, ἔκλιννα, ξέννος, ἔννεκα, ὄρρος, ἔμμι, χέλλιοι</span>). But single <span lang="grc">ν, ρ</span> are also found, as in <span lang="grc">κόρᾱ, μόνος</span>. Aeolic has <span lang="grc">φθέρρω, κλίννω, ὀλοφύρρω</span>; cp. [37 a](#s37).

##### <a id="s38"></a> §38

<span lang="grc"> **ᾱ** </span> arises from <span lang="grc">αι</span> upon the loss of its <span lang="grc">ι</span> ([43](#s43)) in <span lang="grc">ἀ̄εί</span> _always_ (from <span lang="grc">αἰεί</span>), <span lang="grc">ἀ̄ετός</span> _eagle_ (<span lang="grc">αἰετός</span>), <span lang="grc">κλά̄ει</span> _weeps_ (<span lang="grc">κλαίει</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐλά̄ᾱ</span> _olive-tree_ (<span lang="grc">ἐλαίᾱ</span>, cp. Lat. _oliva_).

**a.** This change took place only when <span lang="grc">αι</span> was followed by <span lang="grc">ϝ</span> (<span lang="grc">αἰϝεί, αἰϝετός</span> from <span lang="grc">ἀϝιετος, κλαιϝει</span> from <span lang="grc">κλαϝιει</span>, 111, [128](03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s128)) or <span lang="grc">ι</span> (<span lang="grc">Θηβᾱίς</span> _the Thebaïd_ from <span lang="grc">Θηβαιίς</span>); and only when <span lang="grc">ϝ</span> or <span lang="grc">ι</span> was not followed by <span lang="grc">ο</span>.

## SHORTENING, ADDITION, AND OTHER VOWEL CHANGES

##### <a id="s39"></a> §39

**Shortening**.—A long vowel may be shortened before another long vowel: <span lang="grc">βασιλέων</span> from <span lang="grc">βασιλήων</span> _of kings_, <span lang="grc">νεῶν</span> from <span lang="grc">νηῶν</span> _of ships_, <span lang="grc">τεθνεώς</span> from <span lang="grc">τεθνηώς</span> _dead_.

##### <a id="s39D"></a> §39 D

In the Ionic genitive of Â stems ([214 D.](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/01-first-declension-stems-in-alpha.md#s214) 8) <span lang="grc">-εων</span> is from <span lang="grc">-ηων</span> out of <span lang="grc">-ᾱων</span>. So in Ionic <span lang="grc">βασιλέα</span> from <span lang="grc">βασιλῆα</span> _king_. So even before a short vowel in Hom. <span lang="grc">ἥρω̆ος, ἥρω̆ι</span> _hero_ (cp. [148 D](06-syllables-and-their-quantity.md#s148). 3).

##### <a id="s40"></a> §40

A long vowel before <span lang="grc">ι, υ</span>, a nasal, or a liquid + a following consonant was regularly shortened: <span lang="grc">νᾰῦς</span> from original <span lang="grc">νᾱυς</span> _ship_, <span lang="grc">ἐμίγεν</span> from <span lang="grc">ἐ-μιγη-ντ</span> _were mixed_. The long vowel was often introduced again, as Ion. <span lang="grc">νηῦς</span> _ship_.

##### <a id="s41"></a> §41

**Addition**.—<span lang="grc">α, ε, ο</span> are sometimes prefixed before <span lang="grc">λ, μ, ρ, ϝ</span> (_prothetic vowels_). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀ-λείφω</span> _anoint with oil_, <span lang="grc">λίπος</span> _fat;_ <span lang="grc">ἐ-ρυθρός</span> _red_ (cp. Lat. _ruber_), <span lang="grc">ἐ-είκοσι</span> from <span lang="grc">ἐ-</span>(<span lang="grc">ϝ</span>)<span lang="grc">είκοσι; ὀ-μόργνῡμι</span> _wipe;_ <span lang="grc">ἐ-χθές</span> and <span lang="grc">χθές</span> _yesterday_, <span lang="grc">ἴ-κτις</span> _weasel_ (<span lang="grc">κτιδέη</span> _weasel-skin helmet_) are doubtful cases.

##### <a id="s42"></a> §42

**Development**.—A medial vowel is sometimes developed from <span lang="grc">λ</span> or <span lang="grc">ν</span> between two consonants; thus <span lang="grc">αλ, λα; αρ, ρα; αν</span> ([35 b](#s35)). Also (rarely) in forms like Ion. <span lang="grc">βάραγχος</span> = Att. <span lang="grc">βράγχος</span> _hoarseness_. <!-- page 18 -->

##### <a id="s43"></a> §43

**Disappearance**.—The <span lang="grc">ι</span> and <span lang="grc">υ</span> of diphthongs often disappear before a following vowel. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὑός</span> from <span lang="grc">υἱός</span> _son_, <span lang="grc">βο-ός</span> genitive of <span lang="grc">βοῦ-ς</span> _ox_, _cow_. <span lang="grc">ι</span> and <span lang="grc">υ</span> here became semivowels (<span lang="grc">\[ιγλιδε\], \[υγλιδε\]</span>), which are not written. Cp. [148 D](06-syllables-and-their-quantity.md#s148). 3.

##### <a id="s43D"></a> §43 D

So in Hdt. <span lang="grc">κέεται</span> for <span lang="grc">κείεται</span> _lies_, <span lang="grc">βαθέα</span> for <span lang="grc">βαθεῖα</span> _deep_.

##### <a id="s44"></a> §44

**a.** The disappearance of <span lang="grc">ε</span> before a vowel is often called _hyphaeresis_ (<span lang="grc">ὑφαίρεσις</span> _omission_). Thus Ionic <span lang="grc">νοσσός</span> _chick_ for <span lang="grc">νεοσσός, ὁρτή</span> for <span lang="grc">ἑορτή</span> _festival;_ <span lang="grc">ἀδεῶς</span> _fearlessly_ for <span lang="grc">ἀδεέως</span>. Here <span lang="grc">ε</span> was sounded nearly like _y_ and was not written.

##### <a id="s44aD"></a> §44 a. D

Cp. Hom. <span lang="grc">θεοί</span> A 18 (one syllable). <span lang="grc">ι</span> becomes <span lang="grc">\[ιγλιδε\]</span> in Hom. <span lang="grc">πόλιος</span> (two syllables) <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 21.567"><span lang="grc">Φ</span> 567</cite>. <span lang="grc">ι</span> rarely disappears: <span lang="grc">δῆμον</span> for <span lang="grc">δήμιον</span> _belonging to the people_ M 213.

**b.** The disappearance of a short vowel between consonants is called _syncope_ (<span lang="grc">συγκοπή</span> _cutting up_). Thus <span lang="grc">πί̄πτω</span> _fall_ for <span lang="grc">πι-πετ-ω, πατρός</span> _father_ for <span lang="grc">πατέρος</span>. Syncopated forms show the weak grade of vowel gradation ([35](#s35), [36](#s36)).

##### <a id="s45"></a> §45

**Assimilation**.—A vowel may be assimilated to the vowel standing in the following syllable: <span lang="grc">βιβλίον</span> _book_ from <span lang="grc">βυβλίον</span> (<span lang="grc">βύβλος</span> _papyrus_).

**a.** On assimilation in distracted verbs (<span lang="grc">ὁρόω</span> _see_, etc.), see [643](../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/13-inflection-of-omega-verbs.md#s643) ff., 652.
