# EUPHONY OF VOWELS

## CONTACT OF VOWELS AND HIATUS

##### <a id="s46"></a> §46

Attic more than any other dialect disliked the immediate succession of two vowel sounds in adjoining syllables. To avoid such succession, which often arose in the formation and inflection of words, various means were employed: _contraction_ ([48 f](#s48)f.), when the vowels collided in the middle of a word; or, when the succession occurred between two words (_hiatus_), by _crasis_ ([62 f](#s62)f.), _elision_ ([70 f](#s70)f.), _aphaeresis_ ([76](#s76)), or by affixing a movable consonant at the end of the former word ([134](05-movable-consonants.md#s134)).

##### <a id="s47"></a> §47

Hiatus is usually avoided in prose writers by elision ([70 f](#s70)f.); but in cases where elision is not possible, hiatus is allowed to remain by different writers in different degrees, commonly after short words, such as <span lang="grc">ὦ, εἰ, ἤ, καί, μή</span>, and the forms of the article.

##### <a id="s47D"></a> §47 D

Hiatus is allowed in certain cases.

\1. _In epic poetry:_ a. After <span lang="grc">ι</span> and <span lang="grc">υ</span>: <span lang="grc">ἄξονι ἀμφίς, σύ ἐσσι</span>.

b. After a long final syllable having the rhythmic accent: <span lang="grc">μοι ἐθέλουσα</span> (\[macrdot\]˘˘\[macrdot\]˘).

c. When a long final syllable is shortened before an initial vowel (_weak_ , or _improper_, hiatus): <span lang="grc">ἀκτῇ ἐφ᾽ ὑψηλῇ</span> (\[macrdot\]˘˘\[macrdot\]¯\[macrdot\]).

d. When the concurrent vowels are separated by the caesura; often after the fourth foot: <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἄγ᾽ ἐμῶν ὀχέων ἐπιβήσεο, ι ὄφρα ἴδηαι</span>; very often between the short syllables of the third foot (the feminine caesura): as, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἀκέουσα κάθησο, ι ἐμῷ δ᾽ ἐπιπείθεο μύ̄θῳ</span>; rarely after the first foot: <span lang="grc">αὐτὰρ ὁ ἔγνω</span> A 333.

e. Where <span lang="grc">ϝ</span> has been lost.

\2. _In Attic poetry_ hiatus is allowable, as in 1 c, and after <span lang="grc">τί</span> _what?_ <span lang="grc">εὖ</span> _well_, interjections, <span lang="grc">περί</span> _concerning_, and in <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ</span> (<span lang="grc">μηδὲ</span>) <span lang="grc">εἷς</span> (for <span lang="grc">οὐδείς, μηδείς</span> _no one_).

## CONTRACTION

<!-- page 19 -->

##### <a id="s48"></a> §48

Contraction unites in a single long vowel or diphthong two vowels or a vowel and a diphthong standing next each other in successive syllables in the same word.

**a.** Occasion for contraction is made especially by the concurrence of vowel sounds which were once separated by <span lang="grc">ς, \[υγλιδε\]</span> (<span lang="grc">ϝ</span>), and <span lang="grc">\[ιγλιδε\]</span> ([17](01-the-letters.md#s17), [20](01-the-letters.md#s20) a).

The following are the chief rules governing contraction:

##### <a id="s49"></a> §49

(I) Two vowels which can form a diphthong (5) unite to form that diphthong: <span lang="grc">γένεϊ ῀ γένει, αἰδόϊ ῀ αἰδοῖ, κλήϊθρον ῀ κλῇθρον</span>.

##### <a id="s50"></a> §50

(II) **Like Vowels**.—Like vowels, whether short or long, unite in the common long; <span lang="grc">εε, οο</span> become <span lang="grc">ει, ου</span> (6): <span lang="grc">γέραα ῀ γέρᾱ, φιλέητε ῀ φιλῆτε; ἐφίλεε ῀ ἐφίλει, δηλόομεν ῀ δηλοῦμεν</span>.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ι</span> is rarely contracted with <span lang="grc">ι</span> (<span lang="grc">ὀφι ¨ ιδιον ῀ ὀφί̄διον</span> _small snake_) or <span lang="grc">υ</span> with <span lang="grc">υ</span> (<span lang="grc">ὕ̄ς</span> _son_ in inscriptions, from <span lang="grc">ὑ</span>(<span lang="grc">ι</span>)<span lang="grc">ύς ῀ υἱός</span>, [43](01-the-letters.md#s43)).

##### <a id="s50D"></a> §50 D

<span lang="grc">ι ¨ ι ῀ ῑ</span> occurs chiefly in the Ionic, Doric, and Aeolic dative singular of nouns in <span lang="grc">-ις</span> ([268 D.](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/05-third-declension.md#s268)), as in <span lang="grc">πόλιι ῀ πόλῑ</span>; also in the optative, as in <span lang="grc">φθι-ῑ-το ῀ φθῖτο</span>.

##### <a id="s51"></a> §51

(III) **Unlike Vowels**.—Unlike vowels are assimilated, either the second to the first (_progressive_ assimilation) or the first to the second (_regressive_ assimilation).

**a.** An _o_ sound always prevails over an _a_ or _e_ sound: <span lang="grc"> **ο** </span> or <span lang="grc"> **ω** </span> before or after <span lang="grc"> **α** </span>, and before <span lang="grc"> **η** </span>, forms <span lang="grc">**ω**. **οε**</span> and <span lang="grc"> **εο** </span> form <span lang="grc"> **ου** </span> (a spurious diphthong, 6). Thus, <span lang="grc">τῑμάομεν ῀ τῑμῶμεν, αἰδόα ῀ αἰδῶ, ἥρωα ῀ ἥρω, τῑμάω ῀ τῑμῶ, δηλόητε ῀ δηλῶτε</span>; but <span lang="grc">φιλέομεν ῀ φιλοῦμεν, δηλόετον ῀ δηλοῦτον</span>.

**b.** When <span lang="grc"> **α** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **ε** </span> or <span lang="grc"> **η** </span> come together the vowel sound that precedes prevails, and we have <span lang="grc"> **ᾱ** </span> or <span lang="grc"> **η** </span>: <span lang="grc">ὅραε ῀ ὅρᾱ, τῑμάητε ῀ τί̄μᾶτε, ὄρεα ῀ ὄρη</span>.

**c. <span lang="grc">υ</span>** rarely contracts: <span lang="grc">**υ** ¨ **ι** ῀ ῡ</span> in <span lang="grc">ἰχθύ̄διον</span> from <span lang="grc">ἰχθυίδιον</span> _small fish;_ <span lang="grc">**υ** ¨ **ε**</span> strictly never becomes <span lang="grc">ῡ</span> ([273](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/05-third-declension.md#s273)).

##### <a id="s52"></a> §52

(IV) **Vowels and Diphthongs**.—A vowel disappears before a diphthong beginning with the same sound: <span lang="grc">μνάαι ῀ μναῖ, φιλέει ῀ φιλεῖ, δηλόοι ῀ δηλοῖ</span>.

##### <a id="s53"></a> §53

A vowel before a diphthong not beginning with the same sound generally contracts with the first vowel of the diphthong; the last vowel, if <span lang="grc">ι</span>, is _subscript_ (5): <span lang="grc">τῑμάει ῀ τῑμᾷ, τῑμάοιμεν ῀ τῑμῷμεν, λείπεαι ῀ λείπῃ, μεμνηοίμην ῀ μεμνῴμην</span>.

**a.** But <span lang="grc">**ε** ¨ **οι**</span> becomes <span lang="grc"> **οι** </span>: <span lang="grc">φιλέοι ῀ φιλοῖ;</span> <span lang="grc">**ο** ¨ **ει**, **ο** ¨ **ῃ**</span> become <span lang="grc"> **οι** </span>: <span lang="grc">δηλόει ῀ δηλοῖ, δηλόῃ ῀ δηλοῖ</span>.

##### <a id="s54"></a> §54

Spurious <span lang="grc"> **ει** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **ου** </span> are treated like <span lang="grc">ε</span> and <span lang="grc">ο</span>: <span lang="grc">τῑμάειν ῀ τῑμᾶν, δηλόειν ῀ δηλοῦν, τῑμάουσι ῀ τῑμῶσι</span> (but <span lang="grc">τῑμάει ῀ τῑμᾷ</span> and <span lang="grc">δηλόει ῀ δηλοῑ</span>, since <span lang="grc">ει</span> is here genuine; 6). <!-- page 20 -->

##### <a id="s55"></a> §55

(V) **Three Vowels**.—When three vowels come together, the last two unite first, and the resulting diphthong may be contracted with the first vowel: thus, <span lang="grc">τῑμᾷ</span> is from <span lang="grc">τῑμα-ῃ</span> out of <span lang="grc">τῑμα-ε</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">αι</span>; but <span lang="grc">Περικλέους</span> from <span lang="grc">Περικλέεος</span>.

##### <a id="s55D"></a> §55 D

In Hom. <span lang="grc">δεῖος</span> _of fear_ from <span lang="grc">δέε</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">-ος</span> the _first_ two vowels unite.

##### <a id="s56"></a> §56

Irregularities.—A short vowel preceding <span lang="grc">α</span> or any long vowel or diphthong, in contracts of the first and second declensions, is apparently absorbed ([235](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/03-contracted-substantives.md#s235), [290](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/07-adjectives.md#s290)): <span lang="grc">χρύ̄σεα ῀ χρῡσᾶ</span> (not <span lang="grc">χρῡσῆ</span>), <span lang="grc">ἁπλόα ῀ ἁπλᾶ</span> (not <span lang="grc">ἁπλῶ</span>), by analogy to the <span lang="grc">α</span> which marks the neuter plural, <span lang="grc">χρῡσέαις ῀ χρῡσαῖς</span>. (So <span lang="grc">ἡμέας ῀ ἡμᾶς</span> to show the <span lang="grc">-ας</span> of the accus. pl.) Only in the singular of the first declension does <span lang="grc">εᾱ</span> become <span lang="grc">η</span> (or <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> after a vowel or <span lang="grc">ρ</span>): <span lang="grc">χρῡσέᾱς ῀ χρῡσῆς, ἀργυρέᾳ ῀ ἀργυρᾷ</span>. In the third declension <span lang="grc">εεα</span> becomes <span lang="grc">εᾱ</span> ([265](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/05-third-declension.md#s265)); <span lang="grc">ιεα</span> or <span lang="grc">υεα</span> becomes <span lang="grc">ιᾱ</span> (<span lang="grc">υᾱ</span>) or <span lang="grc">ιη</span> (<span lang="grc">υη</span>). See [292 d](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/07-adjectives.md#s292).

Various special cases will be considered under their appropriate sections.

##### <a id="s57"></a> §57

The contraction of a long vowel with a short vowel sometimes does not occur by reason of analogy. Thus, <span lang="grc">νηΐ</span> (two syllables) follows <span lang="grc">νηός</span>, the older form of <span lang="grc">νεώς</span> ([275](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/05-third-declension.md#s275)). Sometimes the long vowel was shortened ([39](01-the-letters.md#s39)) or transfer of quantity took place ([34](01-the-letters.md#s34)).

##### <a id="s58"></a> §58

Vowels that were once separated by <span lang="grc">ς</span> or <span lang="grc">\[ιγλιδε\]</span> ([20](01-the-letters.md#s20)) are often not contracted in dissyllabic forms, but contracted in polysyllabic forms. Thus, <span lang="grc">θε</span>(<span lang="grc">σ</span>)<span lang="grc">ός</span> _god_, but <span lang="grc">Θουκῡδίδης</span> _Thucydides_ (<span lang="grc">θεός ¨ κῦδος</span> _glory_).

| <span lang="grc">υ ¨ υ</span> | = <span lang="grc">ῡ</span> | <span lang="grc">ὑύς</span> (for <span lang="grc">υἱόσ</span>) | = <span lang="grc">ὕ̄ς</span> |
| <span lang="grc">ω ¨ α</span> | = <span lang="grc">ω</span> | <span lang="grc">ἥρωα</span> | = <span lang="grc">ἥρω</span> |
| <span lang="grc">ω ¨ ι</span> | = <span lang="grc">ῳ</span> | <span lang="grc">ἥρωι</span> | = <span lang="grc">ἥρῳ</span> |
| <span lang="grc">ω ¨ ω</span> | = <span lang="grc">ω</span> | <span lang="grc">δώω</span> (Hom.) | = <span lang="grc">δῶ</span> |

N.—The forms of <span lang="grc">ῥῑγόω</span> _shiver_ contract from the stem <span lang="grc">ῥῑγω-</span> (yielding <span lang="grc">ω</span> or <span lang="grc">ῳ</span>).

##### <a id="s59D"></a> §59 D

Attic contracts more, Ionic less, than the other dialects. The laws of contraction often differ in the different dialects.

\1. Ionic (Old and New) is distinguished by its absence of contraction. Thus, <span lang="grc">πλόος</span> for <span lang="grc">πλοῦς</span> _voyage_, <span lang="grc">τείχεα</span> for <span lang="grc">τείχη</span> _walls_, <span lang="grc">ὀστέα</span> for <span lang="grc">ὀστᾶ</span> _bones_, <span lang="grc">ἀοιδή</span> for <span lang="grc">ᾠδή</span> _song_, <span lang="grc">ἀεργός</span> for <span lang="grc">ἀ̄ργός</span> _idle_. The Mss. of Hdt. generally leave <span lang="grc">εε, εη</span> uncontracted; but this is probably erroneous in most cases. Ionic rarely contracts where Attic does not: <span lang="grc">ὀγδώκοντα</span> for <span lang="grc">ὀγδοήκοντα</span> _eighty_.

\2. <span lang="grc">εο, εω, εου</span> generally remain open in all dialects except Attic. In Ionic <span lang="grc">εω</span> is usually monosyllabic. Ionic (and less often Doric) may contract <span lang="grc">εο, εου</span> to <span lang="grc">ευ</span>: <span lang="grc">σεῦ</span> from <span lang="grc">σέο</span> _of thee_, <span lang="grc">φιλεῦσι</span> from <span lang="grc">φιλέουσι</span> _they love_.

\3. <span lang="grc">αο, ᾱο, αω, ᾱω</span> contract to <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> in Doric and Aeolic. Thus, <span lang="grc">Ἀτρείδᾱ</span> from <span lang="grc">Ἀτρείδᾱο</span>, Dor. <span lang="grc">γελᾶντι</span> _they laugh_ from <span lang="grc">γελάοντι, χωρᾶν</span> from <span lang="grc">χωρά̄ων</span> _of countries_. In Aeolic <span lang="grc">οᾱ ῀ ᾱ</span> in <span lang="grc">βᾱθόεντι</span> (Ion. <span lang="grc">βωθόεντι</span>) = Att. <span lang="grc">βοηθοῦντι</span> _aiding_ (dative).

\4. Doric contracts <span lang="grc">αε</span> to <span lang="grc">η; αη</span> to <span lang="grc">η; αει, αῃ</span> to <span lang="grc">ῃ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">νί̄κη</span> from <span lang="grc">νί̄καε</span> _conquer!_ <span lang="grc">ὁρῇ</span> from <span lang="grc">ὁράει</span> and <span lang="grc">ὁράῃ</span>; but <span lang="grc">ᾱε ῀ ᾱ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἅ̄λιος</span> from <span lang="grc">ἀ̄έλιος</span>, Hom. <span lang="grc">ἠέλιος</span> _sun_).

\5. The Severer (and earlier) Doric contracts <span lang="grc">εε</span> to <span lang="grc">η</span>, and <span lang="grc">οε, οο</span> to <span lang="grc">ω</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">φιλήτω</span> from <span lang="grc">φιλεέτω, δηλῶτε</span> from <span lang="grc">δηλόετε, ἵππω</span> from <span lang="grc">ἵππο-ο</span> ([230 D.](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/02-second-declension-stems-in-omicron.md#s230)); the Milder (and later) Doric and N. W. Greek contract to <span lang="grc">ει</span>, and <span lang="grc">ου</span>. Aeolic agrees with the Severer Doric.

## SYNIZESIS

##### <a id="s60"></a> §60

In poetry two vowels, or a vowel and a diphthong, belonging to successive syllables may unite to form a single syllable in pronunciation, but not in writing. Thus, <span lang="grc">βέλεα</span> _missiles_, <span lang="grc">πόλεως</span> _city_, <span lang="grc">Πηληϊάδεω</span> _son of Peleus_, <span lang="grc">χρῡσέῳ</span> _golden_. This is called _Synizēsis_ (<span lang="grc">συνίζησις</span> _settling together_).

##### <a id="s61"></a> §61

Synizesis may occur between two words when the first ends in a long vowel or diphthong. This is especially the case with <span lang="grc">δή</span> <!-- page 22 --> _now_, <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> _or_, <span lang="grc">ἦ</span> (interrog.), <span lang="grc">μή</span> _not_, <span lang="grc">ἐπεί</span> _since_, <span lang="grc">ἐγώ</span> _I_, <span lang="grc">ὦ</span> _oh;_ as <span lang="grc">ἦ ου᾽</span> O 18.

**a.** The term synizesis is often restricted to cases where the first vowel is long. Where the first vowel is short, <span lang="grc">ε, ι</span> were sounded nearly like _y_; <span lang="grc">υ</span> nearly like <span lang="grc">ω</span>. Cp. [44 a](01-the-letters.md#s44). The single syllable produced by synizesis is almost always long.

## CRASIS

##### <a id="s62"></a> §62

Crasis (<span lang="grc">κρᾶσις</span> _mingling_) is the contraction of a vowel or diphthong at the end of a word with a vowel or diphthong beginning the following word. Over the syllable resulting from contraction is placed a ' called _corōnis_ (<span lang="grc">κορωνίς</span> _hook_), as <span lang="grc">τἄ̄λλα</span> from <span lang="grc">τὰ ἄλλα</span> _the other things, the rest_.

**a.** The coronis is not written when the rough breathing stands on the first word: <span lang="grc">ὁ ἄνθρωπος ῀ ἅ̄νθρωπος</span>.

**b.** Crasis does not occur when the first vowel may be elided. (Some editors write <span lang="grc">τἄλλα</span>, etc.)

##### <a id="s63"></a> §63

Crasis occurs in general only between words that belong together; and the first of the two words united by crasis is usually the less important; as the article, relative pronoun (<span lang="grc">ὅ, ἅ</span>), <span lang="grc">πρό, καί, δή, ὦ</span>. Crasis occurs chiefly in poetry.

**a.** It is rare in Hom., common in the dialogue parts of the drama (especially in comedy), and frequent in the orators.

##### <a id="s64"></a> §64

<span lang="grc">π, τ, κ</span> become <span lang="grc">φ, θ, χ</span> when the next word begins with the rough breathing ([124](03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s124)): <span lang="grc">τῇ ἡμέρᾳ ῀ θἠμέρᾳ</span> _the day_, <span lang="grc">καὶ οἱ</span> _and the_ = <span lang="grc">χοι᾽</span> ([68 c](#s68)).

##### <a id="s65"></a> §65

Iota subscript (5) appears in the syllable resulting from crasis only when the first syllable of the second word contains an <span lang="grc">ι</span>: <span lang="grc">ἐγὼ οἶδα ῀ ἐγᾦδα</span> _I know_ (but <span lang="grc">τῷ ὀργάνῳ ῀ τὠργάνῳ</span> _the instrument_, 68 a).

##### <a id="s66"></a> §66

The rules for crasis are in general the same as those for contraction ([48 f](#s48)f.). Thus, <span lang="grc">τὸ ὄνομα ῀ τοὔνομα</span> _the name_, <span lang="grc">ὁ ἐν ῀ οὑν, ὦ ἄνερ ῀ ὦνερ</span> _oh man_, <span lang="grc">πρὸ ἔχων ῀ προὔχων</span> _excelling_, <span lang="grc">τὸ ἱ̄μάτιον ῀ θοἰμάτιον</span> _the cloak_ ([64](#s64)), <span lang="grc">ἃ ἐγώ ῀ ἁ̄γώ</span>.

But the following exceptions are to be noted ([67](#s67)-[69](#s69)):

##### <a id="s67"></a> §67

A diphthong may lose its final vowel: <span lang="grc">οἱ ἐμοί ῀ οὑμοί, σοι ἐστί ῀ σοὐστί, μου ἐστί ῀ μοὐστί</span>. Cp. [43](01-the-letters.md#s43), [68](#s68).

##### <a id="s68"></a> §68

The final vowel or diphthong of the article, and of <span lang="grc">τοί</span>, is dropped, and an initial <span lang="grc">α</span> of the next word is lengthened unless it is the first vowel of a diphthong. The same rule applies in part to <span lang="grc">καί</span>.

**a. Article**.—<span lang="grc">ὁ ἀνήρ ῀ ἁ̄νήρ, οἱ ἄνδρες ῀ ἅ̄νδρες, αἱ ἀγαθαί ῀ ἁ̄γαθαί, ἡ ἀγήθεια ῀ ἁ̄λήθεια, τοῦ ἀνδρός ῀ τἀ̄νδρός, τῷ ἀνδρί ῀ τἀ̄νδρί, ὁ αὐτός ῀ αὑτός</span> _the same_, <span lang="grc">τοῦ αὐτοῦ ῀ ταὐτοῦ</span> _of the same_.

**b. <span lang="grc">τοί</span>**.—<span lang="grc">τοὶ ἄρα ῀ τἄ̄ρα, μέντοι ἄν ῀ μεντἄ̄ν</span>.

**c. <span lang="grc">καί</span>**.—(1) <span lang="grc">αι</span> is dropped: <span lang="grc">καὶ αὐτός ῀ καὐτός, καὶ οὐ ῀ κου᾽, καὶ ἡ ῀ χη᾽, καὶ οἱ ῀ χοι᾽, καὶ ἱκετεύετε ῀ χἰ̄κετεύετε</span> _and ye beseech_ ([64](#s64)). (2) <span lang="grc">αι</span> is contracted chiefly before <span lang="grc">ε</span> and <span lang="grc">ει</span>: <span lang="grc">καὶ ἐν ῀ κἀ̄ν, καὶ ἐγώ ῀ κἀ̄γώ, καὶ ἐς ῀ κἀ̄ς, καὶ εἶτα ῀ κᾆτα</span> (note however <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ ῀ κει᾽, καὶ εἰς ῀ κεἰς</span>); also before <span lang="grc">ο</span> in <span lang="grc">καὶ ὅτε ῀ χὤτε. καὶ ὅπως ῀ χὤπως</span> ([64](#s64)). <!-- page 23 -->

N.—The exceptions in 68 a-c to the laws of contraction are due to the desire to let the vowel of the more important word prevail: <span lang="grc">ἅ̄νηρ</span>, not <span lang="grc">ὡνηρ</span>, because of <span lang="grc">ἀνήρ</span>.

##### <a id="s68D"></a> §68 D

Hom. has <span lang="grc">ὤριστος ῀ ὁ ἄριστος, ωὐτός ῀ ὁ αὐτός</span>. Hdt. has <span lang="grc">οὕτερος ῀ ὁ ἕτερος, ὡνήρ ῀ ὁ ἀνήρ, ὡυτοί ῀ οἱ αὐτοί, τὠυτό ῀ τὸ αὐτό, τὠυτοῦ ῀ τοῦ αὐτοῦ, ἑωυτοῦ ῀ ἕο αὐτοῦ, ὧνδρες ῀ οἱ ἄνδρες</span>. Doric has <span lang="grc">κἠπί ῀ καὶ ἐπί</span>.

##### <a id="s69"></a> §69

Most crasis forms of <span lang="grc">ἕτερος</span> _other_ are derived from <span lang="grc">ἅτερος</span>, the earlier form: thus, <span lang="grc">ὁ ἕτερος ῀ ἅ̄τερος, οἱ ἕτεροι ῀ ἅ̄τεροι</span>; but <span lang="grc">τοῦ ἑτέρου ῀ θοὐτέρου</span> ([64](#s64)).

## ELISION

##### <a id="s70"></a> §70

Elision is the expulsion of a short vowel at the end of a word before a word beginning with a vowel. An apostrophe (') marks the place where the vowel is elided.

<span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽</span> (<span lang="grc">ὰ</span>) <span lang="grc">ἄγε, ἔδωκ᾽</span> (<span lang="grc">α</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐννέα, ἐφ᾽</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ</span>) <span lang="grc">ἑαυτοῦ</span> ([64](#s64)), <span lang="grc">ἔχοιμ᾽</span> (<span lang="grc">ι</span>) <span lang="grc">ἄν, γένοιτ᾽</span> (<span lang="grc">ο</span>) <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>.

**a.** Elision is often not expressed to the eye except in poetry. Both inscriptions and the Mss. of prose writers are very inconsistent, but even where the elision is not expressed, it seems to have occurred in speaking; i.e. <span lang="grc">ὅδε εἶπε</span> and <span lang="grc">ὅδ᾽ εἶπε</span> were spoken alike. The Mss. are of little value in such cases.

##### <a id="s71"></a> §71

Elision affects only unimportant words or syllables, such as particles, adverbs, prepositions, and conjunctions of two syllables (except <span lang="grc">περί, ἄχρι, μέχρι, ὅτι</span> 72 b, c), and the final syllables of nouns, pronouns, and verbs.

**a.** The final vowel of an emphatic personal pronoun is rarely elided.

##### <a id="s72"></a> §72

Elision does not occur in

**a.** Monosyllables, except such as end in <span lang="grc">ε</span> (<span lang="grc">τέ, δέ, γέ</span>).

**b.** The conjunction <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> _that_? (<span lang="grc">ὅτ᾽</span> is <span lang="grc">ὅτε</span> _when_).

**c.** The prepositions <span lang="grc">πρό</span> _before_, <span lang="grc">ἄχρι, μέχρι</span> _until_, and <span lang="grc">περί</span> _concerning_ (except before <span lang="grc">ι</span>).

**d.** The dative singular ending <span lang="grc">ι</span> of the third declension, and in <span lang="grc">σι</span>, the ending of the dative plural.

**e.** Words with final <span lang="grc">υ</span>.

##### <a id="s72D"></a> §72 D

Absence of elision in Homer often proves the loss of <span lang="grc">ϝ</span> (3), as in <span lang="grc">κατὰ ἄστυ</span> X 1. Epic admits elision in <span lang="grc">σά</span> _thy_, <span lang="grc">ῥά</span>, in the dat. sing. of the third decl., in <span lang="grc">-σι</span> and <span lang="grc">-αι</span> in the personal endings, and in <span lang="grc">-ναι, -σθαι</span> of the infinitive, and (rarely) in <span lang="grc">μοί, σοί, τοί. ἄνα</span> _oh king_, and <span lang="grc">ἄνα ῀ ἀνάστηθι</span> _rise up_, elide only once, <span lang="grc">ἰδέ</span> _and_ never. Hdt. elides less often than Attic prose; but the Mss. are not a sure guide. <span lang="grc">περί</span> sometimes appears as <span lang="grc">πέρ</span> in Doric and Aeolic before words beginning with other vowels than <span lang="grc">ι. ὀξεἶ ὀδύναι Λ</span> 272. Cp. [148 D](06-syllables-and-their-quantity.md#s148). 1.

##### <a id="s73"></a> §73

Except <span lang="grc">ἐστί</span> _is_, forms admitting movable <span lang="grc">ν</span> ([134 a](05-movable-consonants.md#s134)) do not suffer elision in prose. (But some cases of <span lang="grc">ε</span> in the perfect occur in Demosthenes.)

##### <a id="s73D"></a> §73 D

In poetry a vowel capable of taking movable <span lang="grc">ν</span> is often cut off.

##### <a id="s74"></a> §74

<span lang="grc">αι</span> in the personal endings and the infinitive is elided in Aristophanes; scarcely ever, if at all, in tragedy; its elision in prose is doubtful. <span lang="grc">οι</span> is elided in tragedy in <span lang="grc">οἴμοι</span> _alas_. <!-- page 24 -->

##### <a id="s75"></a> §75

Interior elision takes place in forming compound words. Here the apostrophe is not used. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐδείς</span> _no one_ from <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ εἷς, καθοράω</span> _look down upon_ from <span lang="grc">κατὰ ὁράω, μεθί̄ημι</span> _let go_ from <span lang="grc">μετὰ ἵ̄ημι</span> ([124](03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s124)).

**a.** <span lang="grc">ὁδί̄, τουτί̄</span> _this_ are derived from the demonstrative pronouns <span lang="grc">ὅδε, τοῦτο</span> + the deictic ending <span lang="grc">ῑ</span> ([333 g](../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s333)).

**b.** Interior elision does not always occur in the formation of compounds. Thus, <span lang="grc">σκηπτοῦχος</span> _sceptre-bearing_ from <span lang="grc">σκηπτο ¨ οχος</span> (i.e. <span lang="grc">σοχος</span>). Cp. [878](../part-3-formation-of-words/06-compound-words.md#s878).

**c.** On the accent in elision, see [174](07-accent-general-principles.md#s174).

##### <a id="s75D"></a> §75 D

**Apocope** (<span lang="grc">ἀποκοπή</span> _cutting off_) occurs when a final short vowel is cut off before an initial consonant. In literature apocope is confined to poetry, but in the prose inscriptions of the dialects it is frequent. Thus, in Hom., as separate words and in compounds, <span lang="grc">ἄν, κάτ, πάρ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀπ, ὑπ</span> rarely) for <span lang="grc">ἀνά, κατά, παρά</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀπό, ὑπό</span>). Final <span lang="grc">τ</span> is assimilated to a following consonant (but <span lang="grc">κατθανεῖν</span> _to die_, not <span lang="grc">καθθανεῖν</span>, cp. [83](03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s83) a); so final <span lang="grc">ν</span> by 91-95. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀλλέξαι</span> _to pick up_, <span lang="grc">ἂμ πόνον</span> _into the strife;_ <span lang="grc">κάββαλε</span> _threw down_, <span lang="grc">κάλλιπε</span> _left behind_, <span lang="grc">κακκείοντες</span> lit. _lying down_, <span lang="grc">καυάξαις</span> _break in pieces_, for <span lang="grc">καϝϝάξαις ῀ κατ-ϝάξαις, κὰδ δέ, καδδῦσαι</span> _entering into_, <span lang="grc">κὰπ πεδίον</span> _through the plain_, <span lang="grc">κὰγ γόνυ</span> _on the knee_ (_kag_ not _kang_), <span lang="grc">κὰρ ῥόον</span> _in the stream;_ <span lang="grc">ὑββάλλειν</span> _interrupt_, <span lang="grc">ἀππέμψει</span> _will send away_. When three consonants collide, the final consonant of the apocopate word is usually lost, as <span lang="grc">κάκτανε</span> _slew_, from <span lang="grc">κάκκτανε</span> out of <span lang="grc">κατ</span>(<span lang="grc">έ</span>)<span lang="grc">κτανε</span>. Apocope occurs rarely in Attic poetry. <span lang="grc">πότ</span> for <span lang="grc">ποτί</span> (= <span lang="grc">πρός</span> in meaning) is frequent in Doric and Boeotian.

**N.**—The shorter forms may have originated from elision.

## APHAERESIS (INVERSE ELISION)

##### <a id="s76"></a> §76

Aphaeresis (<span lang="grc">ἀφᾳίρεσις</span> _taking away_) is the elision of <span lang="grc">ε</span> at the beginning of a word after a word ending in a long vowel or diphthong. This occurs only in poetry, and chiefly after <span lang="grc">μή</span> _not_, <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> _or_. Thus, <span lang="grc">μὴ ᾿ νταῦθα, ἢ ᾿ μέ, παρέξω ᾿ μαυτόν, αὐτὴ ᾿ ξῆλθεν</span>. In some texts editors prefer to adopt crasis ([62](#s62)) or synizesis ([60](#s60)). <span lang="grc">α</span> is rarely elided thus.
