# FORMATION OF SUBSTANTIVES

##### <a id="s838"></a> §838

Some suffixes have a special significance; of these the most important are given in 839-856. But suffixes commonly used with a special function (such as to denote _agency_, _action_, _instrument_, etc.) are not restricted to this function. Only a few have one function, as <span lang="grc">τερο</span> to denote comparison.

**a.** The instrument may be viewed as the agent, as in <span lang="grc">ῥαι-σ-τήρ</span> _hammer_, lit. _smasher_, from <span lang="grc">ῥαί-ω</span> _smash_. <span lang="grc">τρο</span> ([863](04-list-of-noun-suffixes.md#s863). [16](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s16)) may express the agent, instrument, or place. Suffixes used to denote actions or abstract ideas often make concrete words, as <span lang="grc">τροφ-ή</span> _nurture_ and _nourishment_, <span lang="grc">ἀγγελ-ίᾱ</span> _message_ (cp. Eng. _dwelling_, _clothing_). <span lang="grc">πορθμεῖον</span> means _ferry_, _ferry-boat_, _ferryman's fee_. Words originally denoting an agent have lost that meaning, as <span lang="grc">πα-τήρ</span> _father_ (orig. _protector_), and in many cases the original force is changed.

**AGENCY**

a. The primary suffixes <span lang="grc">τᾱ, τηρ, τορ, τρο, ευ</span>, denoting the _agent_ or <!-- page 230 -->

\1. <span lang="grc"> **τᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τή-ς</span>): <span lang="grc">κρι-τή-ς</span> _judge_ (<span lang="grc">κρί̄νω</span> _decide_, <span lang="grc">κρι-</span>), <span lang="grc">κλέπ-τη-ς</span> _thief_ (<span lang="grc">κλέπ-</span> _steal_), <span lang="grc">ποιη-τή-ς</span> _poet_, i.e. _maker_ (<span lang="grc">ποιέ-ω</span> _make_), <span lang="grc">αὐλη-τή-ς</span> _flute-pl&lt;\*&gt;_ (<span lang="grc">αὐλέ-ω</span> _play the flute_), <span lang="grc">μαθ-η-τή-ς</span> _pupil_ (<span lang="grc">μανθάνω</span> _learn_, <span lang="grc">μαθ-ε-</span>), <span lang="grc">ἱκ-έ-</span> _suppliant_ (<span lang="grc">ἱκ-νέ-ομαι</span> _come_, <span lang="grc">ἱκ-</span>).

\2. <span lang="grc"> **τηρ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τήρ</span>): <span lang="grc">δο-τήρ</span> _giver_ (<span lang="grc">δί-δω-μι</span> _give_, <span lang="grc">δο-, δω-</span>), <span lang="grc">σω-τήρ</span> _saviour_ (<span lang="grc">σῳ</span> _save_).

\3. <span lang="grc"> **τορ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τωρ</span>): <span lang="grc">ῥή-τωρ</span> _orator_ (<span lang="grc">ἐρέω</span> _shall say_, <span lang="grc">ἐρ-, ῥε-</span>), <span lang="grc">εἴ-ρη-κα</span> _h&lt;\*&gt; spoken_, <span lang="grc">κτίσ-τωρ</span> _founder_ (<span lang="grc">κτίζω</span> _found_, <span lang="grc">κτιδ-</span>), <span lang="grc">σημάντωρ</span> _comman&lt;\*&gt;_ poet. (<span lang="grc">σημαίνω</span> _give a signal_, <span lang="grc">σημαν-</span>).

\4. <span lang="grc"> **τρο** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τρό-ς</span>): <span lang="grc">ἰ̄ᾱ-τρό-ς</span> _physician_ (<span lang="grc">ἰ̄ά̄-ομαι</span> _heal_).

\5. <span lang="grc"> **ευ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-εύ-ς</span>): <span lang="grc">γραφ-εύ-ς</span> _writer_ (<span lang="grc">γράφ-ω</span> _write_), <span lang="grc">τοκ-εύ-ς</span> _father_ (<span lang="grc">τί&lt;\*&gt;</span> _beget_, <span lang="grc">τεκ-</span>).

**b.** The primary suffixes <span lang="grc">τριδ, τριᾱ, τειρᾱ, τιδ</span> are feminine.

\1. <span lang="grc"> **τριδ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τρίς</span>): <span lang="grc">αὐλη-τρίς</span> _female flute-player_.

\2. <span lang="grc"> **τριᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τρια</span>): <span lang="grc">ποιή-τρια</span> _poetess_ (late), <span lang="grc">ψάλ-τρια</span> _female harper_ (<span lang="grc">ψάλ&lt;\*&gt;</span> _play the harp_, <span lang="grc">ψαλ-</span>).

\3. <span lang="grc"> **τειρᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τειρα</span> from <span lang="grc">τερ-ῖα</span>): <span lang="grc">σώ-τειρα</span> fem. of <span lang="grc">σω-τήρ, δό-τειρα</span> fem. <span lang="grc">δο-τήρ</span>.

\4. <span lang="grc"> **τιδ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τις</span>): <span lang="grc">ἱκ-ε-τις</span> _female suppliant_ fem. of <span lang="grc">ἱκ-έ-της</span>.

**c.** The same root or verb-stem may have different suffixes denoting the agen&lt;\*&gt; <span lang="grc">γεν-έ-της, γεν-ε-τήρ, γεν-έ-τωρ</span> _begetter;_ <span lang="grc">μαθ-η-τρίς</span>, or <span lang="grc">μαθ-ή-τρια</span> _female pup_ fem. of <span lang="grc">μαθ-η-τής</span>.

**d.** Words in <span lang="grc">-τηρ, -τρις, -ευς</span> are oxytone. Words in <span lang="grc">-τωρ, -τειρα, -τρια</span> ha&lt;\*&gt; recessive accent. Words in <span lang="grc">-της</span> are oxytone or paroxytone.

**e.** See also <span lang="grc">ον</span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ων</span>) 861. 18.

##### <a id="s840"></a> §840

**NAMES OF ACTIONS AND ABSTRACT SUBSTANTIVES**

**a.** Substantives denoting actions often express abstract idea and names of actions and verbal abstracts are often used co&lt;\*&gt; cretely. The following suffixes (except <span lang="grc">μο</span>, nominative <span lang="grc">-μό-ς</span>, an <span lang="grc">ες</span>, nominative <span lang="grc">-ος</span>) form feminines; all are primary except <span lang="grc">ιᾱ</span> i&lt;\*&gt; some words.

\1. <span lang="grc"> **τι** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τι-ς</span>): <span lang="grc">πίσ-τι-ς</span> _faith_ (<span lang="grc">πείθ-ω</span> _persuade_, <span lang="grc">πιθ-</span>), <span lang="grc">φά-τι-ς</span> _rumour_ (<span lang="grc">φη&lt;\*&gt;</span> _say_, <span lang="grc">φα-</span>).

\2. <span lang="grc"> **σι** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-σι-ς</span>): <span lang="grc">λέξις</span> _style_ (<span lang="grc">λέγ-ω</span> _speak_), <span lang="grc">ποίη-σι-ς</span> _poetry_ (<span lang="grc">ποιέ-ω</span> _make_) <span lang="grc">φθί-σι-ς</span> _decay_ (<span lang="grc">φθί-ν-ω</span> _decay_), <span lang="grc">δό-σι-ς</span> _act of giving_ or _gift_ (<span lang="grc">δί-δω-μι</span> _giv&lt;\*&gt;_ <span lang="grc">δο-, δω-</span>), <span lang="grc">θέ-σι-ς</span> _placing_ (<span lang="grc">τί-θη-μι</span> _place_, <span lang="grc">θε-, θη-</span>), <span lang="grc">τά-σι-ς</span> _tension_ (fo&lt;\*&gt; <span lang="grc">τṇ-σι-ς</span> 35 b, from <span lang="grc">τείνω</span> _stretch_, <span lang="grc">τεν-</span>). <span lang="grc">σι</span> is derived from <span lang="grc">τι</span> after vowel ([115](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s115)).

\3. <span lang="grc"> **σιᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-σίᾱ</span>): in substantives from verbs in <span lang="grc">-αζω</span> out of <span lang="grc">-αδ-ῖω</span>; a&lt;\*&gt; <span lang="grc">δοκιμασίᾱ</span> _examination_ (<span lang="grc">δοκιμάζω</span> _examine_, <span lang="grc">δοκ μαδ-</span>).

\4. <span lang="grc"> **τυ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τύ̄-ς</span> 863 a. [17](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s17)): rare, poetic and dialectic, <span lang="grc">ἐδ-η-τύ̄ς</span> _eating_ (poet&lt;\*&gt; <span lang="grc">ἔδ-ω</span> _eat_), <span lang="grc">βοη-τύ̄-ς</span> _shouting_ (<span lang="grc">βοά-ω</span> _shout_).

\5. <span lang="grc"> **μο** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-μό-ς</span>, masc.): <span lang="grc">διωγ-μό-ς</span> _pursuit_ (<span lang="grc">διώκ-ω</span> _pursue_), <span lang="grc">πταρ-μό-ς</span> _sneez&lt;\*&gt; ing_ (<span lang="grc">πτάρ-νυ-μαι</span> _sneeze_). On <span lang="grc">θ-μο</span> see [832](01-word-formation-introductory.md#s832), <span lang="grc">σ-μο</span> 836, <span lang="grc">τ-μο</span> 837. Cp 861. 1. <!-- page 231 -->

\6. <span lang="grc"> **μᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-μη</span>): <span lang="grc">γνώ-μη</span> _knowledge_ (<span lang="grc">γι-γνώ-σκω</span> _know_), <span lang="grc">φή-μη</span> _report_, _omen_ (<span lang="grc">φη-μί</span> _say_), <span lang="grc">τῑ-μή</span> _honour_ (poet. <span lang="grc">τί̄-ω</span> _honour_), <span lang="grc">μνή-μη</span> _memory_ (<span lang="grc">μι-μνῄ-σκω</span> _remind_). See also [861](04-list-of-noun-suffixes.md#s861). 1.

\7. <span lang="grc"> **μᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-μᾰ</span>): <span lang="grc">τόλ-μα</span> _daring_ (<span lang="grc">τλῆ-ναι</span> _dare_).

\8. <span lang="grc"> **ες** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ος</span>, neut.): <span lang="grc">δέ-ος</span> _fear_, <span lang="grc">ῥῖγ-ος</span> _cold_.

\9. <span lang="grc"> **ιᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ίᾱ</span>): primitive, from verb-stems, as <span lang="grc">μαν-ίᾱ</span> _madness_ (<span lang="grc">μαίνομαι</span> _rage_, <span lang="grc">μαν-</span>). Denominative: <span lang="grc">ἡγεμον-ίᾱ</span> _sovereignty_ (<span lang="grc">ἡγεμών</span> _leader_), <span lang="grc">εὐεργεσία</span> _kind service_ (<span lang="grc">εὐεργετ-ια</span> from <span lang="grc">εὐεργέτης</span> _doer of good deeds_). Without any noun-stem: <span lang="grc">πολιορκίᾱ</span> _siege_ (<span lang="grc">πολιορκέω</span> _besiege_). Verbs in <span lang="grc">-ευω</span> derived from substantives, as <span lang="grc">παιδεύ-ω</span> _educate_ (<span lang="grc">παῖς</span> _child_), show abstracts in <span lang="grc">-είᾱ</span> for <span lang="grc">ε</span>(<span lang="grc">ῦ</span>)<span lang="grc">-ιᾱ</span> ([43](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s43)): <span lang="grc">παιδείᾱ</span> _education_, <span lang="grc">στρατείᾱ</span> _campaign_ (<span lang="grc">στρατεύομαι</span> _take the field_), <span lang="grc">βασιλείᾱ</span> _reign_, _kingdom_ (<span lang="grc">βασιλεύ-ω</span> _am king_).

\10. <span lang="grc">**ο**, **α**</span>: see [859](04-list-of-noun-suffixes.md#s859). 1, 2.

**b.** Many feminine substantives expressing the abstract notion of the adjective are derived from adjective stems (a few from substantive or verb stems). Many of these denominatives express _quality_, cp. Eng. _-ness_, _-hood_.

\1. <span lang="grc"> **ιᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ιᾰ</span>): from adjectives in <span lang="grc">-ης</span> and <span lang="grc">-οος, -ους</span>, as <span lang="grc">ἀλήθεια</span> _truth_ for <span lang="grc">ἀληθεσ-ια</span> from <span lang="grc">ἀληθής</span> _true;_ <span lang="grc">ἔνδεια</span> _want_ for <span lang="grc">ἐνδε</span>(<span lang="grc">ε</span>)<span lang="grc">σ-ια</span> from <span lang="grc">ἐνδεής</span> _needy_, 44 a, 292 d; <span lang="grc">εὔνοια</span> _kindness_ for <span lang="grc">εὐνο</span>(<span lang="grc">ο</span>)<span lang="grc">-ια</span> from <span lang="grc">εὐνοο-ς εὔνους</span> _kind_.

\2. <span lang="grc"> **ιᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ίᾱ</span>): <span lang="grc">εὐδαιμον-ίᾱ</span> _happiness_ (<span lang="grc">εὐδαίμων</span> _happy_), <span lang="grc">συμμαχίᾱ</span> _alliance_ (<span lang="grc">σύμμαχος</span> _fighting along with_), <span lang="grc">σοφ-ίᾱ</span> _wisdom_ (<span lang="grc">σοφό-ς</span> _wise_). Since <span lang="grc">τ</span> becomes <span lang="grc">ς</span> before <span lang="grc">ιᾱ</span> we have <span lang="grc">ἀθανασίᾱ</span> _immortality_ (<span lang="grc">ἀθάνατο-ς</span> _immortal_). Cp. [859](04-list-of-noun-suffixes.md#s859). 6.

\3. <span lang="grc"> **συνᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-σύνη</span>): <span lang="grc">δικαιο-σύνη</span> _justice_ (<span lang="grc">δίκαιο-ς</span> _just_). Abstracts in <span lang="grc">-συνη</span> are properly fem. of adj. in <span lang="grc">-συνος</span>, as <span lang="grc">γηθο-σύνη</span> _joy_ (<span lang="grc">γηθό-συνος</span> _joyful_). <span lang="grc">-οσύνη</span> by analogy in <span lang="grc">μαντ-οσύνη</span> _art of divination_ (<span lang="grc">μάντι-ς</span> _seer_). See [865](04-list-of-noun-suffixes.md#s865). 7.

\4. <span lang="grc"> **τητ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-της</span>): <span lang="grc">φιλό-της, -τητος</span> _friendship_ (<span lang="grc">φίλο-ς</span> _friend_), <span lang="grc">ἰσό-της, -τητος</span> _equality_ (<span lang="grc">ἴσο-ς</span> _equal_), <span lang="grc">νεό-της</span> _youth_ (<span lang="grc">νέο-ς</span> _young_), <span lang="grc">παχύ-της</span> _thickness_ (<span lang="grc">παχύ-ς</span> _thick_).

\5. <span lang="grc"> **αδ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-άς</span>): abstract substantives of number, as <span lang="grc">τρι-άς, -άδος</span> _triad_ (<span lang="grc">τρεῖς</span>). <span lang="grc">μον-άς, -άδος</span> _unit_ (<span lang="grc">μόνο-ς</span> _alone_, _single_). See also [863 b](04-list-of-noun-suffixes.md#s863). 8.

**c.** Some neuter abstracts express quality: <span lang="grc">τάχ-ος</span> _speed_ (<span lang="grc">ταχ-ύ-ς</span> _swift_), <span lang="grc">εὖρ-ος</span> _width_ (<span lang="grc">εὐρ-ύ-ς</span> _broad_). See [840 a](#s840). 8.

**d.** A feminine adjective is used substantively in poet. <span lang="grc">πινυτή</span> _wisdom_ from <span lang="grc">πινυτό-ς</span> _wise;_ with recessive accent in <span lang="grc">ἔχθρᾱ</span> _enmity_ from <span lang="grc">ἐχθρό-ς</span> _hostile_, <span lang="grc">θέρμη</span> _warmth_ from <span lang="grc">θερμό-ς</span> _warm_.

**e.** Some compound adjectives in <span lang="grc">-ής</span> yield (by analogy) abstracts in <span lang="grc">-ιᾱ</span> not in <span lang="grc">-ειᾰ</span>; as <span lang="grc">ἀτυχίᾱ</span> _misfortune_ from <span lang="grc">ἀ-τυχ-ής</span> _unfortunate_. Fluctuation often occurs, as in <span lang="grc">κακοήθεια κακοηθίᾱ</span> _malignity_ from <span lang="grc">κακο-ήθης</span> _ill-disposed;_ Old Attic <span lang="grc">ἀληθείᾱ</span> ( = Ion. <span lang="grc">ἀληθείη</span>) for <span lang="grc">ἀλήθεια</span>.

**RESULT OF ACTION**

\1. <span lang="grc"> **ες** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ος</span>, neut.): <span lang="grc">γέν-ος</span> _race_, _family_, stem <span lang="grc">γεν-εσ-</span> (<span lang="grc">γί-γν-ομαι</span> _am born_, <span lang="grc">ἐ-γεν-όμην, γεν-</span>), <span lang="grc">τέκ-ος</span> _child_, stem <span lang="grc">τεκ-εσ-</span> (<span lang="grc">τίκτω</span> _bring forth_, <span lang="grc">τεκ-</span>), <span lang="grc">ψεῦδ-ος</span> _lie_, stem <span lang="grc">ψευδ-εσ-</span> (<span lang="grc">ψεύδ-ω</span> _deceive_).

##### <a id="s842"></a> §842

**INSTRUMENT OR MEANS OF ACTION**

\1. <span lang="grc"> **τρο** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τρο-ν</span>, neut.): <span lang="grc">ἄρο-τρο-ν</span> _plough_ (<span lang="grc">ἀρό-ω</span> _plough_), <span lang="grc">λύ-τρο-ν</span> _ransom_ (<span lang="grc">λύ̄-ω</span> _release_, <span lang="grc">λῠ̄-</span>), <span lang="grc">σεῖ-σ-τρο-ν</span> _rattle_ (<span lang="grc">σεί-ω</span> _shake_, 624 a), <span lang="grc">δί-δακ-τρο-ν</span> _teacher's pay_ (<span lang="grc">διδάσκω</span> _teach_, <span lang="grc">διδαχ-</span>), <span lang="grc">λου-τρό-ν</span> _bath_ (bathing-water; <span lang="grc">λού-ω</span> _wash_).

\5. <span lang="grc"> **ειο** </span> (rare; nom. <span lang="grc">-εῖον</span>, neut.): <span lang="grc">τροφεῖα</span> _pay for rearing_. See [863 a](04-list-of-noun-suffixes.md#s863). 8.

\6. <span lang="grc"> **ρο** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ρό-ν</span>, neut.): <span lang="grc">πτ-ε-ρό-ν</span> _wing_ (<span lang="grc">πέτ-ομαι</span> _fly_).

##### <a id="s843"></a> §843

**THE PERSON CONCERNED**

**a.** The person concerned or occupied with anything is denoted by a denominative formed by one of the following secondary suffixes:

\1. <span lang="grc"> **ευ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-εύ-ς</span>, masc.): <span lang="grc">γραμ-ματ-εύ-ς</span> _secretary_ (<span lang="grc">γράμμα, -ατος</span> _anything written_), <span lang="grc">ἱερ-εύ-ς</span> _priest_ (<span lang="grc">ἱερό-ς</span> _sacred_), <span lang="grc">ἱππ-εύ-ς</span> _horseman_ (<span lang="grc">ἵππο-ς</span> _horse_), <span lang="grc">χαλκεύ-ς</span> _coppersmith_ (<span lang="grc">χαλκό-ς</span> _copper_).

\2. <span lang="grc"> **τᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τη-ς</span>, masc.): <span lang="grc">ναύ-τη-ς</span> _sailor_ (<span lang="grc">ναῦ-ς</span> _ship_), <span lang="grc">τοξό-της</span> _bowman_ (<span lang="grc">τόξο-ν</span> _bow_), <span lang="grc">οἰκέ-της</span> _house-servant_ (<span lang="grc">οἶκο-ς</span> _house_, 834 f), <span lang="grc">δεσμώ-τη-ς</span> _prisoner_ ([834 h](01-word-formation-introductory.md#s834)).

N.—By analogy are formed: <span lang="grc">εὐν-έτη-ς</span> _bed-fellow_ (<span lang="grc">εὐνή</span> _bed_), following <span lang="grc">οἰκέτη-ς; ὁπλ-ί̄τη-ς</span> _heavy-armed soldier_ (<span lang="grc">ὅπλο-ν, ὅπλα</span> _armour_) following <span lang="grc">πολί̄-τη-ς</span> from older <span lang="grc">πόλῑ-ς; στρατι-ώτη-ς</span> _soldier_ (<span lang="grc">στρατιά̄</span> _army_) following <span lang="grc">δεσμώ-τη-ς</span>. See [834 g](01-word-formation-introductory.md#s834).

**b.** The following secondary suffixes form feminine substantives:

\1. <span lang="grc"> **ιᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ιᾰ</span>): corresponding to masculines in <span lang="grc">-εύ-ς</span>, as <span lang="grc">ἱέρεια</span> _priestess_ for <span lang="grc">ἱερ-εῦ-ια</span> (<span lang="grc">ἱερ-εύ-ς</span> _priest_), <span lang="grc">βασίλεια</span> _queen_ (<span lang="grc">βασιλ-εύ-ς</span> _king_). See <span lang="grc">-αινα</span> below.

\2. <span lang="grc"> **ιδ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ίς</span>): <span lang="grc">φαρμακ-ίς</span> _sorceress_ (<span lang="grc">φάρμακο-ν</span> _charm_, _poison_, <span lang="grc">φαρμακ-εύ-ς</span> _sorcerer_), <span lang="grc">καπηλ-ίς</span> _female huckster_ (<span lang="grc">καπηλό-ς</span> _huckster_), <span lang="grc">φυλακ-ίς</span> _female guard_ (<span lang="grc">φύλαξ</span>).

\3. <span lang="grc"> **τιδ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τις</span>): corresponding to masculines in <span lang="grc">-τη-ς</span>: <span lang="grc">οἰκέ-τις</span> _house-maid_ (<span lang="grc">οἰκέ-της</span>), <span lang="grc">πολῖ-τις</span> _female citizen_ (<span lang="grc">πολί̄-της</span>).

\4. <span lang="grc">**ιττᾱ**, **ισσᾱ**</span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ιττα, -ισσα</span>): from <span lang="grc">ῖα</span> added to stems in <span lang="grc">τ</span> or <span lang="grc">κ</span> ([112](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s112), [114](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s114)), as <span lang="grc">θῆττα</span> _female serf_ from <span lang="grc">θητ-ῖα</span> (<span lang="grc">θής, θητ-ός</span> _serf_), <span lang="grc">Κίλισσα</span> _Cilician woman_ from <span lang="grc">Κιλικ-ῖα</span> (<span lang="grc">Κίλιξ</span> _Cilician_); later, by analogy, <span lang="grc">βασίλισσα</span> _queen_.

\5. <span lang="grc"> **αινᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-αινα</span>) corresponding to masculines in <span lang="grc">-ων</span>: <span lang="grc">λέ-αινα</span> _lioness_ (<span lang="grc">λέ-ων</span> <!-- page 233 --> _lion_), <span lang="grc">θεράπ-αινα</span> _handmaid_ (<span lang="grc">θεράπ-ων</span> _attendant_), <span lang="grc">Λάκ-αινα</span> _woman of Laconia_ (<span lang="grc">Λάκ-ων</span> _a Laconian_). By analogy, in <span lang="grc">ο</span> stems: <span lang="grc">λύκ-αινα</span> _she-wolf_ (<span lang="grc">λύκο-ς</span>). <span lang="grc">-αινα</span> stands for <span lang="grc">-αν-ῖα, -αν</span> being a weak form of <span lang="grc">-ων</span> ([833 e](01-word-formation-introductory.md#s833), [35](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s35) b).

N.—Names of _dealers_ in anything usually end in <span lang="grc">-πώλης, -ου</span>; fem. <span lang="grc">-πωλις, -ιδος</span> (<span lang="grc">πωλέω</span> _sell_), as <span lang="grc">βιβλιο-πώλης</span> _bookseller_ (<span lang="grc">βιβλίο-ν</span> _book_), <span lang="grc">σῑτο-πώλης</span> _graindealer_ (<span lang="grc">σῖτο-ς</span> _grain_), <span lang="grc">ἀρτό-πωλις</span> _bread-woman_ (<span lang="grc">ἄρτο-ς</span> _bread_). Cp. also <span lang="grc">καπηλίς</span> under <span lang="grc"> **ιδ** </span>.

##### <a id="s844"></a> §844

**GENTILES OR PLACE NAMES**

Gentiles are denominative nouns denoting belonging to or coming from a particular _country_, _nation_, or _city_. Gentiles are formed from proper nouns by secondary suffixes.

\1. <span lang="grc"> **ευ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-εύς</span>, gen. <span lang="grc">-έως</span>, masc.), <span lang="grc"> **ιδ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ίς</span>, gen. <span lang="grc">ίδ-ος</span>, fem.):

<span lang="grc">Πλαταιεύς -έως, Πλαταιΐς -ίδος</span> _a Plataean_ (<span lang="grc">ἡ Πλάταια</span>); <span lang="grc">Ἐρετριεύς</span> _an Eretrian_ (<span lang="grc">ἡ Ἐρέτρια</span>); <span lang="grc">Μεγαρεύς, Μεγαρίς</span> _a Megarian_ (<span lang="grc">τὰ Μέγαρα</span>); <span lang="grc">Αἰολεύς</span> _Aeolian_ (<span lang="grc">Αἴολος</span>, mythical ancestor of the Aeolians).

**a.** <span lang="grc">-ίς</span> (<span lang="grc">-ίδος</span>) may denote a _land_ or a _dialect:_ <span lang="grc">ἡ Δωρίς</span> (<span lang="grc">γῆ</span>) _Doris_; <span lang="grc">ἡ Αἰολίς</span> (<span lang="grc">γλῶττα</span>) _the Aeolic dialect_.

\2. <span lang="grc"> **τᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τη-ς</span>, masc.), <span lang="grc"> **τιδ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τις</span>, fem.): <span lang="grc">Τεγεά̄-της, Τεγεᾶ-τις</span> _of Tegea_ (<span lang="grc">ἡ Τεγέᾱ</span>); <span lang="grc">Σπαρτ-ιά̄-της, Σπαρτ-ιᾶ-τις</span> _of Sparta_ (<span lang="grc">ἡ Σπάρτᾱ</span>); <span lang="grc">Αἰγῑνή-της, Αἰγῑνῆ-τις</span> _of Aegina_ (<span lang="grc">ἡ Αἴγῑνα</span>); <span lang="grc">Συβαρ-ί̄-της, Συβαρ-ῖ-τις</span> _Sybarite_ (<span lang="grc">ἡ Σύβαρις</span>); <span lang="grc">Σικελι-ώ-της, Σικελι-ῶ-τις</span> _Siciliote_ (<span lang="grc">ἡ Σικελίᾱ</span>).

**a.** The endings <span lang="grc">-ῑτης, -ωτης</span> are due to analogy; see [843 a](#s843). N.

\3. Other gentiles, properly adjectives, end in <span lang="grc">**-ιος**, **-ιᾱ**</span>, as <span lang="grc">Ἀθηναῖο-ς, -αίᾱ</span> _of Athens_ (<span lang="grc">αἱ Ἀθῆναι</span>), <span lang="grc">Μῑλήσ-ιο-ς</span> for <span lang="grc">Μῑλητ-ιο-ς</span> _of Miletus_ (<span lang="grc">Μί̄λητος</span>), <span lang="grc">Ὀπούντιο-ς</span> _of Opus_ (<span lang="grc">Ὀποῦς</span>); (<span lang="grc">ι</span>)<span lang="grc"> **κός** </span>, (<span lang="grc">ι</span>)<span lang="grc"> **κά̄** </span>, as <span lang="grc">Ἰων-ικός</span> _Ionic_ (<span lang="grc">Ἴων-ες</span> _Ionians_); <span lang="grc">**νό-ς**, **νή**</span> preceded by <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span>(<span lang="grc">η</span>), <span lang="grc">ῑ</span>, as <span lang="grc">Σαρδι-ᾱνό-ς</span> _of Sardis_ (<span lang="grc">Σάρδεις</span>), <span lang="grc">Λαμψακ-ηνό-ς</span> _of Lampsacus_ (<span lang="grc">Λάμψακος</span>), <span lang="grc">Βυζαντ-ῖνο-ς</span> _Byzantine_ (<span lang="grc">Βυζάντιον</span>). See [863 b](04-list-of-noun-suffixes.md#s863). 12.

##### <a id="s845"></a> §845

**PATRONYMICS**

Patronymics, or denominative proper names denoting descent from a father or ancestor, are formed from proper names of persons by means of the following suffixes:

\1. <span lang="grc"> **δᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-δη-ς</span>, masc.), <span lang="grc"> **δ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ς</span>, fem.):

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="grc">Βορεά̆-δη-ς</span> | _son of Boreas_ | fem. <span lang="grc">Βορεά̆-ς, -δος</span> | from <span lang="grc">Βορέᾱ-ς</span> |

Stems in <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> shorten <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> to <span lang="grc">α</span>; from such forms arose

\2. <span lang="grc"> **αδᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-άδη-ς</span>, masc.), <span lang="grc"> **αδ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-άς</span>, fem.):

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="grc">Θεστι-άδη-ς</span> | _son of Thestius_ | fem. <span lang="grc">Θεστι-άς, -άδος</span> | from <span lang="grc">Θέστιο-ς</span> |

From this type arose a new formation:

\3. <span lang="grc"> **ιαδᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ιάδη-ς</span>, masc.), <span lang="grc"> **ιαδ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ιάς</span>, fem.):

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="grc">Φερητ-ιάδη-ς</span> | _son of Pheres_ | fem. <span lang="grc">Φερητ-ιάς, -ιάδ-ος</span> | from <span lang="grc">Φέρης</span> (<span lang="grc">-ητοσ</span>) |
| <span lang="grc">Περση-ϊάδη-ς</span> | _son of Perseus_ | (fem. <span lang="grc">Περση-ΐς, -ίδ-οσ</span>) | from <span lang="grc">Περσεύ-ς</span> |
| <span lang="grc">Τελαμων-ιάδη-ς</span> | _son of Telamon_ |  | from <span lang="grc">Τελαμών</span> (<span lang="grc">-ῶνοσ</span>) |

<!-- page 234 -->

\4. <span lang="grc"> **ιδᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ίδη-ς</span>, masc.), <span lang="grc"> **·ιδ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ίς</span>, fem.):

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="grc">Τανταλ-ίδη-ς</span> | _son of Tantalus_ | fem. <span lang="grc">Τανταλ-ίς, -ίδ-ος</span> | from <span lang="grc">Τάνταλο-ς</span> |
| <span lang="grc">Κεκροπ-ίδη-ς</span> | _son of Cecrops_ | fem. <span lang="grc">Κεκροπ-ίς, -ίδ-ος</span> | from <span lang="grc">Κέκροψ</span> (<span lang="grc">-οποσ</span>) |
| <span lang="grc">Οἰνε-ίδη-ς</span> | _son of Oeneus_ | fem. <span lang="grc">Οἰνη-ΐς, -ίδ-ος</span> | from <span lang="grc">Οἰνεύ-ς</span> |
| <span lang="grc">Λητο-ΐδη-ς</span> | _son of Leto_ | fem. <span lang="grc">Λητω-ΐς, -ίδ-ος</span> | from <span lang="grc">Λητώ</span> ([279](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/05-third-declension.md#s279)) |

Stems in <span lang="grc">ο</span> drop <span lang="grc">ο</span>; stems in <span lang="grc">ευ</span> (<span lang="grc">ηυ</span>) drop <span lang="grc">υ</span>; stems in <span lang="grc">οι</span> (<span lang="grc">ωι</span>) drop <span lang="grc">ι</span>.

\5. <span lang="grc"> **ῑον** </span> or <span lang="grc"> **ῐων** </span> (poetic and rare; nom. <span lang="grc">-ί̄ων</span>, masc.):

<span lang="grc">Κρον-ί̄ων</span> _son of Cronus_ (also <span lang="grc">Κρον-ίδη-ς</span>), gen. <span lang="grc">Κρον-ί̄ον-ος</span> or <span lang="grc">Κρον-ί̆ων-ος</span> according to the metre, from <span lang="grc">Κρόνο-ς</span>.

\6. <span lang="grc"> **ιωνᾱ** </span> or <span lang="grc"> **ῑνᾱ** </span> (poetic and rare; nom. <span lang="grc">-ιώνη</span> or <span lang="grc">ί̄νη</span>, fem.):

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="grc">Ἀκρισ-ιώνη</span> _daughter of_ <span lang="grc">Ἀκρίσιο-ς</span> | <span lang="grc">Ἀδρηστ-ί̄νη</span> _daughter of_ <span lang="grc">Ἄδρηστο-ς</span> |

##### <a id="s846"></a> §846

Variations occur especially in poetry: a. Hom. <span lang="grc">Πηλε-ίδη-ς, Πηλε-ΐδη-ς, Πηλη-ϊάδη-ς</span>, and <span lang="grc">Πηλε-ΐων</span>, _son of_ <span lang="grc">Πηλεύ-ς; Ἀτρε-ίδη-ς, Ἀτρε-ΐδη-ς</span>, and <span lang="grc">Ἀτρε-ΐων</span>, _son of_ <span lang="grc">Ἀτρεύ-ς</span>.

**b.** Two patronymic endings: <span lang="grc">Ταλα-ϊ̄ον-ίδη-ς</span> _son of_ <span lang="grc">Ταλαό-ς</span>.

**c.** The stem drops or adds a syllable: <span lang="grc">Δευκαλ-ίδη-ς</span> _son of_ <span lang="grc">Δευκαλίων, -ίων-ος; Λαμπ-ετ-ίδη-ς</span> _son of_ <span lang="grc">Λάμπο-ς</span>.

**d.** <span lang="grc">-ιδης</span> is used in comic formations: <span lang="grc">κλεπτ-ίδη-ς</span> _son of a thief_.

**e.** <span lang="grc">-νδᾱς</span> occurs in the dialects, as <span lang="grc">Ἐπαμεινώνδᾱ-ς</span> _Epaminondas_.

**f.** <span lang="grc">-ιος, -ειος</span>, may indicate descent, as <span lang="grc">Τελαμώνιε παῖ</span> _oh son of Telamon_, <span lang="grc">Τυνδαρείᾱ θυγάτηρ</span> _daughter of Tyndareus;_ cp. Tennyson's “Niobean daughter.”

##### <a id="s847"></a> §847

A patronymic may include the father, as <span lang="grc">Πεισιστρατ ίδαι</span> _the Peisistratidae_ (Peisistratus and his sons).

##### <a id="s848"></a> §848

Most genuine patronymics are poetical and belong to the older language. In the classical period patrony mics rarely indicate descent in the case of historical persons; as <span lang="grc">Εὐρῑπίδης, Ἀριστείδης</span>.

##### <a id="s849"></a> §849

Metronymics denote descent from the mother, as <span lang="grc">Δᾱνα-ΐδη-ς</span> _son of_ <span lang="grc">Δᾰνάη, Φῑλυρ-ίδη-ς</span> _son of_ <span lang="grc">Φῐλύρᾱ</span>.

##### <a id="s850"></a> §850

Relationship is sometimes denoted by the suffixes <span lang="grc"> **ιδεο** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ιδοῦ-ς</span> _son of_) and <span lang="grc"> **ιδεᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ιδῆ</span> _daughter of_); as <span lang="grc">ἀδελφ-ιδοῦ-ς</span> _nephew_, <span lang="grc">ἀδελφ-ιδῆ</span> _niece_ (<span lang="grc">ἀδελφό-ς</span> _brother_).

## PLACE

##### <a id="s851"></a> §851

Place may be expressed by the secondary suffixes

\1. <span lang="grc"> **ιο** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ιο-ν</span>, neut.): <span lang="grc">Διονύ̄σιον</span> (scil. <span lang="grc">ἱερόν</span>) _temple of Dionysus_, <span lang="grc">Ἥραιον</span> _Heraeum_.

Also <span lang="grc"> **-ε-ιο** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-εῖο-ν</span>, neut.): from substantives in <span lang="grc">-εύ-ς</span> and by extension in others; as <span lang="grc">χαλκε-ῖο-ν</span> _forge_ (<span lang="grc">χαλκ-εύ-ς</span> _coppersmith_), <span lang="grc">Θησε-ῖον</span> _Thesēum_ (<span lang="grc">Θησεύ-ς</span>), <span lang="grc">λογ-εῖο-ν</span> _place for speaking_ (<span lang="grc">λόγο-ς</span> _speech_), <span lang="grc">μουσ-εῖο-ν</span> _seat of the Muses_ (<span lang="grc">μοῦσα</span> _muse_), <span lang="grc">Ὀλυμπι-εῖο-ν</span> _Olympiēum_ (<span lang="grc">Ὀλύμπιο-ς</span> _Olympian Zeus_).

\2. <span lang="grc"> **τηρ-ιο** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-τήρ-ιο-ν</span>, neut.): derived from substantives in <span lang="grc">-τήρ</span> (or <span lang="grc">-τής</span>); as <span lang="grc">ἀκροᾱτήρ-ιο-ν</span> _auditorium_ (<span lang="grc">ἀκροᾱτήρ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἀκροᾱτής</span> _hearer_), <span lang="grc">ἐργαστήρ-ιο-ν</span> _workshop_ (<span lang="grc">ἐργαστήρ</span> _workman_), <span lang="grc">βουλευτήριον</span> _senate house_ (<span lang="grc">βουλευτήρ</span> or <span lang="grc">βουλευτής</span> _councillor_, _senator_). See [863 a](04-list-of-noun-suffixes.md#s863). 8. <!-- page 235 -->

\3. <span lang="grc"> **ων** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ών</span>, gen. <span lang="grc">-ῶν-ος</span>, masc.): <span lang="grc">ἀνδρ-ών</span> _apartment for men_ (<span lang="grc">ἀνήρ, ἀνδρ-ός</span> _man_), <span lang="grc">ἱππ-ών</span> _stable_ (<span lang="grc">ἵππο-ς</span> _horse_), <span lang="grc">παρθεν-ών</span> _maiden's apartment_, _Parthenon_, temple of Pallas (<span lang="grc">παρθένο-ς</span> _maiden_), <span lang="grc">οἰν-ών</span> _wine-cellar_ (<span lang="grc">οἶνο-ς</span> _wine_), <span lang="grc">ἀμπελ-ών</span> _vineyard_ (<span lang="grc">ἄμπελο-ς</span> _vine_). Forms in <span lang="grc"> **-εών** </span> occur, as <span lang="grc">περιστερ-εών</span> _dove-cote_ (<span lang="grc">περιστερά̄</span> _dove_), <span lang="grc">οἰνεών</span>.

\4. <span lang="grc"> **ῑτιδ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ῖτις</span>, fem.): added to <span lang="grc">ων, ἀνδρων-ῖτις</span> _apartment for men_, <span lang="grc">γυναικωνῖτις</span> _apartment for women_.

\5. <span lang="grc"> **ωνιᾱ** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ωνιά̄</span>, fem.): <span lang="grc">ῥοδ-ωνιά̄</span> _rose-bed_ (<span lang="grc">ῥόδο-ν</span> _rose_).

\6. <span lang="grc"> **τρᾱ** </span> (rare; nom. <span lang="grc">-τρᾱ</span>, fem.): <span lang="grc">ὀρχή-σ-τρᾱ</span> _dancing-place_ (<span lang="grc">ὀρχέ-ομαι</span> _dance_), <span lang="grc">παλαί-σ-τρᾱ</span> _wrestling-ground_ (<span lang="grc">παλαί-ω</span> _wrestle_). Cp. [836](01-word-formation-introductory.md#s836).

## DIMINUTIVES

##### <a id="s852"></a> §852

Diminutives are denominatives formed from the stems of substantives by various secondary suffixes.

\1. <span lang="grc"> **ιο** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ιο-ν</span>, neut.): <span lang="grc">παιδ-ίο-ν</span> _little child_ (<span lang="grc">παῖς, παιδ-ός</span>), <span lang="grc">ὀρνί̄θ-ιο-ν</span> _small bird_ (<span lang="grc">ὄρνῑς, ὄρνῑθος</span>), <span lang="grc">ἀσπίδ-ιο-ν</span> _small shield_ (<span lang="grc">ἀσπίς, ἀσπίδ-ος</span>).

N.—Trisyllabic words are paroxytone if the first syllable is long by nature or position.

\2. <span lang="grc"> **ιδ-ιο** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ίδιο-ν</span>, neut.): derived from such words as <span lang="grc">ἀσπίδ-ιο-ν</span>; as <span lang="grc">ξιφ-ίδιο-ν</span> _dagger_ (<span lang="grc">ξίφος</span> _sword_, stem <span lang="grc">ξιφεσ-</span>), <span lang="grc">βο-ίδιο-ν</span> _small cow_ (<span lang="grc">βοῦ-ς</span>), <span lang="grc">οἰκί̄διο-ν</span> _small house_, <span lang="grc">οἰκι ¨ ιδιον</span> (<span lang="grc">οἰκίᾱ</span>), <span lang="grc">ἰχθύ̄διο-ν</span> _small fish_ (<span lang="grc">ἰχθύ̄ς</span>). See [833 b](01-word-formation-introductory.md#s833).

\3. <span lang="grc"> **αρ-ιο** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-άριον</span>, neut.): <span lang="grc">παιδ-άριο-ν</span> _little child_.

\4. <span lang="grc"> **υδ-ριο** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ύδριον</span>, neut.): <span lang="grc">μελ-ύδριο-ν</span> _little song_ (<span lang="grc">μέλος</span>).

\5. <span lang="grc"> **υλλιο** </span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ύλλιον</span>, neut.): <span lang="grc">ἐπ-ύλλιο-ν</span> _little epic_ or _cersicle_ (<span lang="grc">ἔπος</span>).

\6. <span lang="grc">**ισκο**, **ισκᾱ**</span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ίσκος</span>, masc., <span lang="grc">-ίσκη</span>, fem.): <span lang="grc">ἀνθρωπ-ίσκο-ς</span> _manikin_, <span lang="grc">παιδ-ίσκο-ς</span> _young boy_, <span lang="grc">παιδ-ίσκη</span> _young girl_. From this comes <span lang="grc">-ισκ-ιο</span> in <span lang="grc">ἀσπιδ-ίσκιο-ν</span> _small shield_.

##### <a id="s853"></a> §853

Many other diminutives occur, as <span lang="grc"> **ακνᾱ** </span>: in <span lang="grc">πιθάκνη</span> _wine-jar_ (<span lang="grc">πίθος</span>); <span lang="grc">**ιδ**, **ῑδ**</span>: in <span lang="grc">ἁμαξίς, -ίδος</span> _small wagon_ (<span lang="grc">ἅμαξα</span>), <span lang="grc">νησί̄ς, -ῖδος</span> _islet_ (<span lang="grc">νῆσο-ς</span>); <span lang="grc"> **ιδ-ευ** </span>: of the young of animals, as <span lang="grc">λυκ-ιδεύς</span> _wolf's whelp_ (<span lang="grc">λύκο-ς</span>), also <span lang="grc">ὑϊδεύς</span> _son's son, grandson_ (<span lang="grc">υἱός</span>); <span lang="grc"> **ιχο** </span>: <span lang="grc">ὀρτάλιχος</span> _young bird_ (<span lang="grc">ὀρταλίς</span>) _chick_; <span lang="grc"> **ιχνᾱ** </span>: <span lang="grc">κυλίχνη</span> (and <span lang="grc">κυλίχνιον, κυλιχνίς</span>) _small cup_ (<span lang="grc">κύλιξ</span>). Rare or late are <span lang="grc">-ᾱκίδιον, -ά̄σιον, -άφιον, -ιδάριον, -ισκάριον, ιον</span>, 861. 19, <span lang="grc">-ύλος</span>, and over 25 others. See <span lang="grc">λο</span>, 860. 1.

##### <a id="s854"></a> §854

Diminutives are often combined: <span lang="grc">παιδ-ισκ-άριον</span> _stripling_, <span lang="grc">μειράκ-ιον, μειρακ-ίσκος, μειρακ-ύλλ-ιον, μειρακ-υλλ-ίδιον</span> _stripling_ (<span lang="grc">μεῖραξ</span> _lass_), <span lang="grc">χλαν-ισκ-ίδιον</span> _cloaklet_ (<span lang="grc">χλανίς</span>), <span lang="grc">ζῳδάριον</span> _insect_ (<span lang="grc">ζῷον</span> _animal_).

##### <a id="s855"></a> §855

Some words, especially such as denote parts of the body, are diminutive in form, but not in meaning; as <span lang="grc">κρᾱνίον</span> _skull_, <span lang="grc">θηρίον</span> _beast_ ( = <span lang="grc">θήρ</span>), <span lang="grc">πεδίον</span> _plain_ (<span lang="grc">πέδον</span> _ground_), all in Homer, who has no diminutives. Diminutives often employed tend to lose their diminutive value.

##### <a id="s856"></a> §856

Diminutives may express affection, familiarity, daintiness, and sometimes pity or contempt (cp. _dar-ling_, _lord-ling_). See the examples under 852, and also <span lang="grc">πατρ-ίδιον</span> _daddy_ (<span lang="grc">πατήρ</span>), <span lang="grc">ἀδελφ-ίδιο-ν</span> _dear little brother_, <span lang="grc">Σωκρατ-ίδιον</span> _dear Socky_, <span lang="grc">ἀνθρώπ-ιον</span> _manikin_. Some endings often have an ironical force, as <span lang="grc">πλούτ-ᾱξ</span> _rich churl_, <span lang="grc">γάστρ-ων</span> _fat-belly_.
