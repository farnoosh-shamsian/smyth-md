# COMPOUND WORDS

<!-- page 247 -->

##### <a id="s869"></a> §869

A compound word is formed by the union of two or more parts; as <span lang="grc">λογο-γράφο-ς</span> _speech-writer_, <span lang="grc">δι-έξ-οδο-ς</span> _outlet_ (lit. _way out through_).

**a.** Compounds of three or more parts usually fall into two separate units; as <span lang="grc">βατραχο-μῡο-μαχίᾱ</span> _battle of the frogs-and-mice_. Such compounds are common in comedy; as <span lang="grc">στρεψο-δικο-παν-ουργίᾱ</span> _rascally perversion of justice_.

**b.** In a compound word two or more members are united under one accent; as in _bláckberry_ contrasted with _black berry_. Most compounds in Greek, an inflected language, are genuine compounds, not mere word-groups such as are common in English, which is for the most part devoid of inflections.

**c.** Every compound contains a defining part and a defined part. The defining part usually precedes: <span lang="grc">εὐ-τυχής</span> _fortunate_, as opposed to <span lang="grc">δυσ-τυχής</span> _unfortunate_. The parts of a compound stand in various syntactical relations to each other, as that of adjective or attributive genitive to a substantive, or that of adverb or object to a verb, etc. Compounds may thus be regarded as abbreviated forms of syntax. Cp. [895 a](#s895), [897](#s897) N. 1.

## FIRST PART OF A COMPOUND

##### <a id="s870"></a> §870

The first part of a compound may be a noun-stem, a verbstem, a numeral, a preposition or adverb, or an inseparable prefix.

**a.** The use of stems in composition is a survival of a period in the history of language in which inflections were not fully developed.

### FIRST PART A NOUN-STEM

##### <a id="s871"></a> §871

**First Declension** (**<span lang="grc">ᾱ-</span>stems**).—The first part may

**a.** end in <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> or <span lang="grc">η</span> (rarely): <span lang="grc">ἀγορᾱ-νόμο-ς</span> _clerk of the market_ (<span lang="grc">ἀγορά̄</span>), <span lang="grc">νῑκη-φόρο-ς</span> _bringing victory_ (<span lang="grc">νί̄κη</span>).

**b.** end in <span lang="grc">ο</span>: <span lang="grc">δικο-γράφο-ς</span> _writer of law-speeches_ (<span lang="grc">δίκη</span> _justice_). Here <span lang="grc">ο</span> is substituted for <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> of the stem by analogy to <span lang="grc">ο</span>-stems.

N.—Compounds of <span lang="grc">γῆ</span> _earth_ have <span lang="grc">γεω-</span> (for <span lang="grc">γηο-</span> by [34](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s34)); as <span lang="grc">γεω-μέτρης</span> _surveyor_ (_land-measurer;_ <span lang="grc">μετρέω</span> _measure_). Doric has <span lang="grc">γᾱ-μέτρης</span>. Cp. [224 a](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/01-first-declension-stems-in-alpha.md#s224).

**c.** lose its vowel before a vowel: <span lang="grc">κεφαλ-αλγής</span> _causing head-ache_ (<span lang="grc">κεφαλή</span> _head_, <span lang="grc">ἄλγ-ος</span> _pain_).

##### <a id="s872"></a> §872

**Second Declension** (**<span lang="grc">ο-</span>stems**).—The first part may

**a.** end in <span lang="grc">ο</span>: <span lang="grc">λογο-γράφο-ς</span> _speech-writer_.

**b.** end in <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span> or <span lang="grc">η</span> (rarely): <span lang="grc">ἐλαφη-βόλο-ς</span> _deer-shooting_ (<span lang="grc">ἔλαφος, βάλλω</span>). Here <span lang="grc">η</span> is due to the analogy of <span lang="grc">ᾱ-</span>stems.

**c.** lose <span lang="grc">ο</span> before a vowel: <span lang="grc">μόν-αρχο-ς</span> _monarch_ (_sole ruler:_ <span lang="grc">μόνο-ς</span> _alone_, <span lang="grc">ἄρχ-ω</span> _rule_).

N.—Words of the ‘Attic’ declension may end in <span lang="grc">ω</span>, as <span lang="grc">νεω-κόρο-ς</span> _custodian of a temple_ (<span lang="grc">νεώς</span>). <!-- page 248 -->

##### <a id="s873"></a> §873

**Third Declension** (**consonant stems**).—The first part may

**a.** show the stem (<span lang="grc">ι, υ, αυ, ου</span>): <span lang="grc">μαντι-πόλο-ς</span> _inspired_ (<span lang="grc">μάντι-ς</span> _seer_, <span lang="grc">πέλ-ω</span>, cp. <span lang="grc">-κολος</span>), <span lang="grc">ἰχθυ-βόλο-ς</span> _catching-fish_ (<span lang="grc">ἰχθύ̄ς, βάλλω</span>), <span lang="grc">βου-κόλο-ς</span> _ox-herd_ (<span lang="grc">βοῦ-ς, -κολο-ς</span>, cp. Lat. _colo_, and [131](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s131)).

N.—A few consonant stems retain the consonant: <span lang="grc">μελάγ-χολος</span> _dipped in black bile_ (<span lang="grc">μέλᾱς, χολή</span>). See also [876](#s876).

**b.** add <span lang="grc">ο</span> to the stem: <span lang="grc">σωματ-ο-φύλαξ</span> _body-guard_ (<span lang="grc">σῶμα</span> _body_, <span lang="grc">φυλάττω</span> _guard_), <span lang="grc">μητρ-ό-πολις</span> _mother-city_, _metropolis_ (<span lang="grc">μήτηρ, πόλις</span>), <span lang="grc">φυσι-ο-λόγος</span> _natural philosopher_ (<span lang="grc">φύσι-ς</span> _nature_), <span lang="grc">ἰχθυ-ο-πώλης</span> _fishmonger_ (<span lang="grc">ἰχθύ̄ς, πωλέω</span> _sell_).

**c.** add <span lang="grc">ᾰ</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">η</span>): <span lang="grc">ποδ-ά-νιπτρο-ν</span> _water for washing the feet_ (<span lang="grc">ποῦς, νίπτω</span>), <span lang="grc">λαμπαδ-η-δρομίᾱ</span> _torch-race_.

##### <a id="s874"></a> §874

Compounds of <span lang="grc">πᾶς</span> _all_ usually show <span lang="grc">πᾰν-</span>, as <span lang="grc">πάν-σοφο-ς</span> (and <span lang="grc">πάσ-σοφος</span> 101 b) _all-wise_, <span lang="grc">παρ-ρησίᾱ</span> _frankness_ (‘all-speaking’); but also <span lang="grc">παντ-</span> in <span lang="grc">πάνταρχος</span> _all-ruling;_ and <span lang="grc">παντ-ο-</span> in <span lang="grc">παντ-ο-πώλιο-ν</span> _bazaar_ (<span lang="grc">πωλέω</span> _sell_).

##### <a id="s875"></a> §875

Neuter stems in <span lang="grc">ματ</span> usually show <span lang="grc">ματ-ο</span>, as <span lang="grc">ἀγαλματ-ο-ποιό-ς</span> _sculptor_ (<span lang="grc">ἄγαλμα</span> _statue_, <span lang="grc">ποιέω</span> _make_). Some have <span lang="grc">μα</span>, as <span lang="grc">ὀνομα-κλυτό-ς</span> _of famous name;_ some show <span lang="grc">μο</span> for <span lang="grc">ματο</span>, as <span lang="grc">αἱμο-ρραγίᾱ</span> _hemorrhage_ (<span lang="grc">αἷμα, -ατος</span> _blood_, <span lang="grc">ῥήγνῡμι</span> _break_, [80](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s80)).

##### <a id="s876"></a> §876

Stems in <span lang="grc">ες</span> (nom. <span lang="grc">-ης</span> or <span lang="grc">-ος</span>) usually drop <span lang="grc">ες</span> and add <span lang="grc">ο</span>; as <span lang="grc">ψευδ-ο μαρτυρίᾱ</span> _false testimony_ (<span lang="grc">ψευδ-ής</span>); and so stems in <span lang="grc">ας</span>, as <span lang="grc">κρεο-φάγο-ς</span> _flesh-eating_ (<span lang="grc">κρέας, φαγεῖν</span> 529. 5). Some stems in <span lang="grc">ες</span> and <span lang="grc">ας</span> retain <span lang="grc">ες</span> and <span lang="grc">ας</span> (in poetry), as <span lang="grc">σακεσ-πάλο-ς</span> _wielding a shield_ (<span lang="grc">σάκος, πάλλω</span>), <span lang="grc">σελασ-φόρο-ς</span> _light-bringing_ (<span lang="grc">σέλας, φέρω</span>); some add <span lang="grc">ι</span> (for sake of the metre), as <span lang="grc">ὀρεσ-ί-τροφος</span> _mountain-bred_ (<span lang="grc">ὄρος, τρέφω</span>); these may belong to 879.

##### <a id="s877"></a> §877

Other abbreviations: <span lang="grc">γαλα-θηνό-ς</span> _nurse_ (<span lang="grc">γαλακτ-</span> _milk_, <span lang="grc">θῆ-σθαι</span> _give suck_), <span lang="grc">μελι-ηδής</span> _honey-sweet_ (<span lang="grc">μελιτ-</span>), <span lang="grc">κελαι-νεφής</span> _black with clouds_ from <span lang="grc">κελαινό-ς</span> _black_ (cp. [129](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s129) c) and <span lang="grc">νέφος</span> _cloud_.

##### <a id="s878"></a> §878

**Words once beginning with <span lang="grc">Ϝ</span> or <span lang="grc">ς</span>**.—When the second part consists of a word beginning with digamma, a preceding vowel is often not elided: <span lang="grc">κακο-εργός</span> (Epic) _doing ill_ (later <span lang="grc">κακοῦργος</span>) from <span lang="grc">ϝέργο-ν</span> _work;_ <span lang="grc">μηνο-ειδής</span> _crescent-shaped_ (<span lang="grc">μήνη</span> _moon_, <span lang="grc">ϝεῖδος</span> _shape_); <span lang="grc">τῑμά̄-ορος</span> (later <span lang="grc">τῑμωρός</span>) _avenging_ (<span lang="grc">τῑμή</span> _honour_, <span lang="grc">ϝοράω</span> _observe_, _defend_).—Compounds of <span lang="grc">-οχος</span>, from <span lang="grc">ἔχω</span> _have_ (orig. <span lang="grc">σέχω, -σοχος</span>) contract: <span lang="grc">κληροῦχος</span> _holding an allotment_ of land (<span lang="grc">κλῆρο-ς</span> _lot_), <span lang="grc">πολι-οῦχος</span> _protecting a city_ (for <span lang="grc">πολι-ο-οχος</span>).

##### <a id="s879"></a> §879

**Flectional Compounds**.—A compound whose first part is a case form, not a stem, is called a flectional compound (cp. _sportsman_, _kinsfolk_): (1) nominative: <span lang="grc">τρεισ-καί-δεκα</span> _thirteen;_ (2) genitive: <span lang="grc">Διόσ-κουροι</span> _Dioscuri_ (_sons of Zeus_), <span lang="grc">Ἑλλήσ-ποντος</span> _Helle's sea_, <span lang="grc">Πελοπόν-νησος</span> (for <span lang="grc">Πελοποσ-νησος</span>, 105 a) _Pelops' island;_ (3) dative: <span lang="grc">δορί-ληπτος</span> _won by the spear;_ (4) locative: <span lang="grc">ὁδοι-πόρος</span> _wayfarer_, <span lang="grc">Πυλοι-γενής</span> _born in Pylus_.—From such compounds derivatives may be formed, as <span lang="grc">Ἑλλησπόντιος</span> _of the Hellespont_, <span lang="grc">θεοισεχθρίᾱ</span> _hatred of the gods_.

### FIRST PART A VERB-STEM

##### <a id="s880"></a> §880

Some compounds have as their first part a verb-stem (cp. _break-water_, _pick-pocket_, _catch-penny_). Such compounds are usually <!-- page 249 --> poetic adjectives. The verb-stem is usually transitive and has the form that appears in the present or aorist.

##### <a id="s881"></a> §881

Before a vowel the verb-stem remains unchanged or drops a final vowel; before a consonant it adds <span lang="grc">ε, ο</span>, or <span lang="grc">ι</span>: <span lang="grc">φέρ-ασπις</span> _shield-bearing_, <span lang="grc">μῑσ-άνθρωπος</span> _man-hating_ (<span lang="grc">μῑσέ-ω</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐκ-ε-χειρίᾱ</span> ([125 d](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s125)) _holding of hands, truce_, <span lang="grc">λιπ-ο-στρατίᾱ</span> _desertion of the army_, <span lang="grc">νῑκ-ό-βουλος</span> _prevailing in the Senate_, <span lang="grc">ἀρχ-ι-τέκτων</span> _masterbuilder_.

##### <a id="s882"></a> §882

The verb-stem adds <span lang="grc">σι</span> (before a vowel, <span lang="grc">ς</span>). Some insert <span lang="grc">ε</span> before <span lang="grc">σι</span> (<span lang="grc">ς</span>): <span lang="grc">σω-σί-πολις</span> _saving the state_ (<span lang="grc">σῴζω</span>), <span lang="grc">ῥί̄ψ-ασπις</span> _craven_, lit. throwing away a shield (<span lang="grc">ῥί̄π-τ-ω</span>), <span lang="grc">δηξί-θῡμος</span> (and <span lang="grc">δακ-έ-θῡμος</span>) _heart-eating_ (<span lang="grc">δάκ-ν-ω</span>), <span lang="grc">ἑλκ-ε-σίπεπλος</span> _with long train_, lit. trailing the robe (cp. <span lang="grc">ἑλκ-ε-χίτων</span>)

**a.** This <span lang="grc">ε</span> is the vowel added in many verb-stems ([485](../part-2-inflection/09-verbs/09-formation-of-the-tense-systems-omega-and-mi-verbs.md#s485)).

### FIRST PART A NUMERAL

##### <a id="s883"></a> §883

The first part of a compound is often a numeral: <span lang="grc">δί-πους</span> _biped_, <span lang="grc">τρί-πους</span> _tripod_ (having _three feet_), <span lang="grc">τέθρ-ιππον</span> _four-horse chariot_, <span lang="grc">πέντ-ᾱθλον</span> _contest in five events_.

### FIRST PART A PREPOSITION OR ADVERB

##### <a id="s884"></a> §884

A preposition or adverb is often the first part of a compound: <span lang="grc">εἴσ-οδος</span> _entrance_, <span lang="grc">ἀπο-φεύγω</span> _flee from_, <span lang="grc">εὐ-τυχής</span> _happy_, <span lang="grc">ἀείμνηστος</span> _ever to be remembered_.

**a.** Except when the substantive is treated as a verbal (as in <span lang="grc">εἴσ-οδος</span> _entrance_, cp. <span lang="grc">εἰσ-ιέναι</span> _enter_), prepositions are rarely compounded with substantives. Thus, <span lang="grc">σύν-δουλος</span> _fellow-slave_, <span lang="grc">ὑπο-διδάσκαλος</span> (= <span lang="grc">ὁ ὑπό τινι δ</span>.) _under-teacher;_ also <span lang="grc">ὑπό-λευκος</span> _whitish_.

**b.** The ordinary euphonic changes occur. Observe that <span lang="grc">πρό</span> _before_ may contract with <span lang="grc">ο</span> or <span lang="grc">ε</span> to <span lang="grc">ου</span>: <span lang="grc">προέχω</span> or <span lang="grc">προὔχω</span> _hold before_ (cp. [449](../part-2-inflection/09-verbs/06-reduplication.md#s449) b). See [124 a](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s124).

**c.** <span lang="grc">η</span> sometimes is inserted after a preposition or takes the place of a final vowel: <span lang="grc">ὑπερ-ή-φανος</span> _conspicuous_, <span lang="grc">ἐπ-ή-βολος</span> _having achieved_.

**d.** Akin to adverbial compounds are some in <span lang="grc">φιλ-ο</span>, as <span lang="grc">φιλο-μαθής</span> _one who gladly learns_.

### FIRST PART AN INSEPARABLE PREFIX

##### <a id="s885"></a> §885

Several prefixes occur only in composition:

\1. <span lang="grc"> **α᾽** </span>(<span lang="grc"> **ν** </span>)- (<span lang="grc">ἀν-</span> before a vowel, <span lang="grc">ἀ-</span> before a consonant; _alpha privative_) with a negative force like Lat. _in-_, Eng. _un-_ (or _-less_): <span lang="grc">ἀν-άξιος</span> _unworthy_ (= <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἄξιος</span>), <span lang="grc">ἀν-όμοιος</span> _unlike_, <span lang="grc">ἀν-ώδυνος</span> _anodyne_ (<span lang="grc">ὀδύνη</span> _pain_, cp. [887](#s887)), <span lang="grc">ἄ-νους</span> _silly_, <span lang="grc">ἄ-τῑμος</span> _unhonoured_, <span lang="grc">ἄ-θεος</span> _godless_, <span lang="grc">γάμος ἄγαμος</span> _marriage that is no marriage_. <span lang="grc">ἀ-</span> is also found before words once beginning with digamma or sigma: <span lang="grc">ἀ-ηδής</span> _unpleasant_ (<span lang="grc">ϝηδύς</span>), <span lang="grc">ἀ-όρᾱτος</span> _unseen_ (<span lang="grc">ϝοράω</span>), <span lang="grc">ἄ-οπλος</span> _without shields_ (<span lang="grc">σοπλον</span>), and, by contraction with the following vowel, <span lang="grc">ἄ̄κων</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀ-ϝέκων</span> _unwilling_). But <span lang="grc">ἀν-</span> often appears: <span lang="grc">ἀν-έλπιστος</span> (and <span lang="grc">ἄ-ελπτος</span>) _unhoped for_ (<span lang="grc">ϝελπίς</span>), <span lang="grc">ἄν-οπλος</span> _without shield_.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ἀ-, ἀν-</span> (for <span lang="grc">ṇ</span>, 35 b) represent weak forms of I. E. _ne_ ‘not.’ <!-- page 250 -->

\2. <span lang="grc"> **ἡμι-** </span> _half_ (Lat. _sēmi-_): <span lang="grc">ἡμι-κύκλιος</span> _semi-circular_ (<span lang="grc">κύκλος</span>), <span lang="grc">ἡμι-όλιος</span> _half as much again_ (<span lang="grc">ὅλος</span> _whole_), <span lang="grc">ἡμι-θνής</span> _half-dead_.

\3. <span lang="grc"> **δυσ-** </span> (opposed to <span lang="grc">εὖ</span> _well_) _ill_, _un-_, _mis-_, denoting something _difficult_, _bad_, or _unfortunate_, as <span lang="grc">δυσ-τυχής</span> _unfortunate_, <span lang="grc">δυσ-χερής</span> _hard to manage_, <span lang="grc">δυσδαίμων</span> _of ill fortune_ (contrast <span lang="grc">εὐ-τυχής, εὐ-χερής, εὐ-δαίμων</span>), <span lang="grc">δυσ-άρεστος</span> _ill-pleased_, <span lang="grc">Δύσ-παρις</span> _ill-starred Paris_.

\4. <span lang="grc"> **ἀ-** </span> (or <span lang="grc">ἁ-</span>) _copulative_ denotes _union_, _likeness_ (cp. Lat. _con-_); <span lang="grc">ἀ-κόλουθος</span> _attendant_, _agreeing with_ (<span lang="grc">κέλευθος</span> _path: i.e._ going on the same road), <span lang="grc">ἀ-τάλαντος</span> _of the same weight_, <span lang="grc">ἅ-πᾱς</span> _all together_. A variation of <span lang="grc">ἀ-</span>copulative is <span lang="grc">ἀ-</span> _intensive:_ <span lang="grc">ἀ-τενής</span> _stretched_ (<span lang="grc">τείνω</span> _stretch_), <span lang="grc">ἄ-πεδος</span> _level_ (<span lang="grc">πέδον</span> _ground_).

**a.** <span lang="grc">ἀ-</span> _copulative_ stands for <span lang="grc">σα-</span> (from <span lang="grc">σṃ</span> 20, 35 c), and is connected with <span lang="grc">ἅμα, ὁμοῦ</span>, and <span lang="grc">ὁμο-</span> _together_.

\5. <span lang="grc"> **νη-** </span> (poetic) with the force of a negative (cp. Lat. _nē_): <span lang="grc">νή-ποινος</span> _unavenged_ (<span lang="grc">ποινή</span> _punishment_), <span lang="grc">νη-πενθής</span> _freeing from pain and sorrow_ (<span lang="grc">πένθος</span>). In some cases <span lang="grc">νη-</span> may be derived from <span lang="grc">ν</span> (_not_) and the <span lang="grc">η</span> of the second part, as <span lang="grc">ν-ῆστις</span> _not eating_ (poetic <span lang="grc">ἔδ-ω</span>, cp. [887](#s887)).

\6. <span lang="grc">**ἀρι-**, **ἐρι-**</span> (poetic) with intensive force (cp. <span lang="grc">ἄρι-στος</span> _best_), <span lang="grc">ἀρι-πρεπής</span> _very distinguished_ (<span lang="grc">πρέπω</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐρί-τῑμος</span> _precious_.

\7. <span lang="grc"> **ἀγα-** </span> (poetic) intensive (cp. <span lang="grc">ἄγαν</span> _very_): <span lang="grc">ἀγά-στονος</span> _loud wailing_ (<span lang="grc">στένω</span> _groan_).

\8. <span lang="grc">**ζα-**, **δα-**</span> (poetic) intensive (for <span lang="grc">δ\[ιγλιδε\]α ῀ δια-</span> _very_, [116](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s116)): <span lang="grc">ζα-μενής</span> _very courageous_ (<span lang="grc">μένος</span> _courage_), <span lang="grc">δά-σκιος</span> _thick-shaded_ (<span lang="grc">σκιά̄</span>).

### LAST PART OF A COMPOUND

##### <a id="s886"></a> §886

**Compound Substantives and Adjectives**.—The last part of a noun-compound consists of a noun-stem or of a verb-stem with a noun-suffix.

##### <a id="s887"></a> §887

Nouns beginning with <span lang="grc">ᾰ, ε, ο</span> lengthen these vowels (<span lang="grc">ᾰ</span> and <span lang="grc">ε</span> to <span lang="grc">η, ο</span> to <span lang="grc">ω</span>) unless they are long by position. <span lang="grc">στρατ-ηγός</span> _army-leading_, _general_ (<span lang="grc">στρατός, ἄγω</span>), <span lang="grc">εὐ-ήνεμος</span> _with fair wind_ (<span lang="grc">εὖ</span> _well_, <span lang="grc">ἄνεμος</span>), <span lang="grc">ξεν-ηλασίᾱ</span> _driving out of foreigners_ (<span lang="grc">ξένος, ἐλαύνω</span>), <span lang="grc">ἀν-ώνυμος</span> _nameless_ (<span lang="grc">ἀν-, ὄνομα</span>), <span lang="grc">ἀν-ώμαλος</span> _uneven_ (<span lang="grc">ἀν-, ὁμαλός</span>).

**a.** Some compounds of <span lang="grc">ἄγω</span> _lead_ show <span lang="grc">ᾱ</span>: <span lang="grc">λοχ-ᾱγός</span> _captain_ (<span lang="grc">λόχος</span> _company_).

**b.** By analogy to the compound the simple form sometimes assumes a long vowel: <span lang="grc">ἠνεμόεσσα</span> _windy_. Cp. [28 D](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s28).

**c.** Lengthening rarely occurs when a preposition or <span lang="grc">πᾶς</span> precedes: <span lang="grc">συν-ωμοσίᾱ</span> _conspiracy_ (<span lang="grc">ὄμνῡμι</span> _swear_), <span lang="grc">παν-ήγυρις</span> _general assembly_ (<span lang="grc">ἄγυρις ῀ ἀγορά̄</span>).

**d.** The lengthening in 887 is properly the result of early contraction (<span lang="grc">στρατο ¨ αγος</span>). On the pattern of such contracted forms irrational lengthening occurs when the first part of the compound ends in a consonant, as <span lang="grc">δυσ-ηλεγής</span> (for <span lang="grc">δυσ-αλεγής</span>) _cruel_ from <span lang="grc">ἀλέγω</span> _care for_.

##### <a id="s888"></a> §888

A noun forming the last part of a compound often changes its final syllable.

N. Masculine or feminine nouns of the second or third declensions usually remain unaltered: <span lang="grc">ἔν-θεος</span> _inspired_, <span lang="grc">ἄ-παις</span> _childless_. <!-- page 251 -->

**a.** <span lang="grc">**-ος**, **-η**, **-ον**</span>: form compound adjectives from nouns of the first declension, neuters of the second declension, nouns of the third declension, and from many verb-stems. <span lang="grc">ἄ-τῑμος</span> _dishonoured_ (<span lang="grc">τῑμή</span>), <span lang="grc">σύν-δειπνος</span> _companion at table_ (<span lang="grc">δεῖπνο-ν</span> _meal_), <span lang="grc">ἄν-αιμος</span> _bloodless_ (<span lang="grc">αἷμα</span>, [875](#s875)), <span lang="grc">ἑκατόγ-χειρος</span> _hundred-handed_ (<span lang="grc">χείρ</span>), <span lang="grc">δασμοφόρος</span> _bringing tribute_ (<span lang="grc">φέρ-ω</span>), <span lang="grc">γεω-γράφος</span> _geographer_ 871 b. N. (<span lang="grc">γράφ-ω</span>), <span lang="grc">ἰχθυο-φάγος</span> _fish-eating_ (<span lang="grc">φαγεῖν</span> 529. 5).

**b. <span lang="grc">-ης</span>**, <span lang="grc"> **-ες** </span>: form compound adjectives from nouns of the first and third declensions, and from many verb-stems: <span lang="grc">ἀ-τυχής</span> _unfortunate_ (<span lang="grc">τύχη</span>), <span lang="grc">δεκα-ετής</span> _of ten years_ (<span lang="grc">ϝέτος</span>), <span lang="grc">εὐ-ειδής</span> _beautiful in form_ (<span lang="grc">εἶδος</span>), <span lang="grc">εὐ-μαθής</span> _quick at learning_ (<span lang="grc">μανθάνω, μαθ-</span>), <span lang="grc">ἀ-φανής</span> _invisible_ (<span lang="grc">φαίνω, φαν-</span>).

**c.** Other endings are <span lang="grc"> **-ης** </span> (gen. <span lang="grc">-ου</span>), <span lang="grc">**-της**, **-τηρ**</span>: <span lang="grc">γεω-μέτρης</span> _surveyor_ ([871 b](#s871). N.), <span lang="grc">νομο-θέτης</span> _law-giver_ (<span lang="grc">νόμος, τίθημι, θε-</span>), <span lang="grc">μηλο-βοτήρ</span> _shepherd_ (<span lang="grc">μῆλον, βό-σκω</span> _feed_).

**d.** Neuters in <span lang="grc"> **-μα** </span> make adjectives in <span lang="grc">-μων</span>: <span lang="grc">πρᾶγμα</span> _thing_, <span lang="grc">ἀ-πρά̄γμων</span> _inactive_. <span lang="grc">φρήν</span> _mind_ becomes <span lang="grc"> **-φρων** </span>: <span lang="grc">εὔ-φρων</span> _well-minded_, _cheerful_.—<span lang="grc">πατήρ</span> _father_ becomes <span lang="grc"> **-πάτωρ** </span>: <span lang="grc">ἀ-πάτωρ</span> _fatherless_, <span lang="grc">φιλο-πάτωρ</span> _loving his father_.

**e.** Compounds of <span lang="grc">γῆ</span> _land_ end in <span lang="grc">-γειος, -γεως</span>: <span lang="grc">κατά-γειος</span> _subterranean_, <span lang="grc">λεπτό-γεως</span> _of thin soil_.—Compounds of <span lang="grc">ναῦς</span> _ship_, <span lang="grc">κέρας</span> _horn_, <span lang="grc">γῆρας</span> _old age_ end in <span lang="grc">-ως</span>, as <span lang="grc">περί-νεως</span> _supercargo_, <span lang="grc">ὑψί-κερως</span> _lofty-antlered_ ([163 a](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s163)), <span lang="grc">ἀ-γήρως</span> _free from old age_.

##### <a id="s889"></a> §889

The last member of a compound is often a verbal element that is not used separately: <span lang="grc">ἀγαλματ-ο-ποιός</span> _statue-maker_, _sculptor_, <span lang="grc">ὑπ-ήκοος</span> _subject_ (<span lang="grc">ἀκούω</span> _hear_, <span lang="grc">ἀκήκοα</span>), <span lang="grc">λογο-γράφος</span> _speech-writer_. <span lang="grc">-φορος</span> _bringing_, <span lang="grc">-δομος</span> _building_, <span lang="grc">-δρομος</span> _running_ are used separately in the meanings _tribute_, _building_, _race_.

##### <a id="s890"></a> §890

An abstract word can enter into composition only by taking a derivative ending (usually <span lang="grc">-ιᾱ</span>) formed from a real or assumed compound adjective: <span lang="grc">ναῦ-ς</span> _ship_, <span lang="grc">μάχη</span> _fight_ = <span lang="grc">ναύ-μαχος</span>, whence <span lang="grc">ναυ-μαχίᾱ</span> _naval battle;_ <span lang="grc">εὖ</span> _well_, <span lang="grc">βουλή</span> _counsel_ = <span lang="grc">εὔ-βουλος</span>, whence <span lang="grc">εὐ-βουλίᾱ</span> _good counsel;_ <span lang="grc">ἀν-</span>neg., <span lang="grc">ἀρχή</span> _rule_ = <span lang="grc">ἄν-αρχος</span>, whence <span lang="grc">ἀν-αρχίᾱ</span> _anarchy;_ <span lang="grc">εὖ</span> _well_, <span lang="grc">πρᾶξις</span> _doing_ = \*<span lang="grc">εὐπρᾱξος</span>, whence <span lang="grc">εὐ-πρᾱξίᾱ</span> _well-doing_. Contrast <span lang="grc">εὐ-βουλίᾱ</span> with <span lang="grc">προ-βουλή</span> _forethought_, <span lang="grc">εὐ-λογίᾱ</span> _eulogy_ with <span lang="grc">πρό-λογος</span> _prologue_.

**a.** Only after a preposition does an abstract word remain unchanged: <span lang="grc">προβουλή</span> _forethought_. Exceptions are rare: <span lang="grc">μισθο-φορά̄</span> _receipt of wages_ (<span lang="grc">μισθός, φορά̄</span>).

##### <a id="s891"></a> §891

**Compound Verbs**.—Verbs can be compounded _directly_ only by prefixing a preposition, as <span lang="grc">συμ-μάχομαι</span> _fight along with_.

**a.** A preposition (<span lang="grc">πρό-θεσις</span>) derived its name from this use as a prefix. Originally all prepositions were adverbs modifying the verb, and in Homer are partly so used. See [1638](../part-4-syntax/19-prepositions.md#s1638), [1639](../part-4-syntax/19-prepositions.md#s1639). Cp. _upheave_ and _heave up_.

##### <a id="s892"></a> §892

All compound verbs not compounded with prepositions are denominatives (ending in <span lang="grc">-εω</span>) and formed from real or assumed compound nouns. From <span lang="grc">ναῦς</span> _ship_ and <span lang="grc">μάχη</span> _fight_ comes <span lang="grc">ναύμαχος</span> _fighting in ships_, whence <span lang="grc">ναυμαχέω</span> _fight in ships;_ so <span lang="grc">οἰκοδομέω</span> _build a house_ from <span lang="grc">οἰκο-δόμος</span> _house-builder_ (<span lang="grc">οἶκος, δέμω</span>). Contrast <span lang="grc">ἀνα-πείθω</span> _bring over, convince_ with <span lang="grc">ἀ-πιστέω</span> _disbelieve_ (<span lang="grc">ἄ-πιστος</span>); <span lang="grc">ἀντι-λέγω</span> _speak against_ with <span lang="grc">ὁμο-λογέω</span> _agree_ (<span lang="grc">ὁμόλογος</span> _agreeing_).—<span lang="grc">εὖ ἀγγέλλω</span> _announce good news_ cannot form a verb <span lang="grc">εὐαγγελλω</span>.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ἀτῑμάω</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀτίω</span>) _dishonour_, <span lang="grc">δακρυχέω</span> _shed tears_ are exceptions. <span lang="grc">ἀν-ομοιόω</span> _make unlike_ is not from <span lang="grc">ἀν-</span> and <span lang="grc">ὁμοιόω</span> but from <span lang="grc">ἀν-όμοιος</span> _unlike_.

### ACCENT OF COMPOUNDS

<!-- page 252 -->

##### <a id="s893"></a> §893

Compounds generally have recessive accent, as <span lang="grc">φιλό-τῑμος</span> loving-&lt;\*&gt; (<span lang="grc">τῑμή</span>). But there are many exceptions, e.g.—

**a.** P&lt;\*&gt;tives in <span lang="grc">-ά̄, -ή, -ής, -εύς, -μός</span>, and <span lang="grc">-έος</span> usually keep their accent when compoun&lt;\*&gt;; except dissyllabic words in <span lang="grc">-ά̄, -ή, -ής</span> whose first part is not a preposition. Thus, <span lang="grc">κριτής</span> _judge_, <span lang="grc">ὑποκριτής</span> _actor_, <span lang="grc">ὀνειροκρίτης</span> _interpreter of dreams_.

**b.** Compound adjectives in <span lang="grc">-ης, -ες</span> are usually oxytone: <span lang="grc">εὐ-γενής</span> _well-born_.

##### <a id="s894"></a> §894

Compounds in <span lang="grc">-ος</span> (not <span lang="grc">-τος</span> or <span lang="grc">-κος</span>) formed by the union of a noun or adverb and the stem of a transitive verb are:

**a.** &lt;\*&gt; when they have a long penult and an active meaning: <span lang="grc">στρατ-ηγός</span> general.

**b.** &lt;\*&gt; when they have short penult and an active meaning: <span lang="grc">πατροκτ&lt;\*&gt;</span> &lt;\*&gt;icide, <span lang="grc">λιθο-βόλος</span> _throwing-stones_, <span lang="grc">λαιμο-τόμος</span> _throat-cutting_, <span lang="grc">ὑδροφο&lt;\*&gt;</span> _&lt;\*&gt;ter-carrier_.

**c.** propar&lt;\*&gt;, when they have a short penult and passive meaning: <span lang="grc">πατρόκτο&lt;\*&gt;</span> _&lt;\*&gt;ain by a father_, <span lang="grc">λιθό-βολος</span> _pelted with stones_, <span lang="grc">λαιμό-τομος</span> _with thr&lt;\*&gt; out_, <span lang="grc">αὐτό-γραφος</span> _written with one's own hand_.

N.—A&lt;\*&gt; compounds of <span lang="grc">-οχος</span> (<span lang="grc">ἔχ-ω</span>, [878](#s878)), <span lang="grc">-αρχος</span> (<span lang="grc">ἄρχ-ω</span>), <span lang="grc">-σῡλος</span> (<span lang="grc">σῡλά-ω</span> rob&lt;\*&gt; <span lang="grc">-πο&lt;\*&gt;</span> (<span lang="grc">πέρθ-ω</span> _destroy_) are proparoxytone; <span lang="grc">ἡνί-οχος</span> (_rein-holder_) _charioteer_, <span lang="grc">&lt;\*&gt;</span> _commander of horses_, <span lang="grc">ἱερό-σῡλος</span> _temple-robber_, <span lang="grc">πτολί-πορθος</span> _sacking cities_. <span lang="grc">&lt;\*&gt;</span> _staff-bearer_ (<span lang="grc">ῥαβδός</span>) is contracted from <span lang="grc">ῥαβδό-οχος</span>.

### MEANING OF COMPOUNDS

##### <a id="s895"></a> §895

Compound nouns (substantives and adjectives) are divided, according to their meaning, into three main classes: _determinative_, _possessive_, and _prepositional-phrase_, compounds.

**a.** The &lt;\*&gt;ical relation of the parts of compounds varies so greatly that boundary-lines between the different classes are difficult to set up, and a complete formal division is impossible. The poets show a much wider range of usage than the pro&lt;\*&gt;-writers.

##### <a id="s896"></a> §896

**Determinative Compounds**.—In most determinative compounds the first part modifies or _determines_ the second part: the modifier stands first, the principal word second.

Thus by _hand-work_ a particular kind of work is meant, as contrasted with _machine-work;_ cp. _speech-writer_ and _letter-writer_, _race-horse_ and _horse-race_.

**a.** The first part may be an adjective, an adverb, a preposition, an inseparable prefix, or, in a few cases, a substantive.

##### <a id="s897"></a> §897

There are two kinds of determinative compounds.

(1) **Descriptive determinative compounds**.—The first part defines or explains the second part in the sense of an adjective or adverb. (This class is less numerous than the second class.)

<span lang="grc">ἀκρό-πολις</span> _upper city, citadel_ (<span lang="grc">ἄκρᾱ πόλις</span>), <span lang="grc">ὁμό-δουλος</span> _fellow-slave_ (<span lang="grc">ὁμοῦ δουλεύων</span>, cp. [885](#s885). 4 a), <span lang="grc">ὀψί-γονος</span> _late-born_ (<span lang="grc">ὀψὲ γενόμενος</span>), <span lang="grc">προ-βουλή</span> _forethought_, <!-- page 253 --> <span lang="grc">ἀμφι-θέᾱτρον</span> _amphitheatre_ (a place-for-seeing round about), <span lang="grc">ἄ-γραφος</span> _not written_ (<span lang="grc">οὐ γεγραμμένος</span>).

**a.** _Copulative compounds_ are formed by the coördination of two substantives or adjectives: <span lang="grc">ἰ̄ᾱτρό-μαντις</span> _physician and seer_, <span lang="grc">γλυκύ-πικρος</span> _sweetly-bitter_. Similar is _deaf-mute_. So also in numerals: <span lang="grc">δώ-δεκα</span> _two_ (_and_) _ten_ = 12.

**b.** _Comparative compounds_ (generally poetic) are <span lang="grc">μελι-ηδής</span> _honey-sweet_ (<span lang="grc">μέλι, ἡδύς</span>), <span lang="grc">ποδ-ήνεμος Ἶρις</span> _Iris_, _with feet swift as the wind_. Cp. _eagle-eyed_, _goldfish_, _blockhead_. Such compounds are often possessive ([898](#s898)), as <span lang="grc">ῥοδο-δάκτυλος</span> _rosy-fingered_, <span lang="grc">χρῡσο-κόμης</span> _golden-haired_.

(2) **Dependent determinative compounds**.—A substantive forming either the first or the second part stands in the sense of an oblique case (with or without a preposition) to the other part.

Accusative: <span lang="grc">λογο-γράφος</span> _speech-writer_ (<span lang="grc">λόγους γράφων</span>), <span lang="grc">στρατ-ηγός</span> _armyleading_, _general_ (<span lang="grc">στρατὸν ἄγων</span>), <span lang="grc">φιλ-άνθρωπος</span> _loving mankind_ (<span lang="grc">φιλῶν ἀνθρώπους</span>), <span lang="grc">δεισι-δαίμων</span> _superstitious_ (<span lang="grc">δεδιὼς τοὺς δαίμονας</span>); cp. _pickpocket_, _sightseer_, _painstaking_, _soothsayer_, _laughter-loving_.

Genitive: <span lang="grc">στρατό-πεδον</span> _camp_ (<span lang="grc">στρατοῦ πέδον</span> _ground on which an army is encamped_). In <span lang="grc">ἀξιό-λογος</span> _worthy of mention_ (<span lang="grc">ἄξιος λόγου</span>) the defining part stands second ([869 c](#s869)) and is governed by the adjective part like a preposition (cp. [899](#s899)). Cp. _ringmaster_, _law-offcer_, _jest-book_.

(Ablative): <span lang="grc">ἀνεμο-σκεπής</span> _sheltering from the wind;_ cp. _land-breeze_, _sea-breeze_.

Dative: <span lang="grc">ἰσό-θεος</span> _godlike_ (<span lang="grc">ἴσος θεῷ</span>); cp. _churchgoer_, _blood-thirsty_.

(Instrumental): <span lang="grc">χειρ-ο-ποίητος</span> _made by hand_ (<span lang="grc">χερσὶ ποιητός</span>), <span lang="grc">χρῡσό-δετος</span> _bound with gold_ (<span lang="grc">χρῡσῷ δετός</span>); cp. _thunder-struck_, _storm-swept_, _star-sown_.

(Locative): <span lang="grc">οἰκο-γενής</span> _born in the house_ (<span lang="grc">ἐν οἴκῳ γενόμενος</span>), <span lang="grc">ὁδοι-πόρος</span> _wayfarer_ ([879](#s879)); cp. _heart-sick_.

N. 1.—The Greeks did not think of any actual case relation as existing in these compounds, and the case relation that exists is purely logical. The same form may be analysed in different ways, as <span lang="grc">φιλάνθρωπος ῀ φιλῶν ἀνθρώπους</span> or = <span lang="grc">φίλος ἀνθρώπων</span>.

N. 2.—Such compounds may often be analysed by a preposition and a dependent noun: <span lang="grc">θεό-δμητος</span> _god-built_ (<span lang="grc">ὑπὸ τῶν θεῶν δμητός</span>).

##### <a id="s898"></a> §898

**Possessive Compounds**.—In possessive compounds the first part defines the second as in determinatives; but the whole compound is an adjective expressing a quality, with the idea of possession understood. In most possessive compounds the idea of _having_ (<span lang="grc">ἔχων</span>) is to be supplied.

So, in English, _redbreast_ is a bird _having_ a red breast, the first part being an attribute of the second.

<span lang="grc">ἀργυρό-τοξος</span> _having a silver bow;_ <span lang="grc">μακρό-χειρ</span> _having long arms, long-armed;_ <span lang="grc">θεο-ειδής</span> _having the appearance_ (<span lang="grc">εἶδος</span>) _of a god, godlike;_ <span lang="grc">σώ-φρων</span> _having sound mind_, _temperate;_ <span lang="grc">τέθρ-ιππος</span> _having four horses;_ <span lang="grc">ὁμό-τροπος</span> _of like character_ (<span lang="grc">ὁμο-</span>occurs only in compounds, but note <span lang="grc">ὅμοιος</span> _like_); <span lang="grc">πολυ-κέφαλος</span> _many-headed;_ <span lang="grc">εὐ-τυχής</span> _having good fortune, fortunate;_ <span lang="grc">δεκα-ετής</span> _lasting ten years_ (cp. _a twoyear-old_); <span lang="grc">ἀμφι-κί̄ων</span> _having pillars round about;_ <span lang="grc">ἔν-θεος</span> _inspired_ (_having a god within:_ <span lang="grc">ἐν ἑαυτῷ θεὸν ἔχων</span>). <!-- page 254 -->

**a.** Adjectives in <span lang="grc">-ειδής</span> from <span lang="grc">εἶδος</span> _form_ (<span lang="grc">ἀστερ-ο-ειδής</span> _star-like_, <span lang="grc">ἰχθυ-ο-ειδής</span> _fish-like_, <span lang="grc">μην-ο-ειδής</span> _crescent_, <span lang="grc">πολυ-ειδής</span> _of many kinds_, <span lang="grc">σφαιρ-ο-ειδής</span> _spherical_) are to be distinguished from those in <span lang="grc">-ώδης</span> derived from <span lang="grc">ὄζω</span> _smell_ ([833 a](01-word-formation-introductory.md#s833)).

**b.** English possessive compounds in _-ed_ apply that ending only to the compound as a whole and not to either member. In Milton: _deep-throated_, _whitehanded_, _open-hearted;_ in Keats: _subtle-cadenced_. Besides those in _-ed_ there are others such as _Bluebeard_.

**c.** Many possessive compounds begin with <span lang="grc">α᾽</span>(<span lang="grc">ν</span>)-negative or <span lang="grc">δυσ-</span> _ill;_ as <span lang="grc">ἄ-παις</span> _childless_ (_having no children_ or _not having children_, <span lang="grc">παῖδας οὐκ ἔχων</span>), <span lang="grc">ἄ-τῑμος</span> _dishonoured_ (_having no honour_), <span lang="grc">δύσ-βουλος</span> _ill advised_ (_having evil counsels_).

##### <a id="s899"></a> §899

**Prepositional-phrase Compounds**.—Many phrases made of a preposition and its object unite to form a compound and take on adjectival inflection. Such compounds are equivalent to the phrases in question with the idea of _being_ or the like added.

<span lang="grc">ἄπ-οικος</span> _colonist_ (_away from home:_ <span lang="grc">ἀπ᾽ οἴκου</span>); <span lang="grc">ἐγχειρίδιος</span> _in the hand, dagger_ (<span lang="grc">ἐν χειρί</span>); <span lang="grc">ἐγχώριος</span> _native_ (_in the country:_ <span lang="grc">ἐν χώρᾳ</span>); <span lang="grc">ἐπιθαλάττιος</span> _dwelling on the coast_ (<span lang="grc">ἐπὶ θαλάττῃ</span>); <span lang="grc">ἐφέστιος</span> _on the hearth_ (<span lang="grc">ἐφ᾽ ἑστίᾳ</span>); <span lang="grc">κατάγειος</span> _underground_, cp. _subterranean_ (<span lang="grc">κατὰ γῆς</span>); <span lang="grc">παρά-δοξος</span> _contrary to opinion_ (<span lang="grc">παρὰ δόξαν</span>); <span lang="grc">παρά-φρων</span> _out of one's mind_, Lat. _de-mens_ (<span lang="grc">παρὰ τὴν φρένα</span>); <span lang="grc">ὑπ-εύθῡνος</span> _under liability to give account_ (<span lang="grc">ὑπ᾽ εὐθύ̄ναις</span>); so <span lang="grc">φροῦδος</span> _gone_ (= <span lang="grc">πρὸ ὁδοῦ γενόμενος</span>, cp. [124](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s124) a).

**a.** From such phrases are derived verbs and substantives: <span lang="grc">ἐγχειρίζω</span> _put into one's hands, entrust_, <span lang="grc">διαχειρίζω</span> _have in hand, manage_ (<span lang="grc">διὰ χειρῶν</span>), <span lang="grc">διαπᾱσῶν</span> _octave-scale_ (<span lang="grc">ἡ διὰ πᾱσῶν χορδῶν συμφωνίᾱ</span> _the concord through all the notes_).
