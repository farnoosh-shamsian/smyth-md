# DEFINITIONS

##### <a id="s900"></a> §900

A sentence expresses a thought. Syntax (<span lang="grc">σύνταξις</span> _arranging together_) shows how the different parts of speech and their different inflectional forms are employed to form sentences.

##### <a id="s901"></a> §901

Sentences are either _complete_ or _incomplete_ ([904](#s904)).

##### <a id="s902"></a> §902

Every complete sentence must contain two members:

\1. The Subject: the person or thing about which something is said.

\2. The Predicate: what is said about the subject.

Thus, <span lang="grc">τὸ θέρος</span> (subj.) <span lang="grc">ἐτελεύτᾱ</span> (pred.) _the summer_ | _came to an end_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.102">T. 3.102</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἦλθε</span> (pred.) <span lang="grc">κῆρυξ</span> (subj.) _a herald_ | _came_ 3. 113.

##### <a id="s903"></a> §903

Complete sentences are _simple_, _compound_, or _complex_. In the _simple_ sentence subject and predicate occur only once. A _compound_ sentence ([2162](26-syntax-of-the-compound-sentence.md#s2162)) consists of two or more simple sentences coördinated: <span lang="grc">τῇ δ᾽ ὑστεραίᾳ ἐπορεύοντο διὰ τοῦ πεδίου, καὶ Τισσαφέρνης εἵπετο</span> _but on the next day they marched through the plain and Tissaphernes kept following them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.4.18">X. A. 3.4.18</cite>. A _complex_ sentence ([2173](27-syntax-of-the-complex-sentence.md#s2173)) consists of a main sentence and one or more subordinate sentences: <span lang="grc">ὁπότε δέοι γέφῡραν διαβαίνειν, ἔσπευδεν ἕκαστος</span> _whenever it was necessary to cross a bridge_, _every one made haste_ 3. 4. 20.

##### <a id="s904"></a> §904

Incomplete sentences consist of a single member only. Such sentences stand outside the structure of the sentence. The chief classes of incomplete sentences are

**a.** Interjections, such as <span lang="grc">ὦ, φεῦ, αἰαῖ, οἴμοι</span>.

**b.** Asseverations which serve as a predicate to a sentence spoken by another: <span lang="grc">ναί</span> _yes_, _surely_, <span lang="grc">οὔ</span> _no_, <span lang="grc">μάλιστα</span> _certainly_, <span lang="grc">καλῶς</span> _very well!_

**c.** Headings, titles: <span lang="grc">Κύ̄ρου Ἀνάβασις</span> _the Expedition of Cyrus_, <span lang="grc">Ἀντιγόνη</span> _the Antigone_, <span lang="grc">συμμαχίᾱ Ἀθηναίων καὶ Θετταλῶν</span> _the Alliance of the Athenians and Thessalians_ C.I.A. /lref&gt;b.

**d.** Vocatives ([1283](18-the-cases/02-vocative.md#s1283)), and nominatives used in exclamation ([1288](18-the-cases/02-vocative.md#s1288)).

**e.** Exclamations without a verb: <span lang="grc">δεῦρο</span> _hither!_

N.—Examples of such incomplete sentences in English are _oh_, _assuredly_, _no wonder, right about face, away_, _fire!_ <!-- page 256 -->

##### <a id="s905"></a> §905

True impersonal verbs ([932](07-omission-of-the-subject.md#s932)) have a grammatical subject in the personal ending; but the real subject is properly an idea more or less vague that is present to the mind of the speaker. Similar in nature are infinitives used in commands ([2013](21-verbal-nouns.md#s2013)).
