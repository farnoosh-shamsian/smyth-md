# ADVERBIAL COMPLEX SENTENCES (2193-2487): CAUSAL CLAUSES

##### <a id="s2240"></a> §2240

Causal clauses are introduced by <span lang="grc">ὅτι, διότι, διόπερ</span> _because_, <span lang="grc">ἐπεί, ἐπειδή, ὅτε, ὁπότε</span> _since_, <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> _as_, _since_, _because_. The negative is <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>.

**a.** Also by poetic <span lang="grc"> **οὕνεκα** </span> ( = <span lang="grc">οὗ ἕνεκα</span>) and <span lang="grc"> **ὁθούνεκα** </span> ( = <span lang="grc">ὅτου ἕνεκα</span>) _because_, <span lang="grc"> **εὖτε** </span> _since_ (poetic and Ionic; also temporal), and by <span lang="grc"> **ὅπου** </span> _since_ (<cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.68">Hdt. 1.68</cite>, <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.4.31">X. C. 8.4.31</cite>, <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.186">I. 4.186</cite>). Homer has <span lang="grc"> **ὅ** </span> or <span lang="grc"> **ὅ τε** </span> _because_.

**b. <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>** frequently denotes a reason imagined to be true by the principal subject and treated by him as a fact ([2241](#s2241)). <span lang="grc"> **ὅτι** </span> often follows <span lang="grc">διὰ τοῦτο, διὰ τόδε, ἐκ τούτου, τούτῳ. **διότι**</span> stands for <span lang="grc">διὰ τοῦτο, ὅτι. **ὅτε**</span> and <span lang="grc"> **ὁπότε** </span> usually mean _when_ (cp. _cum_); as causal conjunctions they are rare, as <span lang="grc">ὅτε τοίνυν τοῦθ᾽</span> <!-- page 504 --> <span lang="grc">οὕτως ἔχει</span> _since then this is the case_, <cite data-ref="Dem. 1.1">D. 1.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">χαλεπὰ . . . τὰ παρόντα ὁπότ᾽ ἀνδρῶν στρατηγῶν τοιούτων στερόμεθα</span> _the present state of affairs is difficult since we are deprived of such generals_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.2">X. A. 3.2.2</cite>. Causal <span lang="grc">ὅτε</span>, temporal <span lang="grc">ὅτε</span> rarely, can begin a sentence. When they approach the meaning _if_, <span lang="grc">ὅτε</span> and <span lang="grc">ὁπότε</span> take <span lang="grc">μή</span>. In Attic prose inscriptions <span lang="grc">ἐπεί</span> is rare, <span lang="grc">διότι</span> does not occur, and <span lang="grc">ὧν ἕνεκα</span> is generally used for <span lang="grc">διόπερ</span>.

##### <a id="s2241"></a> §2241

Causal clauses denoting a fact regularly take the indicative after primary and secondary tenses.

<span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ δὲ ὑ̄μεῖς οὐ βούλεσθε συμπορεύεσθαι, ἀνάγκη δή μοι ἢ ὑ̄μᾶς προδόντα τῇ Κύ̄ρου φιλίᾳ χρῆσθαι κτλ</span>. _but since you do not wish to continue the march with me_, _I must either retain the friendship of Cyrus by renouncing you_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.5">X. A. 1.3.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὃ δ᾽ ἐζήλωσας ἡμᾶς ὡς τοὺς μὲν φίλους . . . εὖ ποιεῖν δυνάμεθα . . ., οὐδὲ ταῦθ᾽ οὕτως ἔχει</span> _but as to that which has excited your envy of us, our supposed ability_ (lit. _because_, _as you think, we are able_) _to benefit our friends, not even is this so_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hiero 6.12">X. Hi. 6.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐτύγχανε γὰρ ἐφ᾽ ἁμάξης πορευόμενος διότι ἐτέτρωτο</span> _for he happened to be riding on a wagon from the fact that he had been wounded_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.2.14">X. A. 2.2.14</cite>.

##### <a id="s2242"></a> §2242

But causal clauses denoting an alleged or reported reason (implied indirect discourse, [2622](../31-dependent-substantive-clauses-2574-2635.md#s2622)) take the optative after secondary tenses.

(<span lang="grc">οἱ Ἀθηναῖοι</span>) <span lang="grc">τὸν Περικλέᾱ ἐκάκιζον ὅτι στρατηγὸς ὢν οὐκ ἐπεξάγοι</span> _the Athenians reviled Pericles on the ground that, though he was general, he did not lead them out_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.21">T. 2.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἶχε λέγειν . . . ὡς Λακεδαιμόνιοι διὰ τοῦτο πολεμήσειαν αὐτοῖς ὅτι οὐκ ἐθελήσαιεν μετ᾽ Ἀ̄γησιλά̄ου ἐλθεῖν ἐπ᾽ αὐτόν</span> Pelopidas _was able to say that the Lacedaemonians had made war upon them_ (the Thebans) _for the reason that they had not been willing to march against him_ (the King of Persia) _with Agesilaus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 7.1.34">X. H. 7.1.34</cite>.

##### <a id="s2243"></a> §2243

Cause may be expressed also by the unreal indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or the potential optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>.

<span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ διά γ᾽ ὑ̄μᾶς αὐτοὺς πάλαι ἂν ἀπολώλειτε</span> _since you would long ago have perished had it depended on yourselves_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.49">D. 18.49</cite>, <span lang="grc">δέομαι οὖν σου παραμεῖναι ἡμῖν· ὡς ἐγὼ οὐδ᾽ ἂν ἑνὸς ἥδῑον ἀκούσαιμι ἢ σοῦ</span> _accordingly I beg you to stay with us; because there is no one_ (_in my opinion_) _to whom I should more gladly listen than to you_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 335d">P. Pr. 335d</cite>.

##### <a id="s2244"></a> §2244

<span lang="grc">ἐπεί</span> may introduce a coördinate _command_ (imperative <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 352">S. El. 352</cite>, potential optative, <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 474b">P. G. 474b</cite>), _wish_ (<cite data-ref="Soph. OT 661">S. O. T. 661</cite>), or _question_ (<cite data-ref="Soph. OT 390">S. O. T. 390</cite>). Cp. the use of <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span>, 2275. Sometimes, with the indicative, <span lang="grc">ἐπεί</span> has the force of _although_ (<cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 187a">P. S. 187a</cite>).—A causal clause may have the value of <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> with a coördinate main clause. So often in tragedy with <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> in _answers_ (<cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 39">S. Aj. 39</cite>; cp. <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.2.25">X. C. 4.2.25</cite>).—A clause with <span lang="grc">ὅτε</span>, apparently introducing a consequence, may give the reason for a preceding question (<cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 4.32"><span lang="grc">Δ</span> 32</cite>).

##### <a id="s2245"></a> §2245

Cause may also be expressed by a relative clause ([2555](../30-adjective-clauses-relative-clauses-2488-2573.md#s2555)), by a participle ([2064](../22-the-participle.md#s2064), [2085](../22-the-participle.md#s2085), [2086](../22-the-participle.md#s2086)), by <span lang="grc">τῷ</span> or <span lang="grc">διὰ τὸ</span> with the infinitive ([2033](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s2033), [2034](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s2034) b).

##### <a id="s2246"></a> §2246

<span lang="grc">εἰ</span> or <span lang="grc">εἴπερ</span>, when it expresses the real opinion of the writer or speaker, <!-- page 505 --> may have a causal force, as <span lang="grc">ἐγὼ . . . ἥδομαι μὲν ὑφ᾽ ὑ̄μῶν τῑμώμενος, εἴπερ ἄνθρωπός εἰμι</span> _I am pleased at being honoured by you, since_ (lit. _if indeed_) _I am a man_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.1.26">X. A. 6.1.26</cite>.

##### <a id="s2247"></a> §2247

Many verbs of emotion state the cause more delicately with <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span>) _if_ as a mere supposition than by <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span>. The negative is <span lang="grc">μή</span> or <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>.

**a.** So with <span lang="grc"> **ἀγανακτῶ** </span> _am indignant_, <span lang="grc"> **ἄγαμαι** </span> _am content_, <span lang="grc"> **αἰσχρόν ἐστι** </span> _it is a shame_, <span lang="grc"> **αἰσχύ̄νομαι** </span> _am ashamed_, <span lang="grc"> **ἄχθομαι** </span> _take hard_, <span lang="grc"> **δεινόν ἐστι** </span> _it is a shame_, <span lang="grc"> **δεινὸν ποιοῦμαι** </span> _am indignant_, <span lang="grc"> **θαυμάζω** </span> _am astonished_, <span lang="grc"> **μέμφομαι** </span> _blame_, <span lang="grc"> **φθονῶ** </span> _am jealous_, etc. The _if_ clause is usually indicative, sometimes an unreal indicative, a subjunctive, or a potential optative. Thus, <span lang="grc">θαυμάζω εἰ μὴ βοηθήσετε ὑ̄μῖν αὐτοῖς</span> _I am surprised if you will not help yourselves_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.3.53">X. H. 2.3.53</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀγανακτῶ εἰ οὑτωσὶ̄ ἃ νοῶ μὴ οἷός τ᾽ εἰμι εἰπεῖν</span> _I am grieved that I am thus unable to say what I mean_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lach. 194a">P. Lach. 194a</cite>, <span lang="grc">δεινὸν ποιούμενοι εἰ τοὺς ἐπιβουλεύοντας σφῶν τῷ πλήθει μὴ εἴσονται</span> _indignant that they could not discover those who were plotting against their commons_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.60">T. 6.60</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄτοπον ἂν εἴη, εἰ μηδὲν μὲν ἐμοῦ λέγοντος αὐτοὶ βοᾶτε τὴν ἐπωνυμίᾱν τῶν ἔργων . . . , ἐμοῦ δὲ λέγοντος ἐπιλέλησθε, καὶ μὴ γενομένης μὲν κρίσεως περὶ τοῦ πρά̄γματος ἥλω ἄν, γεγονότος δὲ ἐλέγχου ἀποφεύξεται</span> _it would be absurd if_, _when I say nothing, you shout out the name of what he has done, but when I do speak_, _you forget it; and_ absurd _if_, _while he should have been condemned when no investigation was instituted concerning the matter, he should yet get off now when the proof has been given_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 1.85">Aes. 1.85</cite> (cp. [2904](../36-particles/21-muepsilonnu.md#s2904) b), <span lang="grc">μὴ θαυμάζετε δ᾽ ἄ̄ν τι φαίνωμαι λέγων</span> _do not be surprised if I seem to say something_ I. Ep. 6. 7, <span lang="grc">τέρας λέγεις, εἰ . . . οὐκ ἂν δύναιντο λαθεῖν</span> _it is a marvel you are telling if they could be undetected_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Meno 91d">P. Men. 91d</cite>.

**b.** After a past tense we have either the form of direct discourse or the optative, as in indirect discourse. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐθαύμαζον εἴ τι ἕξει τις χρήσασθαι τῷ λόγῳ αὐτοῦ</span> _I kept wondering if any one could deal with his theory_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 95a">P. Ph. 95a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπεῖπεν . . . ὡς δεινὸν εἴη εἰ ὁ μὲν . . . Ξανθίᾱς ὑποκρῑνόμενος οὕτως . . . μεγαλόψῡχος γένοιτο</span> _he added that it was a shame if a man who played the rôle of Xanthias should prove himself so noble minded_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 2.157">Aes. 2.157</cite>, <span lang="grc">ᾤκτῑρον εἰ ἁλώσοιντο</span> _they pitied them in case they should be captured_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.7">X. A. 1.4.7</cite> (cp. [2622](../31-dependent-substantive-clauses-2574-2635.md#s2622) a). Sometimes the construction used after a primary tense is retained after a secondary tense (<cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.3.3">X. C. 4.3.3</cite>).

##### <a id="s2248"></a> §2248

These verbs admit also the construction with <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span>.

<span lang="grc">μὴ θαυμάζετε ὅτι χαλεπῶς φέρω</span> _do not be surprised that I take it hard_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.3">X. A. 1.3.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐθαύμαζον ὅτι Κῦρος οὔτε ἄλλον πέμπει . . . οὔτε αὐτὸς φαίνοιτο</span> (implied indirect discourse) _they were surprised that Cyrus neither sent some one else nor appeared himself_ 2. 1. 2, <span lang="grc">ἥκομεν ἀγαπῶντες ὅτι τὰ σώματα διεσωσάμεθα</span> _we have reached here, content that we have saved our lives_ 5. 5. 13. The construction with <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ τῷ</span> and the infinitive ([2033 b](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s2033)) also occurs: (<span lang="grc">Σωκράτης</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐθαυμάζετο ἐπὶ τῷ . . . εὐκόλως ζῆν</span> _Socrates was admired because he lived contentedly_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.8.2">X. M. 4.8.2</cite>.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> after verbs of emotion really means _that_, not _because_.
