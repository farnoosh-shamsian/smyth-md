# ADVERBIAL COMPLEX SENTENCES (2193-2487): RESULT CLAUSES (CONSECUTIVE CLAUSES)

<!-- page 506 -->

##### <a id="s2249"></a> §2249

A clause of result denotes a consequence of what is stated in the principal clause.

##### <a id="s2250"></a> §2250

Result clauses are introduced by the relative word <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> (rarely by <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>) _as_, _that_, _so that_. In the principal clause the demonstrative words <span lang="grc">οὕτως</span> _thus_, <span lang="grc">τοιοῦτος</span> _such_, <span lang="grc">τοσοῦτος</span> _so great_, are often expressed. <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> is from <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> and the connective <span lang="grc">τέ</span>, which has lost its meaning.

**a.** To a clause with <span lang="grc">οὕτως</span>, etc. Herodotus sometimes adds a clause either with <span lang="grc">τέ</span> or without a connective, where Attic would employ <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span>; cp. [3](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s3). 12.

##### <a id="s2251"></a> §2251

There are two main forms of result clauses: <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> with the infinitive and <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> with a finite verb. With the infinitive, the negative is generally <span lang="grc">μή</span>; with a finite verb, <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>. On the use in indirect discourse and on irregularities, see [2759](../35-negative-sentences.md#s2759).

##### <a id="s2252"></a> §2252

Consecutive <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> occurs almost always with the infinitive (chiefly in Herodotus, Xenophon, Aeschylus, and Sophocles); with a finite verb occasionally in Herodotus and Xenophon. With the infinitive, the orators and Thucydides (except 7. [34](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s34)) have <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span>.

##### <a id="s2253"></a> §2253

Consecutive <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> (<span lang="grc">ὡς</span>) with a finite verb does not occur in Homer, who uses coördination instead (cp. <span lang="grc">δέ</span> in A [10](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s10)). Two cases of <span lang="grc">ὥς τε</span> occur with the infinitive (I 42; <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 6.21"><span lang="grc">ζ</span> 21</cite> may mean _and so_), where the infinitive might stand alone, since Homer uses the infinitive to denote an intended or possible result.

##### <a id="s2254"></a> §2254

A clause with <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> and the infinitive is merely added to the clause containing the main thought in order to explain it. The consequence is stated without any distinction of time and only with difference of stage of action.

**a.** Since the infinitive expresses merely the abstract verbal idea, its use with <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> (as with <span lang="grc">πρίν</span>) outside of indirect discourse cannot explicitly denote a _fact_. By its datival nature ([1969](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s1969)), the infinitive is simply a complement to, or explanation of, the governing word. <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> is one of the means to reinforce this explanatory office of the infinitive. The origin of its use is suggested by the comparison with <span lang="grc">ὅσος</span> _sufficient for_, <span lang="grc">οἷος</span> _capable of_ ([2003](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s2003)) and the infinitive, which was not _originally_ dependent on these words.

##### <a id="s2255"></a> §2255

A clause with <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> and a finite verb contains the main thought, and is often so loosely connected with the leading verb as to be practically independent and coördinate. <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> may thus be simply introductory and take any construction found in an independent sentence. The consequence expresses distinctions of time and stage of action.

##### <a id="s2256"></a> §2256

Result may also be expressed by relative clauses ([2556](../30-adjective-clauses-relative-clauses-2488-2573.md#s2556)).

## DIFFERENCE BETWEEN <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> WITH THE INDICATIVE AND <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> WITH THE INFINITIVE

<!-- page 507 -->

##### <a id="s2257"></a> §2257

A clause of result with <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> stating that something actually occurred _as a fact_ must be expressed by the indicative.

##### <a id="s2258"></a> §2258

A clause of result with <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> stating that something may occur in consequence of an _intention_, _tendency_, _capacity_, and in general in consequence of the _nature_ of an object or action, is regularly expressed by the infinitive. When a consequence is stated without affirming or denying its actual occurrence, the infinitive is in place. The infinitive _may_ therefore denote a fact, but does not explicitly state this to be the case; and is, in general, permissible in all cases where the attainment of the result is expected, natural, or possible, and its actual occurrence is not emphasized; as it is emphasized by the indicative.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> with the infinitive does not state a particular fact. The infinitive is preferred in clauses containing or implying a negative. <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> with the indicative is preferred after <span lang="grc">εἰς τοῦτο ἥκει</span> and like phrases when affirmative (cp. [2265](06-omegasigmatauepsilon-rarely-omegasigma-with-the-infinitive.md#s2265), [2266](06-omegasigmatauepsilon-rarely-omegasigma-with-the-infinitive.md#s2266), [2274](07-omegasigmatauepsilon-omegasigma-with-a-finite-verb.md#s2274)).

##### <a id="s2259"></a> §2259

This difference may be illustrated by examples.

<span lang="grc">ἔχω τριήρεις ὥστε ἑλεῖν τὸ ἐκείνων πλοῖον</span> _I have triremes_ (_so as_) _to catch their vessel_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.8">X. A. 1.4.8</cite> (<span lang="grc">ὥστε εἷλον</span> would mean _so that I caught_ with an essentially different meaning), <span lang="grc">πάντας οὕτω διατιθεὶς ὥστε αὐτῷ εἶναι φίλους</span> _treating all in such a manner that they should be his friends_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.5">X. A. 1.1.5</cite> (an intended result, [2267](06-omegasigmatauepsilon-rarely-omegasigma-with-the-infinitive.md#s2267)), <span lang="grc">οὕτω διάκειμαι ὑφ᾽ ὑ̄μῶν ὡς οὐδὲ δεῖπνον ἔχω ἐν τῇ ἐμαυτοῦ χώρᾳ</span> _I am treated by you in such a manner that I cannot even sup in my own country_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.1.33">X. H. 4.1.33</cite> (a fact), <span lang="grc">ὥστε πάροδον μὴ εἶναι παρὰ πύργον, ἀλλὰ δι᾽ αὐτῶν μέσων διῇσαν</span> _so that it was impossible to pass by the side of a tower, but_ the guards _went through the middle of them_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.21">T. 3.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">κραυγὴν πολλὴν ἐποίουν καλοῦντες ἀλλήλους ὥστε καὶ τοὺς πολεμίους ἀκούειν· ὥστε οἱ μὲν ἐγγύτατα τῶν πολεμίων καὶ ἔφυγον</span> _they made a loud noise by calling each other so that even the enemy could hear; consequently those of the enemy who were nearest actually fled_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.2.17">X. A. 2.2.17</cite>. Here the fact that some of the enemy fled is proof that they _actually_ heard the cries; but the Greek states merely that the noise was loud enough to be heard. Had the clause <span lang="grc">ὥστε . . . ἔφυγον</span> not been added, we could only have _inferred_ that the noise was heard.
