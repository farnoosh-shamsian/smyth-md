# ADVERBIAL COMPLEX SENTENCES (2193-2487): OBJECT CLAUSES

##### <a id="s2207"></a> §2207

Two types of object (substantival) clauses are closely connected in construction with final clauses.

\1. Object clauses after _verbs of effort_.

\2. Object clauses after _verbs of fearing_.

Both stand in apposition to a demonstrative expressed or implied.

<span lang="grc">οὐδένα δεῖ τοῦτο μηχανᾶσθαι, ὅπως ἀποφεύξεται πᾶν ποιῶν θάνατον</span> _no man ought to contrive_ (_this_) _how he shall escape death at any cost_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 39a">P. A. 39a</cite>, <span lang="grc">μηχανᾶσθαι ὅκως τὸ σῶμα . . . κομιεῖ</span> _to contrive how he might bring home the body_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 2.121">Hdt. 2.121</cite> <span lang="grc">γ, αὐτὸ τοῦτο φοβοῦμαι, μὴ . . . οὐ δυνηθῶ δηλῶσαι περὶ τῶν πρᾱγμάτων</span> _I am_ <!-- page 497 --> _afraid of this very thing, namely_, _that I may not be able to make the case plain_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 41.2">D. 41.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐφοβεῖτο . . . μὴ οὐ δύναιτο . . . ἐξελθεῖν</span> _he was afraid that he could not escape_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.12">X. A. 3.1.12</cite>.

##### <a id="s2208"></a> §2208

**Connection of Final with Object Clauses**.—(1) Final clauses proper denote a purpose to accomplish or avert a result, which purpose is set forth in a definite action. (2) Object clauses after _verbs of effort_ consider means to accomplish or avert a result; the action of the subordinate clause is the _object purposed_. Such clauses are _incomplete_ final clauses, because, though the purpose is expressed, the action taken to effect the purpose is not expressed. (3) Object clauses after _verbs of fearing_ deprecate an undesired result or express fear that a desired result may not be accomplished. According to the form of expression employed, the construction of these three kinds of clauses may differ in varying degree or be identical. Thus compare these usages of Attic prose:

(1) <span lang="grc">παρακαλεῖ ἰ̄ᾱτρὸν ὅπως μὴ ἀποθάνῃ</span> (common)

<span lang="grc">παρακαλεῖ ἰ̄ᾱτρὸν ὅπως μὴ ἀποθανεῖται</span> (occasionally)

<span lang="grc">παρακαλεῖ ἰ̄ᾱτρὸν μὴ ἀποθάνῃ</span> (rare)

_he summons a physician in order that he may not die_.

(2) <span lang="grc">ἐπιμελεῖται ὅπως μὴ ἀποθανεῖται</span> (common)

<span lang="grc">ἐπιμελεῖται ὅπως μὴ ἀποθάνῃ</span> (occasionally)

_he takes care that he shall not die_.

<span lang="grc">ὅρᾱ μὴ ἀποθάνῃς</span> (occasionally) _see to it that you do not die_.

(3) <span lang="grc">φοβεῖται μὴ ἀποθάνῃ</span> (common)

<span lang="grc">φοβεῖται ὅπως μὴ ἀποθάνῃ</span> (occasionally)

<span lang="grc">φοβεῖται ὅπως μὴ ἀποθανεῖται</span> (occasionally)

_he is afraid lest he die_.

## OBJECT CLAUSES AFTER VERBS OF EFFORT

##### <a id="s2209"></a> §2209

Object clauses after _verbs of effort_ are introduced by <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span>, rarely by <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> (Herodotus, Xenophon), scarcely ever by <span lang="grc">ἵνα</span>. The negative is <span lang="grc">μή</span>.

##### <a id="s2210"></a> §2210

_Verbs of effort_ include verbs denoting _to take care_ or _pains_, _to strive_.

<span lang="grc">**ἐπιμελοῦμαι**, **μέλει μοι**, **μελετῶ**, **φρουρῶ**, **πρόνοιαν ἔχω**, **βουλεύομαι**, **μηχανῶμαι**, **παρασκευάζομαι**, **προθῡμοῦμαι**, **πρά̄ττω**, **πάντα ποιῶ**</span> (<span lang="grc"> **ποιοῦμαι** </span>), <span lang="grc"> **σπουδάζω** </span>, etc.

**a.** The same construction follows certain verbs of will signifying _to ask, command_, _entreat_, _exhort_ , and _forbid_, and which commonly take the infinitive (<span lang="grc">**αἰτῶ**, **δέομαι**, **παραγγέλλω**, **ἱκετεύω**, **δια-**</span> or <span lang="grc">**παρακελεύομαι**, **ἀπαγορεύω**</span>, etc.).

**b.** Some verbs take, by analogy, but in negative clauses only, the construction either of _verbs of effort_ or of _verbs of fearing_. These verbs signify _to see to a thing:_ <span lang="grc">**ὁρῶ**, **σκοπῶ**</span> (<span lang="grc"> **-οῦμαι** </span>), <span lang="grc">**ἐσκεψάμην**, **σκεπτέον ἐστί**, **τηρῶ**</span>; _to be on one's guard:_ <span lang="grc">**εὐλαβοῦμαι**, **φροντίζω**, **φυλάττω**</span> (<span lang="grc"> **-ομαι** </span>). See [2220](#s2220). <!-- page 498 -->

These verbs may take <span lang="grc">μή</span> with the infinitive. <span lang="grc">εὐλαβοῦμαι</span> and <span lang="grc">φυλάττομαι</span> take the infinitive when they mean _to guard against doing something_.

##### <a id="s2211"></a> §2211

Object clauses after _verbs of effort_ take the future indicative with <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> after primary and secondary tenses (rarely the optative after secondary tenses, [2212](#s2212)).

<span lang="grc">ἐπιμελοῦμαι ὅπως ταῦτα ποιήσει</span> _I take care that he shall do this_.

<span lang="grc">ἐπιμελοῦμαι ὅπως μη` ταῦτα ποιήσει</span> _I take care that he shall not do this_.

<span lang="grc">ἐπεμελούμην ὅπως ταῦτα ποιήσει</span> (<span lang="grc">ποιήσοι</span>) _I took care that he should do this_.

<span lang="grc">ἐπεμελούμην ὅπως μὴ ταῦτα ποιήσει</span> (<span lang="grc">ποιήσοι</span>) _I took care that he should not do this_.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ἀνάγκη ἐστὶ μάχεσθαι, τοῦτο δεῖ παρασκευάσασθαι ὅπως ὡς κράτιστα μαχούμεθα</span> _if it is necessary to fight, we must prepare to fight bravely_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.6.10">X. A. 4.6.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔπρᾱσσον ὅπως τις βοήθεια ἥξει</span> _they were managing_ (this, that) _how some reinforcements should come_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.4">T. 3.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">σκοπεῖσθε τοῦτο, ὅπως μὴ λόγους ἐροῦσιν μόνον . . . ἀλλὰ καὶ ἔργον τι δεικνύειν ἕξουσιν</span> _see to this, that they not only make speeches but also are able to show some proof_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 2.12">D. 2.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">σκεπτέον μοι δοκεῖ εἶναι . . . ὅπως ὡς ἀσφαλέστατα ἄπιμεν</span> ([774](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s774)) <span lang="grc">καὶ ὅπως τὰ ἐπιτήδεια ἕξομεν</span> _it seems to me that we must consider how we shall depart in the greatest security and how we shall procure our provisions_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.11">X. A. 1.3.11</cite>. In <span lang="grc">δεῖ σε ὅπως δείξεις</span> _it is needful that thou prove_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 556">S. Aj. 556</cite> there is a confusion between <span lang="grc">δεῖ δεῖξαι</span> and the construction of 2213.

##### <a id="s2212"></a> §2212

After secondary tenses the future optative occasionally occurs.

<span lang="grc">ἐπεμέλετο ὅπως μήτε ἄσῑτοι μήτε ἄποτοί ποτε ἔσοιντο</span> _he took care that they should never be without food or drink_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.1.43">X. C. 8.1.43</cite>.

**a.** The future optative occurs especially in Xenophon, and represents a thought that was originally expressed by the future indicative. Here the indicative would present the thought vividly, i.e. as it was conceived in the mind of the subject.

##### <a id="s2213"></a> §2213

<span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> and <span lang="grc">ὅπως μή</span> with the future indicative may be used without any principal clause, to denote an urgent exhortation or a warning. Originally the <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> clause depended on <span lang="grc">σκόπει</span> (<span lang="grc">σκοπεῖτε</span>), <span lang="grc">ὅρᾱ</span> (<span lang="grc">ὁρᾶτε</span>) _see to it;_ but the ellipsis was gradually forgotten and the construction used independently.

<span lang="grc">ὅπως οὖν ἔσεσθε ἄνδρες ἄξιοι τῆς ἐλευθερίᾱς ἧς ἧς κέκτησθε</span> _be men worthy of the freedom which you possess_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.3">X. A. 1.7.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅπως δὲ τοῦτο μὴ διδάξεις μηδένα</span> _but don't tell anybody this_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 824">Ar. Nub. 824</cite>, and very often in Ar. This use is also preceded by <span lang="grc">ἄγε</span> (<cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 4">X. S. 4</cite>. [20](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s20)). The third person is very rare (<cite data-ref="Lys. 1.21">L. 1.21</cite>).

##### <a id="s2214"></a> §2214

_Verbs of effort_ sometimes have the construction of final clauses, and take, though less often, <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> with the present or second aorist subjunctive or optative (cp. [2196](02-purpose-clauses-final-clauses.md#s2196)). The subjunctive may be used after secondary tenses.

<span lang="grc">ἔπρᾱσσεν . . . ὅπως πόλεμος γένηται</span> _he tried to bring it about that war should_ <!-- page 499 --> _be occasioned_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.57">T. 1.57</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅρᾱ . . . ὅπως μὴ παρὰ δόξαν ὁμολογῇς</span> _see to it that it does not prove that you acquiesce in what you do not really think_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 49c">P. Cr. 49c</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ φυλάξεσθ᾽ ὅπως μὴ . . . δεσπότην εὕρητε</span>; _will you not be on your guard lest you find a master?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 6.25">D. 6.25</cite>. Future and subjunctive occur together in <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.6.10">X. A. 4.6.10</cite>. In Xenophon alone is the subjunctive (and optative) more common than the future.

**a.** The object desired by the subject of a _verb of effort_ is here expressed by the same construction as is the purpose in the mind of the subject of a final clause.

##### <a id="s2215"></a> §2215

<span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is sometimes added to <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> with the subjunctive to denote that the purpose is dependent on certain circumstances.

<span lang="grc">ὅπως ἂν . . . οἱ στρατιῶται περὶ τοῦ στρατεύεσθαι βουλεύωνται, τούτου πειρἁ̄σομαι ἐπεμέλεσθαι</span> _I will endeavour to make it my care that the soldiers deliberate about continuing the war_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.5.48">X. C. 5.5.48</cite>, <span lang="grc">μηχανητέον ὅπως ἂν διαφύγῃ</span> _plans must be made for his escape_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 481a">P. G. 481a</cite> (the same passage has <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> with the subjunctive and the future). In Attic this use occurs in Aristophanes, Xenophon, and Plato.

##### <a id="s2216"></a> §2216

<span lang="grc">ὡς</span> and <span lang="grc">ὡς ἄν</span> with subjunctive and optative and <span lang="grc">ὅπως ἄν</span> with the optative occur in Xenophon, <span lang="grc">ὡς ἄν</span> and <span lang="grc">ὅπως ἄν</span> with the optative being used after primary and secondary tenses. Hdt. has <span lang="grc">ὅκως ἄν</span> after secondary tenses. The optative with <span lang="grc">ὡς ἄν</span> and <span lang="grc">ὅπως ἄν</span> is potential.

##### <a id="s2217"></a> §2217

After verbs meaning _to consider, plan_ , and _try_ <span lang="grc">ὄπως</span> or <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> with the subjunctive (with or without <span lang="grc">κέ</span>) or optative is used by Homer, who does not employ the future indicative in object clauses denoting a purpose. Thus, <span lang="grc">φράζεσθαι . . . ὅππως κε μνηστῆρας . . . κτείνῃς</span> _consider how thou mayest slay the suitors_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 1.295"><span lang="grc">α</span> 295</cite>, <span lang="grc">πείρᾱ ὅπως κεν δὴ σὴν πατρίδα γαῖαν ἵκηαι</span> _try that thou mayest come to thy native land_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 4.545"><span lang="grc">δ</span> 545</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> with the future indicative would be the normal Attic usage.

##### <a id="s2218"></a> §2218

Verbs of will or desire signifying _to ask, command_, _entreat_, _exhort_ , and _forbid_, which usually have an infinitive as their object, may take <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅπως μή</span>) with the future indicative (or optative) or the subjunctive (or optative). The <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> clause states both the _command_, etc. and the purpose in giving it. Between _take care to do this_ and _I bid you take care to do this_ the connection is close. Cp. _impero_, _postulo_ with _ut_ (_ne_).

<span lang="grc">διακελεύονται ὅπως τῑμωρήσεται</span> _they urge him to take revenge_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 549e">P. R. 549e</cite>, <span lang="grc">δεήσεται δ᾽ ὑ̄μῶν ὅπως . . . δίκην μὴ δῷ</span> _he will entreat you that he may not suffer punishment_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 1.23">Ant. 1.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">παραγγέλλουσιν ὅπως ἂν</span> ([2215](#s2215)) <span lang="grc">τῇδε τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τελευτήσῃ</span> _they give orders_ (_to the end_) _that he die to-day_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 59e">P. Ph. 59e</cite>, <span lang="grc">Λακεδαιμονίων ἐδέοντο τὸ ψήφισμ᾽ ὅπως μεταστραφείη</span> _they begged the Lacedaemonians that the decree might be changed_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Ach. 536">Ar. Ach. 536</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπηγόρευες ὅπως μὴ τοῦτο ἀποκρινοίμην</span> _you forbade me to give this answer_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 339a">P. R. 339a</cite>.

##### <a id="s2219"></a> §2219

**Dawes' Canon**.—The rule formulated by Dawes and afterwards extended (that the _first_ aorist subjunctive active and middle after <span lang="grc">ὅπως, ὅπως μή</span>, and <span lang="grc">οὐ μή</span> is incorrect and should be emended) is applicable only in the case of _verbs of effort_. After these verbs the future is far more common than subjunctive or optative (except in Xenophon), and some scholars would emend the <!-- page 500 --> offending sigmatic subjunctives where they occur in the same sentence with second aorists (as <cite data-ref="Andoc. 3.14">And. 3.14</cite>) or even where the future has a widely different form (as <span lang="grc">ἐκπλευσεῖται</span>, subj. <span lang="grc">ἐκπλεύσῃ</span>, cp. <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.6.21">X. A. 5.6.21</cite>).

## VERBS OF CAUTION

##### <a id="s2220"></a> §2220

_Verbs of caution_ ([2210 b](#s2210), [2224](#s2224) a) have, in _negative_ clauses, the construction either of

**a.** _Verbs of effort_, and take <span lang="grc">ὅπως μή</span> with the future indicative:

<span lang="grc">εὐλαβούμενοι ὅπως μὴ . . . οἰχήσομαι</span> _taking care that I do not depart_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 91c">P. Ph. 91c</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅρᾱ ὅκως μή σευ ἀποστήσονται</span> _beware lest they revolt from thee_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 3.36">Hdt. 3.36</cite>.

**b.** _Verbs of fearing_, and take <span lang="grc">μή</span> (<span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span>) or <span lang="grc">ὅπως μή</span> ([2230](#s2230)) with the subjunctive (or optative):

<span lang="grc">ὁρᾶτε μὴ πάθωμεν</span> _take care lest we suffer_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.1.15">X. C. 4.1.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">φυλάττου ὅπως μὴ . . . εἰς τοὐναντίον ἔλθῃς</span> _be on your guard lest you come to the opposite_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.6.16">X. M. 3.6.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑποπτεύομεν . . . ὑ̄μᾶς μὴ οὐ κοινοὶ ἀποβῆτε</span> _we suspect that you will not prove impartial_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.53">T. 3.53</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑποπτεύσᾱς μὴ τὴν θυγατέρα λέγοι, ἤρετο κτλ</span>. _suspecting that he meant his daughter, he asked_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.2.9">X. C. 5.2.9</cite>. So with a past indicative ([2233](#s2233)).

## OBJECT CLAUSES WITH VERBS OF FEARING

##### <a id="s2221"></a> §2221

Object clauses after _verbs of fear_ and _caution_ are introduced by <span lang="grc">μή</span> _that_, _lest_ (Lat. _ne_), <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> _that . . . not_, _lest . . . not_ (Lat. _ut = ne non_).

**a.** <span lang="grc">μή</span> clauses denote a fear that something _may_ or _might_ happen; <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> clauses denote a fear that something _may not_ or _might not_ happen. Observe that the verb is negatived by <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> and not by <span lang="grc">μή</span>, which expresses an apprehension that the result will take place. <span lang="grc">μή</span> is sometimes, for convenience, translated by _whether;_ but it is not an indirect interrogative in such cases.

##### <a id="s2222"></a> §2222

The construction of <span lang="grc">μή</span> after _verbs of fearing_ has been developed from an earlier coördinate construction in which <span lang="grc">μή</span> was not a conjunction (_that_, _lest_) but a prohibitive particle. Thus, <span lang="grc">δείδω μή τι πάθῃσιν</span> (<cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 11.470"><span lang="grc">Λ</span> 470</cite>) _I fear lest he may suffer aught_ was developed from _I fear + may he not suffer aught_ ([1802](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1802)); <span lang="grc">φυλακὴ δέ τις . . . ἔστω, μὴ λόχος εἰσέλθῃσι πόλιν</span> (<cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 8.521"><span lang="grc">Θ</span> 521</cite>) _but let there be a guard_, _lest an ambush enter the city_, where the clause <span lang="grc">μὴ—εἰσέλθῃσι</span> meant originally _may an ambush not enter_. Here <span lang="grc">μή</span> expresses the desire to avert something (negative desire).

**a.** When <span lang="grc">μή</span> had become a pure conjunction of subordination, it was used even with the indicative and with the optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>. Some scholars regard <span lang="grc">μή</span> with the indicative as standing for <span lang="grc">ἆρα μή</span> (hence an indirect interrogative). Observe that the character of <span lang="grc">μή</span> after _verbs of fearing_ is different from that in final clauses, though the construction is the same in both cases.

##### <a id="s2223"></a> §2223

For the use of the subjunctive, without a verb of fearing, with <span lang="grc">μή</span>, see [1801](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1801), [1802](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1802); with <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> see [1801](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1801), with <span lang="grc">οὐ μή</span> see [1804](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1804).

##### <a id="s2224"></a> §2224

Verbs and expressions of _fear_ are: <span lang="grc">**φοβοῦμαι**, **δέδοικα**</span> or <span lang="grc">**δέδια**, **ταρβῶ**, **τρῶ**</span> and <span lang="grc"> **πέφρῑκα** </span> (mostly poetical); <span lang="grc">**δεινός εἰμι**, **δεινόν ἐστι**, **δέος ἐστί**, **φοβερός εἰμι**, **φοβερόν ἐστι**</span>, etc. <!-- page 501 -->

**a.** Sometimes it is not actual _fear_ that is expressed but only _apprehension_, _anxiety_, _suspicion_, etc. These are the verbs and expressions of _caution**:**_ <span lang="grc">**ὀκνῶ**, **ἀθῡμῶ**, **ἀπιστῶ**, **ἀπιστίᾱν ἔχω**</span> (<span lang="grc">παρέχω</span>), <span lang="grc">**ὑποπτεύω**, **ἐνθῡμοῦμαι**, **αἰσχύ̄νομαι**</span> (rare), <span lang="grc">**κίνδῡνός ἐστι**, **προσδοκίᾱ ἐστί**</span>. Here belong also, by analogy, <span lang="grc">**ὁρῶ**, **σκοπῶ**, **ἐννοῶ**, **εὐλαβοῦμαι**, **φροντίζω**, **φυλάττω**</span> (<span lang="grc">-ομαι</span>), which admit also the construction of _verbs of effort_ ([2210 b](#s2210)).

### I. FEAR RELATING TO THE FUTURE

##### <a id="s2225"></a> §2225

Object clauses after _verbs of fear_ and _caution_ take the subjunctive after primary tenses, the optative (or subjunctive, [2226](#s2226)) after secondary tenses.

<span lang="grc">φοβοῦμαι μὴ γένηται</span> _I fear it may happen_.

<span lang="grc">φοβοῦμαι μὴ οὐ γένηται</span> _I fear it may not happen_.

<span lang="grc">ἐφοβούμην μὴ γένξιτο</span> (or <span lang="grc">γένηται</span>) _I feared it might happen_.

<span lang="grc">ἐφοβούμην μὴ οὐ γένοιτο</span> (regularly <span lang="grc">γένηται</span>) _I feared it might not happen_.

<span lang="grc">δέδοικα μὴ . . . ἐπιλαθώμεθα τῆς οἴκαδε ὁδοῦ</span> _I am afraid lest we may forget the way home_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.25">X. A. 3.2.25</cite>, <span lang="grc">φοβεῖται μὴ . . . τὰ ἔσχατα πάθῃ</span> _he is afraid lest he suffer the severest punishment_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.22">X. C. 3.1.22</cite>, <span lang="grc">φροντίζω μὴ κράτιστον ᾖ μοι σῑγᾶν</span> _I am thinking that it may prove_ ([2228](#s2228)) _best for me to be silent_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.2.39">X. M. 4.2.39</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔδεισαν οἱ ῞ Ελληνες μὴ προσάγοιεν πρὸς τὸ κέρας καὶ . . . αὐτοὺς κατακόψειαν</span> _the Greeks were seized with fear lest they might advance against their flank and cut them down_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.10.9">X. A. 1.10.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">δέδιμεν μὴ οὐ βέβαιοι ἦτε</span> _we fear you are not to be depended on_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.57">T. 3.57</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ τοῦτο δέδοικα, μὴ οὐκ ἔχω ὅ τι δῶ ἑκάστῳ τῶν φίλων . . . ἀλλὰ μὴ οὐκ ἔχω ἱκανοὺς οἷς δῶ</span> _I am afraid not that I may not have enough_ (lit. _anything_) _to give to each of my friends, but that I may not have enough friends on whom to bestow my gifts_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.7">X. A. 1.7.7</cite>.

**a.** The aorist is very common after <span lang="grc">μή</span>. After secondary tenses Hom. usually has the optative.

**b.** <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> with the optative is rare and suspicious (<cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.5.3">X. A. 3.5.3</cite>).

##### <a id="s2226"></a> §2226

After secondary tenses, the subjunctive presents the fear vividly, i.e. as it was conceived by the subject. Cp. [2197](02-purpose-clauses-final-clauses.md#s2197).

<span lang="grc">ἐφοβοῦντο μή τι πάθῃ</span> _they feared lest she might_ (_may_) _meet with some accident_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 2">X. S. 2</cite>. 11, <span lang="grc">ἐφοβήθησαν μὴ καὶ ἐπὶ σφῖς ὁ στρατὸς χωρήσῃ</span> _they became fearful that the army might_ (_may_) _advance against themselves too_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.101">T. 2.101</cite>. So when the fear extends up to the present time: <span lang="grc">ἐφοβήθην . . . καὶ νῦν τεθορύβημαι μή τινες ὑ̄μῶν ἀγνοήσωσί με</span> _I was struck with fear and even now I am in a state of agitation lest some of you may disregard me_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 2.4">Aes. 2.4</cite>. The vivid use of subjunctive is common in the historians, especially Thucydides.

##### <a id="s2227"></a> §2227

The optative after a primary tense is rare and suspected (I 245, <cite data-ref="Hdt. 7.103">Hdt. 7.103</cite>, <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 279">S. Aj. 279</cite>).

##### <a id="s2228"></a> §2228

The subjunctive and optative after <span lang="grc">μή</span> (or <span lang="grc">ὅπως μή</span>) may denote what _may prove to be_ an object of fear (future ascertainment).

<span lang="grc">δέδοικα μὴ ἄριστον ᾖ</span> _I am afraid lest it prove to be best_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 1114">S. Ant. 1114</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔδεισαν μὴ λύττα τις . . . ἡμῖν ἐμπεπτώκοι</span> _they feared lest some madness might prove to_ <!-- page 502 --> _have fallen upon us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.7.26">X. A. 5.7.26</cite>. The aorist subjunctive refers to the past in <span lang="grc">δείδοικα . . . μή σε παρείπῃ</span> _I fear it may prove that she beguiled thee_ A 555; cp. K 99, <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 13.216"><span lang="grc">ν</span> 216</cite>, <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 24.491"><span lang="grc">ω</span> 491</cite> (after <span lang="grc">ὁρῶ</span>).

##### <a id="s2229"></a> §2229

The future is rare with _verbs of fearing_ after <span lang="grc">μή</span>.

<span lang="grc">φοβοῦμαι δὲ μή τινας ἡδονὰ̄ς ἡδοναῖς εὑρήσομεν ἐναντίᾱς</span> _and I apprehend that we shall find some pleasures opposite to_ other _pleasures_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phileb. 13a">P. Phil. 13a</cite>. So with _verbs of caution:_ <span lang="grc">ὅρᾱ μὴ πολλῶν ἑκάστῳ ἡμῶν χειρῶν δεήσει</span> _see to it lest each one of us may have need of many hands_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.1.18">X. C. 4.1.18</cite>.

**a.** The future optative seems not to occur except in <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.4.27">X. H. 6.4.27</cite>, <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.2.7">X. M. 1.2.7</cite>, <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyph. 15d">P. Euth. 15d</cite>.

##### <a id="s2230"></a> §2230

<span lang="grc">ὅπως μή</span> with the subjunctive or optative is sometimes used instead of <span lang="grc">μή</span> after _verbs of fear_ and _caution_ to imply fear that something _will_ happen.

<span lang="grc">οὐ φοβεῖ . . . ὅπως μὴ ἀνόσιον πρᾶγμα τυγχάνῃς πρά̄ττων</span>; _are you not afraid that you may chance to be doing an unholy deed?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyph. 4e">P. Euth. 4e</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡδέως γ᾽ ἄν</span> (<span lang="grc">θρέψαιμι τὸν ἄνδρα</span>), <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ φοβοίμην ὅπως μὴ ἐπ᾽ αὐτόν με τράποιτο</span> _I should gladly keep the man if I did not fear lest he might turn against me_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.9.3">X. M. 2.9.3</cite>; see also [2220 b](#s2220).

##### <a id="s2231"></a> §2231

<span lang="grc">ὅπως μή</span> with the future indicative (as after verbs of _effort_) is sometimes used instead of <span lang="grc">μή</span> with the subjunctive.

<span lang="grc">δέδοικα ὅπως μὴ . . . ἀνάγκη γενήσεται</span> (_v. l._ <span lang="grc">γένηται</span>) _I fear lest a necessity may arise_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.75">D. 9.75</cite>. The future optative occurs once (<cite data-ref="Isoc. 17.22">I. 17.22</cite>). On <span lang="grc">μή</span> or <span lang="grc">ὅπως μή</span> with _verbs of caution_, see [2220 a](#s2220).

##### <a id="s2232"></a> §2232

The potential optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is rarely used after <span lang="grc">μή</span>.

<span lang="grc">δεδιότες μὴ καταλυθείη ἂν</span> (Mss. <span lang="grc">καταλυθείησαν</span>) <span lang="grc">ὁ δῆμος</span> _fearful lest the people should be put down_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 13.51">L. 13.51</cite>. The potential use is most evident when an optative occurs in the protasis: <span lang="grc">εἰ δέ τινες φοβοῦνται μὴ ματαίᾱ ἂν γένοιτο αὕτη ἡ κατασκευή, εἰ πόλεμος ἐγερθείη, ἐννοησάτω ὅτι κτλ</span>. _if some are afraid that this condition of things may prove vain, if war should arise, let them_ (_him_) _consider that_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Ways 4.41">X. Vect. 4.41</cite>.

### II. FEAR RELATING TO THE PRESENT OR PAST

##### <a id="s2233"></a> §2233

Fear that something actually _is_ or _was_ is expressed by <span lang="grc">μή</span> with the indicative (negative <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span>).

<span lang="grc">δέδοικα . . . μὴ πληγῶν δέει</span> _I fear that you need a beating_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 493">Ar. Nub. 493</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ὅρᾱ μὴ παίζων ἔλεγεν</span> _but have a care that he was not speaking in jest_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 145b">P. Th. 145b</cite>, <span lang="grc">φοβούμεθα μὴ ἀμφοτέρων ἅμα ἡμαρτήκαμεν</span> _we are afraid that we have failed of both objects at once_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.53">T. 3.53</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁρᾶτε μὴ οὐκ ἐμοὶ . . . προσήκει λόγον δοῦναι</span> _have a care lest it does not rest with me to give an account_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.103">And. 1.103</cite>.

**a.** Contrast <span lang="grc">φοβοῦμαι μὴ ἀληθές ἐστιν</span> _I fear that it is true_ with <span lang="grc">φοβοῦμαι μὴ ἀληθὲς ᾖ</span> _I fear it may prove true_ ([2228](#s2228)).

**b.** The aorist occurs in Homer: <span lang="grc">δείδω μὴ δὴ πάντα θεὰ̄ νημερτέα εἶπεν</span> _I fear that all the goddess said was true_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 5.300"><span lang="grc">ε</span> 300</cite>.

### OTHER CONSTRUCTIONS WITH VERBS OF FEARING

<!-- page 503 -->

##### <a id="s2234"></a> §2234

**In Indirect Questions**.—Here the ideas of fear and doubt are joined. Thus, <span lang="grc">φόβος εἰ πείσω δέσποιναν ἐμήν</span> (direct <span lang="grc">πείσω</span>; [1916](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1916)) _I have my doubts whether I shall_ (_can_) _persuade my mistress_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 184">E. Med. 184</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὴν θεὸν δ᾽ ὅπως λάθω δέδοικα</span> (direct <span lang="grc">πῶς λάθω</span>; [1805](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1805)) _I am fearful how I shall escape the notice of the goddess_ <cite data-ref="Eur. IT 995">E. I. T. 995</cite>, <span lang="grc">δέδοικα ὅ τι ἀποκρινοῦμαι</span> _I am afraid what to answer_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 195c">P. Th. 195c</cite>.

##### <a id="s2235"></a> §2235

**In Indirect Discourse** with <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span>) _that_.—_Verbs of fearing_ may have the construction of _verbs of thinking_ and be followed by a dependent statement. This occurs regularly only when the expression of fear is negatived. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀνδρὸς δὲ τῇ θυγατρὶ μὴ φοβοῦ ὡς ἀπορήσεις</span> _do not fear that you will be at a loss for a husband for your daughter_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.2.12">X. C. 5.2.12</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">μή</span> or <span lang="grc">ὅπως μή</span> would be regular. With <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> the idea is _fear_, _thinking that_.

##### <a id="s2236"></a> §2236

**With <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> (<span lang="grc">ὡς</span>) Causal**.—<span lang="grc">ἐφοβεῖτο ὅτι ἀπὸ Διὸς . . . τὸ ὄναρ ἐδόκει αὐτῷ εἶναι</span> _he was afraid because the dream seemed to him to be from Zeus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.12">X. A. 3.1.12</cite>.

##### <a id="s2237"></a> §2237

**With a Causal Participle**.—<span lang="grc">οὔτε τὴν ἀκρόπολιν . . . προδιδοὺς ἐφοβήθη</span> _nor was he terrified at having betrayed the Acropolis_ Lyc. 17.

##### <a id="s2238"></a> §2238

**With the Infinitive**.—_Verbs of fearing_ often take an object infinitive (present, future or aorist) with or without the article; and with or without <span lang="grc">μή</span> ([2741](../35-negative-sentences.md#s2741)). Thus, <span lang="grc">φοβήσεται ἀδικεῖν</span> _he will be afraid to injure_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.7.15">X. C. 8.7.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ φοβούμεθα ἐλασσώσεσθαι</span> _we are not afraid that we shall be beaten_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.105">T. 5.105</cite> (the future infinitive is less common than <span lang="grc">μή</span> with the subjunctive), <span lang="grc">φυλαττόμενος τὸ λῡπῆσαί τινα</span> ( = <span lang="grc">μὴ λῡπήσω</span>) _taking care to offend no one_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.258">D. 18.258</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐφυλάξατο μὴ ἄπιστος γενέσθαι</span> _he took precautions not to become an object of distrust_ X. Ag. 8. 5.

**a.** With the articular infinitive, <span lang="grc">φοβοῦμαι</span>, etc. means simply _I fear;_ with the infinitive without the article, <span lang="grc">φοβοῦμαι</span> commonly has the force of _hesitate_, _feel repugnance_, etc. Cp. <span lang="grc">φοβοῦμαι ἀδικεῖν</span> and <span lang="grc">φοβοῦμαι μὴ ἀδικεῖν</span>; _I fear to do wrong_ (and do not do it); <span lang="grc">φοβοῦμαι τὸ ἀδικεῖν</span> _I fear wrong-doing_ (in general, by myself or by another), like <span lang="grc">φοβοῦμαι τὴν ἀδικίᾱν</span>.

##### <a id="s2239"></a> §2239

**With <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> of Result** (after a verb of _caution_).—<span lang="grc">ἢν οὖν ἔλθωμεν ἐπ᾽ αὐτοὺς πρὶν φυλάξασθαι ὥστε μὴ ληφθῆναι</span> _if then we move against them before they take precautions_ (_so as_) _not to be caught_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.3.35">X. A. 7.3.35</cite>.
