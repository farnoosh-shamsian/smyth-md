# ADVERBIAL COMPLEX SENTENCES (2193-2487): ὥστε (ὡς) WITH A FINITE VERB

##### <a id="s2273"></a> §2273

Any form used in simple sentences may follow <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>) with a finite verb. <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> has no effect on the mood of a finite verb.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> is found especially in Xenophon.

##### <a id="s2274"></a> §2274

<span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> _so that_ with the indicative states the _actual_ result of the action of the leading verb. This is especially common in narrative statements with the aorist tense. The negative is <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>. <!-- page 511 -->

<span lang="grc">ἐπιπί̄πτει χιὼν ἄπλετος ὥστε ἀπέκρυψε καὶ τὰ ὅπλα καὶ τοὺς ἀνθρώπους</span> _an immense amount of snow fell so that it buried both the arms and the men_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.4.11">X. A. 4.4.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰς τοσοῦτον ὕβρεως ἦλθον ὥστ᾽ ἔπεισαν ὑ̄μᾶς ἐλαύνειν αὐτόν</span> _they reached such a pitch of insolence that they persuaded you to expel him_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 16.9">I. 16.9</cite> (cp. [2258](05-result-clauses-consecutive-clauses.md#s2258) a), <span lang="grc">οὕτω σκαιὸς εἶ . . . ὥστ᾽ ου᾽ δύνασαι κτλ</span>. _are you so stupid that you are not able_, etc. <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.120">D. 18.120</cite> (of a definite fact; with <span lang="grc">μὴ δύνασθαι</span> the meaning would be _so stupid as not to be able_, expressing a characteristic). So after the locution <span lang="grc">τοσούτου δέω</span>, as <span lang="grc">τοσούτου δέω περὶ τῶν μὴ προσηκόντων ἱκανὸς εἶναι λέγειν, ὥστε δέδοικα κτλ</span>. _I am so far from able to speak about that which does not refer to my case that I fear_, etc. <cite data-ref="Lys. 17.1">L. 17.1</cite>. <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> is very rare: <span lang="grc">νομίζω οὕτως ἔχειν ὡς ἀποστήσονται αὐτοῦ αἱ πόλεις</span> _I consider that it is the case that the cities will revolt from him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.1.14">X. H. 6.1.14</cite>.

**a.** So when <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> introducing an independent sentence practically has the force of <span lang="grc">οὖν, τοίνυν, τοιγαροῦν</span> _and so therefore, consequently_. Thus <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰς μὲν τὴν ὑστεραίᾱν οὐχ ἧκεν· ὥσθ᾽ οἱ Ἕλληνες ἐφρόντιζον</span> _and on the next day he did not come; consequently the Greeks were anxious_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.25">X. A. 2.3.25</cite>. Cp. [2275](#s2275). This use appears sometimes with the infinitive: <span lang="grc">ὥστ᾽ ἐμὲ ἐμαυτὸν ἀνερωτᾶν</span> _and so I kept asking myself_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 22e">P. A. 22e</cite>.

##### <a id="s2275"></a> §2275

With an imperative, a hortatory or prohibitory subjunctive, or an interrogative verb, a clause with <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> is coördinate rather than subordinate, and <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> has the force of <span lang="grc">καὶ οὕτως</span>.

<span lang="grc">ὥστε θάρρει</span> _and so be not afraid_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.18">X. C. 1.3.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὥστε . . . μὴ θαυμάσῃς</span> _and so do not wonder_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 274a">P. Phae. 274a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὥστε πόθεν ἴσᾱσιν</span>; _and so how do they know?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 29.47">D. 29.47</cite>.

##### <a id="s2276"></a> §2276

<span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> (<span lang="grc">ὡς</span>) occurs rarely with the participle (instead of the infinitive) by attraction to a preceding participle (<cite data-ref="Andoc. 4.20">And. 4.20</cite>, <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.5.46">X. C. 7.5.46</cite>, <cite data-ref="Dem. 10.40">D. 10.40</cite>, 58. [23](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s23)).

##### <a id="s2277"></a> §2277

<span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> (<span lang="grc">ὡς</span>) may be used with a past tense of the indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (potential indicative and unreal indicative).

<span lang="grc">τοιοῦτόν τι ἐποίησεν ὡς πᾶς ἂν ἔγνω ὅτι ἀσμένη ἤκουσε</span> _she made a movement so that every one could recognize that she heard_ the music _with pleasure_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 9">X. S. 9</cite>. 3, <span lang="grc">κατεφαίνετο πάντα αὐτόθεν ὥστε οὐκ ἂν ἔλαθεν αὐτὸν ὁρμώμενος ὁ Κλέων τῷ στρατῷ</span> _everything was clearly visible from it, so that Cleon could not have escaped his notice in setting out with his force_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.6">T. 5.6</cite>.

##### <a id="s2278"></a> §2278

<span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> (<span lang="grc">ὡς</span>) is used rarely with the optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (by assimilation to a preceding optative) and with the potential optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>.

<span lang="grc">εἴ τις τὴν γυναῖκα τὴν σὴν οὕτω θεραπεύσειεν ὥστε φιλεῖν αὐτὴν μᾶλλον ποιήσειεν ἑαυτὸν ἢ σέ κτλ</span>. _if some one should pay such attention to your wife as to make her love him better than yourself_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.5.30">X. C. 5.5.30</cite> (cp. [2266](06-omegasigmatauepsilon-rarely-omegasigma-with-the-infinitive.md#s2266)), <span lang="grc">τοσούτου δεῖς ἐλέου τινὸς ἄξιος εἶναι ὥστε μῑσηθείης ἂν δικαιότατ᾽ ἀνθρώπων</span> _you are so far unworthy of compassion that you would be detested most justly of all men_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 37.49">D. 37.49</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡς ἄν</span> X. Ag. 6. 7, <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.5.37">X. C. 7.5.37</cite>, 7. 5. &lt;\*&gt;
