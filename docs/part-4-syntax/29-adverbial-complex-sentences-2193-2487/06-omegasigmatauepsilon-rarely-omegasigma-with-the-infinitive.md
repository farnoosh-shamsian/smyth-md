# ADVERBIAL COMPLEX SENTENCES (2193-2487): ὥστε (RARELY ὡς) WITH THE INFINITIVE

##### <a id="s2260"></a> §2260

The infinitive with <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> denotes an anticipated or possible result; but the actual occurrence of the result is not stated, and is to be inferred only. The negative is <span lang="grc">μή</span>, but <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is used when the <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> clause depends on a clause itself subordinate to a _verb of saying_ or _thinking_ ([2269](#s2269)). Cp. [2759](../35-negative-sentences.md#s2759). <!-- page 508 -->

**a.** <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> with the infinitive means _as to, so as to;_ but with a subject necessary in English it must often be translated by _so that_.

##### <a id="s2261"></a> §2261

The infinitive with <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> is usually present or aorist, rarely perfect (e.g. <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.257">D. 18.257</cite>). The future is common only in indirect discourse (<cite data-ref="Dem. 19.72">D. 19.72</cite>).

##### <a id="s2262"></a> §2262

<span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> (<span lang="grc">ὡς</span>) with the infinitive is used when its clause serves only to explain the principal clause. Thus,

##### <a id="s2263"></a> §2263

(I) After expressions denoting _ability_, _capacity_ , or _to effect something_.

<span lang="grc">πολλὰ πρά̄γματα παρεῖχον οἱ βάρβαροι . . . ἐλαφροὶ γὰρ ἦσαν, ὥστε καὶ ἐγγύθεν φεύγοντες ἀποφεύγειν</span> _the barbarians caused great annoyance; for they were_ so _nimble that they could escape even though they made off after they had approached quite near_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.2.27">X. A. 4.2.27</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁ ποταμὸς τοσοῦτος βάθος ὡς μηδὲ τὰ δόρατα ὑπερέχειν</span> _the river of such a depth that the spears could not even project above the surface_ 3. 5. 7 (on <span lang="grc">τοσοῦτος ὅσος</span> etc. see [2003](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s2003)), <span lang="grc">τοσαύτην κραυγὴν . . . ἐποίησαν ὥστε . . . τοὺς ταξιάρχους ἐλθεῖν</span> _they made such an uproar as to bring the taxiarchs_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 54.5">D. 54.5</cite>.

**a.** The idea of _effecting_ may be unexpressed: (<span lang="grc">Κλέαρχος</span>) <span lang="grc">ἤλαυνεν ἐπὶ τοὺς Μένωνος ὥστ᾽ ἐκείνους ἐκπεπλῆχθαι</span> _Clearchus advanced against the soldiers of Menon so_ (i.e. by so doing he brought it about) _that they were thoroughly frightened_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.13">X. A. 1.5.13</cite>; cp. [2267](#s2267). Several _verbs of effecting_ take <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> when the result is intended and where the simple infinitive is common ([2267 b](#s2267)).

##### <a id="s2264"></a> §2264

(II) After a comparative with <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> _than_.

<span lang="grc">ᾔσθοντο αὐτὸν ἐλά̄ττω ἔχοντα δύναμιν ἢ ὥστε τοὺς φίλους ὠφελεῖν</span> _they perceived that he possessed too little power to benefit his friends_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.8.23">X. H. 4.8.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ ἀκοντισταὶ βραχύτερα ἠκόντιζον ἢ ὡς ἐξικνεῖσθαι τῶν σφενδονητ ῶν</span> _the javelin throwers hurled their javelins too short a distance to reach the slingers_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.3.7">X. A. 3.3.7</cite>. After a comparative, <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> is as common as <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span>.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> may here be omitted: <span lang="grc">κρείσσον᾽ ἢ φέρειν κακά</span> _evils too great to be endured_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hec. 1107">E. Hec. 1107</cite>.

**b.** On positive adjectives with a comparative force, see [1063](../14-adjectives.md#s1063).

##### <a id="s2265"></a> §2265

(III) After a principal clause that is negatived.

<span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔχομεν ἀργύριον ὥστε ἀγοράζειν τὰ ἐπιτήδεια</span> _we have no money_ (_so as_) _to buy provisions_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.3.5">X. A. 7.3.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς πώποτ᾽ εἰς τοσοῦτ᾽ ἀναιδείᾱς ἀφί̄κετο ὥστε τοιοῦτόν τι τολμῆσαι ποιεῖν</span> _no one ever reached such a degree of shamelessness as to dare to do anything of the sort_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.62">D. 21.62</cite> (cp. [2258](05-result-clauses-consecutive-clauses.md#s2258) a). Here are included questions expecting the answer _no:_ <span lang="grc">τίς οὕτως ἐστὶ δεινὸς λέγειν ὥστε σε πεῖσαι</span>; _who is so eloquent as to persuade you?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.15">X. A. 2.5.15</cite>. After negative (as after comparative, [2264](#s2264)) clauses, the infinitive is used, since there would be no reason for the <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> clause if the action of the principal clause did not take place. But the indicative occurs occasionally (<cite data-ref="Lys. 13.18">L. 13.18</cite>, <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.43">Ant. 5.43</cite>).

##### <a id="s2266"></a> §2266

(IV) After a principal clause that expresses a condition.

<span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ εἰς τοῦτο μανίᾱς ἀφῑκόμην ὥστε ἐπιθῡμεῖν . . . πολλοῖς μάχεσθαι</span> _if I had not reached such a degree of madness as to desire to contend with many_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 3.29">L. 3.29</cite> (cp. [2258](05-result-clauses-consecutive-clauses.md#s2258) a). <!-- page 509 -->

##### <a id="s2267"></a> §2267

(V) To express an intended result, especially after a _verb of effecting_, as <span lang="grc">ποιῶ, διαπρά̄ττομαι</span>, etc.

<span lang="grc">πᾶν ποιοῦσιν ὥστε δίκην μὴ διδόναι</span> _they use every effort_ (_so as_) _to avoid being punished_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 479c">P. G. 479c</cite>, <span lang="grc">διφθέρᾱς . . . συνέσπων ὡς μὴ ἅπτεσθαι τῆς κάρφης τὸ ὕδωρ</span> _they stitched the skins so that the water should not touch the hay_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.10">X. A. 1.5.10</cite>.

**a.** The infinitive here expresses only the result, while the idea of purpose comes only from the general sense and especially from the meaning of the leading verb. <span lang="grc">ἵνα μή</span> in the above examples would express only purpose.

**b.** A clause of intended result is often used where <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> might occur in an object clause after a _verb of effort_ ([2211](03-object-clauses.md#s2211)); as <span lang="grc">μηχανὰ̄ς εὑρήσομεν ὥστ᾽ ἐς τὸ πᾶν σε τῶνδ᾽ ἀπαλλάξαι πόνων</span> _we will find means_ (_so as_) _to free thee entirely from these troubles_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Eum. 82">A. Eum. 82</cite>. The infinitive alone, denoting purpose, is here more usual.

##### <a id="s2268"></a> §2268

(VI) To state a condition or a proviso (_on condition that_, _provided that_).

<span lang="grc">πολλὰ μὲν ἂν χρήματ᾽ ἔδωκε Φιλιστίδης ὥστ᾽ ἔχειν Ὠρεόν</span> _Philistides would have given a large sum on condition of his holding Oreus_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.81">D. 18.81</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπῑσχνοῦντο ὥστε ἐκπλεῖν</span> _they gave their promise on the condition that they should sail out_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.6.26">X. A. 5.6.26</cite>. _On condition that_ is commonly expressed by <span lang="grc">ἐφ᾽ ᾧ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἐφ᾽ ᾧτε</span> ([2279](08-clauses-with-epsilonphi-omega-and-epsilonphi-omegatauepsilon.md#s2279)) with or without a preceding <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ τούτῳ</span>.

##### <a id="s2269"></a> §2269

A result clause with <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> and the indicative, dependent on an infinitive in indirect discourse, and itself quoted, takes the infinitive, and usually retains the negative of the direct form.

<span lang="grc">ἔφασαν τοὺς στρατιώτᾱς εἰς τοῦτο τρυφῆς ἐλθεῖν ὥστ᾽ οὐκ ἐθέλειν πἱ̄νειν, εἰ μὴ ἀνθοσμίᾱς εἴη</span> _they said that the soldiers reached such a degree of daintiness as to be unwilling to drink wine unless it had a strong bouquet_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.2.6">X. H. 6.2.6</cite> (direct: <span lang="grc">ὥστε οὐκ ἤθελον πἱ̄νειν</span>, with <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> retained in indirect discourse). See also [2270 b](#s2270).

So even when the principal verb takes <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span>, as <span lang="grc">ἐννοησάτω ὅτι οὕτως ἤδη τότε πόρρω τῆς ἡλικίᾱς ἦν ὥστ᾽ . . . οὐκ ἂν πολλῷ ὕστερον τελευτῆσαι τὸν βίον</span> _let him consider that he was then so far advanced in years that he would have died soon afterwards_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.8.1">X. M. 4.8.1</cite>.

**a.** The future infinitive here represents the future indicative: <span lang="grc">οἴεται ὑ̄μᾶς εἰς τοσοῦτον εὐηθείᾱς ἤδη προβεβηκέναι ὥστε καὶ ταῦτα ἀναπεισθήσεσθαι</span> _he thinks that you have already reached such a degree of simplicity as to allow yourselves to be persuaded even of this_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.256">Aes. 3.256</cite>. Outside of indirect discourse, the future infinitive with <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> is rare (<span lang="grc">γενήσεσθαι</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 16.4">D. 16.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἴσεσθαι</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 29.5">D. 29.5</cite>).

**b.** <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> with the optative in indirect discourse is very rare (<cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 3.5.23">X. H. 3.5.23</cite>, <cite data-ref="Isoc. 17.11">I. 17.11</cite>).

##### <a id="s2270"></a> §2270

<span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the infinitive expressing _possibility_, and representing either a potential indicative or a potential optative, occasionally follows <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> (<span lang="grc">ὡς</span>).

**a.** Not in indirect discourse: <span lang="grc">καί μοι οἱ θεοὶ οὕτως ἐν τοῖς ἱεροῖς ἐσήμηναν ὥστε καὶ ἰδιώτην ἂν γνῶναι</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἰδιώτης ἔγνω ἄν</span> or <span lang="grc">γνοίη ἄν</span>) <span lang="grc">ὅτι τῆς μοναρχίᾱς ἀπέχεσθαί με δεῖ</span> _and the gods declared to me so_ clearly _in the sacrifices that even a common man could understand that I must keep aloof from sovereignty_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.1.31">X. A. 6.1.31</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν τῷ</span> <!-- page 510 --> <span lang="grc">ἀσφαλεῖ ἤδη ἔσομαι ὡς μηδὲν ἂν ἔτι κακὸν παθεῖν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν ἂν ἔτι πάθοιμι</span>) _I shall soon be safe from suffering any further evil_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.7.27">X. C. 8.7.27</cite>. The difference in meaning is very slight between the construction with the potential optative and that with the infinitive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> representing the potential optative.

N.—Rarely in other cases. Thus, <span lang="grc">τὰ δὲ ἐντὸς οὕτως ἐκαίετο ὥστε . . . ἥδιστα ἂν ἐς ὕδωρ ψῡχρὸν σφᾶς αὐτοὺς ῥί̄πτειν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἔρρῑπτον</span>, [2304](10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2304)) _but their internal parts were inflamed to such a degree that they would have been most glad to throw themselves into cold water_ (_had they been permitted_) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.49">T. 2.49</cite>.

**b.** In indirect discourse: <span lang="grc">ἆρ᾽ οὖν δοκεῖ τῳ ὑ̄μῶν ὀλιγώρως οὕτως ἔχειν χρημάτων Νῑκόδημος ὥστε παραλιπεῖν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">παρέλιπεν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἄν τι τῶν τοιούτων</span>; _does it seem to any one of you that Nicodemus so despised money that he would have neglected any agreement of the sort?_ <cite data-ref="Isaeus 3.37">Is. 3.37</cite>.

##### <a id="s2271"></a> §2271

<span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> is often used with the infinitive when the infinitive without <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> is regular or more common.

**a.** So with many verbs, especially of _will_ or _desire_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔπεισαν τοὺς Ἀθηναίους ὥστε ἐξαγαγεῖν ἐκ Πύλου Μεσσηνίους</span> _they prevailed upon the Athenians_ (_so as_) _to withdraw the Messenians from Pylus_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.35">T. 5.35</cite>, <span lang="grc">δεηθέντες . . . ἑκάστων ἰδίᾳ ὥστε ψηφίσασθαι τὸν πόλεμον</span> _having begged each privately_ (_so as_) _to vote for the war_ 1. 119, <span lang="grc">ἐποίησα ὥστε δόξαι τούτῳ τοῦ πρὸς ἐμὲ πολέμου παύσασθαι</span> _I brought it about so that it seemed best to him to desist from warring against me_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.6.6">X. A. 1.6.6</cite>.

N.—Such verbs are: <span lang="grc">ἀπέχομαι, δέομαι</span> _ask_, <span lang="grc">διαπρά̄ττομαι, διδάσκω, δικαιῶ, δύναμαι, ἐθέλω, εἴργω, ἐλπίδα τινὰ ἔχω, ἐπαγγέλλομαι, ἐπαίρω, ἔχω</span> _am able_, <span lang="grc">θέσφατόν τί τινι ἱκνεῖται</span>, a phrase with <span lang="grc">καθίσταμαι, ξυγχωρῶ, παραδίδωμι, πείθω</span> (and <span lang="grc">παρασκευάζω ῀ πείθω</span>), <span lang="grc">πέφῡκα, ποιῶ, προθῡμοῦμαι, προτρέπομαι, φυλάττομαι</span> ([2239](03-object-clauses.md#s2239)), <span lang="grc">ψηφίζομαι</span>.

**b.** When the infinitive is the subject: <span lang="grc">πάνυ γάρ μοι ἐμέλησεν ὥστε εἰδέναι</span> _for it concerned me exceedingly to know_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.3.19">X. C. 6.3.19</cite>.

N.—So with <span lang="grc">ἔστι, γίγνεται</span>, etc., <span lang="grc">δόξαν</span> _when it was decreed_, <span lang="grc">συνέβη</span> (Thuc.), <span lang="grc">συνέπῑπτε, συνήνεικε</span> (Hdt.), <span lang="grc">προσήκει</span>. Cp. [1985](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s1985).

**c.** With adjectives, especially such as are positive in form but have a comparative force and denote a deficiency or the like ([1063](../14-adjectives.md#s1063)); as <span lang="grc">ἡμεῖς γὰρ ἔτι νέοι ὥστε τοσοῦτον πρᾶγμα διελέσθαι</span> _for we are still_ too _young to decide so important a matter_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 314b">P. Pr. 314b</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">ἰδιώτης, ὀλίγος, ψῡχρός, γέρων</span>; and with <span lang="grc">ἱκανός, ἀδύνατος</span> (and with <span lang="grc">δύνασθαι</span>).

##### <a id="s2272"></a> §2272

On the absolute infinitive with <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> (less often with <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span>) see [2012](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s2012).
