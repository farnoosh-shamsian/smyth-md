# ADVERBIAL COMPLEX SENTENCES (2193-2487): CLASSIFICATION OF CONDITIONAL SENTENCES

## A. CLASSIFICATION ACCORDING TO FORM

##### <a id="s2289"></a> §2289

Conditional sentences may be classified according to _form_ or _function_ (i.e. with reference to their meaning). Classified according to form, all conditional sentences may be arranged with regard to the form of the protasis or of the apodosis.

Protasis: <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the indicative.

<span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">εἰ</span>) with the subjunctive.

<span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the optative.

Apodosis: with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, denoting what _would_ (_should_) _be_ or _have been_.

without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, not denoting what _would_ (_should_) _be_ or _have been_.

## B. CLASSIFICATION ACCORDING TO FUNCTION

<!-- page 514 -->

##### <a id="s2290"></a> §2290

Greek possesses a great variety of ways to join protasis and apodosis, but certain types, as in English, are more common than others and have clear and distinct meanings. In the case of some of the less usual types the exact shade of difference cannot be accurately known to us; as indeed to the Greeks themselves they were often used with no essential difference from the conventional types. In the following classification only the ordinary forms are given.

### ACCORDING TO TIME

##### <a id="s2291"></a> §2291

This is the only functional distinction that characterizes _all_ conditional sentences. Here are included also 2292, 2295, 2296.

#### 1. _Present_

Protasis: a primary tense of the indicative.

Apodosis: any form of the simple sentence.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ποιεῖς, καλῶς ποιεῖς</span> _if you do this, you do well_.

#### 2. _Past_

Protasis: a secondary tense of the indicative.

Apodosis: any form of the simple sentence.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ἐποίεις, καλῶς ἐποίεις</span> _if you were doing this, you were doing well_, <span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ἐποίησας, καλῶς ἐποίησας</span> _if you did this, you did well_.

#### 3. _Future_

**a.** Protasis: <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> with the subjunctive.

Apodosis: any form expressing future time.

<span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν ταῦτα ποιῇς</span> (<span lang="grc">ποιήσῃς</span>), <span lang="grc">καλῶς ποιήσεις</span> _if you do this, you will do well_.

**b.** Protasis: <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the future indicative.

Apodosis: any form expressing future time.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ποιήσεις, πείσει</span> _if you do this, you will suffer for it_.

**c.** Protasis: <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the optative.

Apodosis: <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the optative.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ποιοίης</span> (<span lang="grc">ποιήσειας</span>), <span lang="grc">καλῶς ἂν ποιοίης</span> (<span lang="grc">ποιήσειας</span>) _if you should_ (_were to_) _do this, you would do well_.

### According to Fulfilment or Non-fulfilment

##### <a id="s2292"></a> §2292

Only one class of conditional sentences distinctly expresses non-fulfilment of the action.

#### 1. _Present or Past_

<!-- page 515 -->

Protasis: <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the imperfect indicative.

Apodosis: <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the imperfect indicative.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ἐποίεις, καλῶς ἂν ἐποίεις</span> _if you were_ (now) _doing this, you would be doing well; if you had been doing this, you would have been doing well_.

#### 2. _Past_

Protasis: <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the aorist indicative.

Apodosis: <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the aorist indicative.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ἐποίησας, καλῶς ἂν ἐποίησας</span> _if you had done this, you would have done well_.

N.—Greek has no special forms to show that an action _is_ or _was_ fulfilled, however clearly this may be implied by the context. Any form of conditional sentence in which the apodosis does not express a rule of action may refer to an impossibility.

### According to Particular or General Conditions

##### <a id="s2293"></a> §2293

A particular condition refers to a definite act or to several definite acts occurring at a definite time or at definite times.

##### <a id="s2294"></a> §2294

A general condition refers to any one of a series of acts that may occur or may have occurred at any time.

##### <a id="s2295"></a> §2295

General conditions are distinguished from particular conditions only in present and past time, and then only when there is no implication as to the fulfilment of the action. General conditions have no obligatory form, as any form of condition may refer to a rule of action or to a particular act; but there are two common types of construction:

#### 1. _Present_

Protasis: <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> with the subjunctive.

Apodosis: present indicative.

<span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν ταῦτα ποιῇς</span> (<span lang="grc">ποιήσῃς</span>), <span lang="grc">σὲ ἐπαινῶ</span> _if_ ever _you do this_, _I_ always _praise you_.

#### 2. _Past_

Protasis: <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the optative.

Apodosis: imperfect indicative.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ποιοίης</span> (<span lang="grc">ποιήσειας</span>), <span lang="grc">σὲ ἐπῄνουν</span> _if_ ever _you did this_, _I_ always _praised you_.

##### <a id="s2296"></a> §2296

But equally possible, though less common, are:

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ποιεῖς, σὲ ἐπαινῶ</span> and <span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ἐποίεις, σὲ ἐπῄνουν</span>.

## PRESENT AND PAST CONDITIONS

### First Form of Conditions: SIMPLE PRESENT AND PAST CONDITIONS

##### <a id="s2298"></a> §2298

Simple present or past conditions simply _state_ a supposition with no implication as to its reality or probability. The protasis has the indicative, the apodosis has commonly the indicative, but also any other form of the simple sentence appropriate to the thought.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ποιεῖς, καλῶς ποιεῖς</span> _if you do this, you do well_.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ἐποίησας, καλῶς ἐποίησας</span> _if you did this, you did well_.

**a.** This form of condition corresponds to the logical formula _if this is so_, _then that is so; if this is not so, then that is not so; if A = B_, _then C = D_. The truth of the conclusion depends solely on the truth of the condition, which <!-- page 517 --> is not implied in any way. In these conditions something is supposed to be true only in order to draw the consequence that something else is true.

**b.** The conditional clause may express what the writer knows is physically impossible. Even when the supposition _is_ true according to the real opinion of the writer, this form of condition is employed. In such cases <span lang="grc">εἴπερ</span> is often used for <span lang="grc">εἰ</span>. Both <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> and <span lang="grc">εἴπερ</span> sometimes have a causal force ([2246](04-causal-clauses.md#s2246)); cp. _si quidem_ and _quia_.

**c.** The simple condition is particular or general. When the protasis has <span lang="grc">εἴ τις</span> and the apodosis a present indicative, the simple condition has a double meaning referring both to an individual case and to a rule of action. When a present general condition is distinctly expressed, <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> with the subjunctive is used ([2337](#s2337).)

##### <a id="s2299"></a> §2299

There are many possible combinations of present and past conditions with different forms of the protasis and apodosis. Protasis and apodosis may be in different tenses, and present and future may be combined.

##### <a id="s2300"></a> §2300

The apodosis may be the simple indicative or any other form of the simple sentence appropriate to the thought.

**a. Simple Indicative**: <span lang="grc">εἰ τοῦτ᾽ ἔχει καλῶς, ἐκεῖνο αἰσχρῶς</span> _if this is excellent_, _that is disgraceful_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.188">Aes. 3.188</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν</span> (<span lang="grc">Ἀσκληπιὸς</span>) <span lang="grc">θεοῦ ἦν, οὐκ ἦν αἰσχροκερδής· εἰ δ᾽ αἰσχροκερδής, οὐκ ἦν θεοῦ</span> _if Asclepius was the son of a god, he was not covetous; if he was covetous, he was not the son of a god_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 408c">P. R. 408c</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἴ τέ τι ἄλλο . . . ἐγένετο ἐπικίνδῡνον τοῖς Ἕλλησι, πάντων . . . μετέσχομεν</span> _and if any other danger befell the Greeks_, _we took our share in all_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.54">T. 3.54</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἦ καλὸν . . . τέχνημα ἄρα κέκτησαι, εἴπερ κέκτησαι</span> _in truth you do possess a noble art, if indeed you do possess it_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 319a">P. Pr. 319a</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἴπερ γε Δᾱρείου . . . ἐστι παῖς . . . , οὐκ ἀμαχεὶ ταῦτ᾽ ἐγὼ λήψομαι</span> _if indeed he is a son of Darius_, _I shall not gain this without a battle_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.9">X. A. 1.7.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">Κλέαρχος εἰ παρὰ τοὺς ὅρκους ἔλῡε τὰ̄ς σπονδά̄ς, τὴν δίκην ἔχει</span> _assuming that Clearchus broke the truce contrary to his oath, he has his deserts_ 2. 5. 41, <span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ δύο ἐξ ἑνὸς ἀγῶνος γεγένησθον, οὐκ ἐγὼ αἴτιος</span> _but if two trials have been made out of one_, _I am not responsible_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.85">Ant. 5.85</cite>.

**b. Indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>** (unreal indicative, [1786](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1786)): <span lang="grc">καίτοι τότε . . . τὸν Ὑπερείδην, εἴπερ ἀληθῆ μου νῦν κατηγορεῖ, μᾶλλον ἂν εἰκότως ἢ τόνδ᾽ ἐδίωκεν</span> _and yet, if indeed his present charge against me is true, he would have had more reason for prosecuting Hyperides than he now has for prosecuting my client_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.223">D. 18.223</cite> (here <span lang="grc">ἂν ἐδίωκεν</span> implies <span lang="grc">εἰ ἐδίωκεν</span>, [2303](#s2303)). So also an unreal indicative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, 1774: <span lang="grc">τοῦτο, εἰ καὶ τἄλλα πάντ᾽ ἀποστεροῦσιν . . . ἀποδοῦναι προσῆκεν</span> _even if they steal everything else, they should have restored this_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 27.37">D. 27.37</cite>. In the above examples each clause has its proper force.

**c. Subjunctive** of exhortation or prohibition (cp. the indicative <span lang="grc">δεῖ</span> or <span lang="grc">χρή</span> with the infinitive, [1807](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1807)): <span lang="grc">ὅθεν δὲ ἀπελίπομεν ἐπανέλθωμεν, εἴ σοι ἡδομένῳ ἐστίν</span> _but let us return to the point whence we digressed, if it is agreeable to you_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 78b">P. Ph. 78b</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν ἴστε με τοιοῦτον . . . μηδὲ φωνὴν ἀνάσχησθε</span> _if you know that I am such a man . . . do not even endure_ the sound of _my voice_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.10">D. 18.10</cite>.

**d. Optative of wish** (cp. the indicative <span lang="grc">ἐλπίζω</span>): <span lang="grc">κάκιστ᾽ ἀπολοίμην, Ξανθίᾱν εἰ μὴ φιλῶ</span> _may I perish most vilely, if I do not love Xanthias_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Frogs 579">Ar. Ran. 579</cite>.

**e. Potential optative**: <span lang="grc">θαυμάζοιμ᾽ ἂν εἰ οἶσθα</span> _I should be surprised if you_ <!-- page 518 --> _know_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 312c">P. Pr. 312c</cite>. The potential optative (or indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, above b) sometimes suggests an inference (cp. the indicative <span lang="grc">δοκεῖ</span> and inf. with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>). Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν γὰρ τοῦτο λέγουσιν, ὁμολογοίην ἂν ἔγωγε οὐ κατὰ τούτους εἶναι ῥήτωρ</span> _for if they mean this_, _I must admit_ (it seems to me that I must admit) _that I am an orator_, _but not after their style_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 17b">P. A. 17b</cite> (cp. <span lang="grc">τοῦτό γέ μοι δοκεῖ καλὸν εἶναι, εἴ τις οἷός τ᾽ εἴη παιδεύειν ἀνθρώπους</span> _this seems to me a fine thing, if any one should be able to train men_ 19 e), <span lang="grc">εἰ γὰρ οὗτοι ὀρθῶς ἀπέστησαν, ὑ̄μεῖς ἂν οὐ χρεὼν ἄρχοιτε&lt;\*&gt;</span> _for if they were right in revolting, you must be wrong in holding your empire_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.40">T. 3.40</cite> (cp. <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἄρα χρὴ ὑ̄μᾶς ἄρχειν</span>).

**f. Imperative** (cp. the indicative <span lang="grc">κελεύω</span> _order_, <span lang="grc">ἀπαγορεύω</span> _forbid_): <span lang="grc">εἴ τις ἀντιλέγει, λεγέτω</span> _if any one objects, let him speak_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.3.14">X. A. 7.3.14</cite>.

##### <a id="s2301"></a> §2301

If the protasis expresses a _present intention_ or _necessity_, the future indicative may be used.

<span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ καὶ τῷ ἡγεμόνι πιστεύσομεν ὃν ἂν Κῦρος διδῷ, τί κωλύ̄ει καὶ τὰ ἄκρα ἡμῖν κελεύειν Κῦρον προκαταλαβεῖν</span>; _but if we are going to trust any guide that Cyrus may give us, what hinders our also ordering Cyrus to occupy the heights in advance in our behalf?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.16">X. A. 1.3.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">αἶρε πλῆκτρον, εἰ μαχεῖ</span> _raise your spur if you mean_ (_are going_) _to fight_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Birds 759">Ar. Av. 759</cite>. The future here has a modal force and expresses something besides futurity; hence it is equivalent to <span lang="grc">μέλλεις μαχεῖσθαι</span> ([1959](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1959)), but not to <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν μάχῃ</span> ([2323](#s2323)) or to <span lang="grc">εἰ μαχεῖ</span> (a threat, [2328](#s2328)), both of which refer to future time. The periphrasis with <span lang="grc">μέλλω</span> and the present or future infinitive is more common in prose.

### Second Form of Conditions: PRESENT AND PAST UNREAL CONDITIONS

##### <a id="s2302"></a> §2302

In present and past unreal conditions the protasis implies that the supposition cannot or could not be realized because contrary to a known fact. The apodosis states what _would be_ or _would have been_ the result if the condition _were_ or _had been_ realized.

##### <a id="s2303"></a> §2303

The protasis has <span lang="grc"> **ει᾽** </span> with the imperfect, aorist, or pluperfect indicative; the apodosis has <span lang="grc"> **ἄν** </span> with these past tenses. The protasis and apodosis may have different tenses. Unreal conditions are either particular or general.

##### <a id="s2304"></a> §2304

The **imperfect** refers to present time or (sometimes) to a continued or habitual past act or state. The imperfect may be conative.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ἐποίεις, καλῶς ἂν ἐποίεις</span> _if you were_ (now) _doing this, you would be doing well_ , or _if you had been doing this, you would have been doing well_.

The implied opposite is a present (<span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ου᾽ ποιεῖς</span> _but you are_ **not** _doing this_) or an imperfect (<span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἐποίεις</span> _but you were_ **not** _doing this_).

The imperfect of past time emphasizes the continuance of the action. <!-- page 519 -->

##### <a id="s2305"></a> §2305

The **aorist** refers to a simple occurrence in the past.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ἐποίησας, καλῶς ἂν ἐποίησας</span> _if you had done this, you would have done well_.

The implied opposite is an aorist (<span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἐποίησας</span> _but you did_ **not** _do this_).

##### <a id="s2306"></a> §2306

The (rare) **pluperfect** refers to an act completed in past or present time or to the state following on such completion.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ἐπεποιήκης, καλῶς ἂν ἐπεποιήκης</span> _if you had finished doing this_ (now or on any past occasion), _you would have done well_.

The implied opposite is a perfect (<span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ου᾽ πεποίηκας</span> _but you have_ **not** _done this_) or a pluperfect (<span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἐπεποιήκης</span> _but you had_ **not** _done this_).

**a.** The pluperfect is used only when stress is laid on the completion of the act or on the continuance of the result of the act, and generally refers to present time. In reference to past time, the aorist is generally used instead of the pluperfect.

##### <a id="s2307"></a> §2307

In reference to _past_ time, the imperfect or aorist is used according as either tense would be used in an affirmative sentence not conditional. The pluperfect is commonly used when the perfect would have been used of present time.

##### <a id="s2308"></a> §2308

In the _form_ of the protasis and the apodosis of unreal conditions there is nothing that denotes unreality, but, in the combination, the unreality of the protasis is always, and that of the apodosis generally, implied. The past tenses of the indicative are used in unreal conditions referring to present time, because the speaker's thought goes back to the past, when the realization of the condition was still possible, though at the time of speaking that realization is impossible.

##### <a id="s2309"></a> §2309

**Same Tenses in Protasis and Apodosis**.—**a. Imperfect** of present time: <span lang="grc">ταῦτα δὲ οὐκ ἂν ἐδύναντο ποιεῖν, εἰ μὴ καὶ διαίτῃ μετρίᾳ ἐχρῶντο</span> _but they would not be able to do this, if they were not also following a temperate diet_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.2.16">X. C. 1.2.16</cite>.

**b. Imperfect** of past time: <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν οὖν νήσων . . . ἐκράτει, εἰ μή τι καὶ ναυτικὸν εἶχεν</span> _accordingly he would not have ruled over islands, if he had not possessed also some naval force_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.9">T. 1.9</cite>. Present and past combined: <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ τότ᾽ ἐπόνουν, νῦν ἂν οὐκ εὐφραινόμην</span> _if I had not toiled then_, _I should not be rejoicing now_ Philemon 153.

**c. Aorist** of past time: <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν ἐποίησεν Ἀγασίᾱς ταῦτα, εἰ μὴ ἐγὼ αὐτὸν ἐκέλευσα</span> _Agasias would not have done this, if I had not ordered him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.6.15">X. A. 6.6.15</cite>.

##### <a id="s2310"></a> §2310

**Different Tenses in Protasis and Apodosis**.—**a. Imperfect and Aorist**: <span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν πρόσθεν ἠπιστάμην, οὐδ᾽ ἂν συνηκολούθησά σοι</span> _if I had known this before_, _I would not even have accompanied you_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.7.11">X. A. 7.7.11</cite>.

N.—With an imperfect of present time in the protasis, <span lang="grc">εἶπον ἄν, ἀπεκρῑνάμην ἄν</span> and like verbs, denote an act in present time (_I should at once say_). Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ πατὴρ ἦσθ᾽, εἶπον ἄν σ᾽ οὐκ εὖ φρονεῖν</span> _if thou wert not my father_, _I would say_ (would have said) _thou wast unwise_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 755">S. Ant. 755</cite>. Often in Plato, as <span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν</span> <!-- page 520 --> <span lang="grc">οὖ&lt;\*&gt; σύ με ἠρώτᾱς τι τῶν νῦν δή, εἶπον ἄν κτλ</span>. _if now you were asking me any one of the questions with which we are now dealing_, _I should say_ etc., <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyph. 12d">P. Euth. 12d</cite>, cp. <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 514d">P. G. 514d</cite>, <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.6.23">X. A. 7.6.23</cite>.

**b. Imperfect and Pluperfect**: <span lang="grc">καὶ τἄλλ᾽ ἂν ἅπαντ᾽ ἀκολούθως τούτοις ἐπέπρᾱκτο, εἴ τις ἐπείθετό μοι</span> _and everything else would have been effected consistently with what I have said, if my advice had been followed_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.173">D. 19.173</cite>.

**c. Aorist and Imperfect**: <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ ὑ̄μεῖς ἤλθετε, ἐπορευόμεθα ἂν ἐπὶ βασιλέᾱ</span> _if you had not come, we should_ now _be marching against the king_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.4">X. A. 2.1.4</cite>.

**d. Aorist and Pluperfect**: <span lang="grc">εἰ ἐγὼ πάλαι ἐπεχείρησα πρά̄ττειν τὰ πολῑτικὰ πρά̄γματα, πάλαι ἂν ἀπολώλη</span> _if I had long ago essayed to meddle with politics_, _I should long ago have perished_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 31d">P. A. 31d</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ μία ψῆφος μετέπεσεν, ὑπερώριστ᾽ ἄν</span> _if one vote had been transferred to the other side, he would have been transported across the borders_ (and _now_ be in exile) <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.252">Aes. 3.252</cite>.

**e. Pluperfect and Imperfect**: <span lang="grc">ἡ πόλις ἐλάμβανεν ἂν δίκην, εἴ τι ἠδίκητο</span> _the State would inflict punishment, if it had been wronged_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 6.10">Ant. 6.10</cite>.

**f. Pluperfect and Aorist**: <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν παρέμεινα, εἰ ἐλελύμην</span> _I should not have stayed_, _if I had been free_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.13">Ant. 5.13</cite>.

##### <a id="s2311"></a> §2311

**Homeric Constructions**.—In Homer the imperfect in unreal conditions refers only to past time. The apodosis may have <span lang="grc">κέ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the optative.

**a.** The present unreal condition with <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the optative in the protasis and <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the optative in the apodosis (in _form_ like a less vivid future condition in Attic) is very rare (<cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 23.274"><span lang="grc">Ψ</span> 274</cite>). In B 80, <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 24.220"><span lang="grc">Ω</span> 220</cite> we have a combination of a past protasis (imperfect or aorist indicative) with present apodosis (with <span lang="grc">κέν</span> and the optative).

**b.** Past unreal conditions have, in the protasis, the imperfect or aorist indicative; in the apodosis, either the imperfect or aorist indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or <span lang="grc">κέ</span> or the aorist or present optative with <span lang="grc">κέ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">καί νύ κεν ἔνθ᾽ ἀπόλοιτο . . . Αἰνείᾱς, εἰ μὴ ἄρ᾽ ὀξυ` νόησε . . . Ἀφροδί̄τη</span> _and here Aeneas had perished, if Aphrodite had not quickly observed him_ E 311.

##### <a id="s2312"></a> §2312

Unreal conditions with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> and the optative in apodosis (cp. [2311](#s2311)) in Attic are rare and some are suspected. Either the common reading is at fault (<cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.5.8">X. M. 3.5.8</cite>), or we have a simple condition with a potential optative ([2300 e](#s2300)), as in <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.57">And. 1.57</cite>, <cite data-ref="Lys. 6.39">L. 6.39</cite>, <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.102">I. 4.102</cite>. In <span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν τοίνυν τοῦτ᾽ ἐπεχείρουν λέγειν . . . , οὐκ ἔσθ᾽ ὅστις οὐκ ἂν εἰκότως ἐπιτῑμήσειέ μοι</span> _if now I were attempting to say this_, _there would be no one who would not censure me with good reason_ (<cite data-ref="Dem. 18.206">D. 18.206</cite>) the implied conclusion is <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν ἦν ὅστις κτλ</span>.

**a.** The optative in protasis and apodosis occur in <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 568">E. Med. 568</cite> (present unreal). Hdt. uses the potential optative occasionally (e.g. 7. [214](../../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/01-first-declension-stems-in-alpha.md#s214)) where English uses a past expression.

#### UNREAL CONDITIONS—APODOSIS WITHOUT <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>

##### <a id="s2313"></a> §2313

<span lang="grc"> **ἄν** </span> may be omitted in the apodosis of an unreal condition when the apodosis consists of an imperfect indicative denoting unfulfilled obligation, possibility, or propriety. Such are the impersonal expressions <span lang="grc">ἔδει, χρῆν, ἐξῆν, εἰκὸς ἦν, καλὸν ἦν</span>, etc., with the infinitive, the action of which is (usually) not realized. <!-- page 521 -->

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ἐποίει, ἔδει</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐξῆν</span>) <span lang="grc">αἰτιᾶσθαι αὐτόν</span> _if he were doing this_ (as he is **not**), _one ought to_ (_might_) _blame him_.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ἐποίησε, ἔδει</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐξῆν</span>) <span lang="grc">αἰτιά̄σασθαι</span> (or <span lang="grc">αἰτιᾶσθαι</span>) <span lang="grc">αὐτόν</span> _if he had done this_ (as he did **not**), _one ought to_ (_might_) _have blamed him_.

**a.** Here <span lang="grc">ἔδει</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐξῆν</span> are auxiliaries and the emphasis falls on the infinitive. The impersonal verb has the effect of a modifying adverb denoting obligation, possibility, or propriety: thus <span lang="grc">ἔδει αἰτιᾶσθαι αὐτόν</span> is virtually equivalent to <span lang="grc">δικαίως ἂν ᾐτιᾶτο</span>, and <span lang="grc">εἰκὸς ἦν αἰτιά̄σασθαι αὐτόν</span> to <span lang="grc">εἰκότως ἂν ᾐτιά̄θη</span> _he would properly have been blamed_.

**b.** <span lang="grc">ἔδει, χρῆν</span>, etc., may be used in simple sentences ([1774 f](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1774)f.) without any protasis either expressed or implied. But a protasis may often be supplied in thought.

##### <a id="s2314"></a> §2314

The present infinitive generally expresses what _would necessarily_, _possibly_, or _properly be done_ now. The aorist, and sometimes the present, infinitive expresses what _would necessarily, possibly_, or _properly have been done_ in the past.

**a. Present infinitive** of present time: <span lang="grc">χρῆν δήπου, εἴτε τινὲς αὐτῶν πρεσβύτεροι γενόμενοι ἔγνωσαν ὅτι νέοις οὖσιν αὐτοῖς ἐγὼ κακὸν πώποτέ τι ξυνεβούλευσα, νῡνὶ̄ αὐτοὺς ἀναβαίνοντας ἐμοῦ κατηγορεῖν</span> _if some of them on growing older had perceived that I ever gave them any bad counsel when they were young, they ought of course now to rise up in person and accuse me_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 33d">P. A. 33d</cite>.

**b. Present infinitive** of past time: <span lang="grc">εἴ τινα</span> (<span lang="grc">προῖκα</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐδίδου, εἰκὸς ἦν καὶ τὴν δοθεῖσαν ὑπὸ τῶν παραγενέσθαι φασκόντων μαρτυρεῖσθαι</span> _if he had given any_ dowry, _that which was actually delivered would naturally have been attested by those who claimed to have been present_ <cite data-ref="Isaeus 3.28">Is. 3.28</cite>.

**c. Aorist infinitive** of past time: <span lang="grc">εἰ ἐβούλετο δίκαιος εἶναι περὶ τοὺς παῖδας, ἐξῆν αὐτῷ . . . μισθῶσαι τὸν οἶκον</span> _if he had wished to be just in regard to the children_, _he might properly have let the house_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 32.23">L. 32.23</cite>.

##### <a id="s2315"></a> §2315

With the same impersonal expressions, <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is regularly used when the obligation, possibility, or propriety, and not the action of the verb dependent on <span lang="grc">ἔδει</span>, etc., is denied. Here the main force of the apodosis falls on the necessity, possibility, or propriety of the act.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ἐποίει, ἔδει</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐξῆν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἂν αι᾿τιᾶσθαι αὐτόν</span> _if he were doing this_ (as he is not), _it would be necessary_ (_possible_) _to blame him;_ but, as the case now stands, it is not necessary (possible). Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν ἠπιστάμεθα σαφῶς ὅτι ἥξει πλοῖα . . . ἄγων ἱκανά, οὐδὲν ἂν ἔδει ὧν μέλλω λέγειν</span> _if we knew for certain that he would return with a sufficient number of vessels, there would be no need to say what I am going to say_ (but there is _need_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.1.10">X. A. 5.1.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">ταῦτα εἰ μὲν δι᾽ ἀσθένειαν ἐπάσχομεν, στέργειν ἂν ἦν ἀνάγκη τὴν τύχην</span> _if we had suffered this because of our weakness_, _we should have_ (necessity would compel us) _to rest content with our lot_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 33.4">L. 33.4</cite>.

##### <a id="s2316"></a> §2316

With <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, it is implied that the obligation does (or did) not exist; without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, it is implied that the action of the dependent infinitive is (or was) <!-- page 522 --> not realized. Thus the first sentence in 2315, without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, would mean: _if he were doing this_ (as he is **not**), _one ought to blame him;_ but, as the case now stands, one does not blame him.

##### <a id="s2317"></a> §2317

<span lang="grc">ἐβουλόμην</span>, or <span lang="grc">ἐβουλόμην ἄν</span>, with the infinitive may stand in the apodosis. Cp. [1782](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1782), [1789](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1789).

##### <a id="s2318"></a> §2318

<span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is regularly omitted in an apodosis formed by the imperfect of <span lang="grc"> **μέλλω** </span> and the infinitive (usually future) to denote an unfulfilled past intention or expectation (cp. the Lat. future participle with _eram_ or _fui_). Cp. [1895 a](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1895), [1960](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1960).

<span lang="grc">ἦ μάλα δὴ Ἀγαμέμνονος . . . φθί̄σεσθαι κακὸν οἶτον ἐνὶ μεγάροισιν ἔμελλον, εἰ μὴ . . . ἔειπες</span> _in sooth I was like to have perished in my halls by the evil fate of Agamemnon_, _hadst thou not spoken_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 13.383"><span lang="grc">ν</span> 383</cite> (_periturus eram, nisi dixisses_).

##### <a id="s2319"></a> §2319

<span lang="grc">ἄν</span> may be omitted with the aorist of <span lang="grc"> **κινδῡνεύω** </span> _run a risk_ when the emphasis falls on the dependent infinitive.

<span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ δρόμῳ μόλις ἐξεφύγομεν εἰς Δελφούς, ἐκινδῡνεύσαμεν ἀπολέσθαι</span> _if we had not escaped with difficulty to Delphi by taking to our heels, we ran the risk of perishing_ ( = _we should_ probably _have perished:_ <span lang="grc">ἂν ἀπωλόμεθα</span>) <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.123">Aes. 3.123</cite>. Contrast <span lang="grc">εἰ μέντοι τότε πλείους συνελέγησαν, ἐκινδύ̄νευσεν ἂν διαφθαρῆναι πολὺ τοῦ στρατεύματος</span> _if they had mustered in larger force at this time, a large part of the troops would have been in danger of being destroyed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.1.11">X. A. 4.1.11</cite>.

##### <a id="s2320"></a> §2320

Some expressions containing a secondary tense of the indicative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, and not followed by a dependent infinitive, are virtually equivalent to the apodosis of an unreal condition.

<span lang="grc">τούτῳ δ᾽ ει᾽ μὴ ὡμολόγουν ἃ οὗτος ἐβούλετο, οὐδεμιᾷ ζημίᾳ ἔνοχος ἦν</span> _but if they had not acknowledged to him what he wished, he would have been_ (lit. _was_) _liable to no penalty_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 7.37">L. 7.37</cite>.

**a.** Imperfects (not impersonal) without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> are often emended, as <span lang="grc">ᾐσχῡνόμην μέντοι</span> (some editors <span lang="grc">μέντἄν</span>), <span lang="grc">εἰ ὑπὸ πολεμίου γε ὄντος ἐξηπατήθην</span> _I should_, _however_, _be ashamed, if I had been deceived by any one who was an enemy_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.6.21">X. A. 7.6.21</cite>. Cp. “Tybalt's death was woe enough, if it had ended there” (Shakesp.). Cases like 1895 a do not belong here.

## FUTURE CONDITIONS

##### <a id="s2321"></a> §2321

Future conditions set forth suppositions the fulfilment of which is still undecided. There are two main forms of future conditions:

More Vivid Future conditions.

Less Vivid Future conditions.

A variety of the first class is the Emotional Future ([2328](#s2328)).

Future conditions may be particular or general ([2293](#s2293), [2294](#s2294)).

##### <a id="s2322"></a> §2322

The difference between the More Vivid Future and the Less Vivid Future, like the difference between _if I_ (_shall_) _do this_ and _if I should do this_, depends on the mental attitude of the speaker. With the Vivid Future the <!-- page 523 --> speaker sets forth a thought as prominent and distinct in his mind; and for any one or more of various reasons. Thus, he may (and generally does) regard the conclusion as more likely to be realized; but even an impossible ([2322 c](#s2322)) or dreaded result may be expressed by this form if the speaker chooses to picture the result vividly and distinctly. The More Vivid Future is thus used whenever the speaker clearly desires to be graphic, impressive, emphatic, and to anticipate a future result with the distinctness of the present.

The Less Vivid Future deals with suppositions less distinctly conceived and of less immediate concern to the speaker, mere assumed or imaginary cases. This is a favourite construction in Greek, and is often used in stating suppositions that are merely possible and often impossible; but the form of the condition itself does not imply an expectation of the speaker that the conclusion may _possibly_ be realized. The difference between the two forms, therefore, is not an inherent difference between _probable_ realization in the one case and _possible_ realization in the other. The same thought may often be expressed in either form without any essential difference in meaning. The only difference is, therefore, often that of temperament, tone, or style.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> with the subjunctive and <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the optative are rarely used in successive sentences. In most such cases the difference lies merely in the degree of distinctness and emphasis of the expression used; but where the speaker wishes to show that the conclusion is expected or desired, he uses <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> with the subjunctive rather than the other form. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ οὖν ἴδοιεν καὶ νῲ καθάπερ τοὺς πολλοὺς ἐν μεσημβρίᾳ μὴ διαλεγομένους, ἀλλὰ νυστάζοντας καὶ κηλουμένους ὑφ᾽ αὑτῶν δι᾽ ἀ̄ργίᾱ&lt;\*&gt; τῆς διανοίᾱς, δικαίως ἂν καταγελῷεν· . . . ἐὰ̄ν δ᾽ ὁρῶσι διαλεγομένους . . ., τάχ᾽ ἂν δοῖεν ἀγασθέντες</span> _if now they should see that we, like the many, are not conversing at noon-day but slumbering and charmed by them because of the indolence of our thoughts, they would rightly laugh at us; but if they see us conversing_, _they will, perhaps_, _out of admiration make us gifts_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 259a">P. Phae. 259a</cite>.

**b.** Cases of both forms in successive sentences are I 135, <cite data-ref="Hdt. 8.21">Hdt. 8.21</cite>, 9. 48; <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 51d">P. Cr. 51d</cite>, Ph. 105 b, Phae. 259 a, Pr. 330 c-331 a, <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.11">D. 4.11</cite>, 18. 147-148. In <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.178">D. 18.178</cite> both the desired and the undesired alternative have <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> with the subjunctive.

**c.** Impossibilities may be expressed by <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> with the subjunctive. Thus, <span lang="grc">τί οὖν, ἂ̄ν εἴπωσιν οἱ νόμοι</span>; _what_, _then_, _if the laws say?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 50c">P. Cr. 50c</cite>; cp. <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyd. 299b">P. Eu. 299b</cite>, R. 610 a, 612 b (opt. in 359 c, 360 b), Ar. Aves 1642, <cite data-ref="Eur. Orest. 1593">E. Or. 1593</cite>, Phoen. 1216. Cp. [2329 a](#s2329).

### Third Form of Conditions: MORE VIVID FUTURE CONDITIONS

##### <a id="s2323"></a> §2323

More vivid future conditions have in the protasis <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> (<span lang="grc">ἤν, ἄ̄ν</span>) with the subjunctive; in the apodosis, the future indicative or any other form referring to future time.

<span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν ταῦτα ποιῇς</span> (<span lang="grc">ποιήσῃς</span>), <span lang="grc">καλῶς ποιήσεις</span> _if you do this, you will do well_.

##### <a id="s2324"></a> §2324

This form of condition corresponds to the use of _shall_ and _will_ in conditional sentences in older English (“if ye shall ask . . . I will do it”: St. John). <!-- page 524 --> Modern English substitutes the present for the more exact future in ordinary future conditions of this class; and often uses _shall_ in the protasis with an emotional force. The English present subjunctive, although somewhat rarely used in the modern language, corresponds more nearly to the Greek subjunctive (“if she be there, he shall not need”: Beaumont and Fletcher).—Since _if you do this_ may be expressed in Greek by <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν ταῦτα ποιῇς</span> or <span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ποιήσεις</span> ([2328](#s2328)), and by <span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ποιεῖς</span> ([2298](#s2298)), the difference in meaning is made clear only by the apodosis. The form <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν ταῦτα ποιῇς</span> in vivid future conditions must be distinguished from the same form in present general conditions (_if ever you do this_, [2337](#s2337)). <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν ταῦτά σοι δοκῇ, ποίει</span> may be particular or general: _if_ (or _if ever_) _this seems good to you, do it_.

##### <a id="s2325"></a> §2325

The present subjunctive views an act as continuing (not completed); the aorist subjunctive as simply occurring (completed). Neither tense has any time of itself. The aorist subjunctive may mark the action of the protasis as completed before the action of the principal clause (cp. the Lat. future perfect). Ingressive aorists ([1924](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1924)) retain their force in the subjunctive.

##### <a id="s2326"></a> §2326

The apodosis of the more vivid future condition is the future indicative or any other form of the simple sentence that refers to future time.

**a. Future Indicative**: <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν ζητῇς καλῶς, εὑρήσεις</span> _if you seek well, you shall find_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 503d">P. G. 503d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν δ᾽ ἔχωμεν χρήμαθ᾽, ἕξομεν φίλους</span> _if we have money, we shall have friends_ Men. Sent. 165, <span lang="grc">χάριν γε εἴσομαι, ἐὰ̄ν ἀκούητε</span> _I shall be grateful, if you listen_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 310a">P. Pr. 310a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἂ̄ν αὐτῷ διδῷς ἀργύριον καὶ πείθῃς αὐτόν, ποιήσει καὶ σὲ σοφόν</span> _if you give him money and persuade him, he will make you too wise_ 310 d, <span lang="grc">ἢν γὰρ τοῦτο λάβωμεν, οὐ δυνήσονται μένειν</span> _for if we take this, they will not be able to remain_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.4.41">X. A. 3.4.41</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν κύκλου ἐπὶ τῆς περιφερείᾱς ληφθῇ δύο τυχόντα σημεῖα, ἡ ἐπὶ τὰ σημεῖα ἐπιζευγνυμένη εὐθεῖα ἐντὸς πεσεῖται τ&lt;\*&gt;ῦ κύκλου</span> _if any two points be taken in the circumference of a circle, the straight line which joins them shall fall within the circle_ Euclid 3. 2.

**b. Primary Tenses** of the indicative other than the future. **Present** ([1879](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1879)): <span lang="grc">ἢν θάνῃς σύ, παῖς ὅδ᾽ ἐκφεύγει μόρον</span> _if thou art slain, yon boy escapes death_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Andr. 381">E. And. 381</cite>, <span lang="grc">δίδωσ᾽ ἑκὼν κτείνειν ἑαυτόν, ἢν τάδε ψευσθῇ λέγων</span> _freely he offers himself to death, if he lies in speaking thus_ (<span lang="grc">δίδωσι</span> = he says that he is ready) S. Phil. 1342. **Aorist**: see [1934](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1934), and cp. <span lang="grc">εἰ μέν κ᾽ αὖθι μένων Τρώων πόλιν ἀμφιμάχωμαι, ὤλετο μέν μοι νόστος</span> _if I tarry here and wage war about the city of the Trojans_, _my return home is lost for me_ I 413. **Perfect**: see [1950](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1950). Cp. “if I shall have an answer no directlier, I am gone”: Beaumont and Fletcher.

**c. Subjunctive** of exhortation, prohibition, or deliberation, and with <span lang="grc">μή</span> (<span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span>) of doubtful assertion ([1801](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1801)). Thus, <span lang="grc">μηδ᾽ ἄ̄ν τι ὠνῶμαι, ἔφη, ἢν πωλῇ νεώτερος τριά̄κοντα ἐτῶν, ἔρωμαι, ὁπόσου πωλεῖ</span>; _even if I am buying something, said he, am I not to ask_ ‘_what do you sell it for?_’ _if the seller is under thirty years of age?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.2.36">X. M. 1.2.36</cite>, <span lang="grc">κἂ̄ν φαινώμεθα ἄδικα αὐτὰ ἐργαζόμενοι, μὴ οὐ δέῃ ὑπολογίζεσθαι κτλ</span>. _and if we appear to do this unjustly_, _I rather think it may not be necessary to take notice_, etc. <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 48d">P. Cr. 48d</cite>.

**d. Optative** of wish, or potential optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (‘something _may_ happen’ instead of ‘something _will_ happen’). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἤν σε τοῦ λοιποῦ ποτ᾽ ἀφέλωμαι χρόνου</span>, <!-- page 525 --> . . . <span lang="grc">κάκιστ᾽ ἀπολοίμην</span> _if ever in the future I take them away from you, may I perish most vilely!_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Frogs 586">Ar. Ran. 586</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν κατὰ μέρος φυλάττωμεν . . ., ἧττον ἂν δύναιντο ἡμᾶς θηρᾶν οἱ πολέμιοι</span> _if we keep guard by turns, the enemy will_ (_would_) _be less able to harry us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.1.9">X. A. 5.1.9</cite>. See also [2356 a](#s2356).

**e. Imperative**, or infinitive for the imperative ([2013](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s2013)): <span lang="grc">ἢν πόλεμον αἱρῆσθε, μηκέτι ἥκετε δεῦρο ἄνευ ὅπλων</span> _if you choose war, do not come here again without your arms_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.2.13">X. C. 3.2.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">σὺ δ᾽, ἄ̄ν τι ἔχῃς βέλτῑόν ποθεν λαβεῖν, πειρᾶσθαι καὶ ἐμοὶ μεταδιδόναι</span> _but if you can find anything better from any quarter, try to communicate it to me too_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crat. 426b">P. Crat. 426b</cite>.

##### <a id="s2327"></a> §2327

**Homeric Constructions**.—**a.** <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> alone without <span lang="grc">κέ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the subjunctive with no appreciable difference from <span lang="grc">εἴ κε</span> (<span lang="grc">ἄν</span>): <span lang="grc">εἴ περ γάρ σε κατακτάνῃ, οὔ σ᾽ . . . κλαύσομαι</span> _for if he slay thee_, _I shall not bewail thee_ X 86. This construction occurs in lyric and dramatic poetry, and in Hdt., as <span lang="grc">δυστάλαινα τἄ̄ρ᾽ ἐγώ, εἴ σου στερηθῶ</span> _wretched indeed shall I be_, _if I am deprived of thee_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 1443">S. O. C. 1443</cite>. In Attic prose it is very rare and suspected (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.21">T. 6.21</cite>).

**b.** Subjunctive with <span lang="grc">κέ</span> in both protasis and apodosis (the anticipatory subjunctive, [1810](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1810)): <span lang="grc">εἰ δέ κε μὴ δώῃσιν, ἐγὼ δέ κεν αὐτὸς ἕλωμαι</span> _and if he do not give her up, then will I seize her myself_ A 324.

**c.** <span lang="grc">εἴ</span> (<span lang="grc">αἴ</span>) <span lang="grc">κε</span> with the future in protasis (rare): <span lang="grc">σοὶ . . . ὄνειδος ἔσσεται, εἴ κ᾽ Ἀχιλῆος . . . ἑταῖρον . . . κύνες ἑλκήσουσιν</span> _it will be a reproach unto thee, if the dogs drag the companion of Achilles_ P 557. Some read here the subjunctive.

##### <a id="s2328"></a> §2328

**Emotional Future Conditions**.—When the protasis expresses strong feeling, the future indicative with <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> is commonly used instead of <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> with the subjunctive, and may often be rendered by _hall_. The protasis commonly suggests something undesired, or feared, or intended independently of the speaker's will; the apodosis commonly conveys a threat, a warning, or an earnest appeal to the feelings. The apodosis is generally expressed by the future indicative, but other forms of 2326 are possible.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα λέξεις, ἐχθαρεῖ μὲν ἐξ ἐμοῦ</span> _if thou speakest thus, thou wilt be hated by me_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 93">S. Ant. 93</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ καθέξεις γλῶσσαν, ἔσται σοι κακά</span> _if you won't hold your tongue_, _there's trouble in store for you_ E. frag. 5, <span lang="grc">ἀποκτενεῖς γάρ, εἴ με γῆς ἔξω βαλεῖς</span> _for thou wilt slay me if thou shalt thrust me out of the land_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Phoen. 1621">E. Phoen. 1621</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ ὧδε στρατευσόμεθα, οὐ δυνησόμεθα μάχεσθαι</span> _if we keep the field thus, we shall not be able to fight_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.1.13">X. C. 6.1.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀ̄θλιώτατος ἂν γενοίμην</span> (potential optative), <span lang="grc">εἰ φυγὰς ἀδίκως καταστήσομαι</span> _I should become most wretched, were I to be driven unjustly into exile_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 7.41">L. 7.41</cite>.

**a.** When <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the future indicative is directly contrasted with <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> with the subjunctive, the former usually presents the unfavourable, the latter the favourable, alternative. Thus,

<span lang="grc">ἢν μὲν γὰρ ἐθέλωμεν ἀποθνῄσκειν ὑπὲρ τῶν δικαίων, εὐδοκιμήσομεν . . ., εἰ δὲ φοβησόμεθα τοὺς κινδύ̄νους, εἰς πολλὰ̄ς ταραχὰ̄ς καταστήσομεν ἡμᾶς αὐτούς</span> _if we are_ (_shall be_) _willing to die for the sake of justice, we shall gain renown; but if we are going to fear dangers, we shall bring ourselves into great confusion_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 6.107">I. 6.107</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.1.15">X. C. 4.1.15</cite>, <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 586">Ar. Nub. 586</cite>-591, <cite data-ref="Lys. 27.7">L. 27.7</cite>, <cite data-ref="Isoc. 12.237">I. 12.237</cite>, 15. 130, 17. 9, <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.17">D. 8.17</cite>, 18. 176, 27. 20-22. Both constructions are rarely used in successive clauses with<!-- page 526 --> out any essential difference (<cite data-ref="Xen. Apol. 6">X. Ap. 6</cite>). <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> with the subjunctive, when used in threats or warnings, is a milder form of statement than <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the future (<cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.71">Hdt. 1.71</cite>). An unfavourable alternative _may_ thus be expressed by <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> with the subjunctive (A 135-137, <cite data-ref="Hdt. 3.36">Hdt. 3.36</cite>, <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.254">Aes. 3.254</cite>).

**b.** <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the future indicative may have a modal force like that of <span lang="grc">δεῖ</span> or <span lang="grc">μέλλω</span> (_am to, must_) with the infinitive: <span lang="grc">βαρεῖα</span> (<span lang="grc">κήρ</span>), <span lang="grc">εἰ τέκνον δαΐξω</span> _hard is fate_, _if I must slay my child_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 208">A. Ag. 208</cite>. The future of present intention ([2301](#s2301)) is different.

### Fourth Form of Conditions: LESS VIVID FUTURE CONDITIONS

##### <a id="s2329"></a> §2329

Less vivid future conditions (_should . . . would_ conditions) have in the protasis <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the optative, in the apodosis <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the optative.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ποιοίης, καλῶς ἂν ποιοίης</span> or <span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ποιήσειας, καλῶς ἂν ποιήσειας</span> _if you should do this, you would do well_.

<span lang="grc">εἴης φορητὸς οὐκ ἄν, εἰ πρά̄σσοις καλῶς</span> _thou wouldst be unendurable shouldst thou be prosperous_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 979">A. Pr. 979</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ δ᾽ ἀναγκαῖον εἴη ἀδικεῖν ἢ ἀδικεῖσθαι, ἑλοίμην ἂν μᾶλλον ἀδικεῖσθαι ἢ ἀδικεῖν</span> _but if it should be necessary to do wrong or be wronged_, _I should prefer to be wronged than to do wrong_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 469c">P. G. 469c</cite>, <span lang="grc">δεινὰ ἂν εἴην εἰργασμένος, . . . εἰ λίποιμι τὴν τάξιν</span> _I should be in the state of having committed a dreadful deed, if I were to desert my post_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 28d">P. A. 28d</cite>.

**a.** Anything physically impossible may be represented as supposable, hence this construction may be used of what is contrary to fact. Thus, <span lang="grc">φαίη δ᾽ ἂν ἡ θανοῦσά γ᾽ ει᾽ φωνὴν λάβοι</span> _the dead would speak if gifted with a voice_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 548">S. El. 548</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 37">A. Ag. 37</cite>, <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 361a">P. Pr. 361a</cite>, Eu. 299 d, and see [2311 a](#s2311), [2322](#s2322) c.

##### <a id="s2330"></a> §2330

Conditional sentences of this class arose partly from optatives of wish ([1814](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1814), [1815](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1815)), partly from potential optatives ([1824](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1824)). Cp. <span lang="grc">εἴθ᾽ ὧς ἡβώοιμι . . . τῷ κε τάχ᾽ ἀντήσειε μάχης . . . Ἕκτωρ</span> _would that I were thus young . . . in that case Hector would soon find his combat_ H 157; see also <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 14.193"><span lang="grc">ξ</span> 193</cite>.

##### <a id="s2331"></a> §2331

The present optative views an action as continuing (not completed); the aorist optative, as simply occurring (completed). (The future optative is never used except to represent a future indicative in indirect discourse.) The perfect (rare) denotes completion with resulting state. In <cite data-ref="Hdt. 7.214">Hdt. 7.214</cite> it is used vaguely of the _past:_ <span lang="grc">εἰδείη μὲν γὰρ ἂν . . . ταύτην τὴν ἀτ ραπὸν Ὀνήτης, εἰ τῇ χώρᾳ πολλὰ ὡμῑληκὼς εἴη</span> _for Onetes might know of this path . . . if he had been well acquainted with the country_.

##### <a id="s2332"></a> §2332

English _would_ is equivocal, being used either in the translation of <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the optative or of <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the past indicative ([2302](#s2302)). Thus, cp. <span lang="grc">εἴ τίς σε ἤρετο . . ., τί ἂν ἀπεκρἰ̄νω</span>; _if any one had asked you_ . . ., _what would you have replied?_ with <span lang="grc">εἰ οὖν τις ἡμᾶς . . . ἔροιτο . . ., τί ἂν αὐτῷ ἀποκρῑναίμεθα</span>; _if then some one should_ (_were to_) _ask us_ . . ., _what would_ (_should_) _we reply to him?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 311b">P. Pr. 311b</cite>, d. _If I were_ may be used to translate both <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the optative and <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the past indicative. English shows examples of _were_ in the protasis <!-- page 527 --> followed by _would_, _shall_, _will_, _is_ (_was_, etc.). _Were_ occurs also in apodosis (“should he be roused out of sleep to-night, it were not well”: Shelley).

##### <a id="s2333"></a> §2333

The apodosis has the optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> in _wishes_.

<span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν συμβουλεύοιμι ἃ βέλτιστά μοι δοκεῖ, πολλά μοι καὶ ἀγαθὰ γένοιτο</span> _if I should give the advice that seems best to me, may many blessings fall to my lot_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.6.4">X. A. 5.6.4</cite>.

On the optative with <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> followed by other forms of the apodosis, see [2359](#s2359).

##### <a id="s2334"></a> §2334

**Homeric Constructions**.—**a.** In the protasis, <span lang="grc">εἴ κε</span> (<span lang="grc">εἰ ἄν</span>) with the optative with the same force as <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> alone. This use is exclusively Homeric. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐ μὲν γάρ τι κακώτερον ἄλλο πάθοιμι, οὐδ᾽ εἴ κεν τοῦ πατρὸς ἀποφθιμένοιο πυθοίμην</span> _for I could not suffer anything worse, not even if I should learn of my father's death_ T 321. On <span lang="grc">εἰ ἄν</span> in Attic, see [2353](#s2353).

**b.** In the apodosis, a primary tense of the indicative: the present (<cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 7.52"><span lang="grc">η</span> 52</cite>), the future (I [388](../../part-2-inflection/09-verbs/00-introduction.md#s388)), the future with <span lang="grc">κέ</span> (<cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 12.345"><span lang="grc">μ</span> 345</cite>: but this may be the aorist subjunctive).

**c.** In the apodosis, the hortatory subjunctive (<cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 23.893"><span lang="grc">Ψ</span> 893</cite>), the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or <span lang="grc">κέ</span> (<cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 11.386"><span lang="grc">Λ</span> 386</cite>).

**d.** In the apodosis, the optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> not in a wish, but with the same force as the optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>. See T 321 in **a.**

**e.** For <span lang="grc">κέ</span> with the optative in the apodosis where we should expect, in Homeric and Attic Greek, a past indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (<span lang="grc">κέ</span>) in an unreal condition, see [2311 b](#s2311).

## GENERAL CONDITIONS

##### <a id="s2335"></a> §2335

General conditions refer indefinitely to any act or series of acts that are supposed to occur or to have occurred at any time; and without any implication as to fulfilment.

The _if_ clause has the force of _if ever_ (_whenever_), the conclusion expresses a repeated or habitual action or a general truth.

##### <a id="s2336"></a> §2336

Any simple or unreal condition of present or past time, or any future condition, may refer to a customary or frequently repeated act or to a general truth. But for the present and past only (when nothing is implied as to fulfilment) there are two forms of expression: either a _special_ kind of conditional sentence or (less frequently) the _simple_ condition, as regularly in English and in Latin:

**Present**. Protasis: <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν ποτε</span>) with the subjunctive; apodosis: the present indicative ([2337](#s2337)).

Protasis: <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> ( = <span lang="grc">εἴ ποτε</span>) with the present indicative; apodosis: the present indicative ([2298 c](#s2298), [2342](#s2342)).

**Past**. Protasis: <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the optative; apodosis: the imperfect indicative ([2340](#s2340)).

Protasis: <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the imperfect; apodosis: the imperfect ([2298 c](#s2298), [2342](#s2342)). <!-- page 528 -->

**a.** By reason of the past apodosis, the optative in the protasis refers to the past. Only in this use (and when the optative in indirect discourse represents a past indicative) does the optative refer distinctly to the past.

**b.** The present subjunctive and optative view the action as continuing (not completed); the aorist subjunctive and optative, as simply occurring (completed). The tenses of the protasis have no time of themselves, but usually the action of the present is relatively contemporaneous with, the action of the aorist relatively antecedent to, the action of the main verb.

**c.** The indicative forms in the protasis are more common in temporal and relative sentences. Observe that it is the character of the _apodosis_ alone which distinguishes the special kind of general condition from the two forms of future conditions.

### Fifth Form of Conditions: PRESENT GENERAL CONDITIONS

##### <a id="s2337"></a> §2337

Present general conditions have, in the protasis, <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> (<span lang="grc">ἤν, ἄ̄ν</span>) with the subjunctive; in the apodosis, the present indicative or an equivalent. <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν ταῦτα ποιῇς</span> (<span lang="grc">ποιήσῃς</span>), <span lang="grc">σὲ ἐπαινῶ</span> _if_ ever _you do this_, _I_ always _praise you_. The conclusion holds true of any time or of all time.

<span lang="grc">ἢν δ᾽ ἐγγὺς ἔλθῃ θάνατος, οὐδεὶς βούλεται θνῄσκειν</span> _but if death draws near, no one wishes to die_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Alc. 671">E. Alc. 671</cite>, <span lang="grc">γελᾷ δ᾽ ὁ μῶρος, κἄ̄ν τι μὴ γελοῖον ᾖ</span> _the fool laughs even if there is nothing to laugh at_ Men. Sent. 108, <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν ἴσοις ἴσα προστεθῇ, τὰ ὅλα ἐστὶν ἴσα</span> _if equals be added to equals, the wholes are equal_ Euclid, Ax. 2.

##### <a id="s2338"></a> §2338

The **gnomic aorist** is equivalent to the present indicative in apodosis. <span lang="grc">ἢν δέ τις τούτων τι παραβαίνῃ, ζημίᾱν αὐτοῖς ἐπέθεσαν</span> _but if any one_ ever _transgresses any one of these regulations, they_ always _impose punishment upon them_ (_him_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.2.2">X. C. 1.2.2</cite>.

##### <a id="s2339"></a> §2339

Homer and Pindar prefer <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> to <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> or <span lang="grc">εἴ κε</span> (A [81](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s81)); and this <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> is sometimes found in Attic poetry (<cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 710">S. Ant. 710</cite>). <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is more often absent in general conditions than in vivid future conditions.

### Sixth Form of Conditions: PAST GENERAL CONDITIONS

##### <a id="s2340"></a> §2340

Past general conditions have, in the protasis, <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the optative; in the apodosis, the imperfect indicative or an equivalent. <span lang="grc">εἰ ταῦτα ποιοίης</span> (<span lang="grc">ποιήσειας</span>), <span lang="grc">σὲ ἐπῄνουν</span> _if_ ever _you did this_, _I_ always _praised you_.

<span lang="grc">εἴ πού τι ὁρῴη βρωτόν, διεδίδου</span> _if_ ever _he saw anything to eat anywhere, he_ always _distributed it_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.5.8">X. A. 4.5.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ δέ τις καὶ ἀντείποι, εὐθὺς . . . ἐτεθνήκει</span> _but if any one even made an objection, he was promptly put to death_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.66">T. 8.66</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν ἐπίοιεν οἱ Ἀθηναῖοι, ὑπεχώρουν, εἰ δ᾽ ἀναχωροῖεν, ἐπέκειντο</span> _if the Athenians advanced_, _they retreated; if they retired, they fell upon them_ 7.79, <span lang="grc">ἐτί̄μᾱ δ᾽ εἴ τι καλὸν πρά̄ττοιεν, παρί̄στατο δ᾽ εἴ τις συμφορὰ̄ συμβαίνοι</span> _he honoured_ them _if_ ever <!-- page 529 --> _they performed some noble action, and stood by_ them _in times of misfortune_ (lit. _if any misfortune befell_) X. Ag. 7.3.

**a.** The optative is here sometimes called the _iterative_ optative. This mood has however no iterative force in itself, the idea of repetition being derived solely from the context. In Homer the iterative optative after <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> (found only <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 24.768"><span lang="grc">Ω</span> 768</cite>) is an extension of the iterative optative in temporal clauses where this use originated.

##### <a id="s2341"></a> §2341

The **iterative imperfect** or **aorist** with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> ([1894](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1894), [1933](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1933)): <span lang="grc">εἰ δέ τις αὐτῷ περί του ἀντιλέγοι . . ., ἐπὶ τὴν ὑπόθεσιν ἐπανῆγεν ἂν πάντα τὸν λόγον</span> _if_ ever _any one opposed him on any matter, he would_ always _bring the entire discussion back to the main point_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.6.13">X. M. 4.6.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἴ τις αὐτῷ δοκοίη . . . βλᾱκεύειν, ἐκλεγόμενος τὸν ἐπιτήδειον ἔπαισεν ἄν</span> _if_ ever _any one seemed to be lagging, he would_ always _pick out the likely man and strike him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.11">X. A. 2.3.11</cite>. These cases are not to be confused with the apodoses of unreal conditions.

### INDICATIVE FORM OF GENERAL CONDITIONS

##### <a id="s2342"></a> §2342

Present: protasis, <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the present; apodosis, the present. Past: protasis, <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the imperfect; apodosis, the imperfect.

The protasis usually has <span lang="grc">εἴ τις, εἴ τι</span> (cp. <span lang="grc">ὅστις, ὅ τι</span>) with the indicative, as <span lang="grc">εἴ τις δύο ἢ καί τι πλείους ἡμέρᾱς λογίζεται, μάταιός ἐστιν</span> _if_ ever _any one counts upon two or even perchance on more days, he is rash_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Trach. 944">S. Tr. 944</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐλευθέρως δὲ . . . πολῑτεύομεν . . ., οὐ δι᾽ ὀργῆς τὸν πέλας, εἰ καθ᾽ ἡδονήν τι δρᾷ, ἔχοντες</span> _we are tolerant in our public life, not being angry at our neighbour if he acts as he likes_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.37">T. 2.37</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ μὲν ἀγώγιμα, εἴ τι ἦγον, ἐξαιρούμενοι φύλακας καθί̄στασαν</span> _taking out the cargoes, if_ the vessels _carried anything, they appointed guards_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.1.16">X. A. 5.1.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἴ τίς τι ἐπηρώτᾱ, ἀπεκρί̄νοντο</span> _if_ ever _anybody asked any questions_ (_for additional information_) _they answered_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.10">T. 7.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐμί̄σει οὐκ εἴ τις κακῶς πάσχων ἠμύ̄νετο, ἀλλ᾽ εἴ τις εὐεργετούμενος ἀχάριστος φαίνοιτο</span> ([2340](#s2340)) _he hated not the man who_, _on suffering ill, retaliated_, _but him who seemed ungrateful though he had received kindness_ X. Ag. 11. 3.

## DIFFERENT FORMS OF CONDITIONAL SENTENCES IN THE SAME SENTENCE

##### <a id="s2343"></a> §2343

The same period may show different forms of conditional sentences according to the exigency of the thought.

<span lang="grc">ταὐτὸ τοίνυν τοῦτ᾽ ἂν ἐποίησε Φίλιππος, εἴ τινα τούτων εἶδε δίκην δόντα, καὶ νῦν, ἂ̄ν ἴδῃ, ποιήσει</span> _this very same thing then Philip would have done, if he had seen any one of these men being punished; and will do so now, if he sees it_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.138">D. 19.138</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ οὖν ἐπιθῡμεῖς εὐδοκιμεῖν . . . , πειρῶ κατεργάσασθαι ὡς μάλιστα τὸ εἰδέναι ἃ βούλει πρά̄ττειν· ἐὰ̄ν γὰρ τούτῳ διενέγκᾱς τῶν ἄλλων ἐπιχειρῇς τὰ τῆς πόλεως πρά̄ττειν, οὐκ ἂν θαυμάσαιμι εἰ πάνυ ῥᾳδίως τύχοις ὧν ἐπιθῡμεῖς</span> _if then you desire to enjoy an honourable fame_ . . . , _try to acquire as far as possible the knowledge of what you wish to do; for if, differing in this regard from other men_, _you attempt to deal with affairs of state_, _I should not be surprised if you were to attain the object of your ambition with great ease_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.6.18">X. M. 3.6.18</cite>.

## VARIATIONS FROM THE ORDINARY FORMS AND MEANINGS OF CONDITIONAL SENTENCES

<!-- page 530 -->

### MODIFICATIONS OF THE PROTASIS

##### <a id="s2344"></a> §2344

**Substitutions for the Protasis**.—For the protasis with <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> there may be substituted a participle, often in the genitive absolute ([2067](../22-the-participle.md#s2067), [2070](../22-the-participle.md#s2070)), an adverb, a prepositional phrase, a relative clause ([2560](../30-adjective-clauses-relative-clauses-2488-2573.md#s2560)), or some other single word or phrase. The present participle represents the imperfect, as the perfect represents the pluperfect.

<span lang="grc">πῶς δῆτα δίκης οὔσης</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἰ δίκη ἐστίν</span>) <span lang="grc">ὁ Ζεὺς οὐκ ἀπόλωλεν τὸν πατέρ᾽ αὑτοῦ δήσᾱς</span>; _how_, _pray_, _if there is_ any _justice_, _has Zeus not perished since he bound his own father?_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 904">Ar. Nub. 904</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ ἦν μοι δήπου βιωτὸν τοῦτο ποιήσαντα</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἰ ἐποίησα</span>) _for of course life had not been worth living if I had done this_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.120">D. 21.120</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ ἂν ἐβλήθη ἀτρεμίζων καὶ μὴ διατρέχων</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἰ ἠτρέμιζε καὶ μὴ διέτρεχε</span>) _for he would not have been hit if he had been keeping quiet and not running across_ Ant. 3. <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 2.5"><span lang="grc">β</span>. 5</cite>, <span lang="grc">δικαίως ἂν ἀπέθανον</span> _I should justly_ (i.e. if I had met with my deserts) _have been put to death_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.209">D. 18.209</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐμοὶ δὲ ἀρκοῦν ἂν ἐδόκει εἶναι</span> _for myself_ (i.e. if I had to decide) _it would seem to be sufficient_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.35">T. 2.35</cite>, <span lang="grc">διά γε ὑ̄μᾶς αὐτοὺς</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἰ ὑ̄μεῖς αὐτοὶ μόνοι ἦτε</span>) <span lang="grc">πάλαι ἂν ἀπολώλειτε</span> _if you had been left to yourselves, you would have perished long ago_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.49">D. 18.49</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὀλοῦμαι μὴ μαθών</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν μὴ μάθω</span>) _I shall be undone if I don't learn_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 792">Ar. Nub. 792</cite>, <span lang="grc">νῑκῶντες</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἰ νῑκῷεν</span>) <span lang="grc">μὲν οὐδένα ἂν κατακάνοιεν, ἡττηθέντων</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἰ ἡττηθεῖεν</span>) <span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς ἂν λειφθείη</span> _should they be victorious they would kill no one, but if defeated no one would be left_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.2">X. A. 3.1.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὕτω</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἰ οὕτως ἔχοιεν</span>) <span lang="grc">γὰρ πρὸς τὸ ἐπιέναι τοῖς ἐναντίοις εὐψῡχότατοι ἂν εἶεν</span> _for thus they would be most courageous in regard to attacking the enemy_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.11">T. 2.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽ ἂν δικαίως ἐς κακὸν πέσοιμί τι</span> _nor should I justly come to any trouble_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 240">S. Ant. 240</cite>.

**a.** Sometimes the protasis has to be supplied from what precedes (example in [1825](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1825)); or from a main clause with <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span>, which follows: <span lang="grc">οὐδέ κεν αὐτὸς ὑπέκφυγε κῆρα μέλαιναν· ἀλλ᾽ Ἥφαιστος ἔρυτο</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ ἔρυτο</span>) _nor would he himself have escaped black fate; but Hephaestus guarded him_ E 23 (cp. <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.24">X. A. 3.2.24</cite>-[25](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s25)).

##### <a id="s2345"></a> §2345

**Verb of the Protasis Omitted**.—The verb of the protasis is usually omitted when the apodosis has the same verb. The protasis is often introduced by <span lang="grc">εἴ τις, εἴ ποτε, εἴπερ</span> (<span lang="grc">ποτέ</span>).

<span lang="grc">εἴ τις καὶ ἄλλος ἀνήρ, καὶ Κῦρος ἄξιός ἐστι θαυμάζεσθαι</span> _if any other man_ (is worthy to be admired), _Cyrus_, _too_, _is worthy to be admired_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.1.6">X. C. 5.1.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">φημὶ δεῖν . . . τῷ πολέμῳ προσέχειν, εἴπερ ποτέ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἔδει</span>), <span lang="grc">καὶ νῦν</span> _I say that we must now, if ever_, _apply ourselves to the war_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 1.6">D. 1.6</cite>.

##### <a id="s2346"></a> §2346

So with certain special phrases:

**a. <span lang="grc">εἰ μή</span>** (_if not_) _except:_ <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ . . . ὁρῶμεν εἰ μὴ ὀλίγους τούτους ἀνθρώπους</span> _for we do not see_ any _except a few men yonder_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.7.5">X. A. 4.7.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ ἄν ποτε ἐξηῦρον ὀρθῶς τὰ μετέωρα πρά̄γματα, εἰ μὴ κρεμάσᾱς τὸ νόημα</span> _for I could never have discovered aright things celestial, except by suspending the intellect_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 229">Ar. Nub. 229</cite>. So <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν μή</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 24.45">D. 24.45</cite> (in a decree).

**b. <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ εἰ</span>** (_if not if, unless if_) _except if:_ <span lang="grc">ἐπρά̄χθη τε οὐδὲν ἀπ᾽ αὐτῶν ἔργον ἀξιόλογον, εἰ μὴ εἴ τι πρὸς τοὺς περιοίκους τοὺς αὐτῶν ἑκάστοις</span> _and nothing noteworthy_ <!-- page 531 --> _was done on their part except it might be_ (lit. _except if_ there was done) _something between each of them and his neighbours_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.17">T. 1.17</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ</span> is adverbial.

**c. <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ διά</span>** (_if not on account of_) _except for:_ (<span lang="grc">οὐ</span>) <span lang="grc">Μιλτιάδην . . . εἰς τὸ βάραθρον ἐμβαλεῖν ἐψηφίσαντο, καὶ εἰ μὴ διὰ τὸν πρύτανιν, ἐνέπεσεν ἄν</span>; _did they not vote to throw Miltiades into the pit, and except for the prytan would he not have been thrown there?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 516e">P. G. 516e</cite>. With <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ διά</span> the ellipsis (which was not conscious to the Greeks) is to be supplied by the negatived predicate of the main clause (here <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἐνέπεσεν</span>).

**d. <span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ μή</span>** (_but if not_ = _si minus, sin aliter_) _otherwise_, in alternatives, introduces a supposition opposed to something just said: <span lang="grc">ἀπῄτει τὰ τῶν Καλχηδονίων χρήματα· εἰ δὲ μή, πολεμήσειν ἔφη αὐτοῖς</span> _he demanded back the property of the Calchedonians; otherwise_ (i.e. if they should not restore it: <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ ἀποδοῖεν</span>) _he said that he should make war upon them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.3.3">X. H. 1.3.3</cite>.

N. 1.—<span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ μή</span> often occurs even where the preceding clause is negative and we expect <span lang="grc">εἰ δέ</span>, as <span lang="grc">μὴ ποιήσῃς ταῦτα· εἰ δὲ μὴ . . . αἰτίᾱν ἕξεις</span> _do not do this; but if you do, you will have the blame_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.1.8">X. A. 7.1.8</cite>. Conversely <span lang="grc">εἰ δέ</span>, where we expect <span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ μή</span>, as <span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν βούλεται, ἑψέτω· εἰ δ᾽, ὅ τι βούλεται, τοῦτο ποιείτω</span> _if he wishes_, _let him boil me; otherwise_, _let him do whatever he wishes_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyd. 285c">P. Eu. 285c</cite>.

N. 2.—<span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ μή</span> is used where (after a preceding <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span>) we expect <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν δὲ μή</span>, as <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν μέν τι ὑ̄μῖν δοκῶ ἀληθὲς λέγειν, ξυνομολογήσατε· εἰ δὲ μή, ἀντιτείνετε</span> _if I seem to you to speak the truth, agree with me; otherwise_, _oppose me_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 91c">P. Ph. 91c</cite>.

N. 3.—The verb of the apodosis of the first of the alternatives is often omitted: <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν μὲν ἑκὼν πείθηται</span> (_scil_. <span lang="grc">καλῶς ἕξει</span>): <span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ μὴ . . . εὐθύ̄νουσιν ἀπειλαῖς</span> _if he willingly obeys_ (_it will be well_)_; otherwise they straighten him by threats_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 325d">P. Pr. 325d</cite>.

##### <a id="s2347"></a> §2347

On <span lang="grc">ὡς εἰ</span> in comparative conditional clauses see [2484](13-clauses-of-comparison.md#s2484).

##### <a id="s2348"></a> §2348

In the Homeric <span lang="grc">εἰ δ᾽ ἄγε</span> _come now, well!_ <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> probably has the force of an interjectional or demonstrative adverb (cp. Lat. _eia age_). Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ δ᾽ ἄγε τοι κεφαλῇ κατανεύσομαι</span> _come now! I will nod assent to thee with my head_ A 524.

##### <a id="s2349"></a> §2349

**Omission of the Protasis**.—The potential optative, and the indicative, with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> stand in independent sentences; in many cases a protasis may be supplied either from the context or generally; in other cases there was probably no conscious ellipsis at all; and in others there was certainly no ellipsis. Cp. [1785](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1785), [1825](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1825).

<span lang="grc">ποῦ δῆτ᾽ ἂν εἶεν οἱ ξένοι</span>; _where_, _pray_ (should I inquire) _would the strangers be found to be?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 1450">S. El. 1450</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀριθμὸν δὲ γράψαι . . . οὐκ ἂν ἐδυνάμην ἀκριβῶς</span> _but to give the number accurately I should not be able_ (if I were trying) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.68">T. 5.68</cite>, <span lang="grc">δεινὸν οὖν ἦν ψεύσασθαι</span> _it had been terrible to break my word_ (if it had been possible) <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.172">D. 19.172</cite>.

### MODIFICATIONS OF THE APODOSIS

##### <a id="s2350"></a> §2350

The apodosis may be expressed in a participle or infinitive with or without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> as the construction may require; cp. [1846](../20-the-verb-voices/09-infinitive-and-participle-with-alphanu.md#s1846), [1848](../20-the-verb-voices/09-infinitive-and-participle-with-alphanu.md#s1848).

<span lang="grc">αἰτεῖ αὐτὸν εἰς δισχῑλίους ξένους καὶ τριῶν μηνῶν μισθόν, ὡς οὕτως περιγενόμενος</span> (= <span lang="grc">περιγενοίμην</span>) <span lang="grc">ἂν τῶν ἀντιστασιωτῶν</span> _he asked him for pay for two thousand mercenaries and for three months, stating that thus he would get the better of his_ <!-- page 532 --> _adversaries_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.10">X. A. 1.1.10</cite>. (Here <span lang="grc">οὕτως</span> represents the protasis, 2344.) <span lang="grc">οὐδενὸς ἀντειπόντος διὰ τὸ μὴ ἀνασχέσθαι ἂν τὴν ἐκκλησίᾱν</span> _no one spoke in opposition because the assembly would not have suffered it_ (= <span lang="grc">εἰ ἀντεῖπε, οὐκ ἂν ἠνέσχετο ἡ ἐκκλησίᾱ</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.4.20">X. H. 1.4.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> (<span lang="grc">Τεγέᾱ</span>) <span lang="grc">σφίσι προσγένοιτο, νομίζοντες ἅπᾱσαν ἂν ἔχειν Πελοπόννησον</span> _they thought that, if Tegea too should come over to them, they would have the whole of the Peloponnese_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.32">T. 5.32</cite>. See [2616](../31-dependent-substantive-clauses-2574-2635.md#s2616).

##### <a id="s2351"></a> §2351

**Verb of the Apodosis Omitted**.—The verb of the apodosis is often omitted, and especially when the protasis has the same verb (cp. [2345](#s2345)). Here a potential optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is represented by <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> alone ([1764 a](../20-the-verb-voices/06-the-particle-alphanu.md#s1764), [1766](../20-the-verb-voices/06-the-particle-alphanu.md#s1766) a). Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ δή τῳ σοφώτερός του φαίην εἶναι, τούτῳ ἄν</span> (<span lang="grc">φαίην εἶναι</span>) _if I should say that in any respect I am wiser than any one_, (_I should say_) _in this_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 29b">P. A. 29b</cite>. Also in other cases, as <span lang="grc">τί δῆτ᾽ ἄν</span> (<span lang="grc">λέγοις</span>), <span lang="grc">ἕτερον εἰ πύθοιο Σωκράτους φρόντισμα</span>; _what then would_ (_you say_), _if you should hear another excogitation of Socrates?_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 154">Ar. Nub. 154</cite>. On <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ εἰ, ὥσπερ ἂν εἰ, ὡς εἰ</span>, see [1766 a](../20-the-verb-voices/06-the-particle-alphanu.md#s1766), [2478](13-clauses-of-comparison.md#s2478), 2484.

##### <a id="s2352"></a> §2352

**Omission of the Apodosis**.—**a.** When the conclusion is _it is well_ (<span lang="grc">καλῶς ἔχει</span>) or the like, it is often omitted. So often when the second of alternative opposing suppositions is expressed by <span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ μή</span> ([2346 d](#s2346), N. 3). Cp. “yet now, if thou wilt forgive this sin, —: and if not, blot me . . . out of thy book” (Exodus 32. [32](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s32)).

**b.** When we should introduce the conclusion by _know that_ or _I tell you:_ <span lang="grc">εἰ καὶ οἴει με ἀδικοῦντά τι ἄγεσθαι, οὔτε ἔπαιον οὐδένα οὔτε ἔβαλλον</span> _if you possibly think that I was taken for some wrong-doing_, know that _I neither struck nor hit any one_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.6.27">X. A. 6.6.27</cite>. Here the apodosis might be introduced by <span lang="grc">σκέψασθε, ἐνθῡμήθητε</span>, etc.

**c.** Sometimes when the protasis is merely parenthetical: <span lang="grc">ὁ χρῡσός, εἰ βούλοιο τἀ̄ληθῆ λέγειν, ἔκτεινε τὸν ἐμὸν παῖδα</span> _it was the gold—wouldst thou only tell the truth—that slew my child_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hec. 1206">E. Hec. 1206</cite>.

**d.** In passionate speech for rhetorical effect (aposiopēsis, [3015](../37-some-grammatical-and-rhetorical-figures.md#s3015)): <span lang="grc">εἴ περ γάρ κ᾽ ἐθέλῃσιν Ὀλύμπιος ἀστεροπητὴς ἐξ ἑδέων στυφελίξαι· ὁ γὰρ πολὺ φέρτατός ἐστιν</span> _for if indeed the Olympian lord of the lightning will to thrust us out from our habitations_, thrust us he will; _for he is by far the most powerful_ A 581.

**e.** There is properly no omission of an apodosis after clauses with <span lang="grc">εἰ, εἰ γάρ, εἴθε</span>, etc., in _wishes_ (see [1816](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1816)). In such clauses it is often _possible_ to find an apodosis in an appended final clause: <span lang="grc">ποτανὰ̄ν εἴ μέ τις θεῶν κτίσαι, διπόταμον ἵνα πόλιν μόλω</span> _if only some one of the gods were to make me winged so that I might come to the city of twin rivers!_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Supp. 621">E. Supp. 621</cite>.

### PROTASIS AND APODOSIS COMBINED

##### <a id="s2353"></a> §2353

**<span lang="grc">εἰ</span> and <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> both in Protasis**.—The potential optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or the unreal indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, standing as the apodosis in the conditional clause with <span lang="grc">εἰ</span>, is the apodosis of another protasis expressed or understood.

**a. Potential Optative**.—<span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ μὴν εἴ γε μηδὲ δοῦλον ἀκρατῆ δεξαίμεθ᾽ ἄν, πῶς οὐκ ἄξιον αὐτόν γε φυλάξασθαι τοιοῦτον γενέσθαι</span>; _and yet indeed if we would not accept even a slave who was intemperate, how is it not right for a man_ (the master) _to guard against becoming so himself?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.5.3">X. M. 1.5.3</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">δεξαίμεθα</span> is <!-- page 533 --> the protasis with <span lang="grc">εἰ</span>; and also, with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, the apodosis to an understood protasis (e.g. if we should think of so doing). The verb of the protasis may be contained in a participle, as <span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ μηδεὶς ἂν ὑ̄μῶν ἀξιώσειε ζῆν ἀποστερούμενος τῆς πατρίδος, προσήκει κτλ</span>. _but if no one of you should think life worth having if he were to be deprived of his country, it is right_, etc. <cite data-ref="Isoc. 6.25">I. 6.25</cite>. Such clauses form simple present conditions (_if it is true that we would accept_, etc.). The verb following the compressed condition stands usually in the present, at times in the future, indicative. <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.55">X. C. 3.3.55</cite>: <span lang="grc">θαυμάζοιμι ἂν . . . εἰ ἂν ὠφελήσειε</span> is an exception.

**b. Unreal Indicative**.—<span lang="grc">εἰ τοίνυν τοῦτο ἰσχῡρὸν ἦν ἂν τούτῳ τεκμήριον . . ., κἀ̄μοὶ γενέσθω τεκμήριον</span> _if then this would have been strong evidence for him_ (_if he had been able to bring it forward_), _let it be evidence for me too_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 49.58">D. 49.58</cite>. This is a present condition (_if it is true that this would_, etc.) except in so far as the unexpressed protasis refers to the past. Such conditions may also be past.

N. 1.—The real protasis is: _if it is_ (or _was_) _the case that something could now_ (or _hereafter_) _be_ (or _could have been_), _it follows that_.

N. 2.—In some of these cases, <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> has almost the force of <span lang="grc">ἐπεί</span> _since_ (<cite data-ref="Dem. 49.58">D. 49.58</cite>).

##### <a id="s2354"></a> §2354

<span lang="grc">**ει᾽**, **ἐά̄ν**</span>, **on the chance that**.—<span lang="grc">εἰ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> may set forth the motive for the action or feeling expressed by the apodosis, and with the force of _on the chance that, in case that, in the hope that, if haply_.

After primary tenses in the apodosis, we have <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the indicative or <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> (<span lang="grc">πως</span>) with the subjunctive; after secondary tenses, <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the optative or, occasionally, <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> (<span lang="grc">πως</span>) with the subjunctive. Homer has sometimes the optative after primary tenses. The reference is to the future as in final clauses.

The protasis here depends, not on the apodosis proper, but on the idea of purpose or desire suggested by the thought. The accomplishment of the purpose may be desired or not desired, and by the subject either of the apodosis or of the protasis.

<span lang="grc">νῦν αὖτ᾽ ἐγχείῃ πειρήσομαι, αἴ κε τύχωμι</span> _but now I will make trial with my spear on the chance_ (_in the hope_) _that I may hit thee_ E279, <span lang="grc">ἄκουσον καὶ ἐμοῦ, ἐά̄ν σοι ἔτι ταὐτὰ δοκῇ</span> _listen to me too on the chance_ (_in the hope_) _that you may still have the same opinion_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 358b">P. R. 358b</cite>, <span lang="grc">πορευόμενοι ἐς τὴν Ἀσίᾱν ὡς βασιλέᾱ, εἴ πως πείσειαν αὐτόν</span> _going into Asia to the king in the hope that somehow they might persuade him_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.67">T. 2.67</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρὸς τὴν πόλιν, εἰ ἐπιβοηθοῖεν, ἐχώρουν</span> _they advanced toward the city on the chance that they_ (the citizens) _should make a sally_ 6. 100.

N.—This use is to be distinguished from that of <span lang="grc">εἰ ἄρα</span> _if perchance_, <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ ἄρα</span> _unless perchance_ (often ironical).

**a.** This construction should be distinguished from cases like <span lang="grc">ἐπιβουλεύουσιν . . . ἐξελθεῖν . . ., ἢν δύνωνται βιάσασθαι</span> _they planned to get out, if they might make their way by force_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.20">T. 3.20</cite>, where we have implied indirect discourse (<span lang="grc">ἐξέλθωμεν, ἢν δυνώμεθα βιάσασθαι</span>).

**b.** Homer uses this construction as an object clause in dependence on <span lang="grc">οἶδα, εἶδον</span>, or on a _verb of saying_. Thus <span lang="grc">τίς δ᾽ οἶδ᾽, εἴ κέν οἱ σὺν δαίμονι θῡμὸν ὀρί̄νω παρειπών</span>; _who knows if, perchance_, _with God's help I may rouse his spirit by persuasion?_ O 403 (i.e. the chances of rousing his spirit, if haply I may), <span lang="grc">ἐνίσπες, εἴ πως . . . ὑπεκπροφύγοιμι Χάρυβδιν</span> _tell me if haply I shall_ (_might_) _escape Charybdis_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 12.112"><span lang="grc">μ</span> 112</cite>. Here the apodosis is entirely suppressed. Observe that this construction is _not_ an indirect question.

### LESS USUAL COMBINATIONS OF COMPLETE PROTASIS AND APODOSIS

<!-- page 534 -->

##### <a id="s2355"></a> §2355

In addition to the ordinary forms of correspondence between protasis and apodosis ([2297](#s2297)), Greek shows many other combinations expressing distinct shades of feeling. Most of these combinations, though less frequent than the ordinary forms, are no less “regular.” Shift of mental attitude is a known fact of all speech, though the relation of cause to effect must not be obscured. A speaker or writer, having begun his sentence with a protasis of one type, may alter the course of his thought: with the result that he may conclude with an apodosis of another form, in some cases even with an apodosis “unsymmetrical” with the protasis and logically dependent upon a protasis that is only suggested by the form actually adopted. Since either protasis or apodosis may choose the form of expression best suited to the meaning, the student should beware of thinking that conditional sentences invariably follow a conventional pattern, departure from which is to be counted as violation of rule. Some combinations are less usual than others: most of the more common variations from the ordinary type have been mentioned under the appropriate sections, and are here summarized ([2356](#s2356)-[2358](#s2358)). Special cases are considered in 2359-2365.

<span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the past indicative in Unreal conditions in Homer (rarely in Attic, [2312](#s2312)),

<span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> with the subjunctive in More Vivid Future conditions ([2326 d](#s2326)).

#### <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the Optative, Apodosis a primary tense of the Indicative, etc.

##### <a id="s2359"></a> §2359

<span lang="grc">εἰ</span> with the optative (instead of <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> with the subjunctive) is not infrequent in the protasis with a primary tense of the indicative, a subjunctive, or an imperative, in the apodosis. The reference is usually either to general present time (with the present indicative), or to future time. When the apodosis contains a present indicative it frequently precedes the protasis.

**a.** Compare the analogous usage in English commonly with _should_, _would:_ “There is some soul of goodness in things evil, would men observingly distil it” (Shakespeare). “If you should die, my death shall follow yours” (Dryden). “I shall scarcely figure in history, if under my guidance such visitations should accrue” (Disraeli). “If he should kill thee . . ., he has nothing to lose” (Sedley). “But if an happy soil should be withheld . . . think it not beneath thy toil” (Philips).

#### TWO OR MORE PROTASES OR APODOSES IN ONE SENTENCE

##### <a id="s2366"></a> §2366

A conditional sentence may have several protases and one apodosis or one protasis and several apodoses. Two such protases or apodoses are coördinate or one of the two is subordinate to the other.

##### <a id="s2367"></a> §2367

Two coördinated protases with a single apodosis, or two coördinated apodoses with a single protasis, may refer to the same time or to different times.

<span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ μήτ᾽ ἔστι</span> (<span lang="grc">τι βέλτῑον</span>) <span lang="grc">μήτ᾽ ἦν μήτ᾽ ἂν εἰπεῖν ἔχοι μηδεὶς μηδέπω καὶ τήμερον, τί τὸν σύμβουλον ἐχρῆν ποιεῖν</span>; _but if there neither is nor was any better plan, and if yet even to-day no one can suggest any, what was it the duty of the statesman to do?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.190">D. 18.190</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ γὰρ ἂν καὶ ὑπερφυὲς εἴη, εἰ κατὰ μὲν τῶν Ὀλυνθίους προδόντων πολλὰ καὶ δείν᾽ ἐψηφίσασθε, τοὺς δὲ παρ᾽ ὑ̄μῖν αὐτοῖς ἀδικοῦντας μὴ κολάζοντες φαίνοισθε</span> _and in fact it would be actually monstrous if, whereas you have passed many severe votes against the betrayers of the Olynthians_, _you appear not to punish the wrongdoers in your midst_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.267">D. 19.267</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ ἐγὼ ἐπεχείρησα πρά̄ττειν τὰ πολῑτικὰ πρά̄γματα, πάλαι ἂν ἀπολώλη καὶ οὔτ᾽ ἂν ὑ̄μᾶς ὠφελήκη οὐδὲν οὔτ᾽ ἂν ἐμαυτόν</span> _if I had tried to engage in politics_, _I should have long ago perished and benefited neither you nor myself at all_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 31d">P. A. 31d</cite>.

##### <a id="s2368"></a> §2368

When two or more protases are not coördinated in the same sentence, one is of chief importance and any other protasis is subordinate to it. Such protases may follow each other or one may be added after the apodosis; and may show the same or a different modal form.

<span lang="grc">ἀξιοῦμεν, εἰ μέν τινα ὁρᾶτε σωτηρίᾱν ἡμῖν</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐσομένην</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν διακαρτερῶμεν πολεμοῦντες, διδάξαι καὶ ἡμᾶς κτλ</span>. _if you see any safety for us if we persist in making war, we beg that you will inform us too_ what it is <cite data-ref="Xen. Prot. 356b">P. Pr. 356b</cite> (here to <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν ἡδέα ἱστῇς</span> are subordinated <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν μέν</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν δέ</span>, and to <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν μέν</span> are subordinated <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν τε . . . ἐά̄ν τε</span>); <span lang="grc">εἰ δέ σε ἠρόμην ἐξ ἀρχῆς τί ἐστι καλόν τε καὶ αἰσχρόν, εἴ μοι ἅπερ νῦν ἀπεκρί̄νω, ἆρ᾽ οὐκ ἂν ὀρθῶς ἀπεκέκρισο</span>; _but if I had asked you at the start what beauty and ugliness is—if you had answered me as you have now done, would you not have answered me rightly?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Hipp. Maj. 289c">P. Hipp. M. 289c</cite>; <span lang="grc">ἢν μὲν πόλεμον αἱρῆσθε, μηκέτι ἥκετε δεῦρο ἄνευ ὅπλων, εἰ σωφρονεῖτε</span> _if you choose war, come no more hither without arms if you are wise_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.2.13">X. C. 3.2.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ μετὰ Θηβαίων ἡμῖν ἀγωνιζομένοις οὕτως εἵμαρτο πρᾶξαι, τί χρῆν προσδοκᾶν εἰ μηδὲ τούτους ἔσχομεν συμμάχους ἀλλὰ Φιλίππῳ προσέθεντο</span>; _if it was decreed by fate that we should fare thus with the Thebans fighting on our side, what ought we to have expected if we had not even secured them as allies but they had joined Philip?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.195">D. 18.195</cite>.
