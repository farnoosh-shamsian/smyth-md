# ADVERBIAL COMPLEX SENTENCES (2193-2487): CLAUSES WITH ἐφ᾽ ᾧ AND ἐφ᾽ ᾧτε INTRODUCING A PROVISO

<!-- page 512 -->

##### <a id="s2279"></a> §2279

<span lang="grc">ἐφ᾽ ᾧ</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐφ᾽ ᾧτε</span> _on condition that, for the purpose of_ take the infinitive or (less often) the future indicative, and may be introduced, in the principal clause, by the demonstrative <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ τούτῳ</span>. Negative <span lang="grc">μή</span>.

<span lang="grc">αἱρεθέντες ἐφ᾽ ᾧτε συγγράψαι νόμους</span> _having been chosen for the purpose of compiling laws_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.3.11">X. H. 2.3.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔφασαν ἀποδώσειν</span> (<span lang="grc">τοὺς νεκροὺς</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐφ᾽ ᾧ μὴ καίειν τὰ̄ς οἰκίᾱς</span> the barbarians _said they would surrender_ the dead _on condition that he would not burn their houses_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.2.19">X. A. 4.2.19</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀφί̄εμέν σε, ἐπὶ τούτῳ μέντοι, ἐφ᾽ ᾧτε μηκέτι . . . φιλοσοφεῖν</span> _we release you, on this condition however, that you no longer search after wisdom_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 29c">P. A. 29c</cite>. Future indicative: <span lang="grc">ξυνέβησαν ἐφ᾽ ᾧτε ἐξίᾱσιν ἐκ Πελοποννήσου ὑπόσπονδοι καὶ μηδέποτε ἐπιβήσονται αὐτῆς</span> _they made an agreement on condition that they should depart from the Peloponnesus under a truce and never set foot on it again_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.103">T. 1.103</cite>.

**a.** These constructions do not occur in Homer. The future indicative is used by Herodotus and Thucydides on the analogy of relative clauses equivalent to consecutive clauses. These authors also use <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ τοῖσδε</span> for <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ τούτῳ</span>.
