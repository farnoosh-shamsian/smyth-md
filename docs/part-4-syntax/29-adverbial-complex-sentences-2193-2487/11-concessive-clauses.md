# ADVERBIAL COMPLEX SENTENCES (2193-2487): CONCESSIVE CLAUSES

##### <a id="s2369"></a> §2369

Concessive clauses are commonly formed by <span lang="grc">καί</span> in conjunction with the <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> of conditional clauses: <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ</span> (<span lang="grc">κει᾽</span>), <span lang="grc">καὶ ἐά̄ν</span> (<span lang="grc">κἄ̄ν</span>) _even if_, <span lang="grc">εἰ καί, ἐὰ̄ν καί</span> _although_.

##### <a id="s2370"></a> §2370

Such concessive clauses are conditional, but indicate that the condition which they introduce may be granted without destroying the conclusion. The apodosis of concessive clauses thus has an adversative meaning, i.e. it states what is regarded as true _notwithstanding_ (<span lang="grc">ὅμως</span>) what is assumed in the protasis.

##### <a id="s2371"></a> §2371

Concessive clauses have the construction of conditional clauses. The protasis, if negative, takes <span lang="grc">μή</span>.

##### <a id="s2372"></a> §2372

<span lang="grc"> **καὶ εἰ** </span> (_even if_) **clauses**.—<span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ</span> commonly implies that the conclusion must be true or must take place even in the extreme, scarcely conceivable, case which these words introduce (_even supposing that, even in the case that_). In such cases the speaker does not grant that the alleged condition really exists. On <span lang="grc">κἂ̄ν εἰ</span> see [1766 b](../20-the-verb-voices/06-the-particle-alphanu.md#s1766).

<span lang="grc">κει᾽ μὴ πέποιθα, τοὖργον ἔστ᾽ ἐργαστέον</span> _even if I have no confidence_, yet _the_ <!-- page 538 --> _deed must be done_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Lib. 296">A. Ch. 296</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ ἐὰ̄ν μὴ ἡμεῖς παρακελευώμεθα</span>, (<span lang="grc">ἡ πόλις</span>), <span lang="grc">ἱκανῶς ἐπιμελήσεται</span> _and even if we do not use exhortations_, the city _will take sufficient care_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Menex. 248d">P. Menex. 248d</cite>, <span lang="grc">γελᾷ δ᾽ ὁ μῶρος, κἄ̄ν τι μὴ γελοῖον η&lt;\*&gt;</span> _the fool laughs even if there is nothing to laugh at_ Men. Sent. 108, <span lang="grc">Μῡσοῖς βασιλεὺς πολλοὺς μὲν ἡγεμόνας ἂν δοίη . . ., καὶ εἰ σὺν τεθρίπποις βούλοιντο ἀπιέναι</span> _the king would give many guides to the Mysians even supposing they should want to depart with four-horse chariots_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.24">X. A. 3.2.24</cite>.

##### <a id="s2373"></a> §2373

The <span lang="grc">καί</span> of <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ</span> may mean simply _and_, as <span lang="grc">κει᾽ τάδ᾽ εἴσεται Κρέων</span> _and if Creon learns this_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 229">S. Ant. 229</cite>.

##### <a id="s2374"></a> §2374

Some scholars hold that the difference between <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ</span> and <span lang="grc">εἰ καί</span> is that <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ</span> concedes a supposition and is used of an assumed fact, while <span lang="grc">εἰ καί</span> concedes a fact and is used of an actual fact. But this distinction cannot be supported. <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ</span> sometimes differs from <span lang="grc">εἰ καὶ</span> only in being more emphatic. When an actual fact is referred to, we expect <span lang="grc">εἰ καί</span>; but <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ</span> sometimes occurs, as <span lang="grc">ἴσως τοι, κει᾽ βλέποντα μὴ ᾿πόθουν, θανόντ᾽ ἂν οἰμώξειαν</span> _perhaps_, _though they did not miss him when alive, they will lament him now that he is dead_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 962">S. Aj. 962</cite>, cp. <span lang="grc">πειστέον, κει᾽ μηδὲν ἡδύ</span> _although it is in no wise sweet_, _I must obey_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 1516">S. O. T. 1516</cite>.

##### <a id="s2375"></a> §2375

<span lang="grc"> **ει᾽ καί** </span> (_although_) **clauses**.—<span lang="grc">εἰ καί</span> commonly admits that a condition exists (_granting that_), but does not regard it as a hindrance. The condition, though it exists, is a matter of no moment so far as the statement in the principal clause is concerned.

<span lang="grc">εἰ καὶ τυραννεῖς</span> _king though thou art_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 408">S. O. T. 408</cite>, <span lang="grc">πόλιν μέν, εἰ καὶ μὴ βλέπεις, φρονεῖς δ᾽ ὅμως οἵᾳ νόσῳ σύνεστιν</span> _though thou canst not see, thou yet dost feel with what a plague our city is afflicted_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 302">S. O. T. 302</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ καί τῳ σμῑκρότερον δοκεῖ εἶναι</span> _although it seems too unimportant to some_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lach. 182c">P. Lach. 182c</cite>.

##### <a id="s2376"></a> §2376

The verb is omitted in <span lang="grc">εἰ καὶ γελοιότερον εἰπεῖν</span> _though the expression be ridiculous_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 30c">P. A. 30c</cite> (cp. [944](../09-the-predicate.md#s944)).

##### <a id="s2377"></a> §2377

The <span lang="grc">καί</span> of <span lang="grc">εἰ καί</span> may go closely with a following word. Here the meaning is either _also_ or _indeed;_ as <span lang="grc">εἰ καὶ δυνήσει γε</span> _if thou shalt also be able_ (besides having the will) <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 90">S. Ant. 90</cite>, <span lang="grc">δεινόν γ᾽ εἶπας, εἰ καὶ ζῇς θανών</span> _a strange thing truly hast thou uttered, if_, _though slain, thou indeed livest_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 1127">S. Aj. 1127</cite>. Where trajection is assumed (<span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ καί</span> for <span lang="grc">εἰ καὶ μή</span>) the <span lang="grc">καί</span> is intensive, as <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ καὶ νῦν . . . ἀλλά</span> _if not_ already . . . _at least_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.11">T. 2.11</cite>. 6, <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ καὶ δέδρᾱκεν</span> _unless he has actually done it_ 6. 60. 3.

##### <a id="s2378"></a> §2378

<span lang="grc">εἰ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν</span>) <span lang="grc">καί</span> not infrequently means _even if_ in prose as well as poetry.

<span lang="grc">εἵλετο μᾶλλον συνειδέναι ὑ̄μᾶς, ἵν᾽, εἰ καὶ βούλοιτο κακὸς εἶναι, μὴ ἐξείη αὐτῷ</span> _he preferred rather that you should know of it, in order that, even if he should wish to be base, it might not be possible_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 20.23">L. 20.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ει᾽ καὶ μηδὲν τούτων ὑπῆρχεν ἡμῖν, οὐδ᾽ ὣς χαλεπόν ἐστι γνῶναι περὶ αὐτῶν ὁπότεροι τἀ̄ληθῆ λέγουσιν</span> _but even if I had none of these points to rely on, even so it is not difficult to find out which tells the truth_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 41.15">D. 41.15</cite>. Cp. also <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.27">Ant. 5.27</cite>, <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.21">And. 1.21</cite>, <cite data-ref="Lys. 31.20">L. 31.20</cite>; <cite data-ref="Isaeus 11.23">Is. 11.23</cite>, <cite data-ref="Dem. 16.24">D. 16.24</cite>, <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.211">Aes. 3.211</cite>. <span lang="grc">εἰ καί</span> for <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ</span> is especially common in Isocrates, who does not use <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ</span> or <span lang="grc">κει᾽</span> except in 21. 11. Demosthenes is not fond of <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ</span>, and often substitutes <span lang="grc">κἂ̄ν εἰ</span> for it ([19](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s19). 282, 24. 109, 45. [12](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s12)). Cp. [1766 b](../20-the-verb-voices/06-the-particle-alphanu.md#s1766).

##### <a id="s2379"></a> §2379

<span lang="grc">εἰ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span>) sometimes has a concessive force (<cite data-ref="Xen. Horse. 1.17">X. Eq. 1.17</cite>). <span lang="grc">εἴπερ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐά̄νπερ</span>) <!-- page 539 --> has, rarely in Attic, a sort of concessive meaning (<cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyph. 4b">P. Euth. 4b</cite>), and especially when the truth of a statement is implicitly denied or doubted. Cp. <cite data-ref="Lys. 16.8">L. 16.8</cite>.

##### <a id="s2380"></a> §2380

<span lang="grc">ἐπεί</span>, usually with a following <span lang="grc">γέ</span>, is sometimes translated _although_, where a speaker is strictly giving the reason for his statement of a fact (or for something in that statement) and _not_ for the fact itself. Here there is a thought in the speaker's mind which is suppressed. Thus, <span lang="grc">αἰσχῡνοίμην ἂν ἔγωγε τοῦτο ὁμολογεῖν, ἐπεὶ πολλοί γέ φᾱσι τῶν ἀνθρώπων</span> _for my part I should be ashamed to acknowledge this_ (and I say this for myself) _since there are many men who do assert it_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 333c">P. Pr. 333c</cite>.

##### <a id="s2381"></a> §2381

Negative concessive clauses have <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽</span> (<span lang="grc">μηδ᾽</span>) <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> _not even if_. Here _not_ (<span lang="grc">οὐ-, μη-</span>) belongs to the leading clause, while _even_ (<span lang="grc">-δέ</span>, cp. <span lang="grc">καί</span>) belongs to the dependent clause. The negative is frequently repeated in the leading clause.

<span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽ ει᾽ πάντες ἔλθοιεν Πέρσαι, πλήθει οὐχ ὑπερβαλοίμεθ᾽ ἂν τοὺς πολεμίους</span> _even if all the Persians should come, we should not exceed the enemy in numbers_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.1.8">X. C. 2.1.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">μὴ θορυβήσητε, μηδ᾽ ἐὰ̄ν δόξω τι ὑ̄μῖν μέγα λέγειν</span> _do not raise a disturbance_, _even if I seem to you to be speaking presumptuously_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 20e">P. A. 20e</cite>. Cp. [2382](#s2382).

##### <a id="s2382"></a> §2382

The idea of concession or opposition is often expressed by the participle alone ([2066](../22-the-participle.md#s2066)) or by the participle with <span lang="grc">καίπερ</span> or <span lang="grc">καὶ ταῦτα</span> ([2083](../22-the-participle.md#s2083)). The negative is <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>. In negative concessive sentences we find also the participle with <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> (<span lang="grc">μηδέ</span>), <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> (<span lang="grc">μηδέ</span>) <span lang="grc">περ</span>.

<span lang="grc">οὐδὲ πεπονθὼς κακῶς ἐχθρὸν εἶναί μοι τοῦτον ὁμολογῶ</span> _not even though I have been ill-treated do I admit that he is my enemy_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.205">D. 21.205</cite>, <span lang="grc">γυναικὶ πείθου μηδὲ τἀ̄ληθῆ κλύων</span> _listen to a woman, even if thou dost not hear the truth_ E. fr. 440.
