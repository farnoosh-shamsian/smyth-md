# ADVERBIAL COMPLEX SENTENCES (2193-2487): CLAUSES OF COMPARISON

##### <a id="s2462"></a> §2462

Clauses of comparison (_as_ clauses) measure an act or state qualitatively or quantitatively with reference to an act or state in the leading clause.

**a.** Comparative clauses with <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> _than_ are used in disjunctive coördinated sentences. See under Particles ([2863](../36-particles/14-eta.md#s2863)).

##### <a id="s2463"></a> §2463

Comparative clauses of quality or manner are introduced by <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> _as_, <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ, καθάπερ</span> _just as_, <span lang="grc">ὅπως, ᾗ, ὅπῃ, ᾗπερ</span> _as_. The principal clause may contain a demonstrative adverb (<span lang="grc">οὕτως, ὧδε</span> _so_). <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ</span> may be correlated with <span lang="grc">ὁ αὐτός</span>. <!-- page 556 -->

<span lang="grc">ὡς</span>, etc., are here properly conjunctive relative adverbs of manner, some uses of which fall under conditional relative clauses.

**a.** Other comparative conjunctive adverbs are <span lang="grc"> **ὥστε** </span> _as_ (poetic and Ionic), <span lang="grc"> **ἠΰτε, εὖτε** </span> _as_, _like as_ (Epic). Demonstrative adverbs in Epic are <span lang="grc">ὥς, τώς, τοίως, αὔτως, ὡσαύτως</span>.

**b.** On other uses of <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>, etc., see under Particles ([2990 f](../36-particles/35-omegasigma-omegasigma.md#s2990)f.). On <span lang="grc">ὡς, ὥσπερ</span> with a participle, see [2086](../22-the-participle.md#s2086), [2087](../22-the-participle.md#s2087).

##### <a id="s2464"></a> §2464

The verb of the comparative clause is commonly omitted if it is the same as the verb of the leading clause. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν σοὶ ξυνδοκῇ ὥσπερ ἐμοί</span> _if it seems good also to you as_ (_it seems_) _to me_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 100c">P. Ph. 100c</cite>.

##### <a id="s2465"></a> §2465

The subject of a comparative clause with <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> or <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ</span>, the verb of which is omitted, is often attracted into the case (usually the accusative) of the other member of the comparison. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐδαμοῦ γὰρ ἔστιν Ἀγόρατον Ἀθηναῖον εἶναι ὥσπερ Θρασύβουλον</span> _it is in no wise possible for Agoratus to be an Athenian as Thrasybulus_ is ( = <span lang="grc">Θρασύβουλος Ἀθηναῖός ἐστι</span>) <cite data-ref="Lys. 13.72">L. 13.72</cite>. Attraction into the dative is less common: <span lang="grc">Κύ̄ρῳ ἥδετο . . . ὥσπερ σκύλακι γενναίῳ ἀνακλάζοντι</span> _he was delighted with Cyrus_, _who set up a cry like a young and noble dog_ ( = <span lang="grc">σκύλαξ γενναῖος ἀνακλάζει</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.15">X. C. 1.4.15</cite>.

**a.** Usually, however, we have the nominative with the verb omitted: <span lang="grc">πέπεισμαί σε μᾶλλον ἀποθανεῖν ἂν ἑλέσθαι ἢ ζῆν ὥσπερ ἐγώ</span> _I am persuaded that you would prefer to die rather than live as I_ live <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.6.4">X. M. 1.6.4</cite>.

##### <a id="s2466"></a> §2466

Comparative clauses of quality are often fused with the leading clause by the omission of the preposition in the correlated member of the comparison, but only when <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> precedes. Cp. [1673](../19-prepositions.md#s1673).

##### <a id="s2467"></a> §2467

The antecedent clause may contain a wish: <span lang="grc">οὕτω</span> (<span lang="grc">ὥς</span>) . . . <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> (which may be omitted); as <span lang="grc">οὕτω νῑκήσαιμί τ᾽ ἐγὼ καὶ νομιζοίμην σοφός, ὡς ὑ̄μᾶς ἡγούμενος εἶναι θεᾱτὰ̄ς δεξιοὺς . . . πρώτους ἠξίωσ᾽ ἀναγεῦσ᾽ ὑ̄μᾶς</span> _as surely as I thought it proper to let you first taste this comedy because I thought you were clever spectators_, _so_ surely _may I win and be accounted a master_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 520">Ar. Nub. 520</cite>. Cp. N 825, <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Thes. 469">Ar. Thesm. 469</cite>.

##### <a id="s2468"></a> §2468

Comparative clauses of quantity or degree are introduced by <span lang="grc">ὅσῳ, ὅσον</span> _in proportion as_. The principal clause usually contains the corresponding demonstratives <span lang="grc">τοσούτῳ, τοσοῦτον</span> (<span lang="grc">τόσῳ, τόσον</span> are usually poetic).

**a.** Greek, like Latin, uses the adjective relative pronoun <span lang="grc">ὅσος</span> (_quantus_) in the subordinate clause in correlation to <span lang="grc">τοσοῦτος</span> agreeing with a substantive. Here English uses the conjunctive adverb _as_. So with <span lang="grc">τοιοῦτος . . . οἷος.— τοσούτῳ, τοσοῦτον</span> may be followed by <span lang="grc">ὡς, ὥστε</span>.

##### <a id="s2469"></a> §2469

<span lang="grc">τοσοῦτον . . . ὅσον</span> or <span lang="grc">ὅσον . . . τοσοῦτον</span> denotes that the action of the main clause takes place in the same degree as the action of the subordinate clause. <span lang="grc">ὅσῳ . . . τοσούτῳ</span> with a comparative or superlative adjective or adverb is equivalent to _the more . . . the more, the less . . . the less_.

##### <a id="s2470"></a> §2470

The demonstrative antecedent may be omitted, especially when its clause precedes: <span lang="grc">καὶ χαλεπώτεροι ἔσονται ὅσῳ νεώτεροί εἰσιν</span> _and they will be the more severe the younger they are_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 39d">P. A. 39d</cite>. <!-- page 557 -->

##### <a id="s2471"></a> §2471

One member may contain a comparative, the other a superlative; as <span lang="grc">ὅσῳ γὰρ ἑτοιμότατ᾽ αὐτῷ</span> (<span lang="grc">τῷ λόγῳ</span>) <span lang="grc">δοκοῦμεν χρῆσθαι, τοσούτῳ μᾶλλον ἀπιστοῦσι πάντες αὐτῷ</span> _for the more we are thought to excel all others in ability to speak, so much the more do all distrust it_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 2.12">D. 2.12</cite>.

##### <a id="s2472"></a> §2472

<span lang="grc">ὅσῳ</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅσον</span>) may be used without a comparative or superlative when the correlative clause has a comparative or superlative with or without <span lang="grc">τοσούτῳ</span> (<span lang="grc">τοσοῦτον</span>). Thus, <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ ἐν ἵπποις, οὕτω καὶ ἐν ἀνθρώποις τισὶν ἐγγίγνεται, ὅσῳ ἂν ἔκπλεα τὰ δέοντα ἔχωσι, τοσούτῳ ὑβριστέροις εἶναι</span> _as it is in the nature of horses_, _so it is in the case of certain men: in so far as they have their wants satisfied_, _the more they are wanton_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hiero 10.2">X. Hi. 10.2</cite>. <span lang="grc">ὅσῳ</span> may stand for the logical <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> in <span lang="grc">τοσούτῳ Σύρων κακί̄ων ἐγένετο, ὅσῳ Σύροι ἔφυγον</span> _he proved himself a greater coward than the Syrians all the more because they fled_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.2.19">X. C. 6.2.19</cite>.

##### <a id="s2473"></a> §2473

The correlated clauses may be fused when both <span lang="grc">ὅσῳ</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅσον</span>) and <span lang="grc">τοσούτῳ</span> (<span lang="grc">τοσοῦτον</span>) are omitted and the predicate of the subordinate clause is a comparative or superlative with a form of <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐνδεεστέροις γὰρ οὖσι ταπεινοτέροις αὐτοῖς οἴονται χρῆσθαι</span> _for the more indigent they are so much the more submissive do they expect to find them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hiero 5.4">X. Hi. 5.4</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">ὅσῳ ἐνδεέστεροί εἰσι, τοσούτῳ ταπεινοτέροις</span>).

## EXAMPLES OF COMPARATIVE CLAUSES

##### <a id="s2474"></a> §2474

The moods in comparative clauses are used with the same meaning as in conditional clauses or other conditional relative clauses.

##### <a id="s2475"></a> §2475

**Indicative**: in assertions and statements of fact: <span lang="grc">ἔρξον ὅπως ἐθέλεις</span> _do as thou wilt_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 4.37"><span lang="grc">Δ</span> 37</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡς δὲ πρὸς τὴν οὐσίᾱν ἥρμοττεν, οὕτως ἑκάστοις προσέταττον</span> _but as was suitable to their property, so they gave directions to each_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 7.44">I. 7.44</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔστιν γὰρ οὕτως ὥσπερ οὗτος ἐννέπει</span> _for it is so even as he says_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Trach. 475">S. Tr. 475</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅσον αἱ κατὰ τὸ σῶμα ἡδοναὶ ἀπομαραίνονται, τοσοῦτον αὔξονται αἱ περὶ τοὺς λόγους ἐπιθῡμίαι</span> _in proportion as the pleasures of the body wane the appetite for philosophical conversation increases_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 328d">P. R. 328d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἧκεν ἄγων στρατιὰ̄ν ὅσην πλείστην ἐδύνατο</span> _he came with an army as large as possible_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.21">T. 7.21</cite> (cp. [1087](../14-adjectives.md#s1087)).

**a.** With <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> and the potential or unreal indicative: <span lang="grc">εἰσπηδήσαντες . . . θᾶττον ἢ ὡς τις ἂν ᾤετο</span> _jumping in quicker than_ (_as_) _one could think_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.8">X. A. 1.5.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ οὖν, εἰ ἀληθῆ ἦν ταῦτα ἅ μου κατηγόρησαν, ἐμοὶ ἂν ὠργίζεσθε . . ., οὕτως ἀξιῶ κτλ</span>. _for just as you would be angry with me if their accusations against me were true_, _so I beg_, etc. <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.24">And. 1.24</cite>.

##### <a id="s2476"></a> §2476

**Subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>**.—**a.** Of future time, as <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἄγεθ᾽, ὡς ἂν ἐγὼ εἴπω, πειθώμεθα</span> _but come, as I shall direct, let us obey_ B 139, <span lang="grc">ὅπως γὰρ ἂν τοὺς ἄλλους πρὸς σαυτὸν διαθῇς, οὕτω καὶ σὺ πρὸς ἐκείνους ἕξεις</span> _for as you dispose others towards yourself, so you too will feel towards them_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 2.23">I. 2.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῖς αὐτοῖς ἐνεχέσθω καθάπερ ἂν τὸν᾽ Αθηναῖον ἀποκτείνῃ</span> _let him be subject to the same penalties just as if he kills the Athenian_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.41">D. 23.41</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν τοῖς ἀργυρείοις ὅσῳπερ ἂν πλείους ἐργάζωνται, τόσῳ πλείονα τἀ̄γαθὰ εὑρήσουσι</span> _in silver mines the larger the number who coöperate_, _so much the more abundant will be the riches they find_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ways 4.32">X. Vect. 4.32</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν ὅσῳ ἄν τις μείζω ἀγαθὰ παθὼν μὴ ἀποδιδῷ χάριν, τοσούτῳ ἀδικώτερος ἂν εἴη</span>; _then will he be the more unjust in proportion to the greatness of the benefits he_ <!-- page 558 --> _has received and for which he does not return proper gratitude?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.2.3">X. M. 2.2.3</cite> (cp. [2326](10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2326) d).

**b.** Of present time, as in general conditions: <span lang="grc">τὸ μὲν γὰρ πέρας, ὡς ἂν ὁ δαίμων βουληθῇ, πάντων γίγνεται</span> _for the end of all events happens as God wills_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.92">D. 18.92</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοσούτῳ χαλεπώτερον ἀκούειν τῶν λεγομένων, ὅσῳ περ ἂν αὐτῶν τις ἀκριβέστερον ἐξετάζῃ τὰ̄ς ἁμαρτίᾱς</span> _it is the more difficult_ for them _to pay heed to what is said in proportion to the precision with which their errors are scrutinized_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 11.3">I. 11.3</cite>.

##### <a id="s2477"></a> §2477

**Optative**.—**a.** With <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (potential): <span lang="grc">ἔστι μείζω τἀ̄κείνων ἔργα ἢ ὡς τῷ λόγῳ τις ἂν εἴποι</span> _their deeds are too great for any one to tell in words_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 6.11">D. 6.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ αὐτοὶ οὐκ ἂν ἀξιώσαιτε κακῶς ἀκούειν ὑπὸ τῶν ὑ̄μετέρων παίδων, οὕτω μηδὲ τούτῳ ἐπίτρεπε περὶ τοῦ πατρὸς βλασφημεῖν</span> _just as you yourselves would not think it right to be ill spoken of by your children, even so do not permit him either to utter slanders about his father_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 40.45">D. 40.45</cite>.

**b.** With <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, as in less vivid future conditions: <span lang="grc">ὅσῳ δὲ πρεσβύτερος γίγνοιτο, μᾶλλον ἀεὶ ἀσπάζοιτο ἂν</span> (<span lang="grc">χρήματα</span>) _the older he grows, the more he would always respect wealth_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 549b">P. R. 549b</cite>.

**c.** The optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> in indirect discourse may represent <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the subjunctive of direct discourse; as <span lang="grc">νομίζων, ὅσῳ μὲν θᾶττον ἔλθοι, τοσούτῳ ἀπαρασκευαστοτέρῳ βασιλεῖ μαχεῖσθαι, ὅσῳ δὲ σχολαιότερον, τοσούτῳ πλέον συναγείρεσθαι βασιλεῖ στράτευμα</span> _in the belief that, the more quickly he advanced, the more unprepared for battle would the king be, while the slower_ he advanced, _the greater would be the army that was collecting for the king_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.9">X. A. 1.5.9</cite> (direct = <span lang="grc">ὅσῳ ἂν θᾶττον ἔλθω . . . μαχοῦμαι, ὅσῳ ἂν σχολ. ἔλθω τοσούτῳ πλέον συναγείρεται</span>).

**d.** Without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, of past time, as in general conditions. Thus, <span lang="grc">ξυνετίθεσαν ὡς ἕκαστόν τι ξυμβαίνοι</span> _they put_ the stones _together as each happened to fit_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.4">T. 4.4</cite>.— Also after a present tense: <span lang="grc">εἰκῆ κράτιστον ζῆν, ὅπως δύναιτό τις</span> '_tis best to live at hazard_, _as one may_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 979">S. O. T. 979</cite> (cp. [2573](../30-adjective-clauses-relative-clauses-2488-2573.md#s2573)).

##### <a id="s2478"></a> §2478

<span lang="grc"> **ὥσπερ εἰ** </span> (<span lang="grc">ὡσπερεί</span>), <span lang="grc"> **ὥσπερ ἂν εἰ** </span> (<span lang="grc">ὡσπερανεί</span>) _just as if_ ( = _just as would be the case, if_) form a combination of a comparison and a condition, and are used with the indicative imperfect (of past time) or aorist or with the optative (commonly when <span lang="grc">τὶς</span> is the subject). <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἄν</span>) here represents the suppressed apodosis to the condition with <span lang="grc">εἰ</span>. In some cases the ellipsis may easily be supplied, but it was usually unconscious.

**a.** When <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ ἄν</span> has its own verb it is used like _for instance_, as <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ ἄν</span> ([1766 a](../20-the-verb-voices/06-the-particle-alphanu.md#s1766)), <span lang="grc">εἴ τίς με ἔροιτο . . ., εἴποιμ᾽ ἄν</span> _for instance, if any one were to ask me_, _I should say_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 451a">P. G. 451a</cite>.

**b.** With <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ εἰ, ὥσπερ ἂν εἰ</span> cp. <span lang="grc">καθάπερ εἰ, καθάπερ ἂν εἰ</span>.

##### <a id="s2479"></a> §2479

<span lang="grc"> **ὥσπερ εἰ** </span>: <span lang="grc">θαυμάζω δέ σε . . . ἀλλόθρουν πόλιν κυρεῖν λέγουσαν, ὥσπερ εἰ παρεστάτεις</span> _but I marvel that of a city speaking another tongue thou dost as truly tell as_ (_thou wouldst tell_) _if thou hadst always been dwelling therein_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 1201">A. Ag. 1201</cite>.

##### <a id="s2480"></a> §2480

<span lang="grc"> **ὥσπερ ἂν εἰ** </span> is more common than <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ εἰ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">πρὸς μόνους τοὺς προγόνους τοὺς ἡμετέρους συμβαλόντες ὁμοίως διεφθάρησαν, ὥσπερ ἂν</span> (<span lang="grc">διεφθάρησαν</span>) <span lang="grc">εἰ πρὸς ἅπαντας ἀνθρώπους ἐπολέμησαν</span> _in contending against our ancestors alone they were destroyed as_ completely _as if they had waged war against all mankind_ <!-- page 559 --> <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.69">I. 4.69</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅμοια γάρ μοι δοκοῦσι πάσχειν ὥσπερ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἄν τις πάσχοι</span>) <span lang="grc">εἴ τις πολλὰ ἐσθίων μηδέποτε ἐμπίπλαιτο</span> _for they seem to me to be in the same condition as if any one for all his eating were never to be filled_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 4">X. S. 4</cite>. 37, <span lang="grc">ἠσπάζετο αὐτὸν ὥσπερ ἂν</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀσπάζοιτο</span>) <span lang="grc">εἴ τις . . . πάλαι φιλῶν ἀσπάζοιτο</span> _he greeted him as one would do who had long loved him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.2">X. C. 1.3.2</cite>.

**a.** With a participle <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ ἂν εἰ</span> is sometimes used with much the same force as <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ</span>, the <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> being added by a confusion of constructions. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ ἂν εἰ καὶ κατακλυσμὸν γεγενῆσθαι τῶν πρᾱγμάτων ἡγούμενοι</span> _as if you believed that there had been also a revolution in politics_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.214">D. 18.214</cite>: lit. _as_ (you would think) _if you believed_ (for <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ ἂν ἡγούμενοι</span> or <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ ἂν εἰ ἡγεῖσθε</span>). Cp. [1766 a](../20-the-verb-voices/06-the-particle-alphanu.md#s1766). Similarly <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ εἰ</span> has virtually the force of <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ</span> alone ([2087](../22-the-participle.md#s2087)).

## SIMILES AND COMPARISONS

##### <a id="s2481"></a> §2481

<span lang="grc">ὡς, ὡς εἰ, ὡς εἴ τε</span> _as if_, <span lang="grc">ὡς ὅτε, ὡς ὁπότε</span> _as when_ are often used in poetry in similes and comparisons.

**a.** The present and aorist indicative and subjunctive (usually without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>) are regularly used. The optative occurs only with <span lang="grc">ὡς εἰ</span> or <span lang="grc">ὡς εἴ τε</span>. The verb of the apodosis may sometimes be supplied from the main clause, and the sense may be satisfied in other cases by supplying _as happens, as is the case;_ but as early as Homer the ellipsis was probably unconscious, as it is in English _as if, as when_. Hence <span lang="grc">ὡς εἰ, ὡς ὅτε</span> are scarcely to be distinguished from <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>.

**b.** The tense of the main clause may be primary or secondary without influence on the construction. Cp. [1935](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1935) and 1935 a.

##### <a id="s2482"></a> §2482

<span lang="grc"> **ὡς** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **ὥς τε** </span>) is followed by the indicative present (less often aorist) or by the subjunctive. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὡς δὲ πατὴρ οὗ παιδὸς ὀδύ̄ρεται ὀστέα καίων . . . , ὣς Ἀχιλεὺς ἑτάροιο ὀδύ̄ρετο ὀστέα καίων</span> _and as a father waileth when he burneth the bones of his son, so Achilles wailed as he burned the bones of his comrade_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 23.222"><span lang="grc">Ψ</span> 222</cite>.

##### <a id="s2483"></a> §2483

<span lang="grc">ὡς</span> is common in Homer with the subjunctive (without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>) depending on the verb of the introductory clause, which is usually past. The simile may begin with <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> or with a demonstrative (<span lang="grc">οἱ</span> or <span lang="grc">τούς</span>) after which <span lang="grc">ὥς τε</span> is placed. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὡς δὲ λέων μήλοισιν ἀσημάντοισιν ἐπελθών . . . κακὰ φρονέων ἐνορούσῃ, ὣς μὲν Θρήικας ἄνδρας ἐπῴχετο Τῡδέος υἱός</span> _and as a lion, coming on flocks without a shepherd_, _with evil purpose leaps upon them, so the son of Tydeus attacked the men of Thrace_ K 485, <span lang="grc">οἱ δ᾽, ὥς τ᾽ αἰγυπιοὶ . . . πέτρῃ ἐφ᾽ ὑψηλῇ μεγάλα κλάζοντε μάχωνται, ὣς οἱ κεκλήγοντες ἐπ᾽ ἀλλήλοισιν ὄρουσαν</span> _and they, like vultures who contend with loud screams on a lofty cliff, even so they rushed screaming against each other_ II 429. After the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> or <span lang="grc">ὡς ὅτε</span> an independent indicative may follow (M 167, II [296](../../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/07-adjectives.md#s296)).

##### <a id="s2484"></a> §2484

<span lang="grc"> **ὡς εἰ** </span>, commonly <span lang="grc"> **ὡς εἴ τε** </span>, in Homer is used rarely with the indicative and subjunctive, more frequently with the optative; but usually without any finite verb. Thus, <span lang="grc">λᾱοὶ ἕπονθ᾽ ὡς εἴ τε μετὰ κτίλον ἕσπετο μῆλα</span> _the soldiers followed as sheep follow after the ram_ N 492 (the only occurrence in Homer of the indicative), <span lang="grc">καί με φίλησ᾽ ὡς εἴ τε πατὴρ δ̀ν παῖδα φιλήσῃ</span> _and he loved me as a father loveth his son_ I 481 (the only occurrence in Homer of the subjunctive), <span lang="grc">δόκησε δ᾽ ἄρα σφίσι θῡμὸς ὣς ἔμεν, ὡς εἰ πατρίδ᾽ ἱκοίατο</span> _and their feeling seemed to be as_ (it would be) _if they had come to their own country_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 10.416"><span lang="grc">κ</span> 416</cite> (the optative <!-- page 560 --> occurs only after a past tense, except <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 11.389"><span lang="grc">Λ</span> 389</cite>, a negative present); <span lang="grc">τὼ δέ οἱ ὄσσε λαμπέσθην ὡς εἴ τε πυρὸς σέλας</span> _and his eyes flashed like gleaming fire_ T 366.

##### <a id="s2485"></a> §2485

Attic poetry does not use the Epic and Lyric <span lang="grc">ὡς εἴ τε</span> for <span lang="grc">ὡς εἰ</span>. In Attic <span lang="grc">ὡς εἰ</span> (<span lang="grc">ὡσεί</span>) is practically equivalent to <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> _as_, _like;_ thus, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ οὖν εὐνοίᾳ γ᾽ αὐδῶ, μά̄τηρ ὡσεί τις πιστά̄</span> _but at any rate I speak in good-will at least as some faithful mother_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 234">S. El. 234</cite>.

##### <a id="s2486"></a> §2486

<span lang="grc">**ὡς ὅτε**, **ὡς ὁπότε**</span> are used with the indicative (present or aorist) or the subjunctive (as in general conditions). With the subjunctive <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is generally absent in Homer; but <span lang="grc">ὡς δ᾽ ὅτ᾽ ἄν</span> (never <span lang="grc">κέν</span>) occurs. The clause with <span lang="grc">ὡς ὅτε, ὡς ὁπότε</span> generally precedes the main clause. <span lang="grc">ὡς ὅτε</span> without appreciable difference from <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> in <span lang="grc">Ἐριφύλᾱν, ὅρκιον ὡς ὅτε πιστόν, δόντες Οἰκλείδᾳ γυναῖκα</span> _having given to the son of Oecles Eriphyle to wife, as a sure pledge_ Pind. Nem. 9. 16.

##### <a id="s2487"></a> §2487

A relative pronoun referring to a substantive accompanied by <span lang="grc">ὥς, ὥστε</span> _as_ often takes the subjunctive (without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>). Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁ δ᾽ ἐν κονίῃσι χαμαὶ πέσεν αἴγειρος ὥς, ἥ ῥά τ᾽ ἐν εἱαμενῇ ἕλεος μεγάλοιο πεφύ̄κῃ λείη</span> _and he fell to the ground amid the dust like a poplar that has grown up smooth in the lowland of a great marsh_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 4.483"><span lang="grc">Δ</span> 483</cite>.
