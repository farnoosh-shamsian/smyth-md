# ADVERBIAL COMPLEX SENTENCES (2193-2487): TEMPORAL CLAUSES

##### <a id="s2383"></a> §2383

Temporal clauses are introduced by conjunctions or relative expressions having the force of conjunctions

A. Denoting time usually _the same_ as that of the principal verb:

<span lang="grc">ὅτε, ὁπότε, ἡνίκα, ὁπηνίκα</span> _when;_ <span lang="grc">ὁσάκις</span> _as often as;_ <span lang="grc">ἕως, μέχρι</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">ἄχρι</span>), <span lang="grc">ὅσον χρόνον</span> _so long as;_ <span lang="grc">ἕως, ἐν ᾧ</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">ἐν ὅσῳ</span> and <span lang="grc">ἔστε</span>) _while_.

N. 1.—<span lang="grc"> **ἕως** </span> means _so long as_ in reference to actions that are coëxtensive; _while_, in reference to actions not coëxtensive.

N. 2.—<span lang="grc">**ἡνίκα**, **ὁπηνίκα**</span> have the force of _what time, at the moment when, when_, (rarely _while_), and are more precise than <span lang="grc">ὅτε</span>.

N. 3.—Poetic or Ionic are <span lang="grc"> **εὖτε** </span> (= <span lang="grc">ὅτε</span>) _when_, <span lang="grc"> **ἦμος** </span> (only with the indicative) _when_, <span lang="grc"> **ὅπως** </span> _when_ (<span lang="grc">ὅκως</span> in Hdt. of antecedent action), <span lang="grc"> **ὄφρα** </span> _so long as_. Hom. has <span lang="grc"> **εἷος** </span> (i.e. <span lang="grc">ἧος</span>) or <span lang="grc">εἵως</span> for <span lang="grc">ἕως</span>.

N. 4.—<span lang="grc"> **ἔστε** </span> is used (rarely) in lyric, Sophocles, Euripides, Herodotus, Xenophon.

B. Denoting time usually _prior_ to that of the principal verb:

<span lang="grc">ἐπεί, ἐπειδή</span> _after_, _after that_ (less exactly _when_); <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ πρῶτον, ὡς</span> (or <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ</span>) <span lang="grc">τάχιστα, ἐπειδὴ τάχιστα</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">ὅπως τάχιστα</span>) _as soon as;_ <span lang="grc">ἐξ οὗ</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">ἐξ ὧν</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐξ ὅτου, ἀφ᾽ οὗ</span> _since_, _ever since;_ <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> _when_, _as soon as, since_.

N.—<span lang="grc"> **ἐπείτε** </span> _after_ is very common in Herodotus. <!-- page 540 -->

C. Denoting time _subsequent_ to that of the principal verb:

<span lang="grc">ἕως, ἔστε, μέχρι, μέχρι οὗ</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">ἄχρι</span>), <span lang="grc">ἄχρι οὗ</span> _until:_ followed by a finite verb.

<span lang="grc">πρίν, πρότερον ἤ</span> _before_, _until:_ followed by a finite verb or by an infinitive.

N.—Homer has also <span lang="grc"> **ὄφρα** </span> (also final), <span lang="grc"> **εἰς ὅτε** </span> (<span lang="grc">κε</span>), <span lang="grc"> **εἰς ὅ** </span> (<span lang="grc">κε</span>). Herodotus has <span lang="grc"> **ἐς ὅ, ἕως οὗ, ἐς οὗ** </span> _until_. <span lang="grc"> **ὁππότε** </span> with the optative in Homer after a past tense of a _verb of waiting_ or _expecting_ means _for the time when_ (H [414](../../part-2-inflection/09-verbs/00-introduction.md#s414)). <span lang="grc"> **ἔστε** </span> (first in Hesiod) is rare in lyric, tragedy, Herodotus, and Plato, very common in Xenophon.—<span lang="grc"> **μέχρι** </span> is avoided by the orators.—<span lang="grc">μέχρι</span> and <span lang="grc">ἄχρι</span> take the articular infinitive in Demosthenes.—<span lang="grc"> **τέως** </span> for <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> is rare ([2171](../26-syntax-of-the-compound-sentence.md#s2171)).

##### <a id="s2384"></a> §2384

Demonstrative adverbs in the principal clause often correspond to the relative conjunctions, as <span lang="grc">ὅτε . . . τότε, ἐν ᾧ . . . ἐντούτῳ, ἕως . . . τέως</span> (<span lang="grc">μέχρι τούτου</span>). So also <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ . . . τότε, ὡς</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅτε</span>) . . . <span lang="grc">ἐνταῦθα</span>, etc.

##### <a id="s2385"></a> §2385

Some temporal conjunctions also denote _cause:_

<span lang="grc">ὅτε, ὁπότε, ἐπεί, εὖτε</span> (poet.), <span lang="grc">ἐπειδή</span> _since_, _whereas_, <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> _because_. <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> means also _as_, _as to_, rarely, in prose, _in order that_. <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> in Homer has in part become a final conjunction ([2419](#s2419)); for the Attic use, see [2420](#s2420).

##### <a id="s2386"></a> §2386

A temporal sentence and a conditional sentence may occur in close conjunction without marked difference of signification.

<span lang="grc">ὅταν δὲ νοσήσωσιν, ὑγιεῖς γενόμενοι σῴζονται· ἐά̄ν τέ τις ἄλλη συμφορὰ̄ καταλαμβάνῃ αὐτούς, τὰ ἐναντία ἐπιγιγνόμενα ὀνίνησιν</span> _whenever they fall ill, they are saved by regaining their health; and if ever any other calamity overtakes them, the reversal_ to prosperity _that follows is to their benefit_ Ant. 2. <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 2.1"><span lang="grc">β</span>. 1</cite>.

##### <a id="s2387"></a> §2387

A temporal conjunction is often used in Greek where English employs a conditional or a concessive conjunction.

<span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν ἔγωγε Κρονί̄ονος ἆσσον ἱκοίμην, . . . ὅτε μὴ αὐτός γε κελεύοι</span> _I would not draw nearer to Cronus' son unless_ (lit. _when not_) _he should himself bid me_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 14.248"><span lang="grc">Ξ</span> 248</cite>.

##### <a id="s2388"></a> §2388

The time denoted by a temporal clause is not always solely contemporaneous, antecedent, or subsequent to that of the principal clause, but may overlap with the time of the principal clause (before and at the same time, at the same time and after, until and after).

<span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ δὲ ἠσθένει Δᾱρεῖος καὶ ὑπώπτευε τελευτὴν τοῦ βίου, ἐβούλετο τὼ παῖδε παρεῖναι</span> _when Darius was ill and suspected that his life was coming to an end, he wished his two sons to be with him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.1">X. A. 1.1.1</cite> (here the situation set forth by <span lang="grc">ἠσθένει</span> and <span lang="grc">ὑπώπτευε</span> occurred both before and after the time indicated in <span lang="grc">ἐβούλετο</span>), <span lang="grc">τοιαῦτα ἐποίει ἕως διεδίδου πάντα ἃ ἔλαβε κρέᾱ</span> _he kept doing thus until he saw that_ (and _so long as_) _he was distributing all the meat he had received_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.7">X. C. 1.3.7</cite> (the imperfect is rare with <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> or <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> _until_), <span lang="grc">ὁ δ᾽ ἔν τε τῷ παρόντι πρὸς τὰ μηνύ̄ματα ἀπελογεῖτο καὶ ἑτοῖμος ἦν πρὶν ἐκπλεῖν κρί̄νεσθαι</span> _he both defended himself then and there against the charges and offered to be tried before he sailed_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.29">T. 6.29</cite>.

**a.** Conjunctions of antecedent action usually take the aorist, rarely the imperfect except when that tense represents overlapping action, as in <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.72">T. 5.72</cite>. 3. Cp. <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.13">T. 1.13</cite>. 5 with 1. 5. 1. <!-- page 541 -->

**b.** A verb of aoristic action is used: in the temporal clause when complete priority, in the main clause when complete subsequence, is to be clearly marked.

##### <a id="s2389"></a> §2389

Clauses introduced by relative adverbs (or conjunctions) of time, have, in general, the same constructions as clauses introduced by relative pronouns ([340](../../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s340), [2493](../30-adjective-clauses-relative-clauses-2488-2573.md#s2493) ff.) and by relative adverbs of place and manner. Temporal clauses are treated separately for the sake of clearness.

**a.** Temporal clauses introduced by a word meaning _until_ differ from ordinary conditional relatives in some respects, as in the use of the optative in implied indirect discourse ([2408](#s2408), [2420](#s2420)); and in the frequency of the absence of <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> ([2402](#s2402)).

**b.** Strictly <span lang="grc">ὅτε, ἔνθα, ὡς</span>, etc., are _subordinating conjunctions_ when the clause introduced by them fixes the time, place, or manner of the main clause; but are _relative adverbs_ when they serve only to define the antecedent and introduce a clause merely supplementary to the main clause.

##### <a id="s2390"></a> §2390

Temporal clauses are either _definite_ or _indefinite_.

##### <a id="s2391"></a> §2391

A temporal clause is definite when the action occurs at a definite point of time (negative <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>, except when the special construction requires <span lang="grc">μή</span>). Definite temporal clauses usually refer to the present or to the past.

##### <a id="s2392"></a> §2392

A temporal clause is indefinite when the action (1) occurs in the indefinite future, (2) recurs an indefinite number of times, (3) continues for an indefinite period. The same clause may have more than one of these meanings. (3) is rare. The negative is <span lang="grc">μή</span>. Indefinite temporal clauses refer either to the future or to general present or past time.

##### <a id="s2393"></a> §2393

The same temporal conjunction may refer either to definite or to indefinite time; sometimes with a difference of meaning.

##### <a id="s2394"></a> §2394

When the time is definite, the indicative is used; when indefinite, the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, the optative, or (rarely) the indicative.

Temporal conjunctions with the subjunctive take <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>. (For exceptions, see [2402](#s2402), [2412](#s2412), 2444 b.) <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is not used with the optative except when the optative is potential, 2406, 2421 (cp. [2452](#s2452)).

## INDICATIVE TEMPORAL CLAUSES REFERRING TO PRESENT OR PAST TIME

##### <a id="s2395"></a> §2395

Present or past temporal clauses take the indicative when the action is marked as a _fact_ and refers to a definite occasion (negative <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>). The principal clause commonly has the indicative, but may take any form of the simple sentence.

A. Temporal clauses denoting the _same_ time as that of the principal verb ([2383](#s2383) A). <!-- page 542 -->

<span lang="grc">ὅτε ταῦτα ἦν, σχεδὸν μέσαι ἦσαν νύκτες</span> _it was about midnight when this was taking place_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.33">X. A. 3.1.33</cite>, cp. [1](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s1). 1. 1, cited in 2388, <span lang="grc">ἡνίκα δὲ δείλη ἐγίγνετο, ἐφάνη κονιορτός</span> _but when it was getting to be afternoon, a cloud of dust appeared_ 1. 8. 8, <span lang="grc">μέχρι ἀπὸ τοῦ ἴσου ἡγοῦντο, προθύ̄μως εἱπόμεθα</span> _as long as they led on equal terms we followed willingly_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.10">T. 3.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅσον χρόνον ἐκαθέζετο . . . ἀμφὶ τὴν περὶ τὸ φρούριον οἰκονομίᾱν, . . . ἀπῆγον ἵππους</span> _as long as he was employed with regulations about the fortress, they kept bringing horses_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.3.25">X. C. 5.3.25</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν ᾧ ὡπλίζοντο, ἧκον . . . οἱ σκοποί</span> _while they were arming, the scouts came_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.2.15">X. A. 2.2.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἕως ἐστὶ καιρός, ἀντιλάβεσθε τῶν πρᾱγμάτων</span> ‘_while there is opportunity, take our public policy in hand_’ <cite data-ref="Dem. 1.20">D. 1.20</cite>.

N. <span lang="grc">μέμνημαι, οἶδα, ἀκούω</span> often take <span lang="grc">ὅτε</span> _when_ instead of <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> _that_. Thus, <span lang="grc">μέμνημαι ὅτε ἐγὼ πρὸς σὲ ἦλθον</span> _I remember when_ (_that_) _I came to you_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.6.12">X. C. 1.6.12</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἡνίκα</span> (and <span lang="grc">ἦμος</span> in poetry) has a similar use. <span lang="grc">οἶδα ὅτε, ἀκούω ὅτε</span> are probably due to the analogy of <span lang="grc">μέμνημαι ὅτε</span>, originally _I remember_ (the moment) _when_.

B. Temporal clauses denoting time _prior_ to that of the principal verb ([2383](#s2383) B).

<span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ δ᾽ ἐξῆλθεν, ἐξήγγειλε τοῖς φίλοις τὴν κρίσιν τοῦ Ὀρόντᾱ ὡς ἐγένετο</span> _but after he came out, he announced to his friends how the trial of Orontas had resulted_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.6.5">X. A. 1.6.5</cite> (observe that the aorist, and not the pluperfect, is commonly used to denote time previous to that of the main verb; cp. [1943](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1943)), <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὴ δὲ ἐτελεύτησε Δᾱρεῖος . . ., Τισσαφέρνης διαβάλλει τὸν Κῦρον</span> _after Darius died Tissaphernes calumniated Cyrus_ 1. 1. 3, <span lang="grc">ὡς τάχιστα ἕως ὑπέφαινεν, ἐθύ̄οντο</span> _as soon as daylight indistinctly appeared, they sacrificed_ 4. 3. 9, <span lang="grc">ἐξ οὗ φίλος εἶναι προσποιεῖται, ἐκ τούτου ὑ̄μᾶς ἐξηπάτηκεν</span> _ever since_ Philip _pretended to be friendly, from that time on he had deceived you_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.193">D. 23.193</cite>. (On _ever since_ expressed by the dative of the participle, see [1498](../18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1498).)

C. Temporal clauses denoting time _subsequent_ to that of the principal verb ([2383](#s2383) C).

<span lang="grc">ἔμειναν ἕως ἀφί̄κοντο οἱ στρατηγοί</span> _they waited until the generals arrived_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.1.29">X. H. 1.1.29</cite>, <span lang="grc">λοιδοροῦσι τὸν Σωτηρίδᾱν ἔστε ἠνάγκασαν . . . πορεύεσθαι</span> _they kept reviling Soteridas until they forced him to march on_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.4.49">X. A. 3.4.49</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ ταῦτα ἐποίουν μέχρι σκότος ἐγένετο</span> _and they kept doing this until darkness came on_ 4. 2. 4, <span lang="grc">τοὺς Ἕλληνας ἀπελύ̄σατο δουλείᾱς ὥστ᾽ ἐλευθέρους εἶναι μέχρι οὗ πάλιν αὐτοὶ αὑτοὺς κατεδουλώσαντο</span> _she released the Greeks from slavery so as to be free until they enslaved themselves_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Menex. 245a">P. Menex. 245a</cite>.

##### <a id="s2396"></a> §2396

When the principal verb is a past indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> and denotes non-fulfilment, a temporal clause has, by assimilation of mood, a past tense of the indicative denoting non-fulfilment.

<span lang="grc">ὁπηνίκ᾽ ἐφαίνετο ταῦτα πεποιηκὼς . . ., ὡμολογεῖτ᾽ ἂν ἡ κατηγορίᾱ τοῖς ἔργοις αὐτοῦ</span> _if it appeared that he had ever done this, his form of accusation would tally with his acts_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.14">D. 18.14</cite> (here _whenever_ would make the condition ambiguous), <span lang="grc">ἐβασάνιζον ἂν μέχρι οὗ αὐτοῖς ἐδόκει</span> _they would have kept questioning them under &lt;\*&gt;orture as long as they pleased_ 53. 25, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν ἐπαυόμην . . . ἕως ἀπεπειρά̄θην τῆς σοφίᾱς ταυτησί̄</span> _I would not cease until I had made trial of this wisdom_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crat. 396c">P. Crat. 396c</cite>. See [2185 b](../27-syntax-of-the-complex-sentence.md#s2185). <!-- page 543 -->

##### <a id="s2397"></a> §2397

The negative is <span lang="grc">μή</span> only when the temporal relation is regarded as conditional.

<span lang="grc">ὁπότε τὸ δίκαιον μὴ οἶδα, ὅ ἐστι, σχολῇ εἴσομαι εἴτε ἀρετή τις οὖσα τυγχάνει εἴτε καὶ οὔ</span> _when_ (_if_) _I do not know what justice is_, _I am scarcely likely to know whether it is or is not a virtue_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 354c">P. R. 354c</cite>.

## TEMPORAL CLAUSES REFERRING TO THE FUTURE

##### <a id="s2398"></a> §2398

The future indicative is rarely used in temporal clauses; and when used refers to definite time.

<span lang="grc">τηνικαῦτα . . . ὅτε οὐδ᾽ ὅ τι χρὴ ποιεῖν ἕξετε</span> _at that time, when you will not be able to do even what is necessary_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.262">D. 19.262</cite>.

**a.** The future is rare because that tense does not usually make clear the difference between action continuing and action simply occurring in the future. <span lang="grc">ὅτε</span> with the future indicative has thus been almost entirely displaced by <span lang="grc">ὅταν</span> with the subjunctive.

**b.** For the future with <span lang="grc">κέ</span> in <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 8.318"><span lang="grc">θ</span> 318</cite> the subjunctive is probably correct.

##### <a id="s2399"></a> §2399

Temporal clauses referring indefinitely to the future take either the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or the optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>.

**a.** The addition of <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> produces the forms <span lang="grc">**ὅταν**, **ὁπόταν**; **ἐπά̄ν**, **ἐπήν**</span> (both rare in Attic), <span lang="grc">**ἐπειδά̆ν**. **ἕως ἄν**, **μέχρι ἄν**, **ἔστ᾽ ἄν**</span> mean _as long as_ or _until_. <span lang="grc"> **ὡς** </span> _when_ scarcely ever takes <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (for <span lang="grc">ὡς ἄν</span> _while_ <span lang="grc">ἕως ἄν</span> is read in <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 1117">S. Aj. 1117</cite>, Ph. [1330](../18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1330)).

**b.** The temporal conjunctions have here, in general, the same constructions as conditional <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> or <span lang="grc">εἰ</span>. Thus <span lang="grc">ὁπόταν ῀ ἐά̄ν ποτε, ὁπότε ῀ εἴ ποτε</span>.

##### <a id="s2400"></a> §2400

The present marks the action as continuing (not completed), the aorist marks the action as simply occurring (completed). The present usually sets forth an action contemporaneous with that of the leading verb; the aorist, an action antecedent to that of the leading verb.

**a.** The present may denote time antecedent when the verb has no aorist, and in the case of some other verbs: Thus, (<span lang="grc">ὁ πόλεμος</span>) <span lang="grc">δ̀ς λῡπήσει ἕκαστον, ἐπειδὰν παρῇ</span> the war _which will afflict every one when it comes_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 6.35">D. 6.35</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὰν ἀκούῃ . . . ἑτέρους κρί̄νοντας, τί καὶ ποιήσῃ</span>; _when he hears that they are prosecuting other men, what should he then do?_ 19. 138.

### FUTURE TEMPORAL CLAUSES WITH THE SUBJUNCTIVE

##### <a id="s2401"></a> §2401

Temporal clauses referring to the future take the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> in sentences corresponding to more vivid future conditions. The principal clause has the future indicative or any form of the verb referring to the future except the simple optative. The negative is <span lang="grc">μή</span>.

<span lang="grc">ἡνίκα δ᾽ ἄν τις ὑ̄μᾶς ἀδικῇ, ἡμεῖς ὑπὲρ ὑ̄μῶν μαχούμεθα</span> _but when any one wrongs you_, _we will fight in your defence_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.4.11">X. C. 4.4.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅταν μὴ σθένω, πεπαύσομαι</span> _when my strength fails_, _I shall cease_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 91">S. Ant. 91</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὰν ἅπαντ᾽ ἀκούσητε, κρί̄νατε</span> _when you have heard everything, decide_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.14">D. 4.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐμοὶ . . . δοκεῖ, ἐπὰ̄ν τάχιστα ἀ̄ριστήσωμεν, ἰέναι</span> _in my judgment we must go as soon as we have breakfasted_ X. A. 4. <!-- page 544 --> 6. 9, <span lang="grc">μέχρι δ᾽ ἂν ἐγὼ ἥκω, αἱ σπονδαὶ μενόντων</span> _but until I return_, _let the armistice continue_ 2. 3. 24, <span lang="grc">λέξω . . . ἕως ἂν ἀκούειν βούλησθε</span> _I will speak so long as you wish to listen_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.130">D. 21.130</cite>, <span lang="grc">περιμένετε ἔστ᾽ ἂν ἐγὼ ἔλθω</span> _wait until I come_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.1.4">X. A. 5.1.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">μὴ ἀναμείνωμεν ἕως ἂν πλείους ἡμῶν γένωνται</span> _let us not wait until_ the enemy _outnumbers us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.46">X. C. 3.3.46</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀναμένομεν</span> (present as emphatic future) <span lang="grc">ἕως ἂν ἡ ἡμετέρᾱ χώρᾱ κακῶται</span> _we do not wait until our land shall be ravaged_ 3. 3. 18. The present subjunctive is rare with <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> _until_, and marks overlapping action (here = <span lang="grc">ἕως ἂν ἴδωμεν κακουμένην</span>).

##### <a id="s2402"></a> §2402

The subjunctive without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (<span lang="grc">κέ</span>) is sometimes found in poetry and in Herodotus; in Attic prose only with <span lang="grc">μέχρι, μέχρι οὗ</span> _until_ (and <span lang="grc">πρίν</span>, 2444 b). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐβούλευσαν δεσμοῖς αὐτοὺς φυλάσσειν μέχρι οὗ τι ξυμβῶσιν</span> _they decided to guard them in fetters until they should reach some agreement_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.41">T. 4.41</cite>. The omission of <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is more common after temporal conjunctions than after <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> ([2327 a](10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2327)) and in writers later than Homer lends an archaic colouring to the style.

##### <a id="s2403"></a> §2403

The principal clause may be a potential optative, which is at times nearly equivalent to the future: <span lang="grc">ἐγὼ δὲ ταύτην μὲν τὴν εἰρήνην, ἕως ἂν εἷς Ἀθηναίων λείπηται, οὐδέποτ᾽ ἂν συμβουλεύσαιμι ποιήσασθαι τῇ πόλει</span> _so long as a single Athenian is left_, _I never would recommend the city to make peace_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.14">D. 19.14</cite>.

### FUTURE TEMPORAL CLAUSES WITH THE OPTATIVE

##### <a id="s2404"></a> §2404

Temporal clauses referring to the future in sentences corresponding to less vivid future conditions usually take the optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>. An optative referring to the future stands in the principal clause ([2186 b](../27-syntax-of-the-complex-sentence.md#s2186)). The negative is <span lang="grc">μή</span>.

<span lang="grc">τεθναίην, ὅτε μοι μηκέτι ταῦτα μέλοι</span> _may I die_, _when I shall no longer care for these_ delights Mimnermus 1. 2, <span lang="grc">πεινῶν φάγοι ἂν ὁπότε βούλοιτο</span> _when hungry he would eat whenever he wished_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.1.18">X. M. 2.1.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ βούλοιο τῶν φίλων τινὰ προτρέψασθαι, ὁπότε ἀποδημοίης, ἐπιμελεῖσθαι τῶν σῶν, τί ἂν ποιοίης</span>; _should you desire to induce one of your friends to care for your interests when you were away from home_, _what would you do?_ 2. 3. 12, <span lang="grc">δέοιτό γ᾽ ἂν αὐτοῦ μένειν, ἕως ἀπέλθοις</span> _he would beg him to remain until you should depart_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.3.13">X. C. 5.3.13</cite> (here the temporal clause depends on <span lang="grc">μένειν</span>, itself dependent on <span lang="grc">δέοιτο ἄν</span>).

##### <a id="s2405"></a> §2405

The optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (<span lang="grc">κέ</span>) in Homer, where Attic would have the simple optative, is potential or virtually equivalent to a future. Thus, <span lang="grc">αὐτίκα γάρ με κατακτείνειεν Ἀχιλλεὺς . . ., ἐπὴν γόου ἐξ ἔρον εἵην</span> _for let Achilles slay me forthwith_, _when I have satisfied my desire for lamentation_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 24.227"><span lang="grc">Ω</span> 227</cite>. Cp. I 304, <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 4.222"><span lang="grc">δ</span> 222</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἕως κε β</span> 78 (potential), <span lang="grc">εἰς ὅ κε Ο</span> 70 (elsewhere this expression always takes the subjunctive in Homer).

##### <a id="s2406"></a> §2406

The potential optative or indicative (with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>) having its proper force may appear in temporal clauses (cp. [2353](10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2353)).

<span lang="grc">φυλάξᾱς . . . τὸν χειμῶν᾽ ἐπιχειρεῖ, ἡνίκ᾽ ἂν ἡμεῖς μὴ δυναίμεθ᾽ ἐκεῖσ᾽ ἀφικέσθαι</span> _by watching for winter_ to set in _he begins his operations when we are unable_ (he thinks) _to reach the spot_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.31">D. 4.31</cite>. Cp. [2405](#s2405).

##### <a id="s2407"></a> §2407

The principal clause rarely has the present or future indica<!-- page 545 --> tive, when the temporal clause has the optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (cp. [2360](10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2360), [2361](10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2361), 2573 b, c).

<span lang="grc">φρονήσεως δεῖ πολλῆς πρὸς τοὺς πολὺ πλείους . . ., ὁπότε καιρὸς παραπέσοι</span> _when the critical moment arrives, he must have great judgment to cope with forces much more numerous_ than his own <cite data-ref="Xen. Cav. 7.4">X. Hipp. 7.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">αἰπύ οἱ ἐσσεῖται . . . νῆας ἐνιπρῆσαι, ὅτε μὴ αὐτός γε Κρονί̄ων ἐμβάλοι αἰθόμενον δᾱλὸν νήεσσι</span> _hard will it be for him to fire the ships unless_ (when . . . not) _Kronion himself hurl upon the ships a blazing brand_ N 317.

**a.** Homer has <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (<span lang="grc">κέ</span>) with the subjunctive; as <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἄν τοι χραίσμῃ κίθαρις . . ., ὅτ᾽ ἐν κονίῃσι μιγείης</span> _thy cithern will not avail thee when thou grovellest in the dust_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 3.55"><span lang="grc">Γ</span> 55</cite>.

##### <a id="s2408"></a> §2408

After a secondary tense introducing indirect discourse (real or implied) the optative may represent the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> as the form in which the thought was conceived.

<span lang="grc">παρήγγειλαν, ἐπειδὴ δειπνήσαιεν . . . πάντας ἀναπαύεσθαι καὶ ἕπεσθαι ἡνίκ᾽ ἄν τις παραγγέλλῃ</span> _they issued orders that, when they had supped, all should rest and follow when any one should give the command_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὰν δειπνήσητε . . . ἀναπαύεσθε</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.5.18">X. A. 3.5.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπιμεῖναι κελεύσαντες ἔστε βουλεύσαιντο, ἐθύ̄οντο</span> _ordering them to wait until they had taken counsel, they proceeded to sacrifice_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἐπιμείνατε ἔστ᾽ ἂν βουλευσώμεθα</span>) 5. 5. 2, <span lang="grc">ἔδοξεν αὐτοῖς . . . προϊέναι . . ., ἕως Κύ̄ρῳ συμμείξειαν</span> _they resolved to keep advancing until they should join Cyrus_ ( = <span lang="grc">προΐωμεν ἕως ἂν συμμείξωμεν</span>) 2. 1. 2.

## TEMPORAL CLAUSES IN GENERIC SENTENCES

##### <a id="s2409"></a> §2409

If the leading verb denotes a repeated or customary action or a general truth, a temporal clause takes the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> after primary tenses, the optative after secondary tenses. The negative is <span lang="grc">μή</span>. Cp. [2336](10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2336).

**a.** A present tense denotes action continuing (not completed) and is of the same time as that of the leading verb; an aorist tense denotes action simply occurring (completed) and time usually antecedent to that of the leading verb when the action of the dependent clause takes place before the action of the main clause. In clauses of contemporaneous action the aorist denotes the same time as that of the main verb; in clauses of subsequent action, time later than that of the main verb.

**b.** <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> is rare in these temporal clauses (<cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.17">Hdt. 1.17</cite>, 4. 172; <span lang="grc">ὅκως</span> with the optative occurs in 1. 17, 1. [68](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/02-euphony-of-vowels.md#s68)).

**c.** On Homeric similes with <span lang="grc">ὡς ὅτε, ὡς ὁπότε</span>, see [2486](13-clauses-of-comparison.md#s2486).

##### <a id="s2410"></a> §2410

In temporal sentences of indefinite frequency the temporal clause has the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> when the principal clause has the present indicative, or any other tense denoting a present customary or repeated action or a general truth. Cp. [2337](10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2337).

<span lang="grc">μαινόμεθα πάντες ὁπόταν ὀργιζώμεθα</span> _we are all mad whenever we are angry_ Philemon 184, <span lang="grc">φωνή τις, ἥ, ὅταν γένηται, ἀεὶ ἀποτρέπει με</span> _a kind of voice which_, <!-- page 546 --> _whenever it comes, always deters me_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 31d">P. A. 31d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅταν σπεύδῃ τις αὐτός, χὡ θεὸς συνάπτεται</span> _whenever a man is eager himself_, _God too works with him_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Pers. 742">A. Pers. 742</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἕως ἂν σῴζηται τὸ σκάφος . . ., χρὴ καὶ ναύτην καὶ κυβερνήτην . . . προθύ̄μους εἶναι . . ., ἐπειδὰν δ᾽ ἡ θάλαττα ὑπέρσχῃ, μάταιος ἡ σπουδή</span> _as long as the vessel remains in safety, both sailor and pilot should exert themselves; but when the sea has overwhelmed it, their efforts are fruitless_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.69">D. 9.69</cite>, <span lang="grc">ποιοῦμεν ταῦθ᾽ ἑκάστοθ᾽ . . . ἕως ἂν αὐτὸν ἐμβάλωμεν ἐς κακόν</span> _we do this on each occasion until we plunge him into misfortune_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 1458">Ar. Nub. 1458</cite>.

##### <a id="s2411"></a> §2411

The verb of the main clause may stand in the participle, or in other tenses than the present indicative: <span lang="grc">καίπερ τῶν ἀνθρώπων, ἐν ᾧ μὲν ἂν πολεμῶσι, τὸν παρόντα</span> (<span lang="grc">πόλεμον</span>) <span lang="grc">ἀεὶ μέγιστον κρῑνόντων</span> _although men always consider the present war the greatest so long as they are engaged in it_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.21">T. 1.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅταν δ᾽ ἑτέρῳ ταῦτα παραδῷ, καταλέλυκε τὴν αὐτὸς αὑτοῦ δυναστείᾱν</span> _but whenever he surrenders these_ rights _to another, he destroys once and for all his own sovereignty_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.233">Aes. 3.233</cite>, <span lang="grc">πολέμιοι . . . ἤδη ὅταν . . . καταδουλώσωνταί τινας, πολλοὺς δὴ βελτί̄ους ἠνάγκασαν εἶναι</span> _enemies ere now have forced improvement upon those whom they have enslaved_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 1.23">X. O. 1.23</cite> (cp. [2338](10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2338)), <span lang="grc">πολλάκις ἐθαύμασα τῆς τόλμης τῶν λεγόντων ὑπὲρ αὐτοῦ, πλὴν ὅταν ἐνθῡμηθῶ κτλ</span>. _I have often marvelled at the effrontery of the speakers in his behalf, except when_ (_ever_) _I consider_, etc. <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.41">L. 12.41</cite>.

##### <a id="s2412"></a> §2412

<span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (<span lang="grc">κέ</span>) is frequently omitted in Homer, and occasionally in lyric and dramatic poetry and in Herodotus, e.g. <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ δ᾽ ἁμάρτῃ, κεῖνος οὐκέτ᾽ ἔστ᾽ ἀνὴρ ἄβουλος</span> _but whenever a man commits an error, that man is no longer heedless_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 1025">S. Ant. 1025</cite>.

##### <a id="s2413"></a> §2413

The present indicative is very rarely used instead of the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> in temporal clauses of indefinite frequency. Thus, <span lang="grc">περὶ τῶν ἄλλων τῶν ἀδικούντων, ὅτε</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅτου</span> conj.) <span lang="grc">δικάζονται, δεῖ παρὰ τῶν κατηγόρων πυθέσθαι</span> _with regard to other malefactors, one has to learn during their trial_ (lit. _when they are tried_) _from the accusers_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 22.22">L. 22.22</cite>. Cp. [2342](10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2342).

##### <a id="s2414"></a> §2414

In temporal sentences of indefinite frequency the temporal clause has the optative when the principal clause has the imperfect or any other tense denoting a past customary or repeated action.

<span lang="grc">ἐθήρευεν ἀπὸ ἵππου ὁπότε γυμνάσαι βούλοιτο ἑαυτόν</span> _he was wont to hunt on horseback_, _whenever he wanted to exercise himself_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.7">X. A. 1.2.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁπότε ὥρᾱ εἴη ἀ̄ρίστου, ἀνέμενεν αὐτοὺς ἔστε ἐμφάγοιέν τι</span> _whenever it was breakfast time, he used to wait until they had eaten something_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.1.44">X. C. 8.1.44</cite>, <span lang="grc">περιεμένομεν ἑκάστοτε ἕως ἀνοιχθείη τὸ δεσμωτήριον· ἐπειδὴ δὲ ἀνοιχθείη, εἰσῇμεν</span> _we used to wait about on each occasion until the prison was opened; but when_ (_ever_) _it was opened, we used to go in_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 59d">P. Ph. 59d</cite>. Observe that <span lang="grc">ἀνοιχθείη</span> marks a repeated past action (_until it was regularly opened_) and represents the thought of the subject (_until it should be opened_, cf. 2420; i.e. direct = <span lang="grc">ἕως ἂν ἀνοιχθῇ</span>).

##### <a id="s2415"></a> §2415

The optative is rare after a primary tense, and occurs only when that tense includes a reference to the past (<cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 24.254"><span lang="grc">ω</span> 254</cite>; cp. [2573](../30-adjective-clauses-relative-clauses-2488-2573.md#s2573)).—<span lang="grc">ὅτε κεν</span> with the optative occurs once (I [525](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/02-1-present-system.md#s525)).

##### <a id="s2416"></a> §2416

Other tenses than the imperfect in the principal clause: <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ὅτε δὴ</span> . . . <!-- page 547 --> <span lang="grc">ἀνᾱΐξειεν Ὀδυσσεύς, στάσκεν, ὑπαὶ δὲ ἴδεσκε κτλ</span>. (cp. [495](../../part-2-inflection/09-verbs/09-formation-of-the-tense-systems-omega-and-mi-verbs.md#s495)) _but whenever Odysseus arose_, _he_ always _kept his position and looked down_ T 215, <span lang="grc">ὁπότε προσβλέψειέ τινας τῶν ἐν ταῖς τάξεσιν, εἶπεν ἄν κτλ</span>. _whenever he looked toward any of the men in the ranks_, _he would say_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.1.10">X. C. 7.1.10</cite>. Cp. [2341](10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2341).

##### <a id="s2417"></a> §2417

The indicative (cp. [2342](10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2342)) is rare in temporal clauses of past indefinite frequency, as <span lang="grc">καὶ ᾖδον καὶ ἐχόρευον ὁπότε οἱ πολέμιοι αὐτοὺς ὄψεσθαι ἔμελλον</span> _they both sang and danced whenever the enemy were likely to look at them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.7.16">X. A. 4.7.16</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">ὁσάκις</span> referring to particular events of repeated occurrence, as <span lang="grc">ὁσάκις κεχορήγηκε . . . νενί̄κηκε</span> _as often as he has been choregus_, _he has gained a victory_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.4.3">X. M. 3.4.3</cite>.

### TEMPORAL CLAUSES DENOTING PURPOSE

##### <a id="s2418"></a> §2418

Temporal conjunctions denoting limit as to duration (_so long as_, _while_) or limit as to termination (_until_, _till_) may imply purpose.

**a.** So <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> _till_, _against the time when, in order that_, <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> _before_, _in order that not_. <span lang="grc">ὄφρα</span> (poet.) is usually final (_in order that_) rather than temporal (_so long as_, _while_, _till_, _up to the time that_). Sometimes in post-Homeric Greek <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> and the subjunctive (with or without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>) has a touch of purpose.

##### <a id="s2419"></a> §2419

In the _Odyssey_ <span lang="grc">ἕως</span>, usually with the aorist optative after a secondary tense, is almost a final conjunction. Thus, <span lang="grc">δῶκεν . . . ἔλαιον εἵως χυτλώσαιτο</span> _she gave olive oil that_ (_against the time when_) _she might anoint herself_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 6.79"><span lang="grc">ζ</span> 79</cite>. So <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 4.799"><span lang="grc">δ</span> 799</cite>, <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 5.385"><span lang="grc">ε</span> 385</cite>, <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 19.367"><span lang="grc">τ</span> 367</cite>. In <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 9.375"><span lang="grc">ι</span> 375</cite> the present optative expresses durative action (<span lang="grc">θερμαίνοιτο</span> _gradually get hot_).

##### <a id="s2420"></a> §2420

After a secondary tense <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> with the aorist optative sometimes in Attic prose implies an _expectation_, _hope_, or _purpose_ on the part of the subject of the main verb that the action of the temporal clause may be attained. Since such optatives are due to the principle of indirect discourse, the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, denoting mere futurity, might have been used instead.

<span lang="grc">σπονδὰ̄ς ἐποιήσαντο ἕως ἀπαγγελθείη τὰ λεχθέντα</span> _they made a truce_ (which they hoped would last) _until the terms should be announced_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 3.2.20">X. H. 3.2.20</cite> (here we might have had <span lang="grc">ἕως ἂν ἀπαγγελθῇ</span>), <span lang="grc">τὰ ἄλλα χωρία εἶχον μένοντες ἕως σφίσι κἀ̄κεῖνοι ποιήσειαν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἂν ποιήσωσι</span>) <span lang="grc">τὰ εἰρημένα</span> _they retained the other places, waiting until they_ (the Lacedaemonians) _on their part should have performed for them_ (the Athenians) _what had been agreed on_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.35">T. 5.35</cite>. Compare <span lang="grc">ἕως ἂν ταῦτα διαπρά̄ξωνται φυλακὴν . . . κατέλιπε</span> _he left a garrison_ (to remain there) _until they should settle these matters_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 5.3.25">X. H. 5.3.25</cite> (here <span lang="grc">ἕως διαπρά̄ξαιντο</span> might have been used). Other examples are <cite data-ref="Lys. 13.25">L. 13.25</cite>, <cite data-ref="Isaeus 1.10">Is. 1.10</cite>, 7. 8 (<span lang="grc">ἕως οὗ</span>?), <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.4.9">X. H. 4.4.9</cite>, <cite data-ref="Dem. 27.5">D. 27.5</cite>, 29. 43 (<span lang="grc">τέως</span>), 33. 8; cp. also <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Kn. 133">Ar. Eq. 133</cite>. Present optative in <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.102">T. 3.102</cite>, <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 5.4.37">X. H. 5.4.37</cite>.

##### <a id="s2421"></a> §2421

<span lang="grc">ἕως ἄν</span> with the optative occurs rarely where it might be thought that the simple optative or <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the subjunctive should be used. Many editors emend, but <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> may generally be defended as potential, expressing the conviction of the agent. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἵλεσθε ἄνδρας εἴκοσι· τούτους δὲ ἐπιμελεῖσθαι τῆς πόλεως, ἕως ἂν οἱ νόμοι τεθεῖεν</span> _you elected twenty men_ whose duty it should be _to care for the State until such a time as_ in all probability _the laws would be made_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.81">And. 1.81</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Soph. Trach. 687">S. Tr. 687</cite>, I.17, 15, <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 101d">P. Ph. 101d</cite>. So <span lang="grc">ὅταν</span> <cite data-ref="Aesch. Pers. 450">A. Pers. 450</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρὶν ἄν</span> <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.3.48">X. H. 2.3.48</cite>,2.4.18.

### SUMMARY OF THE CONSTRUCTIONS OF <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> AND OF OTHER WORDS MEANING BOTH _SO LONG AS_ AND _UNTIL_

<!-- page 548 -->

#### <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> _so long as_, _while_: Temporal Limit as to Duration (_during the time when_)

##### <a id="s2422"></a> §2422

**Indicative**, when the action of the temporal clause denotes definite duration in the present or past. The present often connotes cause (_while_, _now that, because_). The imperfect is used of past action: the main clause has the imperfect usually, but the aorist occurs (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.60">T. 5.60</cite>).

##### <a id="s2423"></a> §2423

**Subjunctive** (present) with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, when the action lies in the

**a.** Future, and the verb of the main clause is future indicative or an equivalent form.

**b.** Present, and the verb of the main clause states a present customary or repeated action or a general truth.

##### <a id="s2424"></a> §2424

The present optative (of future time) is very rare: in dependence on a past tense (<cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 5.4.37">X. H. 5.4.37</cite>, Aristotle, Athen. Pol. 28 end); by regular assimilation ([2186 b](../27-syntax-of-the-complex-sentence.md#s2186)) in a **less** vivid condition (<cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 155a">P. Th. 155a</cite>).

#### <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> _until_, _till_: Temporal Limit as to Termination (_up to the time when_)

##### <a id="s2425"></a> §2425

**Indicative**, of a definite present or past action. The present connotes cause. The aorist is normally used of past action: the main verb is usually imperfect, but the aorist occurs (<cite data-ref="Isoc. 17.12">I. 17.12</cite>).

**a.** Of a future action the future is very rare: <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.5.39">X. C. 7.5.39</cite> (<span lang="grc">ἐς ὅ</span> <cite data-ref="Hdt. 9.58">Hdt. 9.58</cite>).

##### <a id="s2426"></a> §2426

**Subjunctive** with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, when the action lies in the

**a.** Future, and the main clause contains a verb referring to the future (except the optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>). The tense is usually the aorist: the present marks overlapping.

**b.** Present, and the verb of the main clause states a present customary or repeated action or a general truth.

##### <a id="s2427"></a> §2427

**Optative** (usually aorist), when the action lies in the

**a.** Future, and depends on an optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>.

**b.** Past, and depends on a secondary tense expressing or implying indirect discourse. Here the optative represents <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the subjunctive after a primary tense.

**c.** Past, and the verb of the main clause states a past customary or repeated action.

N.—The present optative in **b** is rare; the future optative occurs only in <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.4.9">X. H. 4.4.9</cite>, where some read the aorist.

##### <a id="s2428"></a> §2428

Conjunctions meaning _until_ may have, as an implied or expressed <!-- page 549 --> antecedent, <span lang="grc">μέχρι τούτου</span> _up to the time_. Thus, <span lang="grc">μέχρι τούτου Λᾱσθένης φίλος ὠνομάζετο, ἕως προὔδωκεν ῎ Ολυνθον</span> _Lasthenes was called a friend_ (_up to the time when_) _until he betrayed Olynthus_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.48">D. 18.48</cite>.

##### <a id="s2429"></a> §2429

With conjunctions meaning _until_, when the principal clause is _affirmative_, it is implied that the action of the verb of the principal clause continues only up to the time when the action of the verb of the _until_ clause takes place. Thus, in the passage cited in 2428, it is implied that Lasthenes ceased to be called a friend after he had betrayed Olynthus.

**a.** When the principal clause is _negative_, it is implied that the action of the verb of the principal clause does not take place until the action of the _until_ clause takes place; as in <span lang="grc">οὐ πρότερον ἐπαύσαντο ἕως τὴν πόλιν εἰς στάσεις κατέστησαν</span> _they did not stop until they divided the city into factions_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 25.26">L. 25.26</cite>. In sentences like <span lang="grc">δεῖ μὴ περιμένειν ἕως ἂν ἐπιστῶσιν</span> _we must not wait until they are upon us_ (<cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.165">I. 4.165</cite>), by reason of the meaning of <span lang="grc">περιμένειν</span> the action of the principal clause ceases before the action of the _until_ clause takes place.

### GENERAL RULE FOR <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> BEFORE, UNTIL

##### <a id="s2430"></a> §2430

<span lang="grc">πρίν</span> is construed like other conjunctions meaning _until_ except that it takes the infinitive as well as the indicative, subjunctive, and optative.

##### <a id="s2431"></a> §2431

After an _affirmative_ clause <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> usually takes the infinitive and means _before_.

##### <a id="s2432"></a> §2432

After a _negative_ clause <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> means _until_, and usually takes the indicative (of definite time), the subjunctive or optative (of indefinite time).

**a.** The subjunctive or optative is never used with <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> unless the principal clause is negative.

**b.** When the principal clause is negative, <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> is construed like <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> and other words for _until_ (<span lang="grc">οὐ πρίν ῀ ἕως</span>).

##### <a id="s2433"></a> §2433

When the principal clause is affirmative, the clause with <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> simply adds a closer definition of the time. When the principal clause is negative, <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> defines the time as before, but the closer definition serves also as a _condition_ that must be realized before the action of the principal clause can be realized. Thus, <span lang="grc">μὴ ἀπέλθητε πρὶν ἂν ἀκούσητε</span> _do not go away until you hear_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.7.12">X. A. 5.7.12</cite> (i.e. _without hearing_ = <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν μὴ ἀκούσητε</span>). Cp. <span lang="grc">οὔτε γὰρ εἰρήνην οἷόν τε βεβαίᾱν ἀγαγεῖν, ἢν μὴ κοινῇ τοῖς βαρβάροις πολεμήσωμεν, οὔθ᾽ ὁμονοῆσαι τοὺς Ἕλληνας, π ρ ὶ ν ἂν . . . τοὺς κινδύ̄νους πρὸς τοὺς αὐτοὺς ποιησώμεθα</span> _neither is it possible to make a lasting peace unless we war in common against the barbarians_, _nor can the Greeks attain unanimity of sentiment until we encounter our perils in the front of the same enemies_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.173">I. 4.173</cite>.

##### <a id="s2434"></a> §2434

<span lang="grc">πρίν</span> is used with the aorist or (less often) with the imperfect indicative only when <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> is equivalent to <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> _until;_ but, when the verb of the main clause is negatived, <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> _may_ be translated by _before_ or _until_. When <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> _must_ be rendered by _before_, it takes the infinitive. <!-- page 550 -->

<span lang="grc">ταῦτα ἐποίουν πρὶν Σωκράτης ἀφί̄κετο</span> _I was doing this until Socrates arrived_ (rare even in poetry; cp. [2441](#s2441) c).

<span lang="grc">οὐ ταῦτα ἐποίουν πρὶν Σωκράτης ἀφί̄κετο</span> _I was not doing this until_ (or _before_) _Socrates arrived_.

<span lang="grc">ταῦτα ἐποίουν πρὶν Σωκράτην ἀφικέσθαι</span> (not <span lang="grc">Σωκράτης ἀφί̄κετο</span>) _I was doing this before Socrates arrived_.

##### <a id="s2435"></a> §2435

It is correct to say <span lang="grc">οὐ ποιήσω τοῦτο πρὶν ἂν κελεύσῃς, ποιήσω</span> (or <span lang="grc">οὐ ποιήσω</span>) <span lang="grc">τοῦτο πρὶν κελεῦσαι</span>, but incorrect to say <span lang="grc">ποιήσω τοῦτο πρὶν ἂν κελεύσῃς</span>.

##### <a id="s2436"></a> §2436

The action of an infinitive introduced by <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> _before_ may or may not (according to the sense) actually take place at some time later than the action of the leading verb. The clause with <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> signifies merely that the action of the infinitive had not taken place at the time of the leading verb.

##### <a id="s2437"></a> §2437

The clause with <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> may precede or follow the correlated clause. Cp. [2455](#s2455).

##### <a id="s2438"></a> §2438

<span lang="grc">πρίν</span> is originally a comparative adverb meaning _before_, i.e. _sooner_ or _formerly;_ and seems to be connected with <span lang="grc">πρό, πρότερον</span> _before_. The adverbial force survives in Attic only after the article, as <span lang="grc">ἐν τοῖς πρὶν λόγοις</span> _in the foregoing statements_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.62">T. 2.62</cite>. The adverbial and original use appears also in Homer wherever <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> occurs with the indicative, the anticipatory (futural) subjunctive ([1810](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1810)), or the optative with <span lang="grc">κέ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">τὴν δ᾽ ἐγὼ οὐ λύ̄σω· πρίν μιν καὶ γῆρας ἕπεισιν</span> _but her I will not release; sooner shall old age come upon her_ A 29, <span lang="grc">οὐδέ μιν ἀνστήσεις· πρὶν καὶ κακὸν ἄλλο πάθῃσθα</span> _nor shalt thou recall him to life; sooner_ (_before this_) _thou wilt suffer yet another affliction_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 24.551"><span lang="grc">Ω</span> 551</cite>.—From this early coördination was developed the construction of the _conjunction_ <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> with the finite moods; but in general only after Homer, who never uses the indicative, and the optative only once (<cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 21.580"><span lang="grc">Φ</span> 580</cite>), with <span lang="grc">πρίν</span>. The required sense was given by <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> or <span lang="grc">πρίν γ᾽ ὅτε δή</span>. A finite mood was first used of the future, and after negative clauses (<span lang="grc">οὐ πρότερον πρίν</span> like <span lang="grc">οὐ πρότερον ἕως</span>).—Homer commonly uses the infinitive with <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> meaning _before_ and _until_. Here the infinitive (as with <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span>) simply states the abstract verbal notion, and thus has no reference to differences of time or mood; <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> being used almost like <span lang="grc">πρό</span> _before_ as <span lang="grc">πρὶν ἰδεῖν ῀ πρὸ τοῦ ἰδεῖν</span> _before seeing_ (first in Xenophon). This early use with the infinitive was, with some restrictions, retained in Attic, where the infinitive _may_ sometimes be used instead of the finite verb. <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> came more and more to take the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> and to assume conditional relations (cp. [2433](#s2433)); while the use with the infinitive was more and more confined to cases where the leading verb was affirmative.

##### <a id="s2439"></a> §2439

The comparative idea in <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> explains its negative force: an event A happened before another event B, i.e. A occurred when B had _not yet_ (<span lang="grc">οὔπω</span>) occurred. Because of its negative force <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> commonly takes the aorist in all the moods. The aorist has an affinity for the negative because it marks simple and total negation of an action regarded in its mere occurrence; whereas the imperfect with a negative denotes resistance or refusal ([1896](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1896)) in respect of an action regarded as continuing. When <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> takes the present in any mood the actions of the correlated clauses usually overlap. The present occurs chiefly in the prose writers of the fourth century. <!-- page 551 -->

##### <a id="s2440"></a> §2440

<span lang="grc">πρότερον</span> or <span lang="grc">πρόσθεν</span> may be used in the principal clause as a forerunner of <span lang="grc">πρίν</span>. Examples in 2441, 2444, 2445.

**a.** Homer has <span lang="grc">πρὶν . . . πρίν</span> B 348. Attic has also <span lang="grc">φθάνω . . . πρίν</span>, as <span lang="grc">φθήσονται πλεύσαντες πρὶν τὴν ξυμφορὰ̄ν Χίους αἰσθέσθαι</span> _they will succeed in making their voyage before the Chians hear of the disaster_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.12">T. 8.12</cite>.

### <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> WITH THE INDICATIVE

##### <a id="s2441"></a> §2441

<span lang="grc">πρίν</span> in Attic prose takes the indicative of a definite past action when the verb of the principal clause is negative or implies a negative, rarely when it is affirmative.

<span lang="grc">οὔτε τότε Κύ̄ρῳ ἰέναι ἤθελε πρὶν ἡ γυνὴ αὐτὸν ἔπεισε</span> _nor was he willing then to enter into relations with Cyrus until his wife persuaded him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.26">X. A. 1.2.26</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ πρότερον ἐπαύσαντο πρὶν τόν τε πατέρ᾽ ἐκ τοῦ στρατοπέδου μετεπέμψαντο καὶ τῶν φίλων αὐτοῦ τοὺς μὲν ἀπέκτειναν, τοὺς δ᾽ ἐκ τῆς πόλεως ἐξέβαλον</span> _they did not stop until they sent for his father from the camp, put some of his friends to death and expelled others from the city_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 16.8">I. 16.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ πρόσθεν ἐπαύσαντο πρὶν ἐξεπολιόρκησαν τὸν Ὄλουρον</span> _they did not cease from hostilities until they had captured Olurus by siege_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 7.4.18">X. H. 7.4.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽ ὣς . . . ἠξίωσαν νεώτερόν τι ποιεῖν ἐς αὐτόν . . . πρίν γε δὴ . . . ἀνὴρ Ἀργίλιος μηνῡτὴς γίγνεται</span> (historical present = aorist) _not even under these circumstances did they think it right to take any severe measures against him_, _until finally a man of Argilus turned informer_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.132">T. 1.132</cite>.

**a.** The tense in the <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> clause is usually the aorist (the tense of negation, 2439, and of prior action); rarely the imperfect (of contemporaneous, overlapping action), as <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.61">D. 9.61</cite>. The historical present is also used as an equivalent of the aorist. The principal clause usually has a secondary tense of the indicative. <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> with the indicative is not common until Herodotus and the Attic writers.

**b.** The verb of the principal clause may be _virtually_ negative, as <span lang="grc">τοὺς . . . Ἀθηναίους λανθάνουσι πρὶν δὴ τῇ Δήλῳ ἔσχον</span> _they escaped the notice of the Athenians_ (i.e. <span lang="grc">οὐχ ὁρῶνται</span>) _until they reached Delos_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.29">T. 3.29</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.104">T. 3.104</cite>, <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.33">X. A. 2.5.33</cite>. Observe that <span lang="grc">οὐ παύομαι</span> ([2441](#s2441)) is not regarded as virtually affirmative.

**c.** The verb of the principal clause is affirmative in prose only in <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.39">T. 7.39</cite>, 7. 71, <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 1.64">Aes. 1.64</cite>. In all of these cases the leading verb is an imperfect, which emphasizes the continuation of the action up to the point of time expressed by the <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> clause.

**d.** The use in Herodotus is the same as in Attic prose. Homer has the indicative (after affirmative or negative clauses) only with <span lang="grc">πρίν γ᾽ ὅτε</span> _until_. In the drama <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> with the indicative is rare. Euripides uses it only after affirmative clauses. When <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> is = <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> it often takes <span lang="grc">δή</span>.

##### <a id="s2442"></a> §2442

A <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> clause, depending on a past tense denoting non-fulfilment, itself denotes non-fulfilment and takes a past indicative by assimilation ([2185 b](../27-syntax-of-the-complex-sentence.md#s2185)).

<span lang="grc">χρῆν τοίνυν Λεπτίνην μὴ πρότερον τιθέναι τὸν ἑαυτοῦ νόμον πρὶν τοῦτον ἔλῡσε</span> _Leptines ought not then to have proposed his own law until_ (_before_) _he had repealed this_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 20.96">D. 20.96</cite>. Cp. [2455 b](#s2455). <!-- page 552 -->

### <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> WITH THE SUBJUNCTIVE

##### <a id="s2443"></a> §2443

<span lang="grc">πρίν</span> with the subjunctive and <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> refers to the future or to general present time.

##### <a id="s2444"></a> §2444

(I) <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> takes the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> to denote a future action anticipated by the subject of the leading verb. The principal clause is negative, and contains any verb referring to the future except the simple optative.

<span lang="grc">οὐ πρότερον κακῶν παύσονται αἱ πόλεις πρὶν ἂν ἐν αὐταῖς οἱ φιλόσοφοι ἄρξωσιν</span> _States will not cease from evil until philosophers become rulers in them_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 487e">P. R. 487e</cite>, <span lang="grc">μὴ ἀπέλθητε πρὶν ἂν ἀκούσητε</span> _do not go away until you hear_ (_shall have heard_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.7.12">X. A. 5.7.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ χρή μ᾽ ἐνθένδε ἀπελθεῖν πρὶν ἂν δῶ δίκην</span> _I must not depart hence until I have suffered punishment_ 5. 7. 5, <span lang="grc">μηδένα φίλον ποιοῦ πρὶν ἂν ἐξετάσῃς πῶς κέχρηται τοῖς πρότερον φίλοις</span> _make no one your friend until you have inquired how he has treated his former friends_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.24">I. 1.24</cite>, <span lang="grc">μή ποτ᾽ ἐπαινήσῃς πρὶν ἂν εἰδῇς ἄνδρα σαφηνέως</span> _never praise a man until you have come to know him well_ Theognis 963. Observe that the last two examples contain a general truth.

**a.** The aorist subjunctive is usual (the tense of negation, 2439, and of action prior to that of the principal clause); much less common is the present subjunctive (usually of contemporaneous, overlapping action) as <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.2.8">X. C. 2.2.8</cite> ([2446](#s2446)).

**b.** Homer does not use <span lang="grc">κέ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> in this construction since <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> is here adverbial and its clause is simply coördinated. But Hom. has <span lang="grc">πρίν γ᾽ ὅτ᾽ ἄν</span>. The subjunctive without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> occurs occasionally as an archaism in Hdt. and the dramatic poets. In Attic prose especially in Thuc. (e.g. 6. 10, 29, [38](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s38)); but <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is often inserted by editors.

**c.** The leading verb is rarely the optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (as a form of future expression): <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν ἀπέλθοιμι πρὶν παντάπᾱσιν ἡ ἀγορὰ̄ λυθῇ</span> (cp. b) _I will not go away until the market is entirely over_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 12.1">X. O. 12.1</cite>.

##### <a id="s2445"></a> §2445

(II) After a negative clause of present time that expresses a customary or repeated action or a general truth, <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> takes the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>.

<span lang="grc">οὐ πρότερον παύονται πρὶν ἂν πείσωσιν οὓς ἠδίκησαν</span> _they do not cease_ to endure _until they have won over those whom they have wronged_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 114b">P. Ph. 114b</cite>.

**a.** The leading verb may stand in another tense than the present indicative, as <span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς πώποτε ἐπέθετο</span> (empiric aorist, [1930](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1930)) <span lang="grc">πρότερον δήμου καταλύσει πρὶν ἂν μεῖζον τῶν δικαστηρίων ἰσχύ̄σῃ</span> _no one has ever attempted the subversion of the people until he became superior to the courts of justice_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.235">Aes. 3.235</cite>.

##### <a id="s2446"></a> §2446

After a secondary tense in actual or implied indirect discourse, <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> with the subjunctive and <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is common instead of the optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> ([2449](#s2449)).

<span lang="grc">εἶπον μηδένα τῶν ὄπισθεν κῑνεῖσθαι πρὶν ἂν ὁ πρόσθεν ἡγῆται</span> _I ordered that none in the rear should move until the one before him led the way_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.2.8">X. C. 2.2.8</cite> (here <span lang="grc">πρὶν ἡγοῖτο</span> is possible). <!-- page 553 -->

##### <a id="s2447"></a> §2447

The principal clause may be affirmative in form, but virtually negative.

<span lang="grc">αἰσχρὸν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐ καλὸν</span> or <span lang="grc">οὐ δεῖν</span>) <span lang="grc">δ᾽ ἡγοῦμαι πρότερον παύσασθαι πρὶν ἂν ὑ̄μεῖς περὶ αὐτῶν ὅ τι ἂν βούλησθε ψηφίσησθε</span> _I consider it base_ (i.e. I do not consider it to be honourable) _to stop until you have voted what you wish_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 22.4">L. 22.4</cite>. Cf. Thuc. 6. 38, <cite data-ref="Dem. 38.24">D. 38.24</cite>, E. Heracl. 179.

### <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> WITH THE OPTATIVE

##### <a id="s2448"></a> §2448

<span lang="grc">πρίν</span> with the optative is used only in indirect discourse or by assimilation to another optative.

##### <a id="s2449"></a> §2449

(I) The optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> follows <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> to denote an action anticipated in the past when the principal clause is negative and its verb is in a secondary tense. The optative is here in indirect discourse (actual or implied) and represents <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the subjunctive, which is often retained ([2446](#s2446)). Cp. [2420](#s2420).

<span lang="grc">ἀπηγόρευε μηδένα βάλλειν πρὶν Κῦρος ἐμπλησθείη θηρῶν</span> _he forbade any one to shoot until Cyrus should have had his fill of hunting_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.14">X. C. 1.4.14</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">μηδεὶς βαλλέτω πρὶν ἂν Κῦρος ἐμπλησθῇ</span>), <span lang="grc">οἱ Ἠλεῖοι . . . ἔπειθον</span> (<span lang="grc">αὐτοὺς</span>) <span lang="grc">μὴ ποιεῖσθαι μάχην πρὶν οἱ Θηβαῖοι παραγένοιντο</span> _the Eleans persuaded them not to engage in battle until the Thebans should have come up_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.5.19">X. H. 6.5.19</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">μὴ ποιεῖτε μάχην πρὶν ἂν παραγένωνται</span>).

**a.** In indirect discourse the infinitive is preferred ([2455 d](#s2455)).

##### <a id="s2450"></a> §2450

(II) By assimilation of mood, <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> may take the optative when the negative principal clause has the optative. Cp. [2186 b](../27-syntax-of-the-complex-sentence.md#s2186).

<span lang="grc">εἰ ἕλκοι τις αὐτόν . . . καὶ μὴ ἀνείη πρὶν ἐξελκύ̄σειεν ἐς τὸ τοῦ ἡλίου φῶς κτλ</span>. _if one should drag him and not let him go until he had dragged him out into the sunlight_, etc. <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 515e">P. R. 515e</cite>.

##### <a id="s2451"></a> §2451

The optative with <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> in clauses of customary or repeated action seems not to be used.

##### <a id="s2452"></a> §2452

<span lang="grc">πρὶν ἄν</span> with the optative is rare and suspected (cp. [2421](#s2421)).

### <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> WITH THE INFINITIVE

##### <a id="s2453"></a> §2453

<span lang="grc">πρίν</span> takes the infinitive in Attic especially when the principal clause is affirmative. The infinitive must be used, even with negative clauses, when <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> must mean only _before_ (and not _until_).

**a.** The infinitive is obligatory in Attic when the action of the <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> clause _does not take place_ or _is not to take place_ (cp. <span lang="grc">ὥστε μή</span> with the infinitive).

**b.** The infinitive takes the accusative when its subject is different from that of the principal clause.

**c.** The usual tense is the aorist, the tense of negation ([2439](#s2439)) and of the simple occurrence of the action. Less frequent is the present (chiefly in Xenophon), of action continuing, repeated, or attempted (_before undertaking to_, <!-- page 554 --> _before proceeding to_). The perfect, of action completed with permanent result, is rare.

<span lang="grc">οἱ καὶ πρὶν ἐμὲ εἰπεῖν ὁτιοῦν εἰδότες</span> _who know even before I say anything at all_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.50">D. 18.50</cite>, <span lang="grc">σύνιστε μὲν καὶ πρὶν ἐμὲ λέγειν</span> _you know as well as I do even before I proceed to set forth in detail the matter of my speech_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 1.116">Aes. 1.116</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπετράποντο ἐς τὴν πόλιν πρὶν ὑπερβαίνειν</span> _they turned back to the city before they attempted to scale_ the wall <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.24">T. 3.24</cite>.

##### <a id="s2454"></a> §2454

When the principal clause is affirmative, <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> _before_ regularly takes the infinitive.

<span lang="grc">ἐπὶ τὸ ἄκρον ἀναβαίνει Χειρίσοφος πρίν τινας αἰσθέσθαι τῶν πολεμίων</span> _Chirisophus ascended the height before any of the enemy perceived him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.1.7">X. A. 4.1.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρὶν καταλῦσαι τὸ στράτευμα πρὸς ἄ̄ριστον βασιλεὺς ἐφάνη</span> _before the army halted for breakfast_, _the king appeared_ 1. 10. 19, <span lang="grc">πέμψᾱς, πρὶν ἐν Τεγέᾳ αὐτὸς εἶναι, πρὸς τὸν ἄρχοντα τῶν ξένων, ἐκέλευε κτλ</span>. lit. _before he himself arrived at Tegea_, _sending to the commander of the mercenaries, he gave orders_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 5.4.37">X. H. 5.4.37</cite> (<span lang="grc">αὐτός</span>, by attraction to the subject of <span lang="grc">πέμψᾱς</span>).

##### <a id="s2455"></a> §2455

When the main clause is negative, <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> sometimes takes the infinitive in Attic, and generally means _before_, rarely _until_. When _before_ and _after_ are contrasted, _until_ is out of place, and the <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> clause often precedes.

**a.** In reference to present or past time: <span lang="grc">πρὶν ὡς ?̓́αφοβον ἐλθεῖν μίαν ἡμέρᾱν οὐκ ἐχήρευσεν</span> _before she came to Aphobus she was not a widow a single day_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 30.33">D. 30.33</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρὶν μὲν τοῦτο πρᾶξαι Λεωκράτην ἄδηλον ἦν ὁποῖοί τινες ἐτύγχανον, νῦν δέ κτλ</span>. _before Leocrates did this, it was uncertain what sort of men they were; but now_, etc. Lyc. 135, <span lang="grc">πρὶν ἀνάγεσθαί με εἰς τὴν Αἶνον . . . οὐδεὶς ᾐτιά̄σατό με</span> _before I proceeded to set sail for Aenus no one accused me_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.25">Ant. 5.25</cite>.

**b.** In reference to action unfulfilled: <span lang="grc">οὓς</span> (<span lang="grc">λόγους</span>) <span lang="grc">εἴ τις ἐπέδειξεν αὐτοῖς πρὶν ἐμὲ διαλεχθῆναι περὶ αὐτῶν, οὐκ ἔστιν ὅπως οὐκ ἂν . . . δυσκόλως πρὸς σὲ διετέθησαν</span> _and if any one had shown these words to them before I discussed them, it is inevitable that they would have been discontented with you_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 12.250">I. 12.250</cite>.

**c.** In reference to future time: <span lang="grc">οὐχ οἷόν τ᾽ ἐστὶν αἰσθέσθαι πρὶν κακῶς τινας παθεῖν ὑπ᾽ αὐτῶν</span> _it is not possible to perceive this before some suffer injury at their hands_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 20.14">I. 20.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">καί μοι μὴ θορυβήσῃ μηδεὶς πρὶν ἀκοῦσαι</span> _and let no one raise a disturbance before he hears_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 5.15">D. 5.15</cite> (cp. <span lang="grc">ὅπως μὴ θορυβήσει μηδεὶς πρὶν ἂν ἅπαντα εἴπω</span> D. 13, [14](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s14)).

N.—With _verbs of fearing_, the positive being the thing dreaded; as <span lang="grc">δέδοικα μὴ πρὶν πόνοις ὑπερβάλῃ με γῆρας πρὶν σὰ̄ν χαρίεσσαν προσιδεῖν ὥρᾱν</span> _I fear lest old age overcome me with its troubles before I live to behold thy gracious beauty_ E. fr. 453.

**d.** Infinitive instead of the optative after a leading verb in a secondary tense: <span lang="grc">ἱ̄κέτευον μηδαμῶς ἀποτρέπεσθαι πρὶν ἐμβαλεῖν εἰς τὴν τῶν Λακεδαιμονίων χώρᾱν</span> _they entreated them by no means to turn aside until they should invade the territory of the Lacedaemonians_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.5.23">X. H. 6.5.23</cite> (here the optative might stand in indirect discourse to represent the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>), <span lang="grc">οὔτ᾽ αὐτός ποτε πρὶν ἱδρῶσαι δεῖπνον ᾑρεῖτο</span> _neither was he ever accustomed to take his supper until he got into a sweat_ by exercise <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.1.38">X. C. 8.1.38</cite> (for <span lang="grc">ἱδρώσειε</span>, see [2451](#s2451)).

**e.** Infinitive after an optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> in a principal clause: <span lang="grc">εἴ τίς τινα μηχανὴν</span> <!-- page 555 --> <span lang="grc">ἔχοι πρὸς τοῦτο . . ., οὐκ ἄν ποτε λέγων ἀπείποι τὸ τοιοῦτον πρὶν ἐπὶ τέλος ἐλθεῖν</span>; _if ever any_ lawgiver _should have any plan for this, would he ever be weary of discussing such a scheme until he reached the end?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 769e">P. L. 769e</cite>. Here the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is permitted.

##### <a id="s2456"></a> §2456

The lyric poets and Herodotus use <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> with the infinitive as it is used in Attic prose and poetry. Homer has the infinitive after affirmative or negative clauses alike (_before_ and _until_), and often where a finite verb would be used in Attic; as <span lang="grc">ναῖε δὲ Πήδαιον πρὶν ἐλθεῖν υἷας Ἀχαιῶν</span> _he dwelt in Pedaeon before the sons of the Achaeans came_ N 172, <span lang="grc">οὔ μ᾽ ἀποτρέψεις πρὶν χαλκῷ μαχέσασθαι</span> ( = Attic <span lang="grc">ἂν μαχέσῃ</span>) _thou shalt not dissuade me until thou hast fought with the spear_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 20.257"><span lang="grc">Υ</span> 257</cite>; often in correspondence with the adverbial <span lang="grc">πρίν</span>, as <span lang="grc">οὐδέ τις ἔτλη πρὶν πιέειν, πρὶν λεῖψαι</span> _nor durst any man_ (_sooner_) _drink before he had offered a libation_ H 480.

##### <a id="s2457"></a> §2457

<span lang="grc"> **ἢ πρίν** </span> _than before_, with a past tense suppressed after <span lang="grc">ἤ</span>, occurs first in Xenophon (C. 5. 2. 36, 7. 5. [77](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s77)).

### <span lang="grc"> **πρότερον ἤ, πρόσθεν ἤ, πρὶν ἤ, πάρος** </span>

##### <a id="s2458"></a> §2458

<span lang="grc"> **πρότερον ἤ** </span> _sooner than, before_ is used especially in Herodotus and Thucydides. (**a**) With the indicative: <span lang="grc">οὐ πρότερον ἐνέδοσαν ἢ αὐτοὶ ἐν σφίσιν αὐτοῖς . . . ἐσφάλησαν</span> _they did not succumb before they were overthrown by themselves_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.65">T. 2.65</cite>. (**b**) With the infinitive: <span lang="grc">τὰ̄ς δ᾽ ἄλλᾱς πόλεις ἔφη ἀδικεῖν, αἳ ἐς Ἀθηναίους πρότερον ἢ ἀποστῆναι ἀνήλουν</span> _he said the other States were wrong, which_, _before they revolted, used to pay money into the treasury of the Athenians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.45">T. 8.45</cite>. (**c**) With the subjunctive (without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>) rarely (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.63">T. 7.63</cite>). Chiefly in Hdt.

##### <a id="s2459"></a> §2459

So <span lang="grc"> **πρόσθεν ἤ** </span> _sooner than, before:_ <span lang="grc">ἀπεκρί̄νατο . . . ὅτι πρόσθεν ἂν ἀποθάνοιεν ἢ τὰ ὅπλα παραδοίησαν</span> _he answered that they would die before_ (_sooner than that_) _they would surrender their arms_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.10">X. A. 2.1.10</cite>. <span lang="grc"> **ὕστερον ἤ** </span> _later than_ takes, by analogy, the infinitive once in Thuc. (6. 4).

##### <a id="s2460"></a> §2460

<span lang="grc"> **πρὶν ἤ** </span> _sooner than, before_ with the infinitive occurs in Homer (only E 288, X [266](../../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/05-third-declension.md#s266)) and Hdt. (2. 2); and in Hdt. also with the indicative (6. [45](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s45)) and subjunctive (7. 10 <span lang="grc">η</span>, without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>). <span lang="grc">πρὶν ἤ</span> is rare and suspected in Attic (<cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.23">X. C. 1.4.23</cite>); but is common in late Greek.

##### <a id="s2461"></a> §2461

<span lang="grc"> **πάρος** </span> _before_ in Homer takes the infinitive (Z [348](../../part-2-inflection/08-numerals.md#s348)).
