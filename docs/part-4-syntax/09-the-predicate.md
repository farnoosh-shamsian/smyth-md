# THE PREDICATE

## Omission of the Verb

##### <a id="s944"></a> §944

**Ellipsis of the Copula**.—The copulative verb <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span> is often omitted, especially the forms <span lang="grc">ἐστί</span> and <span lang="grc">εἰσί</span>. This occurs chiefly <!-- page 262 -->

**a.** In general or proverbial statements: <span lang="grc">κοινὴ ἡ τύχη καὶ τὸ μέλλον ἀόρᾱτον</span> _chance is common to all and the future cannot be scanned_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.29">I. 1.29</cite>; b. in expressions of necessity, duty, etc.: <span lang="grc">ἀνάγκη φυλάττεσθαι</span> _it is necessary to be on our guard_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.6">D. 9.6</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">ὥρᾱ, καιρός, εἰκός, χρεών, δέον</span>, verbals in <span lang="grc">-τέον</span> ([2152](23-verbal-adjectives-in-tauepsilonomicronsigma.md#s2152)), as <span lang="grc">θεραπευτέον τοὺς θεούς</span> _we must serve the gods_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.1.28">X. M. 2.1.28</cite>; c. with various adjectives: <span lang="grc">ἄξιος, δυνατός, πρόθῡμος, δίκαιος, οἷος, φροῦδος, ἕτοιμος</span>; thus, <span lang="grc">ἡ ψῡχὴ δουλεύειν ἑτοίμη</span> _the soul is ready to be a servant_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 252a">P. Phae. 252a</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἴ τις ἐπερωτῴη πότερον κρεῖττον</span> _if anybody should ask whether it is better_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.1.9">X. M. 1.1.9</cite>.

##### <a id="s945"></a> §945

Other forms of <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span> are less commonly omitted: <span lang="grc">κοινωνεῖν ἕτοιμος</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">εἰμί</span>), <span lang="grc">οἶμαι δὲ καὶ Λάχητα τόνδε</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ἕτοιμον εἶναι</span>) _I am ready to assist you and I think that Laches here is also ready_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lach. 180a">P. Lach. 180a</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ σὺ λογογράφος</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">εἶ</span>); _are you not a speech-writer?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.250">D. 19.250</cite>, <span lang="grc">νὺξ ἐν μέσῳ</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ἦν</span>) _the night was half gone_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.71">Aes. 3.71</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄτοπα λέγεις καὶ οὐδαμῶς πρὸς σοῦ</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ὄντα</span>) _you are talking absurdly and not at all like yourself_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.3.15">X. M. 2.3.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῖς θεοῖς μεγίστη χάρις</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ἔστω</span>) _to the gods let our heartiest thanks be given_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.5.72">X. C. 7.5.72</cite>. Cp. [1041](14-adjectives.md#s1041).

##### <a id="s946"></a> §946

In lively discourse the form of a verb signifying _to do, speak_, _come_, _go_, etc., may be omitted for brevity. The ellipsis is often unconscious and it is frequently uncertain what is to be supplied to complete the thought. Thus, <span lang="grc">τί ἄλλο</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ἐποίησαν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἢ ἐπεβούλευσαν</span>; _what else_ did they do _except plot against_ us? <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.39">T. 3.39</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν ἄλλο</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ποιῶν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἢ πόλιν τὴν αὑτοῦ ἀπολείπων</span> doing _nothing else except leaving his native city_ 2. 16, <span lang="grc">ἵνα τί</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">γένηται</span>); _to what purpose?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.257">D. 19.257</cite>, <span lang="grc">περὶ μὲν τούτου κατὰ σχολήν</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">λέξω</span>) _about this by and by_ 24. 187, <span lang="grc">μή μοί γε μύ̄θους</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">λέξητε</span>) _none of your legends for me!_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Wasps 1179">Ar. Vesp. 1179</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽</span> (<span lang="grc">σκέψασθε</span>) <span lang="grc">ἕτερον</span> _but_ consider _another point_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 13.79">L. 13.79</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὦ φίλε Φαῖδρε, ποῖ δὴ</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">εἶ</span>) <span lang="grc">καὶ πόθεν</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ἥκεις</span>); _my dear Phaedrus whither_, _I beg of you_, are you going _and whence_ do you come? <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 227a">P. Phae. 227a</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἐς κόρακας</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ἐρρήσεις</span>); _will you not_ be off _to the crows?_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 871">Ar. Nub. 871</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρός σε</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ἱκετεύω</span>) <span lang="grc">γονάτων</span> I entreat _thee by thy knees_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 324">E. Med. 324</cite>. Cp. [1599](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1599).

##### <a id="s947"></a> §947

<span lang="grc">Καὶ ταῦτα</span> _and that too_ takes up a preceding expression: <span lang="grc">ἀγριωτέρους αὐτοὺς ἀπέφηνε . . . καὶ ταῦτ᾽ εἰς αὑτόν</span> _he made them more savage and that too towards himself_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 516c">P. G. 516c</cite>; often with concessive participles ([2083](22-the-participle.md#s2083)): <span lang="grc">Μένωνα δ᾽ οὐκ ἐζήτει, καὶ ταῦτα παρ᾽ Ἀριαίου ὢν τοῦ Μένωνος ξένου</span> _he did not ask for Menon and that too although he came from Ariaeus_, _Menon's guest-friend_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.4.15">X. A. 2.4.15</cite>. Cp. [1246](17-pronouns.md#s1246), [2083](22-the-participle.md#s2083).

##### <a id="s948"></a> §948

A verb that may easily be supplied from the context is often omitted. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐὰν μάθω, παύσομαι</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ποιῶν</span>) <span lang="grc">ὅ γε ἄ̄κων ποιῶ</span> _if I learn_ better, _I shall leave off_ doing _what I do unintentionally_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 26a">P. A. 26a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀμελήσᾱς ὧνπερ οἱ πολλοί</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ἐπιμελοῦνται</span>) _not caring for what most men_ care for 36 b, <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν αὖθις ζητήσετε ταῦτα, οὕτως</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ἔχοντα</span>) <span lang="grc">εὑρήσετε</span> _if you inquire about this later, you will find that it_ is _so_ 24 b. See under Brachylogy (Index).
