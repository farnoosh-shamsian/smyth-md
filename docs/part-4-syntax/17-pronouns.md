# PRONOUNS

## THE PERSONAL PRONOUNS

##### <a id="s1190"></a> §1190

The nominative of the personal pronoun is usually omitted except when emphatic, e.g. in contrasts, whether expressed or implied: <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ ὑ̄μεῖς ἐμοὶ οὐ θέλετε πείθεσθαι, ἐγω σὺν ὑ̄μῖν ἕψομαι</span> _since you are not willing to obey me_, _I will follow along with you_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.6">X. A. 1.3.6</cite>. In contrasts the first pronoun is sometimes omitted ([930](07-omission-of-the-subject.md#s930)).

##### <a id="s1191"></a> §1191

Where there is no contrast the addition of the pronoun may strengthen the verb: <span lang="grc">εἰ μηδὲ τοῦτο βούλει ἀποκρί̄νασθαι, σὺ δὲ τοὐντεῦθεν λέγε</span> _if you do not wish to reply even to this, tell me then_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.5.21">X. C. 5.5.21</cite>. <!-- page 299 -->

##### <a id="s1192"></a> §1192

The forms <span lang="grc">ἐμοῦ, ἐμοί</span>, and <span lang="grc">ἐμέ</span> and the accented forms of the pronoun of the second person ([325 a](../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s325)) are used when emphatic and usually after prepositions: <span lang="grc">καὶ πείσᾱς ἐμὲ πιστὰ ἔδωκάς μοι καὶ ἔλαβες παρ᾽ ἐμοῦ</span> _and after prevailing on me you gave me pledges of faith and received them from me_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.6.7">X. A. 1.6.7</cite>. Cp. [187](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s187) N. 2. On the reflexive use of the personal pronouns of the first and second persons, see [1222](#s1222)-1224.

##### <a id="s1193"></a> §1193

<span lang="grc">ἐγώ, σύ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐμός, σός</span>) are rarely used of an imaginary person (‘anybody’): <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.17">D. 9.17</cite>, <cite data-ref="Ps. Xen. Const. Ath. 1.11">X. R. A. 1.11</cite>.

##### <a id="s1194"></a> §1194

The nominative of the pronoun of the third person is replaced by <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span> (of absent persons), <span lang="grc">ὅδε, οὗτος</span> (of present persons), <span lang="grc">ὁ μέν . . . ὁ δέ</span> (at the beginning of a sentence), and by <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> _in contrasts_. The oblique cases of the foregoing replace <span lang="grc">οὗ</span>, etc., which in Attic prose are usually indirect reflexives ([1228](#s1228), [1229](#s1229)). <span lang="grc">οὗ</span> and <span lang="grc">ἕ</span> in Attic prose occur chiefly in poetical passages of Plato; in Attic poetry they are personal pronouns. The pronoun of the third person is very rare in the orators.

##### <a id="s1195"></a> §1195

Homer uses <span lang="grc">ἕο, οἷ</span>, etc., as personal pronouns ( = <span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ, αὐτῷ</span>, etc., in Attic), in which case they are enclitic: <span lang="grc">διὰ μαντοσύνην, τήν οἱ πόρε Φοῖβος</span> _by the art of divination, which Phoebus gave to him_ A 72. Homer also uses <span lang="grc">ἕο, οἷ</span>, etc., either as direct ( = <span lang="grc">ἑαυτοῦ</span>, etc., [1218](#s1218)) or as indirect reflexives ( = <span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ</span>, etc., [1225](#s1225)). In the former case they are orthotone; in the latter, either enclitic or orthotone. Thus, <span lang="grc">οί̂ παῖδα ἐοικότα γείνατο</span> _he begat a son like unto himself_ E 800, <span lang="grc">οὔ τινά φησιν ὁμοῖον οἶ ἔμεναι Δαναῶν</span> _he says there is no one of the Danaans like unto himself_ I 306. Hdt. agrees with Hom. except that <span lang="grc">εὗ, οἷ</span> are not direct reflexives and orthotone; <span lang="grc">σφίσι</span> (not <span lang="grc">σφί</span>) is reflexive.

## THE POSSESSIVE PRONOUNS

For the article with a possessive pronoun see [1182](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1182)-1183.

##### <a id="s1196"></a> §1196

The possessive pronouns ([330](../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s330)) of the first and second persons are the equivalents of the possessive genitive of the personal pronouns: <span lang="grc">ἐμός ῀ μου, σός ῀ σου, ἡμέτερος ῀ ἡμῶν, ὑ̄μέτερος ῀ ὑ̄μῶν</span>.

**a.** When the possessives refer to a definite, particular thing, they have the article, which always precedes ([1182](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1182)); the personal pronouns have the predicate position ([1185](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1185)). Distinguish <span lang="grc">ὁ ἐμὸς φίλος, ὁ φίλος ὁ ἐμός, ὁ φίλος μου</span> _my friend_ from <span lang="grc">φίλος ἐμός, φίλος μου</span> _a friend of mine_.

**b.** A word may stand in the genitive in apposition to the personal pronoun implied in a possessive pronoun. See [977](10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s977).

##### <a id="s1197"></a> §1197

A possessive pronoun may have the force of an objective genitive (cp. [1331](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1331)) of the personal pronoun: <span lang="grc">φιλίᾳ τῇ ἐμῇ</span> _out of friendship for me_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.28">X. C. 3.1.28</cite>. (<span lang="grc">φιλίᾱ ἡ ἐμή</span> usually means _my friendship_ (_for others_)).

##### <a id="s1198"></a> §1198

The possessive pronouns of the first and second persons are sometimes reflexive (when the subject of the sentence and the possessor are the same person), sometimes not reflexive. <!-- page 300 -->

##### <a id="s1199"></a> §1199

**FIRST AND SECOND PERSONS SINGULAR**

\1. Not reflexive (adjective _my_, _thy_ (_your_); pronoun _mine_, _thine_ (_yours_)).

<span lang="grc">**ἐμός**, **σός**</span>: <span lang="grc">ὁρᾷ τὸν ἐμὸν φίλον</span> _he sees my friend_, <span lang="grc">ὁρᾷ τὸν σὸν πατέρα</span> _she sees your father_, <span lang="grc">στέργει τὸν ἐμὸν πατέρα</span> _he loves my father_ (or <span lang="grc">τὸν πατέρα τὸν ἐμόν</span> or <span lang="grc">πατέρα τὸν ἐμόν</span>; or <span lang="grc">τὸν πατέρα μου</span> or <span lang="grc">μου τὸν πατέρα</span>), <span lang="grc">οἱ ἐμοὶ ὀφθαλμοὶ καλλί̄ονες ἂν τῶν σῶν εἴησαν</span> _my eyes will prove to be more beautiful than yours_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 5">X. S. 5</cite>. 5.

\2. Reflexive (_my own, thine_ (_your_) _own_).

**a. <span lang="grc">ἐμαυτοῦ</span>**, <span lang="grc"> **σεαυτοῦ** </span>, in the attributive position (very common): <span lang="grc">ἔλαβον τὸν ἐμαυτοῦ μισθόν</span> (or <span lang="grc">τὸν μισθὸν τὸν ἐμαυτοῦ</span>) _I received my_ (_own_) _pay_, <span lang="grc">τὸν ἀδελφὸν τὸν ἐμαυτοῦ ἔπεμψα</span> _I sent my_ (_own_) _brother_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 2.94">Aes. 2.94</cite>, <span lang="grc">κἀ̄πὶ τοῖς σαυτῆς κακοῖσι κἀ̄πὶ τοῖς ἐμοῖς γελᾷς</span>; _art thou laughing at thine own misery and at mine?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 879">S. El. 879</cite>.

**b. <span lang="grc">ἐμός</span>**, <span lang="grc"> **σός** </span> (less common): <span lang="grc">στέργω τὸν ἐμὸν πατέρα</span> _I love my_ (_own_) _father_, <span lang="grc">στέργεις τὴν σὴν μητέρα</span> _you love your_ (_own_) _mother_, <span lang="grc">ἡ ἐμὴ γυνὴ</span> _my wife_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.2.28">X. C. 7.2.28</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀδελφὸς τῆς μητρὸς τῆς ἐμῆς</span> _brother of my mother_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.117">And. 1.117</cite>.

**c. <span lang="grc">ἐμὸς αυ᾿τοῦ</span>**, <span lang="grc"> **σὸς αὐτοῦ** </span> (poetical): <span lang="grc">τὸν ἐμὸν αὐτοῦ πατέρα</span> (<cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 2.45"><span lang="grc">β</span> 45</cite>, <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 416">S. O. T. 416</cite>).

**d. <span lang="grc">μου</span>**, <span lang="grc"> **σου** </span> (rare): <span lang="grc">τὸν πατέρα μου</span> <cite data-ref="Antiph. 1.23">Ant. 1.23</cite>.

N.—When the possessor is not to be mistaken, the article alone is placed before the substantive and the possessive or reflexive pronoun is omitted (cp. [1121](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1121)). Thus, <span lang="grc">στέργεις τὸν πατέρα</span> _you love your_ (own) _father_, <span lang="grc">στέργει τὸν πατέρα</span> _he loves his_ (own) _father_, <span lang="grc">στέργουσι τὸν πατέρα</span> _they love their_ (own) _father_.

##### <a id="s1200"></a> §1200

**FIRST AND SECOND PERSONS PLURAL**

\1. Not reflexive (adjective _our_, _your;_ pronoun _ours_, _yours_).

**a. <span lang="grc">ἡμέτερος</span>**, <span lang="grc"> **ὑ̄μέτερος** </span>: <span lang="grc">ὁ ἡμέτερος φίλος</span> _our friend_ (more common than <span lang="grc">ὁ φίλο&lt;\*&gt; ἡμῶν</span>), <span lang="grc">ὁ ὑ̄μέτερος φίλος</span> _your friend_ (more common than <span lang="grc">ὁ φίλος ὑ̄μῶν</span>), <span lang="grc">ζήτησιν ποιούμενοι ἢ ὑ̄μῶν ἢ τῶν ὑ̄μετέρων τινός</span> _making a search for you or for anything of yours_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.30">L. 12.30</cite>.

\2. Reflexive (_our own, your own_).

**a. <span lang="grc">ἡμέτερος</span>**, <span lang="grc"> **ὑ̄μέτερος** </span> (common): <span lang="grc">στέργομεν τὸν ἡμέτερον φίλον</span> _we love our own friend_, <span lang="grc">στέργετε τὸν ὑ̄μέτερον φίλον</span> _you love your own friend_.

**b.** Usually the intensive <span lang="grc">αὐτῶν</span> is used with <span lang="grc">ἡμέτερος, ὑ̄μέτερος</span> in agreement with <span lang="grc">ἡμῶν</span> (<span lang="grc">ὑ̄μῶν</span>) implied in the possessive forms. This gives a stronger form of reflexive. Thus:

<span lang="grc">**ἡμέτερος αὐτῶν**, **ὑ̄μέτερος αὐτῶν**</span>: <span lang="grc">στέργομεν τὸν ἡμέτερον αὐτῶν φίλον</span> _we love our own friend_, <span lang="grc">οἰκοδόμημα ἢ τῶν φίλων τινὶ ἢ ἡμέτερον αὐτῶν</span> _a house either for some one of our friends or our own_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 514b">P. G. 514b</cite>; <span lang="grc">στέργετε τὸν ὑ̄μέτερον αὐτῶν φίλον</span> _you love your own friend_, <span lang="grc">διδάσκετε τοὺς παῖδας τοὺς ὑ̄μετέρους αὐτῶν</span> _teach your own children_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 3.57">I. 3.57</cite>.

**c. <span lang="grc">ἡμῶν</span>**, <span lang="grc"> **ὑ̄μῶν** </span> (rare): <span lang="grc">αἰτιώμεθατοὺς πατέρας ἡμῶν</span> _let us accuse our_ (_own_) _fathers_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lach. 179c">P. Lach. 179c</cite>.

**d. <span lang="grc">ἡμῶν αὐτῶν</span>**, <span lang="grc"> **ὑ̄μῶν αὐτῶν** </span> (very rare): <span lang="grc">δίκαιον ἡμᾶς . . . φαίνεσθαι μήτε ἡμῶν</span> <!-- page 301 --> <span lang="grc">αὐτῶν τῆς δόξης ἐνδεεστέρους</span> _it is not right for us to show ourselves inferior to our own fame_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.11">T. 2.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ τῶν ἵππων καὶ τὰ ὑ̄μῶν αὐτῶν ὅπλα</span> _the equipments both of your horses and yourselves_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.3.21">X. C. 6.3.21</cite>.

##### <a id="s1201"></a> §1201

**THIRD PERSON SINGULAR**

\1. Not reflexive (_his_, _her_, _its_).

**a. <span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ</span>**, <span lang="grc">**αὐτῆς**, **αὐτοῦ**</span> in the predicate position (very common): <span lang="grc">ὁρῶ τὸν φίλον αὐτοῦ</span> (<span lang="grc">αὐτῆς</span>) _I see his_ (_her_) _friend_, <span lang="grc">γιγνώσκων αὐτοῦ τὴν ἀνδρείᾱν</span> _knowing his courage_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 310d">P. Pr. 310d</cite>.

**b. <span lang="grc">ἐκείνου</span>**, etc., or <span lang="grc"> **τούτου** </span>, etc. in the attributive position (very common): <span lang="grc">ὁρῶ τὸν ἐμὸν φίλον, οὐ τὸν ἐκείνου</span> _I see my friend, not his_, <span lang="grc">ἀφικνοῦνται παρ᾽ Ἀριαῖον καὶ τὴν ἐκείνου στρατιά̄ν</span> _they come up with Ariaeus and his army_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.2.8">X. A. 2.2.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">παρεκάλεσέ τινας τῶν τούτου ἐπιτηδείων</span> _he summoned some of his friends_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 3.11">L. 3.11</cite>.

**c. <span lang="grc">ὅς</span>**, <span lang="grc">**ἥ**, **ὅν**</span>, Hom. <span lang="grc">ἑός, ἑή, ἑόν</span> (poetical): <span lang="grc">τὴν γῆμεν ἑὸν διὰ κάλλος</span> _he married her because of her beauty_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 11.282"><span lang="grc">λ</span> 282</cite>. Hom. has <span lang="grc">εὗ</span> rarely for <span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ, αὐτῆς</span>.

\2. Reflexive (_his own, her own_).

**a. <span lang="grc">ἑαυτοῦ</span>**, <span lang="grc"> **ἑαυτῆς** </span>, in the attributive position (very common): <span lang="grc">στέργει τὸν ἑαυτοῦ φίλον</span> _he loves his own friend_, <span lang="grc">ο·ρᾷ τὴν ἑαυτῆς μητέρα</span> _she sees her own mother_, <span lang="grc">τὴν ἑαυτοῦ ἀδελφὴν δίδωσι Σεύθῃ</span> _he gives his own sister in marriage to Seuthes_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.101">T. 2.101</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑβρίζει γυναῖκα τὴν ἑαυτοῦ</span> _he misuses his own wife_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 4.15">And. 4.15</cite>. This is the only way in prose to express _his own, her own_.

**b. <span lang="grc">ὅς</span>** (<span lang="grc"> **ἑός** </span>): poetical. Sometimes in Homer <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> (<span lang="grc">ἑός</span>) has the sense of _own_ with no reference to the third person ([1230 a](#s1230)).

**c. <span lang="grc">ὃς αὐτοῦ</span>**, <span lang="grc"> **αὐτῆς** </span> (poetical): <span lang="grc">ὃν αὐτοῦ πατέρα</span> (K [204](../part-2-inflection/02-declension.md#s204)).

##### <a id="s1202"></a> §1202

**THIRD PERSON PLURAL**

\1. Not reflexive (_their_).

**a. <span lang="grc">αὐτῶν</span>** in the predicate position (very common): <span lang="grc">ὁ φίλος αὐτῶν</span> _their friend_.

**b. <span lang="grc">ἐκείνων</span>**, <span lang="grc"> **τούτων** </span> in the attributive position (very common): <span lang="grc">ὁ τούτων</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐκείνων</span>) <span lang="grc">φίλος</span> _their friend_, <span lang="grc">διὰ τὴν ἐκείνων ἀπιστίᾱν</span> _because of distrust of them_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 3.2">And. 3.2</cite>.

**c. <span lang="grc">σφέων</span>** (Ionic): <cite data-ref="Hdt. 5.58">Hdt. 5.58</cite>.

\2. Reflexive (_their own_).

**a. <span lang="grc">ἑαυτῶν</span>** (very common): <span lang="grc">στέργουσι τοὺς ἑαυτῶν φίλους</span> _they love their own friends_, <span lang="grc">τῶν ἑαυτῶν συμμάχων κατεφρόνουν</span> _they despised their own allies_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.4.7">X. H. 4.4.7</cite>.

**b. <span lang="grc">σφέτερος αὐτῶν</span>**, the intensive <span lang="grc">αὐτῶν</span> agreeing with <span lang="grc">σφῶν</span> implied in <span lang="grc">σφέτερος</span> (common): <span lang="grc">οἰκέτᾱς τοὺς σφετέρους αὐτῶν ἐπικαλοῦνται</span> _they call their own slaves as witnesses_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 1.30">Ant. 1.30</cite>.

**c. <span lang="grc">σφῶν αὐτῶν</span>**, without the article (rare): <span lang="grc">τὰ ὀνόματα διαπρά̄ττονται σφῶν αὐτῶν προσγραφῆναι</span> _they contrived that their own names were added_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 13.72">L. 13.72</cite>. Cp. [1234](#s1234). <span lang="grc">τὸν σφῶν αὐτῶν</span> is not used.

**d. <span lang="grc">σφέτερος</span>** (rare in prose): <span lang="grc">Βοιωτοὶ μέρος τὸ σφέτερον παρείχοντο</span> _the Boeotians furnished their own contingent_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.12">T. 2.12</cite>. <!-- page 302 -->

**e. <span lang="grc">σφῶν</span>** in the predicate position, occasionally in Thucydides, as <span lang="grc">τοὺς ξυμμάχουι ἐδέδισαν σφῶν</span> _they were afraid of their own allies_ 5. 14. Cp. [1228](#s1228) N. 2.

## THE PRONOUN <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span>

##### <a id="s1204"></a> §1204

<span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> is used as an adjective and as a pronoun. It has three distinct uses: (1) as an intensive adjective pronoun it means _self_ (_ipse_). (2) As an adjective pronoun, when preceded by the article, it means _same_ (_idem_). (3) In oblique cases as the personal pronoun of the third person, _him_, _her_, _it_, _them_ (_eum_, _eam_, _id_, _eos_, _eas_, _ea_).

##### <a id="s1205"></a> §1205

Only the first two uses are Homeric. In Hom. <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> denotes the principal person or thing, in opposition to what is subordinate, and is intensive by contrast: <span lang="grc">αὐτὸν καὶ θεράποντα</span> _the man himself and his attendant_ Z 18 (cp. <span lang="grc">σώσᾱσ᾽ αὐτὸν καὶ παῖδας</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 511e">P. G. 511e</cite> and see [1208](#s1208) d). On <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> as a reflexive, see [1228](#s1228) a; on <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> emphatic with other pronouns, see [1233](#s1233) ff.

##### <a id="s1206"></a> §1206

<span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> is intensive (_self_)

**a.** In the _nominative_ case, when standing alone: <span lang="grc">αὐτοὶ τὴν γῆν ἔσχον</span> _they_ (the Athenians) _seized the land themselves_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.114">T. 1.114</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> emphasizes the word understood and is not a personal pronoun.

**b.** In _any_ case, when in the predicate position ([1168](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1168)) with a substantive, or in agreement with a pronoun: <span lang="grc">αὐτὸς ὁ ἀνήρ, ὁ ἀνὴρ αὐτός</span> _the man himself_, <span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ τοῦ ἀνδρός, τοῦ ἀνδρὸς αὐτοῦ</span>, etc.

##### <a id="s1207"></a> §1207

With a proper name or a word denoting an individual, the article is omitted: <span lang="grc">αὐτὸς Μένων</span> _Menon himself_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.5">X. A. 2.1.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρὸ αὐτοῦ βασιλέως</span> _in front of the Great King himself_ 1. 7. 11.

##### <a id="s1208"></a> §1208

The word emphasized may be an oblique case which must be supplied: <span lang="grc">ἔλεγε δὲ καὶ αὐτὸς ὁ Βρᾱσίδᾱς τῇ Θεσσαλῶν γῇ καὶ αὐτοῖς</span> (_scil_. <span lang="grc">τοῖς Θεσσαλοῖς</span>) <span lang="grc">φίλος ὢν ἰέναι</span> _and Brasidas himself also said that he came as a friend to the country_ <!-- page 303 --> _of the Thessalians and to the Thessalians themselves_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.78">T. 4.78</cite>, <span lang="grc">δεῖ τοίνυν τοῦτ᾽ ἤδη σκοπεῖν</span> (_scil_. <span lang="grc">ἡμᾶς</span>) <span lang="grc">αὐτούς</span> _we must forthwith consider this matter ourselves_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 2.2">D. 2.2</cite>.

##### <a id="s1209"></a> §1209

Special renderings of the emphatic <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span>:

**a.** _By itself, in itself, unaided_, _alone_, etc.: <span lang="grc">αὐτὴ ἡ ἀλήθεια</span> _the naked truth_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.207">Aes. 3.207</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ πλέον τοῦ χωρίου αὐτὸ καρτερὸν ὑπῆρχε</span> _the greater part of the place was strong in itself_ (without artificial fortification) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.4">T. 4.4</cite>. On <span lang="grc">αὐτοῖς ἀνδράσι</span> _men and all_, see [1525](18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1525). <span lang="grc">αὐτό</span> with a noun of any gender is used by Plato to denote the _abstract idea_ of a thing: <span lang="grc">αὐτὸ τὸ καλόν</span> _ideal beauty_ R. 493 e, <span lang="grc">αὐτὸ δικαιοσύνη</span> _ideal justice_ 472 c.

**b.** _Just_, _merely:_ <span lang="grc">αὐτὸ τὸ δέον</span> _just what we want_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.7.7">X. A. 4.7.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">αὐτὰ τάδε</span> _merely this_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.139">T. 1.139</cite>.

**c.** _Voluntarily:_ <span lang="grc">ἄνδρας οἳ καὶ τοῖς μὴ ἐπικαλουμένοις αὐτοὶ ἐπιστρατεύουσι</span> _men who uninvited turn their arms even against those who do not ask their assistance_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.60">T. 4.60</cite>.

**d.** _The Master_ (said by a pupil or slave): <span lang="grc">Αὐτὸς ἔφᾱ</span> _the Master_ (Pythagoras) _said it_ (_ipse dixit_) Diog. Laert. 8.1. 46, <span lang="grc">τίς οὗτος; Αὐτός. τίς Αὐτός; Σωκράτης</span> _Who's this? The Master. Who's the Master? Socrates_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 220">Ar. Nub. 220</cite>.

**e.** With ordinals: <span lang="grc">ᾑρέθη πρεσβευτὴς δέκατος αὐτός</span> _he was chosen envoy with nine others_ (i.e. himself the tenth) <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.2.17">X. H. 2.2.17</cite>.

##### <a id="s1210"></a> §1210

After the article, in the attributive position ([1154](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1154)), <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> in any case means _same_.

Thus <span lang="grc">ὁ αὐτὸς ἀνήρ</span>, rarely (<span lang="grc">ὁ</span>) <span lang="grc">ἀνὴρ ὁ αὐτός</span> _the same man;_ <span lang="grc">τοῦ αὐτοῦ θέρους</span> _in the same summer_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.58">T. 4.58</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ αὐτὰ ταῦτα</span> _these same things_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.7">X. A. 1.1.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ τοὺς αὐτοὺς αἰεὶ περὶ τῶν αὐτῶν λόγους λέγοντες</span> _the people who are continually making the same speeches about the same things_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.50">Ant. 5.50</cite>.

**a.** So as a predicate: <span lang="grc">ἐγὼ μὲν ὁ αὐτός εἰμι, ἱ̄μεῖς δὲ μεταβάλλετε</span> _I am the same_, _it is you who change_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.61">T. 2.61</cite>.

##### <a id="s1211"></a> §1211

In Hom. <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span>, without the article, may mean _the same:_ <span lang="grc">ἦρχε δὲ τῷ αὐτὴν ὁδόν, ἥνπερ οἱ ἄλλοι</span> _and he guided him by the same way as the others had gone_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 8.107"><span lang="grc">θ</span> 107</cite>.

##### <a id="s1212"></a> §1212

<span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> when unemphatic and standing alone in the oblique cases means _him_, _her_, _it_, _them_. <span lang="grc">ἐκέλευον αὐτὴν ἀπιέναι</span> _they ordered her to depart_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 1.12">L. 1.12</cite>.

##### <a id="s1213"></a> §1213

Unemphatic <span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ</span>, etc., do not stand at the beginning of a sentence.

##### <a id="s1214"></a> §1214

<span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ</span>, etc., usually take up a preceding noun (the anaphoric use): <span lang="grc">καλέσᾱς δὲ Δάμνιππον λέγω πρὸς αὐτὸν τάδε</span> _summoning Damnippus_, _I speak to him as follows_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.14">L. 12.14</cite>. But an oblique case of <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> is often suppressed where English employs the pronoun of the third person: <span lang="grc">ἐμπιπλὰ̄ς ἁπάντων τὴν γνώμην&lt;\*&gt; ἀπέπεμπε</span> _having satisfied the minds of all he dismissed_ them <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.8">X. A. 1.7.8</cite>.

##### <a id="s1215"></a> §1215

<span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ</span>, etc., may be added pleonastically; <span lang="grc">πειρά̄σομαι τῷ πάππῳ, κράτιστος ὢν ἱππεύς, συμμαχεῖν αὐτῷ</span> _I will try, since I am an excellent horseman, to be an ally to my grandfather_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.15">X. C. 1.3.15</cite>.

##### <a id="s1216"></a> §1216

<span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ</span>, etc., are emphatic (= <span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ τούτου</span>, etc.) in a main clause when followed by a relative clause referring to <span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ</span>, etc.: <span lang="grc">εἴρηκας αὐτό, δι᾽ ὅπερ ἔγωγε</span> <!-- page 304 --> <span lang="grc">τὰ ἐμὰ ἔργα πλείστου ἄξια νομίζω εἶναι</span> _you have mentioned the very quality for which I consider my work worth the highest price_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.10.14">X. M. 3.10.14</cite>. But when the relative clause precedes, <span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ</span>, etc., are not emphatic: <span lang="grc">οὓς δὲ μὴ εὕρισκον, κενοτάφιον αὐτοῖς ἐποίησαν</span> _they built a cenotaph for those whom they could not find_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.4.9">X. A. 6.4.9</cite>.

##### <a id="s1217"></a> §1217

<span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ</span>, etc., are often used where, after a conjunction, we expect the oblique case of a relative pronoun: <span lang="grc">ὃ μὴ οἶδε μηδ᾽ ἔχει αὐτοῦ σφρᾱγῖδα</span> _which he does not know nor does he have the seal of it_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 192a">P. Th. 192a</cite>.

## THE REFLEXIVE PRONOUNS

##### <a id="s1218"></a> §1218

**Direct Reflexives**.—The reflexive pronouns are used _directly_ when they refer to the chief word (usually the subject) of the sentence or clause in which they stand.

<span lang="grc">γνῶθι σεαυτόν</span> _learn to know thyself_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Charm. 164e">P. Charm. 164e</cite>, <span lang="grc">σφάττει ἑαυτήν</span> _she kills herself_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.3.14">X. C. 7.3.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">καθ᾽ ἑαυτοὺς βουλευσάμενοι τὰ ὅπλα παρέδοσαν καὶ σφᾶς αὐτούς</span> _after deliberating apart by themselves they surrendered their arms and themselves_ (their persons) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.38">T. 4.38</cite>. Less commonly the reference is to the object, which often stands in a prominent place: <span lang="grc">τοὺς δὲ περιοίκους ἀφῆκεν ἐπὶ τὰ̄ς ἑαυτῶν πόλεις</span> _but the perioeci he dismissed to their own cities_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.5.21">X. H. 6.5.21</cite>.

##### <a id="s1219"></a> §1219

The direct reflexives are regular in prose if, in the same clause, the pronoun refers emphatically to the subject and is the direct object of the main verb: <span lang="grc">ἐμαυτὸν</span> (_not_ <span lang="grc">ἐμὲ</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐπαινῶ</span> _I praise myself_. The usage of poetry is freer: <span lang="grc">στένω σὲ μᾶλλον ἢ ᾿μέ</span> _I mourn thee rather than myself_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hipp. 1409">E. Hipp. 1409</cite>.

##### <a id="s1220"></a> §1220

The reflexives may retain or abandon their differentiating force. Contrast the third example in 1218 with <span lang="grc">παρέδοσαν σφᾶς αὐτούς</span> _they surrendered_ (themselves) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.82">T. 7.82</cite>.

##### <a id="s1221"></a> §1221

The reflexives of the first and second persons are not used in a subordinate clause to refer to the subject of the main clause.

##### <a id="s1222"></a> §1222

The personal pronouns are sometimes used in a reflexive sense: <span lang="grc">θρηνοῦντός τέ μου καὶ λέγοντος πολλὰ καὶ ἀνάξια ἐμοῦ</span> _wailing and saying much unworthy of myself_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 38e">P. A. 38e</cite> (contrast <span lang="grc">ἀκούσει πολλὰ καὶ ἀνάξια σαυτοῦ</span> _you will hear much unworthy of yourself_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 53e">P. Cr. 53e</cite>), <span lang="grc">δοκῶ μοι ἀδύνατος εἶναι</span> _I_ (_seem to myself to be_) _think I am unable_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 368b">P. R. 368b</cite> (less usually <span lang="grc">δοκῶ ἐμαυτῷ</span>). So in Hom.: <span lang="grc">ἐγὼν ἐμὲ λύ̄σομαι</span> _I will ransom myself_ K 378. Cp. [1195](#s1195).

##### <a id="s1223"></a> §1223

<span lang="grc">ἐμέ, σέ</span>, not <span lang="grc">ἐμαυτόν, σεαυτόν</span>, are generally used as subject of the infinitive: <span lang="grc">ἐγὼ οἶμαι καὶ ἐμὲ καὶ σὲ τὸ ἀδικεῖν τοῦ ἀδικεῖσθαι κάκῑον ἡγεῖσθαι</span> _I think that both you and I believe that it is worse to do wrong than to be wronged_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 474b">P. G. 474b</cite>.

##### <a id="s1224"></a> §1224

The use in 1222, 1223 generally occurs when there is a contrast between two persons, or when the speaker is not thinking of himself to the exclusion of others. Cp. [1974](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1974).

##### <a id="s1225"></a> §1225

**Indirect Reflexives**.—The reflexive pronouns are used _indirectly_ when, in a dependent clause, they refer to the subject of the main clause. <!-- page 305 -->

<span lang="grc">Ὀρέστης ἔπεισεν Ἀθηναίους ἑαυτὸν κατάγειν</span> _Orestes persuaded the Athenians to restore him_ (_self_) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.111">T. 1.111</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐβούλετο ὁ Κλέαρχος ἅπαν τὸ στράτευμα πρὸς ἑαυτὸν ἔχειν τὴν γνώμην</span> _Clearchus wished the entire army to be devoted to himself_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.29">X. A. 2.5.29</cite>. Cp. _sibi_, _se_.

##### <a id="s1226"></a> §1226

When the subject of the leading clause is not the same as the subject of the subordinate clause or of the accusative with the infinitive ([1975](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1975)), the context must decide to which subject the reflexive pronoun refers: (<span lang="grc">ὁ κατήγορος</span>) <span lang="grc">ἔφη . . . ἀναπείθοντα τοὺς νέους αὐτὸν . . . οὕτω διατιθέναι τοὺς ἐαυτῷ συνόντας κ.τ.λ.</span> the accuser _said that, by persuading the young, he_ (Socrates) _so disposed his_ (i.e. Socrates') _pupils_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.2.52">X. M. 1.2.52</cite>.

##### <a id="s1227"></a> §1227

<span lang="grc">ἑαυτοῦ</span>, etc., are rarely used as indirect reflexives in adjectival clauses: <span lang="grc">τὰ ναυά̄για, ὅσα πρὸς τῇ ἑαυτῶν</span> (<span lang="grc">γῇ</span>) <span lang="grc">ἦν, ἀνείλοντο</span> _they took up the wrecks, as many as were close to their own land_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.92">T. 2.92</cite>.

##### <a id="s1228"></a> §1228

Instead of the indirect <span lang="grc">ἑαυτοῦ</span>, etc., there may be used

**a.** The oblique cases of <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span>: <span lang="grc">ἐπειρᾶτο τοὺς Ἀθηναίους τῆς ἐς αὐτὸν ὀργῆς παραλύ̄ειν</span> _he tried to divert the Athenians from their anger against himself_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.65">T. 2.65</cite>. When <span lang="grc">ἑαυτοῦ</span>, etc. precede, <span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ</span>, etc. are usual instead of the direct reflexive: <span lang="grc">τὴν ἑαυτοῦ γνώμην ἀπεφαίνετο Σωκράτης πρὸς τοὺς ὁμῑλοῦντας αὐτῷ</span> _Socrates was wont to set forth his opinion to those who conversed with him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.7.1">X. M. 4.7.1</cite>.

**b.** Of the forms of the third personal pronoun, <span lang="grc">οἷ</span> and <span lang="grc">σφίσι</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">οὗ, σφεῖς, σφῶν</span>, and <span lang="grc">σφᾶς</span>). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἠρώτᾱ αὐτὴν εἰ ἐθελήσοι διᾱκονῆσαί οἱ</span> _he asked her if she would be willing to do him a service_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 1.16">Ant. 1.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοὺς παῖδας ἐκέλευον τοῦ Κύ̄ρου δεῖσθαι διαπρά̄ξασθαι σφίσιν</span> _they ordered their boys to ask Cyrus to get it done for them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.1">X. C. 1.4.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">κελεύουσι γὰρ ἡμᾶς κοινῇ μετὰ σφῶν πολεμεῖν</span> _for they urge us to make war in common with them_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 3.27">And. 3.27</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔφη δέ, ἐπειδὴ οὗ ἐκβῆναι τὴν ψῡχὴν . . . . . . ἀφικνεῖσθαι σφᾶς εἰς τόπον τινὰ δαιμόνιον</span> _he said that when his soul had departed out of him, they_ (he and others) _came to a mysterious place_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 614b">P. R. 614b</cite>. See [1195](#s1195).

N. 1.—<span lang="grc">σφεῖς</span> may be employed in a dependent sentence if the pronoun is itself the subject of a subordinate statement, and when the reference to the subject of the leading verb is demanded by way of contrast or emphasis: <span lang="grc">εἰσαγαγὼν τοὺς ἄλλους στρατηγοὺς . . . λέγειν ἐκέλευεν αὐτοὺς ὅτι οὐδὲν ἂν ἧττον σφεῖς ἀγάγοιεν τὴν στρατιὰ̄ν ἢ Ξενοφῶν</span> _after bringing in the rest of the generals he urged them to say that they could lead the army just as well as Xenophon_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.5.9">X. A. 7.5.9</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">αὐτοί</span> (_ipsi_) is possible. In the singular <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> is necessary.

N. 2.—Thucydides often uses the plural forms in reference to the _nearest_ subject: <span lang="grc">τοὺς ξυμμάχους ἐδέδισαν σφῶν</span> _they were afraid of their own allies_ ( = <span lang="grc">σφῶν αὐτῶν</span>) 5. 14.

N. 3.—<span lang="grc">ἑαυτοῦ</span>, etc., are either direct or indirect reflexives, <span lang="grc">οἷ</span> and <span lang="grc">σφίσι</span> are only indirect reflexives.

##### <a id="s1229"></a> §1229

<span lang="grc">οὗ, σφίσι</span>, etc., and the oblique cases of <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> are used when the subordinate clause does not form a part of the thought of the principal subject. This is usual in subordinate indicative clauses, and very common in <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> and <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> clauses, in indirect questions, and in general in subordinate clauses not directly dependent on the main verb: <span lang="grc">τῶν πρέσβεων, οἳ σφίσι</span> ([1481](18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1481)) <span lang="grc">περὶ τῶν σπονδῶν ἔτυχον ἀπόντες, ἠμέλουν</span> _they thought no more about their envoys, who were absent_ <!-- page 306 --> _on the subject of the truce_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.44">T. 5.44</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐφοβοῦντο μὴ ἐπιθοῖντο αὐτοῖς οἱ πολέμιοι</span> _they were afraid lest the enemy should attack them_ (_selves_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.4.1">X. A. 3.4.1</cite>.

##### <a id="s1230"></a> §1230

The reflexive pronoun of the third person is sometimes used for that of the first or second: <span lang="grc">δεῖ ἡμᾶς ἀνερέσθαι ἑαυτούς</span> _we must ask ourselves_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 78b">P. Ph. 78b</cite>, <span lang="grc">παράγγελλε τοῖς ἑαυτοῦ</span> _give orders to your men_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.3.27">X. C. 6.3.27</cite>.

**a.** In Homer <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> _his_ is used for <span lang="grc">ἐμός</span> or <span lang="grc">σός</span>: <span lang="grc">οὔτοι ἔγωγε ἧς γαίης δύναμαι γλυκερώτερον ἄλλο ἰδέσθαι</span> _I can look on nothing sweeter than my own land_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 9.28"><span lang="grc">ι</span> 28</cite>.

##### <a id="s1231"></a> §1231

**Reciprocal Reflexive**.—The plural forms of the reflexive pronouns are often used for the reciprocal <span lang="grc">ἀλλήλων, ἀλλήλοις</span>, etc.: <span lang="grc">ἡμῖν αὐτοῖς διαλεξόμεθα</span> _we will converse with_ (_ourselves_) _one another_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 48.6">D. 48.6</cite>.

##### <a id="s1232"></a> §1232

But the reciprocal must be used when the idea ‘each for or with himself’ is expressed or implied: <span lang="grc">μᾶλλον χαίρουσιν ἐπὶ τοῖς ἀλλήλων κακοῖς ἢ τοῖς αὑτῶν ἰδίοις ἀγαθοῖς</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἢ ἐπὶ τοῖς αὑτοῦ ἕκαστος ἀγαθοῖς</span>) _they take greater pleasure in one another's troubles than each man in his own good fortune_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.168">I. 4.168</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὔτε γὰρ ἑαυτοῖς οὔτε ἀλλήλοις ὁμολογοῦσιν</span> _they are in agreement neither with themselves nor with one another_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 237c">P. Phae. 237c</cite>. Reciprocal and reflexive may occur in the same sentence without difference of meaning (<cite data-ref="Dem. 48.9">D. 48.9</cite>). The reflexive is regularly used when there is a contrast (expressed or implied) with <span lang="grc">ἄλλοι</span>: <span lang="grc">φθονοῦσιν ἑαυτοῖς μᾶλλον ἢ τοῖς ἄλλοις ἀνθρώποις</span> _they envy one another more than_ (they envy) _the rest of mankind_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.5.16">X. M. 3.5.16</cite>.

### <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> EMPHATIC OR REFLEXIVE WITH OTHER PRONOUNS

##### <a id="s1233"></a> §1233

Of the plural forms, <span lang="grc">ἡμῶν αὐτῶν</span>, etc. may be either emphatic or reflexive; <span lang="grc">αὐτῶν ἡμῶν</span>, etc. are emphatic only; but <span lang="grc">σφῶν αὐτῶν</span> is only reflexive (<span lang="grc">αὐτῶν σφῶν</span> is not used). In Hom. <span lang="grc">αὐτόν</span> may mean _myself_, _thyself_ , or _himself_, and <span lang="grc">ἓ αὐτόν, οἷ αὐτῷ</span>, etc. are either emphatic or reflexive.

##### <a id="s1234"></a> §1234

<span lang="grc">ἡμῶν</span> (<span lang="grc">ὑ̄μῶν, σφῶν</span>) <span lang="grc">αὐτῶν</span> often mean ‘their own men,’ ‘their own side’: <span lang="grc">φυλακὴν σφῶν τε αὐτῶν καὶ τῶν ξυμμάχων καταλιπόντες</span> _leaving a garrison_ (consisting) _of their own men and of the allies_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.114">T. 5.114</cite>.

##### <a id="s1235"></a> §1235

<span lang="grc">αὐτός</span>, in agreement with the subject, may be used in conjunction with a reflexive pronoun for the sake of emphasis: <span lang="grc">αὐτοὶ ἐφ᾽ ἑαυτῶν ἐχώρουν</span> _they marched by themselves_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.4.10">X. A. 2.4.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">αὐτὸς . . . ἑαυτὸν ἐν μέσῳ κατετίθετο τοῦ στρατοπέδου</span> _he located himself in the centre of the camp_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.5.8">X. C. 8.5.8</cite>.

##### <a id="s1236"></a> §1236

<span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> may be added to a personal pronoun for emphasis. The forms <span lang="grc">ἐμὲ αὐτόν, αὐτόν με</span>, etc. are not reflexive like <span lang="grc">ἐμαυτόν</span>, etc. Thus, <span lang="grc">τοὺς παῖδας τοὺς ἐμοὺς ᾔσχῡνε καὶ ἐμὲ αὐτὸν ὕ̄βρισε</span> _he disgraced my children and insulted me myself_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 1.4">L. 1.4</cite>. Cp. <span lang="grc">αὐτῷ μοι ἐπέσσυτο</span> _he sprang upon me myself_ E 459. Cp. [329 D](../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s329).

##### <a id="s1237"></a> §1237

The force of <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> thus added is to differentiate. Thus <span lang="grc">ἐμὲ αὐτόν</span> means _myself and no other_, <span lang="grc">ἐμαυτόν</span> means simply _myself_ without reference to others. <span lang="grc">ὑ̄μᾶς αὐτούς</span> is the usual order in the reflexive combination; but the differentiating _you yourselves_ (_and no others_) may be <span lang="grc">ὑ̄μᾶς αὐτούς</span> or <span lang="grc">αὐτοὺς ὑ̄μᾶς</span>.

## THE DEMONSTRATIVE PRONOUNS

<!-- page 307 -->

##### <a id="s1238"></a> §1238

The demonstrative pronouns are used substantively or adjectively: <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span>, or <span lang="grc">οὗτος ὁ ἀνήρ</span>, _this man_.

##### <a id="s1239"></a> §1239

A demonstrative pronoun may agree in gender with a substantive predicated of it, if connected with the substantive by a copulative verb ([917](02-syntax-of-the-simple-sentence.md#s917)) expressed or understood: <span lang="grc">αὕτη</span> (for <span lang="grc">τοῦτο</span>) <span lang="grc">ἀρίστη διδασκαλίᾱ</span> _this is the best manner of learning_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.7.24">X. C. 8.7.24</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ δέ τις ταύτην</span> (for <span lang="grc">τοῦτο</span>) <span lang="grc">εἰρήνην ὑπολαμβάνει</span> _but if any one regards this as peace_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.9">D. 9.9</cite>.

**a.** But the unattracted neuter is common, especially in definitions where the pronoun is the predicate: <span lang="grc">τοῦτ᾽ ἔστιν ἡ δικαιοσύνη</span> _this is_ (what we call) _justice_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 432b">P. R. 432b</cite>. So <span lang="grc">οὐχ ὕβρις ταῦτ᾽ ἐστί</span>; _is not this insolence?_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Frogs 21">Ar. Ran. 21</cite>.

##### <a id="s1240"></a> §1240

<span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> and <span lang="grc">ὅδε</span> _this_ usually refer to something near in place, time, or thought; <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span> _that_ refers to something more remote. <span lang="grc">οὑτοσί̄</span> and <span lang="grc">ὁδί̄</span> are emphatic, deictic ([333 g](../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s333)) forms (_this here_).

##### <a id="s1241"></a> §1241

**Distinction between <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> and <span lang="grc">ὅδε</span>**.—<span lang="grc">ὅδε</span> _hic_ points with emphasis to an object in the immediate (actual or mental) vicinity of the speaker, or to something just noticed. In the drama it announces the approach of a new actor. <span lang="grc">ὅδε</span> is even used of the speaker himself as the demonstrative of the first person ([1242](#s1242)). <span lang="grc"> **οὗτος** </span> _iste_ may refer to a person close at hand, but less vividly, as in statements in regard to a person concerning whom a question has been asked. When <span lang="grc">ὅδε</span> and <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> are contrasted, <span lang="grc">ὅδε</span> refers to the more important, <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> to the less important, object. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ὅδε βασιλεὺς χωρεῖ</span> _but lo! here comes the king_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 155">S. Ant. 155</cite>, <span lang="grc">αὕτη πέλας σοῦ</span> _here she_ (the person you ask for) _is near thee_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 1474">S. El. 1474</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ ταῦτ᾽ ἀκούειν κἄ̄τι τῶνδ᾽ ἀλγί̄ονα</span> _so that we obey both in these things and in things yet more grievous_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 64">S. Ant. 64</cite>. See also [1245](#s1245). <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> has a wider range of use than the other demonstratives.

##### <a id="s1242"></a> §1242

<span lang="grc">ὅδε</span> is used in poetry for <span lang="grc">ἐγώ</span>: <span lang="grc">τῆσδέ</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἐμοῦ</span>) <span lang="grc">γε ζώσης ἔτι</span> _while I still live_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Trach. 305">S. Tr. 305</cite>. Also for the possessive pronoun of the first person: <span lang="grc">εἴ τις τούσδ᾽ ἀκούσεται λόγους</span> _if any one shall hear these my words_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 1004">S. El. 1004</cite>.

##### <a id="s1243"></a> §1243

<span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> is sometimes used of the second person: <span lang="grc">τίς οὑτοσί̄</span>; _who's this here?_ ( = _who are you?_) <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Ach. 1048">Ar. Ach. 1048</cite>. So in exclamations: <span lang="grc">οὗτος, τί ποιεῖς</span>; _you there! what are you doing?_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Frogs 198">Ar. Ran. 198</cite>.

##### <a id="s1244"></a> §1244

<span lang="grc">τάδε, τάδε πάντα</span> (<span lang="grc">ταῦτα πάντα</span>) are used of something close at hand: <span lang="grc">οὐκ Ἴωνες τάδε εἰσίν</span> _the people here are not Ionians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.77">T. 6.77</cite>.

##### <a id="s1245"></a> §1245

<span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> (<span lang="grc">τοιοῦτος, τοσοῦτος</span>, and <span lang="grc">οὕτως</span>) generally refers to what precedes, <span lang="grc">ὅδε</span> (<span lang="grc">τοιόσδε, τοσόσδε, τηλικόσδε</span>, and <span lang="grc">ὧδε</span>) to what follows.

Thus, <span lang="grc">τοιάδε ἔλεξεν</span> _he spoke as follows_, but <span lang="grc">τοιαῦτα</span> (<span lang="grc">τοσαῦτα</span>) <span lang="grc">εἰπών</span> _after speaking thus_. Cp. <span lang="grc">ὁ Κῦρος ἀκούσᾱς τοῦ Γωβρύου τοιαῦτα τοιάδε πρὸς αὐτὸν ἔλεξε</span> _Cyrus after hearing these words of Gobryas answered him as follows_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.2.31">X. C. 5.2.31</cite>.

##### <a id="s1246"></a> §1246

<span lang="grc">καὶ οὗτος</span> meaning (1) _he too, likewise;_ (2) _and in fact, and that too_, points back: <span lang="grc">Ἀγίᾱς καὶ Σωκράτης . . . καὶ τούτω ἀπεθανέτην</span> _Agias and Socrates . . . they too were put to death_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.6.30">X. A. 2.6.30</cite>; <span lang="grc">ἀπόρων ἐστὶ . . . καὶ τούτων πονηρῶν</span> _it is characteristic of men without resources and that too worthless_ 2. 5. 21 (cp. [1320](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1320)). On <span lang="grc">καὶ ταῦτα</span> see [947](09-the-predicate.md#s947). <!-- page 308 -->

##### <a id="s1247"></a> §1247

But <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span>, etc. sometimes (especially in the neuter) refer to what follows, and <span lang="grc">ὅδε</span>, etc. (though much less often) refer to what precedes: <span lang="grc">μετὰ δὲ τοῦτον εἰ̄πε τοσοῦτον</span> _but after him he spoke as follows_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.14">X. A. 1.3.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοιούτους λόγους εἶπεν</span> _he spoke as follows_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.58">T. 4.58</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοιάδε παρακελευόμενος</span> _exhorting them thus_ (as set forth before) 7. 78, <span lang="grc">ὧδε θάπτουσιν</span> _they bury them thus_ (as described before) 2. 34, <span lang="grc">οὕτως ἔχει</span> _the case is as follows_ (often in the orators).

##### <a id="s1248"></a> §1248

<span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> (especially in the neuter <span lang="grc">τοῦτο</span>) may refer forward to a word or sentence in apposition: <span lang="grc">ὡς μὴ τοῦτο μόνον ἐννοῶνται, τί πείσονται</span> _that they may not consider this alone_ (namely) _what they shall suffer_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.41">X. A. 3.1.41</cite>. So also <span lang="grc">οὕτως. ἐκεῖνος</span> also may refer forward: <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνο κερδαίνειν ἡγεῖται τὴν ἡδονήν</span> _this_ (namely) _pleasure_, _it regards as gain_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 606b">P. R. 606b</cite>. Cp. [990](10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s990).

##### <a id="s1249"></a> §1249

<span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> (<span lang="grc">τοιοῦτος</span>, etc.) is regularly, <span lang="grc">ὅδε</span> (<span lang="grc">τοιόσδε</span>, etc.) rarely, used as the demonstrative antecedent of a relative: <span lang="grc">ὅταν τοιαῦτα λέγῃς, ἃ οὐδεὶς ἂν φήσειεν ἀνθρώπων</span> _when you say such things as no one in the world would say_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 473e">P. G. 473e</cite>. <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> is often used without a conjunction at the beginning of a sentence.

##### <a id="s1250"></a> §1250

When <span lang="grc">ὅδε</span> retains its full force the relative clause is to be regarded as a supplementary addition: <span lang="grc">οὗ δὴ οὖν ἕνεκα λέγω ταῦτα πάντα τόδ᾽ ἐστί</span> _but here's the reason why I say all this!_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Charm. 165a">P. Charm. 165a</cite>.

##### <a id="s1251"></a> §1251

The demonstratives <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span>, etc., when used as antecedents, have an emphatic force that does not reproduce the (unemphatic) English demonstrative _those_, e.g. in _you released those who were present_. Here Greek uses the participle (<span lang="grc">τοὺς παρόντας ἀπελύ̄σατε</span> <cite data-ref="Lys. 20.20">L. 20.20</cite>) or omits the antecedent.

##### <a id="s1252"></a> §1252

<span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> (less often <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span>) may take up and emphasize a preceding subject or object. In this use the pronoun generally comes first, but may be placed after an emphatic word: <span lang="grc">ποιήσαντες στήλην ἐψηφίσαντο εἰς ταύτην ἀναγράφειν τοὺς α᾽·λιτηρίους</span> _having made a slab they voted to inscribe on it the_ (names of the) _offenders_ Lyc. 117, <span lang="grc">ἃ ἂν εἴπῃς, ἔμμενε τούτοις</span> _whatever you say, hold to it_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 345b">P. R. 345b</cite>. The anaphoric <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> in its oblique cases is weaker ([1214](#s1214)).

##### <a id="s1253"></a> §1253

<span lang="grc">τοῦτο, ταῦτα</span> (and <span lang="grc">αὐτό</span>) may take up a substantive idea not expressed by a preceding neuter word: <span lang="grc">οἳ τὴν Ἑλλάδα ἠλευθέρωσαν· ἡμεῖς δὲ οὐδ᾽ ἡμῖν αὐτοῖς βεβαιοῦμεν αὐτό</span> (i.e. <span lang="grc">τὴν ἐλευθερίᾱν</span>) _who freed Greece; whereas we cannot secure this_ (liberty) _even for ourselves_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.122">T. 1.122</cite>.

##### <a id="s1254"></a> §1254

<span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> (less frequently <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span>) is used of well known persons and things. Thus, <span lang="grc">Γοργίᾱς οὗτος</span> _this_ (famous) _Gorgias_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Hipp. Maj. 282b">P. Hipp. M. 282b</cite> (cp. _ille_), <span lang="grc">τούτους τοὺς σῡκοφάντᾱς</span> _these_ (notorious) _informers_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 45a">P. Cr. 45a</cite> (cp. _iste_), <span lang="grc">τὸν Ἀριστείδην ἐκεῖνον</span> _that_ (famous) _Aristides_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 3.21">D. 3.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">Καλλίᾱν ἐκεῖνον</span> _that_ (infamous) _Callias_ 2. 19. <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span> may be used of a deceased person (<cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 368a">P. R. 368a</cite>).

##### <a id="s1255"></a> §1255

When, in the same sentence, and referring to the same object, <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> (or <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span>) is used more than once, the object thus designated is more or less emphatic: <span lang="grc">ὁ θεὸς ἐξαιρούμενος τούτων τὸν νοῦν τούτοις χρῆται ὑπηρέταις</span> _the god deprives them of their senses and employs them as his ministers_ P. Ion 534 c. For the repeated <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span>) an oblique case of <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> is usual.

##### <a id="s1256"></a> §1256

<span lang="grc">τοῦτο μέν . . . τοῦτο δέ</span> _first . . . secondly_, _partly . . . partly_ has, especially in Hdt., nearly the sense of <span lang="grc">τὸ μέν . . . τὸ δέ</span> ([1111](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1111)). <!-- page 309 -->

##### <a id="s1257"></a> §1257

<span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span> refers back (rarely forward, [1248](#s1248)), but implies remoteness in place, time, or thought.

<span lang="grc">Κῦρος καθορᾷ βασιλέᾱ καὶ τὸ ἀμφ᾽ ἐκεῖνον στῖφος</span> _Cyrus perceives the king and the band around him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.26">X. A. 1.8.26</cite>, <span lang="grc">νῆες ἐκεῖναι ἐπιπλέουσιν</span> _yonder are ships sailing up_ to us <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.51">T. 1.51</cite>.

##### <a id="s1258"></a> §1258

<span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span> may refer to any person other than the speaker and the person addressed; and may be employed of a person not definitely described, but referred to in a supposed case. It is even used of the person already referred to by <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> in an oblique case: <span lang="grc">ἂ̄ν αὐτῷ διδῷς ἀργύριον καὶ πείθῃς ἐκεῖνον</span> _if you give him money and persuade him_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 310d">P. Pr. 310d</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span>, when so used, usually stands in a different case than <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span>. The order <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος . . . αὐτός</span> is found: <span lang="grc">πρὸς μὲν ἐκείνους οὐκ εῖπεν ἣν ἔχοι γνώμην, ἀλλ᾽ ἀπέπεμψεν αὐτούς</span> _he did not tell them the plan he had, but dismissed them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 3.2.9">X. H. 3.2.9</cite>.

##### <a id="s1259"></a> §1259

When used to set forth a contrast to another person, <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span> may even refer to the subject of the leading verb (apparent reflexive use): <span lang="grc">ὅταν ἐν τῇ γῇ ὁρῶσιν ἡμᾶς δῃοῦντάς τε καὶ τἀ̄κείνων φθείροντας</span> _when they_ (the Athenians) _see us_ (the Dorians) _in their land plundering and destroying their property_ ( = <span lang="grc">τὰ ἑαυτῶν</span>) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.11">T. 2.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔλεξε τοῖς Χαλδαίοις ὅτι ἥκοι οἴτε ἀπολέσαι ἐπιθῡμῶν ἐκείνους οὔτε πολεμεῖν δεόμενος</span> _he said to the Chaldaeans that he had come neither with the desire to destroy them_ (<span lang="grc">ἐκείνους</span> is stronger than <span lang="grc">αὐτούς</span>) _nor because he wanted to war with them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.2.12">X. C. 3.2.12</cite>.

##### <a id="s1260"></a> §1260

In the phrase <span lang="grc">ὅδε ἐκεῖνος, ὅδε</span> marks a person or thing as present, <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span> a person or thing mentioned before or well known: <span lang="grc">ὅδ᾽ ἐκεῖνος ἐγώ</span> _lo! I am he_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 138">S. O. C. 138</cite>. Colloquial expressions are <span lang="grc">τοῦτ᾽ ἐκεῖνο</span> _there it is!_ (lit. _this is that_) <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Ach. 41">Ar. Ach. 41</cite>, and <span lang="grc">τόδ᾽ ἐκεῖνο</span> _I told you so_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 98">E. Med. 98</cite>.

##### <a id="s1261"></a> §1261

**Distinction between <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span>**.—When reference is made to one of two contrasted objects, <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> refers to the object nearer to the speaker's thought, or to the more important object, or to the object last mentioned. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὥστε πολὺ ἂν δικαιότερον ἐκείνοις τοῖς γράμμασιν ἢ τούτοις πιστεύοιτε</span> _so that you must with more justice put your trust in those lists_ (not yet put in as evidence) _than in these muster-rolls_ (already mentioned) <cite data-ref="Lys. 16.7">L. 16.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ τοῦτό σοι δοκεῖ μῑκρὸν εἶναι, ἐκεῖνο κατανόησον</span> _but if this appear to you unimportant, consider the following_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.5.29">X. C. 5.5.29</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span> may refer to an object that has immediately preceded: <span lang="grc">καὶ</span> (<span lang="grc">δεῖ</span>) <span lang="grc">τὸ βέλτιστον ἀεί, μὴ τὸ ῥᾷστον, ἅπαντας λέγειν· ἐπ᾽ ἐκεῖνο μὲν</span> (i.e. <span lang="grc">τὸ ῥᾷστον</span>) <span lang="grc">γὰρ ἡ φύσις αὐτὴ βαδιεῖται, ἐπὶ τοῦτο δὲ</span> (<span lang="grc">τὸ βέλτιστον</span>) <span lang="grc">τῷ λόγῳ δεῖ προάγεσθαι διδάσκοντα τὸν ἀγαθὸν πολί̄την</span> _it is necessary that all should speak what is always most salutary, not what is most agreeable; for to the latter nature herself will incline; to the former a good citizen must direct by argument and instruction_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.72">D. 8.72</cite>.

## THE INTERROGATIVE PRONOUNS

##### <a id="s1262"></a> §1262

The interrogative pronouns are used substantively <span lang="grc">τίς</span>; _who?_ or adjectively <span lang="grc">τίς ἀνήρ</span>; _what man?_

##### <a id="s1263"></a> §1263

The interrogatives (pronouns and adverbs, 340, [346](../part-2-inflection/07-adverbs.md#s346)) are used in direct and in indirect questions. In indirect questions <!-- page 310 --> the indefinite relatives <span lang="grc">ὅστις</span>, etc., are generally used instead of the interrogatives.

<span lang="grc">τί βούλεται ἡμῖν χρῆσθαι</span>; _for what purpose does he desire to employ us?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.18">X. A. 1.3.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ οἶδα ὅ τι ἄν τις χρήσαιτο αὐτοῖς</span> _I do not know for what service any one could employ them_ 3. 1. 40, A. <span lang="grc">πηνίκ᾽ ἐστὶν ἄρα τῆς ἡμέρας</span>; B. <span lang="grc">ὁπηνίκα</span>; A. _What's the time of day?_ B. (_You ask_), _what time of day it is?_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Birds 1499">Ar. Av. 1499</cite>.

N.—For peculiarities of Interrogative Sentences, see [2666](33-indirect-dependent-questions.md#s2666), [2668](33-indirect-dependent-questions.md#s2668).

##### <a id="s1264"></a> §1264

<span lang="grc">τί</span> is used for <span lang="grc">τίνα</span> as the predicate of a neuter plural subject when the general result is sought and the subject is considered as a unit: <span lang="grc">ταῦτα δὲ τί ἐστιν</span>; _but these things, what are they?_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.167">Aes. 3.167</cite>. <span lang="grc">τίνα</span> emphasizes the details: <span lang="grc">τίν᾽ οὖν ἐστι ταῦτα</span>; <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.246">D. 18.246</cite>.

##### <a id="s1265"></a> §1265

<span lang="grc">τίς</span> asks a question concerning the class, <span lang="grc">τί</span> concerning the nature of a thing: <span lang="grc">εἰπὲ τίς ἡ τέχνη</span> _say of what sort the art is_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 449a">P. G. 449a</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί σωφροσύνη, τί πολῑτικός</span>; _what is temperance, what is a statesman?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.1.16">X. M. 1.1.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">φθόνον δὲ σκοπῶν ὅ τι εἴη</span> _considering what envy is_ (_quid sit invidia_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.9.8">X. M. 3.9.8</cite>.

## THE INDEFINITE PRONOUNS

##### <a id="s1266"></a> §1266

The indefinite pronoun <span lang="grc">τὶς, τὶ</span> is used both substantively (_some one_) and adjectively (_any_, _some_). <span lang="grc">τὶς, τὶ</span> cannot stand at the beginning of a sentence ([181 b](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s181)).

##### <a id="s1267"></a> §1267

In the singular, <span lang="grc">τὶς</span> is used in a collective sense: _everybody_ (for _anybody_); cp. Germ. _man_, Fr. _on_: <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ μῑσεῖ τις ἐκεῖνον</span> _but everybody detests him_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.8">D. 4.8</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἕκαστός τις, πᾶς τις</span> _each one, every one_ are generally used in this sense. <span lang="grc">τὶς</span> may be a covert allusion to a known person: <span lang="grc">δώσει τις δίκην</span> _some one_ (i.e. _you_) _will pay the penalty_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Frogs 554">Ar. Ran. 554</cite>. It may also stand for _I_ or _we_. Even when added to a noun with the article, <span lang="grc">τὶς</span> denotes the indefiniteness of the person referred to: <span lang="grc">ὅταν δ᾽ ὁ κύ̄ριος παρῇ τις, ὑ̄μῶν ὅστις ἐστὶν ἡγεμών κτλ</span>. _but whenever your master arrives, whoever he be that is your leader_, etc. <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 289">S. O. C. 289</cite>. With a substantive, <span lang="grc">τὶς</span> may often be rendered _a_, _an_, as in <span lang="grc">ἕτερός τις δυνάστης</span> _another dignitary_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.20">X. A. 1.2.20</cite>; or, to express indefiniteness of nature, by _a sort of_, etc., as in <span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν θεοί τινές εἰσιν οἱ δαίμονες</span> _if the_ ‘_daimones_’ _are a sort of gods_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 27d">P. A. 27d</cite>.

##### <a id="s1268"></a> §1268

With adjectives, adverbs, and numerals, <span lang="grc">τὶς</span> may strengthen or weaken an assertion, apologize for a comparison, and in general qualify a statement: <span lang="grc">δεινός τις ἀνήρ</span> _a very terrible man_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 596c">P. R. 596c</cite>, <span lang="grc">μύωψ τις</span> _a sort of gad-fly_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 30e">P. A. 30e</cite>, <span lang="grc">σχεδόν τι</span> _pretty nearly_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 4.11">X. O. 4.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">τριά̄κοντά τινες</span> _about 30_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.73">T. 8.73</cite>. But in <span lang="grc">παρεγένοντό τινες δύο νῆες</span> the numeral is appositional to <span lang="grc">τινές</span> (_certain_, _that is, two ships joined_ them) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.100">T. 8.100</cite>.

##### <a id="s1269"></a> §1269

<span lang="grc">τὶς, τὶ</span> sometimes means _somebody_ , or _something_, _of importance:_ <span lang="grc">τὸ δοκεῖν τινὲς εἶναι</span> _the seeming to be somebody_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.213">D. 21.213</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔδοξέ τι λέγειν</span> _he seemed to say something of moment_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.20">X. C. 1.4.20</cite>.

##### <a id="s1270"></a> §1270

<span lang="grc">τὶ</span> is not omitted in <span lang="grc">θαυμαστὸν λέγεις</span> _what you say is wonderful_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 657a">P. L. 657a</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἤ τις ἢ οὐδείς</span> means _few or none_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.5.45">X. C. 7.5.45</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἤ τι ἢ οὐδέν</span> _little or nothing_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 17b">P. A. 17b</cite>.

## THE ADJECTIVE PRONOUNS <span lang="grc">ἄλλος</span> AND <span lang="grc">ἕτερος</span>

<!-- page 311 -->

##### <a id="s1271"></a> §1271

<span lang="grc">ἄλλος</span> strictly means _other_ (of several), <span lang="grc">ἕτερος</span> _other_ (of two). On <span lang="grc">ὁ ἄλλος, οἱ ἄλλοι</span> see [1188](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1188).

**a.** <span lang="grc">ἕτερος</span> is sometimes used loosely for <span lang="grc">ἄλλος</span>, but always with a sense of _difference;_ when so used it does not take the article.

##### <a id="s1272"></a> §1272

<span lang="grc">ἄλλος</span>, and <span lang="grc">ἕτερος</span> (rarely), may be used attributively with a substantive, which is to be regarded as an appositive. In this sense they may be rendered _besides_, _moreover_, _as well:_ <span lang="grc">οἱ ἄλλοι Ἀθηναῖοι</span> _the Athenians as well_ (_the others_, i.e. _the Athenians_) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.70">T. 7.70</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοὺς ὁπλί̄τᾱς καὶ τοὺς ἄλλους ἱππέᾱς</span> _the hoplites and the cavalry besides_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.4.9">X. H. 2.4.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">γέρων χωρεῖ μεθ᾽ ἑτέρου νεᾱνίου</span> _an old man comes with_ (_a second person, a young man_) _a young man besides_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Eccl. 849">Ar. Eccl. 849</cite>. Cp. “And there were also two other malefactors led with him to be put to death” St. Luke 23. 32.

##### <a id="s1273"></a> §1273

<span lang="grc">ἄλλος</span> _other_, _rest_ often precedes the particular thing with which it is contrasted: <span lang="grc">τά τε ἄλλα ἐτί̄μησε καὶ μῡρίους ἔδωκε δᾱρεικούς</span> _he gave me ten thousand darics besides honouring me in other ways_ (lit. _he both honoured me in other ways and_ etc.) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.3">X. A. 1.3.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῷ μὲν ἄλλῳ στρατῷ ἡσύχαζεν, ἑκατὸν δὲ πελταστὰ̄ς προπέμπει</span> _with the rest of the army he kept quiet, but sent forward a hundred peltasts_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.111">T. 4.111</cite>.

##### <a id="s1274"></a> §1274

<span lang="grc">ἄλλος</span> followed by another of its own cases or by an adverb derived from itself (cp. _alius aliud, one . . . one_, _another . . . another_) does not require the second half of the statement to be expressed: <span lang="grc">ἄλλος ἄλλα λέγει</span> _one says one thing, another_ (_says_) _another_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.15">X. A. 2.1.15</cite> (lit. _another other things_). So <span lang="grc">ἄλλοι ἄλλως, ἄλλοι ἄλλοθεν</span>.

**a.** Similarly <span lang="grc">ἕτερος</span>, as <span lang="grc">συμφορὰ̄ ἑτέρᾱ ἑτέρους πιέζει</span> _one calamity oppresses one_, _another others_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Alc. 893">E. Alc. 893</cite>.

##### <a id="s1275"></a> §1275

After <span lang="grc">ὁ ἄλλος</span> an adjective or a participle used substantively usually requires the article: <span lang="grc">τἆλλα τὰ μέγιστα</span> _the other matters of the highest moment_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 22d">P. A. 22d</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">τὰ μέγιστα</span> is in apposition to <span lang="grc">τἆλλα</span> ([1272](#s1272)). <span lang="grc">οἱ ἄλλοι πάντες οἱ, τἆλλα πάντα τά</span> sometimes omit the final article.

##### <a id="s1276"></a> §1276

<span lang="grc">ὁ ἄλλος</span> often means _usual_, _general:_ <span lang="grc">παρὰ τὸν ἄλλον τρόπον</span> _contrary to my usual disposition_ Ant. 3. <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 2.1"><span lang="grc">β</span>. 1</cite>.

## THE RECIPROCAL PRONOUN

##### <a id="s1277"></a> §1277

The pronoun <span lang="grc">ἀλλήλοιν</span> expresses reciprocal relation: <span lang="grc">ὡς δ᾽ εἰδέτην ἀλλήλους ἡ γυνὴ καὶ ὁ Ἀβραδάτᾱς, ἠσπάζοντο ἀλλήλους</span> _when Abradatas and his wife saw each other, they mutually embraced_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.1.47">X. C. 6.1.47</cite>.

##### <a id="s1278"></a> §1278

To express reciprocal relation Greek uses also (1) the middle forms ([1726](20-the-verb-voices/03-middle-voice.md#s1726)); (2) the reflexive pronoun ([1231](#s1231)); or (3) a substantive is repeated: <span lang="grc">ἀνὴρ ἕλεν ἄνδρα</span> _man fell upon man_ O 328.

On Relative Pronouns see under Complex Sentences ([2493 f](30-adjective-clauses-relative-clauses-2488-2573.md#s2493)f.).
