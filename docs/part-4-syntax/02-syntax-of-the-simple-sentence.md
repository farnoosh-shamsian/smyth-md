# SYNTAX OF THE SIMPLE SENTENCE

##### <a id="s906"></a> §906

The most simple form of sentence is the finite verb: <span lang="grc">ἐσ-τί</span> _he-is_, <span lang="grc">λέγο-μεν</span> _we-say_, <span lang="grc">ἕπε-σθε</span> _you-follow_.

Here the subject is in the personal ending, the predicate in the verbal stem. No other single word than a verb can of itself form a complete sentence.

##### <a id="s907"></a> §907

The subject of a sentence is a substantive or one of its equivalents.

##### <a id="s908"></a> §908

**Equivalents of the Substantive**.—The function of the substantive may be assumed by a pronoun, adjective (in masculine and feminine more frequently with the article), numeral, participle, relative clause (<span lang="grc">οἳ ἐλήφθησαν τῶν πολεμίων ταὐτὰ ἤγγελλον</span> _those of the enemy who were captured made the same report_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.13">X. A. 1.7.13</cite>); by the article with an adverb (<span lang="grc">οἱ τότε</span> _the men of that day_), or with the genitive (<span lang="grc">τὰ τῆς τύχης</span> _the incidents of fortune, fortune_ ([1299](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1299))); by a prepositional phrase (<span lang="grc">οἱ ἀμφὶ τὸν Σωκράτη</span> _Socrates and his followers;_ <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ μέγα</span> _a great part_), a preposition with a numeral (<span lang="grc">ἔφυγον περὶ ὀκτακοσίους</span> _about eight hundred took to flight_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.5.10">X. H. 6.5.10</cite>); by an infinitive with or without the article ([1984](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1984), [2025](21-verbal-nouns.md#s2025)); and by any word or phrase viewed merely as a thing (<span lang="grc">τὸ ὑ̄μεῖς ὅταν λέγω, τὴν πόλιν λέγω</span> _when I say You_, _I mean the State_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.88">D. 18.88</cite>). Cp. [1153 g](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1153). (Furthermore, by a clause in a complex sentence, 2189. 1.)

##### <a id="s909"></a> §909

The predicate of a sentence is always a verb. The verb may either stand alone, as in <span lang="grc">Περικλῆς ἀπῆλθε</span> _Pericles departed;_ or it may have certain modifiers, called _complements to the predicate_ (nouns, participles, adverbs), as <span lang="grc">Περικλῆς ἀπῆλθε πρῶτος</span> _first_ (<span lang="grc">ὀργιζόμενος</span> _in anger;_ <span lang="grc">τότε</span> _then_). Cp. [924](04-expansion-of-the-simple-sentence.md#s924).

##### <a id="s910"></a> §910

**Predicate Nouns**.—Nouns (substantival or adjectival) are often used as complements to the predicate. Thus,

**a.** A _predicate substantive_ is a substantive forming part of the predicate and asserting something of its substantive: <span lang="grc">Περικλῆς ᾑρέθη στρατηγός</span> _Pericles was elected general_, <span lang="grc">εἵλεσθε ἐκεῖνον πρεσβευτήν</span> _you elected him envoy_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 13.10">L. 13.10</cite>.

**b.** A _predicate adjective_ is an adjective forming part of the predicate and asserting something of its substantive: <span lang="grc">ὁ ἀνὴρ δίκαιός ἐστι</span> _the man is just_, <span lang="grc">ἐνόμισαν Περικλέᾱ εὐτυχῆ</span> _they thought Pericles fortunate_.

##### <a id="s911"></a> §911

A predicate substantive or adjective may often be distinguished from an _attributive_ ([912](#s912)) in that the former implies some form of <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span> _be_. Thus, <span lang="grc">πρεσβευτήν</span> and <span lang="grc">εὐτυχῆ</span> in 910. After verbs signifying _to name_ or _call_, <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span> is sometimes expressed ([1615](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1615)).

##### <a id="s912"></a> §912

**Attributive Adjective**.—An attributive adjective is an adjective simply added to a noun to describe it, and not forming any part of an assertion made about it: <span lang="grc">ὁ δίκαιος ἀνήρ</span> _the just-man_. <!-- page 257 -->

##### <a id="s913"></a> §913

All adjectives that are not attributive are predicate. So <span lang="grc">πρῶτοι ἀφί̄&lt;\*&gt;οντο</span> _they were the first to arrive_ ([1042 b](14-adjectives.md#s1042)), <span lang="grc">τούτῳ φίλῳ χρῶμαι</span> _I treat this man &lt;\*&gt;s a friend_ (= <span lang="grc">οὗτος, ᾧ χρῶμαι, φίλος ἐστί</span>).

##### <a id="s914"></a> §914

Under adjectives are included participles: <span lang="grc">ὁ μέλλων</span> (attrib.) <span lang="grc">πόλεμος</span> _the future war_, <span lang="grc">ταῦτα εἰπὼν</span> (pred.) <span lang="grc">ἀπῄειν</span> _saying this he went off_, <span lang="grc">ὁρῶ σε κρύπτοντα</span> (pred.) _I see you hiding_.

##### <a id="s915"></a> §915

Predicate substantives, adjectives, and participles, in agreement either with subject or object, are more common in Greek than in English, and often call for special shifts in translation: <span lang="grc">μετεώρους ἐξεκόμισαν τὰ̄ς ἁμάξᾱς</span> _they lifted the wagons and carried them out_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.8">X. A. 1.5.8</cite>. Cp. [1579](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1579).

##### <a id="s916"></a> §916

**Appositive**.—An appositive is a noun added to another noun or to a pronoun to describe or define it: <span lang="grc">Μιλτιάδης ὁ στρατηγός</span> _Miltiades_, _the general_, <span lang="grc">ὑ̄μεῖς οἱ ἱερεῖς</span> _you_, _the priests_, <span lang="grc">τοῦτο, ὃ σὺ εἶπες, ἀ̄εὶ πάρεστι, σχολή</span> _this_, _which you mentioned, is always present_, (I mean) _leisure_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 172d">P. Th. 172d</cite>.

##### <a id="s917"></a> §917

**Copula**.—An indeterminate verb that serves simply to couple a predicate substantive or adjective to the subject is called a _copula:_ <span lang="grc">Ξενοφῶν ἦν Ἀθηναῖος</span> _Xenophon was an Athenian_.

**a.** The most common copulative verbs are <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span> _be_ and <span lang="grc">γίγνεσθαι</span> _become_. Many other verbs serve as copulas: <span lang="grc">καθίστασθαι</span> _become_, <span lang="grc">πεφῡκέναι, ὑπάρχειν, πέλειν</span> (poetical) _be_, <span lang="grc">δοκεῖν</span> _seem_, <span lang="grc">φαίνεσθαι</span> _appear_. <span lang="grc">καλεῖσθαι, ὀνομάζεσθαι, ἀκούειν, κλύειν</span> (poetical) _be called_, <span lang="grc">τυγχάνειν, κυρεῖν</span> (poet.) _happen_, _turn out_, <span lang="grc">αἱρεῖσθαι</span> _be chosen_, <span lang="grc">νομίζεσθαι</span> _be regarded_, <span lang="grc">κρί̄νεσθαι</span> _be judged_, and the like.

##### <a id="s918"></a> §918

**a.** The copula is strictly the predicate or is a part of the predicate with its supplements.

**b.** The above verbs may also be complete predicates: <span lang="grc">ἔστι θεός</span> _there is a god_.

**c.** For the omission of the copula, see [944](09-the-predicate.md#s944).

**d.** A predicate substantive or adjective stands in the same case as the subject when coupled to it by a copulative verb ([939](08-case-of-the-subject-the-nominative.md#s939)).

**e.** For <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span> added to a copulative verb, see [1615](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1615).

##### <a id="s919"></a> §919

**Object**.—A verb may have an object on which its action is exerted. The object is a substantive (or its equivalent, [908](#s908)) in an oblique case. An object may be _direct_ (in the accusative) or _indirect_ (in the genitive or dative): <span lang="grc">Κῦρος δώσει ἓξ μνᾶς</span> (direct) <span lang="grc">τῷ δούλῳ</span> (indirect) _Cyrus will give six minae to the slave_, <span lang="grc">ἔλαβον τῆς ζώνης</span> (indirect) <span lang="grc">τὸν Ὀρόντᾱν</span> (direct) _they took hold of Orontas by the girdle_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.6.10">X. A. 1.6.10</cite>.

##### <a id="s920"></a> §920

**Transitive and Intransitive Verbs**.—Verbs capable of taking a direct object are called _transitive_ because their action _passes over_ to an object Other verbs are called _intransitive_.

**a.** But many intransitive verbs, as in English, are used transitively ([1558](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1558), [1559](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1559)), and verbs usually transitive often take an indirect object ([1341 f](18-the-cases/04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1341)f., 1460 ff., 1471 ff.).
