# OMISSION OF THE SUBJECT

##### <a id="s929"></a> §929

An unemphatic pronoun of the first or second person is generally omitted: <span lang="grc">λέγε τὸν νόμον</span> _read the law_ (spoken to the clerk of the court) <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.8">D. 21.8</cite>.

##### <a id="s930"></a> §930

An emphatic pronoun is generally expressed, as in contrasts: <span lang="grc">σὺ μὲν κεῖνον ἐκδέχου, ἐγὼ δ᾽ ἄπειμι</span> _do thou wait for him, but I will depart_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 123">S. Ph. 123</cite>. But often in poetry and sometimes in prose the pronoun is expressed when no contrast is intended. The first of two contrasted pronouns is sometimes omitted: <span lang="grc">ἀλλά, εἰ βούλει, μέν᾽ ἐπὶ τῷ στρατεύματι, ἐγὼ δ᾽ ἐθέλω πορεύεσθαι</span> _but_, _if you prefer_, _remain with your division_, _I am willing to go_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.4.41">X. A. 3.4.41</cite>. Cp. [1190](17-pronouns.md#s1190), [1191](17-pronouns.md#s1191).

##### <a id="s931"></a> §931

The nominative subject of the third person may be omitted

**a.** When it is expressed or implied in the context: <span lang="grc">ὁ σὸς πατὴρ φοβεῖται μὴ τὰ ἔσχατα πάθῃ</span> _your father is afraid lest he suffer death_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.22">X. C. 3.1.22</cite>.

**b.** When the subject is indefinite, especially when it is the same person or thing as the omitted subject of a preceding infinitive ([937 a](#s937)): <span lang="grc">ἡ τοῦ οἴεσθαι εἰδένάι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀμαθίᾱ</span>), <span lang="grc">ἃ οὐκ οἶδεν</span> _the ignorance of thinking_ one _knows what_ one _does not know_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 29b">P. A. 29b</cite>. Often in legal language: <span lang="grc">ὁ νόμος, ὃς κελεύει τὰ ἑαυτοῦ ἐξεῖναι διαθέσθαι ὅπως ἂν ἐθέλῃ</span> _the law, which enjoins that_ a man _has the right to dispose of his property as he wishes_ <cite data-ref="Isaeus 2.13">Is. 2.13</cite>.

**c.** When a particular person is meant, who is easily understood from the situation: <span lang="grc">τοὺς νόμους ἀναγνώσεται</span> _he_ (the clerk) _will read the laws_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.15">Aes. 3.15</cite>.

**d.** When it is a general idea of person, and usually in the third person plural of _verbs of saying_ and _thinking:_ <span lang="grc">ὡς λέγουσιν</span> _as they say_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 5.18">D. 5.18</cite>. So <span lang="grc">φᾱσί</span> _they say_, <span lang="grc">οἴονται</span> _people think;_ cp. _aiunt_, _ferunt_, _tradunt_. <!-- page 260 -->

**e.** In descriptions of locality: <span lang="grc">ἦν δὲ κρημνῶδες</span> _for it_ (the place) _was steep_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.84">T. 7.84</cite>.

**f.** In impersonal verbs ([932](#s932), [934](#s934)).

##### <a id="s932"></a> §932

**Impersonal Verbs** ([905](01-definitions.md#s905)).—The subject of a true impersonal verb is a vague notion that cannot be supplied from the context: <span lang="grc">ὀψὲ ἦν</span> _it was late_, <span lang="grc">καλῶς ἔχει</span> _it is well_, <span lang="grc">ἤδη ἦν ἀμφὶ ἀγορὰ̄ν πλήθουσαν</span> _it was already about the time when the market-place is full_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.1">X. A. 1.8.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">αὐτῷ οὐ προυχώρει</span> _it_ (the course of events) _did not go well with him_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.109">T. 1.109</cite>.

##### <a id="s933"></a> §933

An impersonal verb the subject of which may be derived from the context is called _quasi-impersonal_.

**a.** When the indefinite _it_ anticipates an infinitive or subordinate proposition which forms the logical subject ([1985](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1985)). So with <span lang="grc">δοκεῖ</span> _it seems_, <span lang="grc">συμβαίνει</span> _it happens_, <span lang="grc">ἔξεστι</span> _it is permitted_, <span lang="grc">πρέπει, προσήκει</span> _it is fitting_, <span lang="grc">φαίνεται</span> _it appears_, <span lang="grc">ἐγένετο</span> _it happened_, <span lang="grc">εἰσῄει με</span> _venit me in mentem_, <span lang="grc">δηλοῖ</span> _it is evident_, etc. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὑ̄μᾶς προσήκει προθῡμοτέρους εἶναι</span> _it behooves you to be more zealous_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.15">X. A. 3.2.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰσῄει αὐτοὺς ὅπως ἂν οἴκαδε ἀφίκωνται</span> _it came into their thoughts how they should reach home_ 6. 1. 17.

**b.** So also with <span lang="grc">χρή, δεῖ</span> _it is necessary;_ as, <span lang="grc">δεῖ σ᾽ ἐλθεῖν</span> _you ought to go_ (lit. _to go binds you_). The impersonal construction with <span lang="grc">-τέον</span> is equivalent to <span lang="grc">δεῖ</span> ([2152 a](23-verbal-adjectives-in-tauepsilonomicronsigma.md#s2152)): <span lang="grc">βοηθητέον ἐστὶ τοῖς πρά̄γμασιν ὑ̄μῖν</span> _you must rescue the interests at stake_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 1.17">D. 1.17</cite>.

##### <a id="s934"></a> §934

In some so-called impersonal verbs the person is left unexpressed because the actor is understood or implied in the action. So

**a.** In expressions of natural phenomena originally viewed as produced by a divine agent: <span lang="grc">βροντᾷ</span> _tonat_, <span lang="grc">ὕ̄ει</span> _pluit_, <span lang="grc">νείφει</span> _ningit_, <span lang="grc">χειμάζει</span> _it is stormy_, <span lang="grc">ἔσεισε</span> _it shook, there was an earthquake_. The agent (<span lang="grc">Ζεύς, ὁ θεός</span>) is often (in Hom. always) expressed, as <span lang="grc">Ζεὺς ἀστράπτει</span> _Iuppiter fulget_.

**b.** When the agent is known from the action, which is viewed as alone of importance: <span lang="grc">σαλπίζει</span> _the trumpet sounds_ (i.e. <span lang="grc">ὁ σαλπιγκτὴς σαλπίζει</span> _the trumpeter sounds the trumpet_), <span lang="grc">ἐκήρυξε</span> _proclamation-was made_ (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ὁ κῆρυξ</span>), <span lang="grc">σημαίνει</span> _the signal is given_ (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ὁ κῆρυξ</span> or <span lang="grc">ὁ σαλπιγκτής</span>).

##### <a id="s935"></a> §935

In impersonal passives the subject is merely indicated in the verbal ending: <span lang="grc">λέγεταί τε καὶ γράφεται</span> _speeches_ (<span lang="grc">λόγοι</span>) _and writings_ (<span lang="grc">γράμματα</span>) _are composed_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 261b">P. Phae. 261b</cite>. This construction is relatively rare, but commonest in the perfect and pluperfect: <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἄλλως αὐτοῖς πεπόνηται</span> _their labour has not been lost_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 232a">P. Phae. 232a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ αὐτοῖς παρεσκεύαστο</span> _when their preparations were completed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.3.20">X. H. 1.3.20</cite>.

##### <a id="s936"></a> §936

**Subject of the Infinitive**.—The subject of the infinitive is in the accusative: <span lang="grc">ἐκέλευον αὐτοὺς πορεύεσθαι</span> _they ordered that they should proceed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.2.1">X. A. 4.2.1</cite>.

**a.** See [1975](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1975). On the nominative subject of the infinitive, see [1973](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1973).

##### <a id="s937"></a> §937

**Omission of the Subject of the Infinitive**.—The subject of the infinitive is usually not expressed when it is the same as the subject or object (direct or indirect) of the principal verb: <span lang="grc">ἔφη ἐθέλειν</span> _he said he was willing_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.1.27">X. A. 4.1.27</cite> (contrast _dixit se velle_), <span lang="grc">πάντες αἰτοῦνται τοὺς θεοὺς τὰ φαῦλα ἀπο-</span> <!-- page 261 --> <span lang="grc">τρέπειν</span> _everybody prays the gods to avert evil_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 4">X. S. 4</cite>. 47, <span lang="grc">δός μοι τρεῖς ἡμέρᾱς ἄρξαι αὐτοῦ</span> _grant me the control of him for three days_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.11">X. C. 1.3.11</cite>. Cp. [1060](14-adjectives.md#s1060), [1973](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1973).

**a.** An indefinite subject of the infinitive (<span lang="grc">τινά, ἀνθρώπους</span>) is usually omitted. Cp. [931 b](#s931), [1980](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1980).
