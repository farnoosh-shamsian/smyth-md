# EXCLAMATORY SENTENCES

##### <a id="s2681"></a> §2681

**Direct** (independent) exclamatory sentences with a verb expressed (or easily supplied) are formed

##### <a id="s2682"></a> §2682

(I) By the relative pronouns <span lang="grc">οἷος, ὅσος</span>, or by the relative adverb <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> in exclamations of wonder. The sentences introduced by these words are commonly associated with vocatives or interjections. Cp. [340](../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s340).

<span lang="grc">οἷα ποιεῖς, ὦ ἑταῖρε</span> _what are you about, my friend!_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyph. 15c">P. Euth. 15c</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὦ φίλοι . . . , οἵην τερπωλὴν θεοὶ ἤγαγον ἐς τόδε δῶμα</span> _friends_, _such sport the gods have brought into this house!_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 18.37"><span lang="grc">ς</span> 37</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅσην ἔχεις τὴν δύναμιν</span> _how great your power is!_ Ar. Pl. 748, <span lang="grc">ὦ πάππε, ὅσα πρά̄γματα ἔχεις</span> _oh grandfather, how much trouble you have!_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.4">X. C. 1.3.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὦ φίλταθ᾽ Αἷμον, ὥς σ᾽ ἀτῑμάζει πατήρ</span>, _oh dearest Haemon_, _how thy father insults thee!_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 572">S. Ant. 572</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡς ἀστεῖος ὁ ἄνθρωπος</span> _how charming the man is!_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 116d">P. Ph. 116d</cite>. <!-- page 607 -->

**a.** Exclamatory <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> may be the relative <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>; but if it is the demonstrative <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>, it means properly not _how_ but _so_. Cp. [2998](36-particles/35-omegasigma-omegasigma.md#s2998).

**b.** Double <span lang="grc"> **οἷος** </span> (exclamation within an exclamation) marks a strong contrast (cp. [2646](32-interrogative-sentences-questions.md#s2646)) in direct and indirect exclamations. Thus, <span lang="grc">οἷα πρὸς οἵων ἀνδρῶν πάσχω</span> _what I suffer and at the hands of what men!_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 942">S. Ant. 942</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ οἵᾱς . . . αὐχήματος τοῦ πρώτου ἐς οἵᾱν τελευτὴν καὶ ταπεινότητα ἀφί̄κατο</span> _from what boasting at first they had come to what a humiliating end_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.75">T. 7.75</cite>. Triple <span lang="grc">οἷος</span> in Gorgias, Helen 10.

**c.** Cp. [2647](32-interrogative-sentences-questions.md#s2647) for such sentences as <span lang="grc">οἵᾱν ἔχιδναν τήνδ᾽ ἔφῡσας</span> _what a viper is this woman whom thou hast begotten!_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Ion 1262">E. Ion 1262</cite>.

**d.** <span lang="grc"> **οἴμ᾽ ὡς** </span> is common in expressions of impatience, anger, pity, grief, or fear; as <span lang="grc">οἴμ᾽ ὡς καταγελᾷς</span> _ah me, how you mock me!_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 1238">Ar. Nub. 1238</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἴμ᾽ ὡς ἔοικας ὀψὲ τὴν δίκην ἰδεῖν</span> _ah me, how thou seemest all too late to see the right!_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 1270">S. Ant. 1270</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἴμ᾽ ὡς δέδοικα</span> _ah me, how I fear!_ Ar. Pax 173.

##### <a id="s2683"></a> §2683

(II) By the infinitive ([2015](21-verbal-nouns.md#s2015), [2036](21-verbal-nouns.md#s2036)).

##### <a id="s2684"></a> §2684

Direct exclamations without a verb may be expressed by the vocative or nominative ([1288](18-the-cases/02-vocative.md#s1288)) or by the genitive of cause ([1407](18-the-cases/05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1407)).

##### <a id="s2685"></a> §2685

**Indirect** (dependent) exclamations form subordinate clauses in sentences which, taken as a whole, are _statements_ ([2575](31-dependent-substantive-clauses-2574-2635.md#s2575). 4). They are introduced by <span lang="grc">οἷος, ὅσος, οἵως, ὡς, οἷ, ἵνα</span>. The negative is <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>. It is often difficult to distinguish between indirect exclamations and indirect questions introduced by <span lang="grc">οἷος</span> or <span lang="grc">ὅσος</span>. But observe that dependent exclamations are not introduced by the direct interrogatives <span lang="grc">ποῖος, πόσος, πῶς</span>, etc., nor by the indefinite relatives <span lang="grc">ὁποῖος, ὁπόσος, ὅπως</span>, etc., both of which classes of words may stand in indirect questions.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ὁποῖος</span> in <cite data-ref="Lys. 30.4">L. 30.4</cite> and <span lang="grc">ὁπόσος</span> in <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 522a">P. G. 522a</cite> are suspected.

**b.** The introductory verb is sometimes omitted; as <span lang="grc">ὦ μιαρώτατος, ἵν᾽ ὑποδέδῡκεν</span> _oh the rascal!_ (to think) _where he crept in!_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Wasps 188">Ar. Vesp. 188</cite>.

##### <a id="s2686"></a> §2686

Dependent exclamatory clauses follow, as regards mood and tense, the same rules as govern indirect questions ([2677](33-indirect-dependent-questions.md#s2677)). An original indicative remains unchanged after primary tenses of verbs followed by a finite mood, but may become optative after secondary tenses on the principle of indirect discourse.

**a.** Indicative: <span lang="grc">οἷον ἄνδρα λέγεις ἐν κινδύ̄νῳ εἶναι</span> _what a noble man you say is in danger!_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 142b">P. Th. 142b</cite>, <span lang="grc">σκόπει . . . ἵν᾽ ἥκει τοῦ θεοῦ μαντεύματα</span> _judge to what the oracles of the god have come_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 953">S. O. T. 953</cite> (cp. <span lang="grc">ὦ θεῶν μαντεύματα, ἵν᾽ ἐστέ</span> [946](09-the-predicate.md#s946)), <span lang="grc">τίς οὐκ οἶδεν ἐξ οἵων συμφορῶν εἰς ὅσην εὐδαιμονίᾱν κατέστησαν</span>; _who does not know into what good fortune they came and after what sufferings?_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 6.42">I. 6.42</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐνθῡμούμενοι ὅσον πλοῦν . . . ἀπεστέλλοντο</span> _reflecting on how long a voyage they were on the point of being sent_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.30">T. 6.30</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐννοηθέντες οἷά τε πάσχουσιν ὑπὸ τῶν Ἀσσυρίων καὶ ὅτι νῦν τεθναίη ὁ ἄρχων αὐτῶν</span> _reflecting what they were suffering at the hands of the Assyrians and that their ruler was now dead_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.2.3">X. C. 4.2.3</cite>.

**b.** Optative: <span lang="grc">διαθεώμενος αὐτῶν ὅσην μὲν χώρᾱν καὶ οἵᾱν ἔχοιεν</span> _observing how great the extent of their country was and how excellent its quality_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.19">X. A. 3.1.19</cite>, <!-- page 608 --> <span lang="grc">ἐπιδεικύντες οἵᾱ εἴη ἡ ἀπορίᾱ</span> _pointing out what their difficulty was_ 1. 3. 13. See also [2687](#s2687).

##### <a id="s2687"></a> §2687

Verbs and other words of _emotion_ (praise, blame, wonder, etc.) and the expression of its results are often followed by a dependent exclamatory clause with <span lang="grc">οἷος, ὅσος, ὡς</span>, etc. Here a causal sentence would have <span lang="grc">ὅτι τοιοῦτος, ὅτι τοσοῦτος, ὅτι οὕτως</span>. English generally introduces such clauses by _considering_, _thinking_, _upon the reflection how_, etc. Thus, <span lang="grc">τῶ σ᾽ αὖ νῦν ὀίω ἀποτεισέμεν, ὅσσα μ᾽ ἔοργας</span> _therefore I think now thou shalt in turn atone for all thou hast done unto me_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 21.399"><span lang="grc">Φ</span> 399</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπέκλᾱον . . . τὴν ἐμαυτοῦ τύχην, οἵου ἀνδρὸς ἑταίρου ἐστερημένος εἴην</span> _I bewailed my fate considering what a companion I had lost_ (direct = <span lang="grc">οἵου ἀνδρὸς ἑταίρου</span> (<span lang="grc">ὄντος</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐστέρημαι</span>) <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 117c">P. Ph. 117c</cite>, <span lang="grc">μάκαρ ὦ Στρεψίαδες, αὐτός τ᾽ ἔφῡς ὡς σοφὸς χοἶον τὸν υἱὸν τρέφεις</span> _oh happy Strepsiades_, _how wise you are yourself and what a son you have!_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 1206">Ar. Nub. 1206</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ γῆρας ὑμνοῦσιν ὅσων κακῶν σφίσιν αἴτιον</span> _they rehearse how many evils old age occasions them_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 329b">P. R. 329b</cite>, <span lang="grc">εὐδαίμων μοι ἁ̄νὴρ ἐφαίνετο . . . ὡς ἀδεῶς καὶ γενναίως ἐτελεύτᾱ</span> _the man seemed to me to be happy so fearlessly and nobly did he die_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 58e">P. Ph. 58e</cite>, <span lang="grc">ζηλῶ γε τῆς εὐτυχίᾱς τὸν πρέσβυν, οἷ μετέστη ξηρῶν τρόπων</span> _I envy the old fellow his fortune, how_ (lit. _whither_) _he has changed his arid ways_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Wasps 1451">Ar. Vesp. 1451</cite>.
