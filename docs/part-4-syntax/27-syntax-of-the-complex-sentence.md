# SYNTAX OF THE COMPLEX SENTENCE

##### <a id="s2173"></a> §2173

A complex sentence consists of a principal sentence and one or more subordinate, or dependent, sentences. The principal sentence, as each subordinate sentence, has its own subject and predicate. The principal sentence of a complex sentence is called the principal clause, the subordinate sentence is called the subordinate clause. The principal clause may precede or follow the subordinate clause.

##### <a id="s2174"></a> §2174

The principal clause may have any form of the simple sentence.

**a.** Parentheses belonging to the thought of the entire sentence, but standing in no close grammatical relation to it, count as principal clauses. So <span lang="grc">οἶμαι, δοκῶ, φημί, ὁρᾷς; οἶδα, οἶδ᾽ ὅτι</span> _certainly_ ([2585](31-dependent-substantive-clauses-2574-2635.md#s2585)), <span lang="grc">εὖ ἴσθι</span> _know well_, <span lang="grc">αἰτοῦμαί σε</span> _I beseech thee;_ <span lang="grc">πῶς</span> (<span lang="grc">πόσον</span>) <span lang="grc">δοκεῖς</span>; and <span lang="grc">πῶς οἴει</span>; in the comic poets and Euripides, etc. Some of these expressions are almost adverbial.

##### <a id="s2175"></a> §2175

The subordinate clause is always introduced by a subordinating conjunction, as <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> _if_, <span lang="grc">ἐπεί</span> _since_ or _when_, <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> _that_, <span lang="grc">ἕως</span> _until_, etc.

##### <a id="s2176"></a> §2176

A finite mood in a subordinate clause may be influenced by the tense of the principal clause. If the verb of the principal clause stands in a secondary tense, the verb of the subordinate clause is often optative instead of indicative or subjunctive, as it would have been after a primary tense. Dependence of mood after a secondary tense is never indicated by the subjunctive.

##### <a id="s2177"></a> §2177

Each tense in a subordinate clause denotes stage of action; the _time_ is only relative to that of the leading verb. A subordinate clause may be marked by change of person in verb and pronoun.

##### <a id="s2178"></a> §2178

A subordinate clause in English may be expressed in Greek by a predicate adjective or substantive. Cp. [1169](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1169), [2647](32-interrogative-sentences-questions.md#s2647).

##### <a id="s2179"></a> §2179

A subordinate clause may be coördinate in structure.

<span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ δ᾽ ἠσθένει Δᾱρεῖος καὶ ὑπώπτευε τελευτὴν τοῦ βίου, ἐβούλετό οἱ τὼ παῖδε παρεῖναι</span> _but when Darius was ill and suspected that his end was near, he wished his two sons to be by him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.1">X. A. 1.1.1</cite>.

**a.** So a relative clause, though properly subordinate, may be equivalent to a coördinating clause: <span lang="grc">εἰ δ᾽ ὑ̄μεῖς ἄλλο τι γνώσεσθε, ὃ μὴ γένοιτο, τίν᾽ οἴεσθ᾽ αὐτὴν ψῡχὴν ἕξειν</span>; _but if you decide otherwise_, —_and may this never come to pass!— what do you think will be her feelings?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 28.21">D. 28.21</cite>. In such cases <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> is equivalent to <span lang="grc">καὶ οὗτος, οὗτος δέ, οὗτος γάρ</span>.

##### <a id="s2180"></a> §2180

A clause dependent upon the principal clause may itself be followed by a clause dependent upon itself (a sub-dependent clause). <!-- page 488 -->

<span lang="grc">οἱ δ᾽ ἔλεγον</span> (principal clause) <span lang="grc">ὅτι περὶ σπονδῶν ἥκοιεν ἄνδρες</span> (dependent clause) <span lang="grc">οἵτινες ἱκανοὶ ἔσονται . . . ἀπαγγεῖλαι</span> (sub-dependent clause) _and they said that they had come with regard to a truce and were men who were competent to . . . report_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.4">X. A. 2.3.4</cite>.

##### <a id="s2181"></a> §2181

A verb common to two clauses is generally placed in one clause and omitted from the other (so especially in comparative and relative clauses).

<span lang="grc">ἥπερ</span> (<span lang="grc">τύχη</span>) <span lang="grc">ἀεὶ βέλτῑον</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ἐπιμελεῖται</span>) <span lang="grc">ἢ ἡμεῖς ἡμῶν αὐτῶν ἐπιμελούμεθα</span> _fortune_, _which always cares better for us than we for ourselves_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.12">D. 4.12</cite>. Also as in English: <span lang="grc">ὅ τι δὲ μέλλετε</span> (<span lang="grc">πρά̄σσειν</span>), . . . <span lang="grc">εὐθὺς . . . πρά̄σσετε</span> _but whatever you intend_, _do it at once_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.15">T. 7.15</cite>. In comparative clauses with <span lang="grc">οὐχ ὥσπερ</span> (or <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>) the main and the subordinate clause are sometimes compressed, the predicate of the clause with <span lang="grc">οὐχ</span> being supplied from the <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ</span> clause, which is made independent; as <span lang="grc">οὐχ</span> (<span lang="grc">οὐδὲν ἂν ἐγίγνετο</span>) <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ νῦν τούτων οὐδὲν γίγνεται περὶ αὐτόν</span> _it would not be as now, when none of these things is done for him_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 189c">P. S. 189c</cite>.

## ANTICIPATION (OR PROLEPSIS)

##### <a id="s2182"></a> §2182

The subject of the dependent clause is often anticipated and made the object of the verb of the principal clause. This transference, which gives a more prominent place to the subject of the subordinate clause, is called _anticipation_ or _prolepsis_ (<span lang="grc">πρόληψις</span> _taking before_).

<span lang="grc">δέδοικα δ᾽ αὐτὴν μή τι βουλεύσῃ νέον</span> _but I fear lest she may devise something untoward_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 37">E. Med. 37</cite>, <span lang="grc">ᾔδει αὐτὸν ὅτι μέσον ἔχοι τοῦ Περσικοῦ στρατεύματος</span> _he knew that he held the centre of the Persian army_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.21">X. A. 1.8.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπεμέλετο αὐτῶν ὅπως ἀεὶ ἀνδράποδα διατελοῖεν</span> _he took care that they should always continue to be slaves_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.1.44">X. C. 8.1.44</cite>. Note <span lang="grc">ὁρᾷς τὸν εὐτράπεζον ὡς ἡδὺς βίος</span> _thou seest how sweet is the luxurious life_ E. fr. 1052. 3.

**a.** Anticipation is especially common after _verbs of saying_, _seeing_, _hearing_, _knowing_, _fearing_, _effecting_.

**b.** When a subordinate clause defines a verbal idea consisting of a verb and a substantive, its subject may pass into the principal clause as a genitive depending on the substantive of that clause: <span lang="grc">ἦλθε δὲ καὶ τοῖς Ἀθηναίοις εὐθὺς ἡ ἀγγελίᾱ τῶ πόλεων ὅτι ἀφεστᾶσι</span> _and there came straightway to the Athenians also the report that the cities had revolted_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.61">T. 1.61</cite> (= <span lang="grc">ὅτι αἱ πόλεις ἀφεστᾶσι</span>).

**c.** The subject of the dependent clause may be put first in its own clause: <span lang="grc">ἐπιχειρήσωμεν εἰπεῖν, ἀνδρείᾱ τί ποτ᾽ ἐστίν</span> _let us try to say what courage is_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lach. 190d">P. Lach. 190d</cite>.

**d.** The object of the subordinate clause may be anticipated and made the object of the principal clause. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰρώτᾱ ὁ Δᾱρεῖος τὴν τέχνην εἰ ἐπίσταιτο</span> _Darius asked if he understood the art_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 3.130">Hdt. 3.130</cite>.

**e.** A still freer use is seen in <span lang="grc">ἐθαύμαζεν αὐτὸν ὁ Λύ̄σανδρος ὡς καλὰ τὰ δένδρα εἴη</span> _Lysander marvelled at the beauty of his trees_ (for <span lang="grc">τὰ δένδρα αὐτοῦ ὡς κτλ</span>.) X. O. 4. &lt;\*&gt;.

## ASSIMILATION OF MOODS

<!-- page 489 -->

##### <a id="s2183"></a> §2183

The mood of a subordinate clause which is intimately connected with the thought of the clause on which it depends, is often assimilated to the mood of that clause. Such subordinate clauses may be simply dependent or sub-dependent ([2180](#s2180)).

**a.** This idiom is most marked in Unreal and Less Vivid Future conditions where the mood of the protasis is the same as that of the principal clause. It is also very common when a past indicative or an optative attracts the mood of a subordinate clause introduced by a relative word referring to indefinite persons or things or to an indefinite time or place. But subordinate clauses standing in a less close relation to the main clause, because they do not continue the same mental attitude but present a new shade of thought, retain their mood unassimilated; e.g. a relative clause, or a temporal clause expressing purpose, after an unreal condition may stand in the optative (<cite data-ref="Isaeus 4.11">Is. 4.11</cite>, <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 600e">P. R. 600e</cite>). On the other hand, there are many cases where the writer may, or may not, adopt modal assimilation without any great difference of meaning. The following sections give the chief occurrences of mood-assimilation apart from that found in Unreal and Less Vivid Future conditions ([2302](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2302), [2329](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2329)):

##### <a id="s2184"></a> §2184

An indicative referring simply to the present or past remains unassimilated.

<span lang="grc">ξυνενέγκοι μὲν ταῦτα ὡς βουλόμεθα</span> _may this result as we desire_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.20">T. 6.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">νῑκῴη δ᾽ ὅ τι πᾶσιν μέλλει συνοίσειν</span> _but may that prevail which is likely to be for the common weal_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.51">D. 4.51</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὰν διαπρά̄ξωμαι ἃ δέομαι, ἥξω</span> _when I shall have transacted what I want_, _I will return_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.29">X. A. 2.3.29</cite>.

##### <a id="s2185"></a> §2185

**Assimilation to the Indicative**.—The subordinate clause takes a past tense of the indicative in dependence on a past tense of the indicative (or its equivalent) denoting unreality.

**a.** Conditional relative clauses: <span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν γὰρ ἦν μοι χρήματα, ἐτῑμησάμην ἂν χρημάτων ὅσα ἔμελλον ἐκτείσειν</span> _for if I had money_, _I should have assessed my penalty at the full sum that I was likely to pay_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 38b">P. A. 38b</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ . . . κατεμαρτύρουν ἃ μὴ σαφῶς ᾔδη ἀκοῇ δὲ ἠπιστάμην, δεινὰ ἂν ἔφη πάσχειν ὑπ᾽ ἐμοῦ</span> _if I brought in as evidence against him matters which I did not know certainly but had learned by hearsay, he would have said that he was suffering a grave injustice at my hands_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.74">Ant. 5.74</cite>.

**b.** Temporal clauses: <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν ἐπαυόμην . . ., ἕως ἀπεπειρά̄θην τῆς σοφίᾱς ταυτησί̄</span> _I would not have ceased until I had made trial of this wisdom_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crat. 396c">P. Crat. 396c</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐχρῆν . . . μὴ πρότερον περὶ τῶν ὁμολογουμένων συμβουλεύειν, πρὶν πρὶν περὶ τῶν ἀμφισβητουμένων ἡμᾶς ἐδίδαξαν</span> _they ought not to have given advice concerning the matters of common agreement before they instructed us on the matters in dispute_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.19">I. 4.19</cite>.

**c.** Final clauses: here the principal clause is an unfulfilled wish, an unfulfilled apodosis, or a question with <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>; and the indicative in the final clause denotes that the purpose _was not_ or _cannot be_ attained, and cannot be reached by the will of the speaker. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ γὰρ ὤφελον οἷοί τε εἶναι οἱ πολλοὶ τὰ μέγιστα</span> <!-- page 490 --> <span lang="grc">κακὰ ἐργάζεσθαι, ἵνα οἷοί τε ἦσαν καὶ ἀγαθὰ τὰ μέγιστα</span> _would that the many were able to work the greatest evil in order that they might be able_ (as they are _not_) _to work also the greatest good_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 44d">P. Cr. 44d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐβουλόμην ἂν Σίμωνα τὴν αὐτὴν γνώμην ἐμοὶ ἔχειν ἵνα . . . ῥᾳδίως ἔγνωτε τὰ δίκαια</span> _I should have liked Simon to be of the same opinion as myself in order that you might easily have rendered a just verdict_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 3.21">L. 3.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔδει τὰ ἐνέχυρα τότε λαβεῖν, ὡς μηδ᾽ ει᾽ ἐβούλετο ἐδύνατο ἐξαπατᾶν</span> _I ought to have taken security at the time in order that he could not have deceived us even if he wished_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.6.23">X. A. 7.6.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί δῆτ᾽ οὐκ ἔρρῑψ᾽ ἐμαυτὸν τῆσδ᾽ ἀπὸ πέτρᾱς, ὅπως τῶν πάντων πόνων ἀπηλλάγην</span>; _why indeed did I not hurl myself from this rock, that I might have been freed from all these toils?_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 747">A. Pr. 747</cite>.

N. 1.—In this (post-Homeric) construction, <span lang="grc">ἵνα</span> is the regular conjunction in prose; <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> and <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> are rare. <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is very rarely added and is suspected (<cite data-ref="Isaeus 11.6">Is. 11.6</cite>, <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 959e">P. L. 959e</cite>).

N. 2.—Assimilation does not take place when the final clause is the essential thing and sets forth a real future purpose of the agent of the leading verb, or does not show whether or not the purpose was realized. This occurs especially after <span lang="grc">ἵνα</span> = _eo consilio ut_, rarely after <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> (<cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.6.16">X. A. 7.6.16</cite>); after <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> only in poetry and Xenophon. The subjunctive or optative is used when the purpose of the agent, and not the non-fulfilment of the action, is emphasized. Thus, <span lang="grc">καίτοι χρῆν σε . . . ἢ τοῦτον μὴ γράφειν ἢ ἐκεῖνον λύ̄ειν, οὐχ, ἵν᾽ ὃ βούλει σὺ γένηται, πάντα τὰ πρά̄γματα συνταράξαι</span> _you ought either not to have proposed this law or to have repealed the other; not to have thrown everything into confusion to accomplish your desire_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 24.44">D. 24.44</cite>.

**d.** Causal clauses (rarely, as <cite data-ref="Dem. 50.67">D. 50.67</cite>). Modal assimilation never takes place in indirect questions or in clauses dependent on a _verb of fearing_.

##### <a id="s2186"></a> §2186

**Assimilation to the Optative**.—When an optative of the principal clause refers to _future_ time (potential optative and optative of wish), the subordinate clause takes the optative by assimilation in the following cases.

**a.** Conditional relative clauses (regularly): <span lang="grc">πῶς γὰρ ἄν</span> ([1832](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1832)) <span lang="grc">τις, ἅ γε μὴ ἐπίσταιτο, ταῦτα σοφὸς εἴη</span>; _for how could any one be wise in that which he does not know?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.6.7">X. M. 4.6.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">τίς μῑσεῖν δύναιτ᾽ ἄν ὑφ᾽ οὗ εἰδείη καλός τε καὶ ἀγαθὸς νομιζόμενος</span>; _who could hate one by whom he knew that he was regarded as both beautiful and good?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 8">X. S. 8</cite>. 17, <span lang="grc">ἔρδοι τις ἣν ἕκαστος εἰδείη τέχνην</span> _would that every man would practise the craft that he understood_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Wasps 1431">Ar. Vesp. 1431</cite>, <span lang="grc">τίς ἂν . . . μόλοι</span> ([1832](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1832)), <span lang="grc">ὅστις διαγγείλειε τἀ̄μ᾽ εἴσω κακά</span> _would that some one would come to report within my tale of woe_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hel. 435">E. Hel. 435</cite>.

N. 1.—If the relative has a definite antecedent, assimilation does not take place; but not all relative clauses with an indefinite antecedent are assimilated. Cp. <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ ἂν ὑ̄μῶν ἕκαστος αἰσχυνθείη τὴν τάξιν λιπεῖν ἣν ἂν ταχθῇ ἐν τῷ πολέμῳ</span> _as each one of you would be ashamed to leave the post to which he may be appointed in war_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.7">Aes. 3.7</cite>.

N. 2.—A relative clause depending on an infinitive rarely takes the optative: <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ τοῦ μὲν αὐτὸν λέγειν ἃ μὴ σαφῶς εἰδείη εἵργεσθαι δεῖ</span> _one should abstain from saying oneself what one does not know for certain_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.6.19">X. C. 1.6.19</cite>. (See [2573](30-adjective-clauses-relative-clauses-2488-2573.md#s2573).)

**b.** Temporal clauses (regularly): <span lang="grc">τεθναίην, ὅτε μοι μηκέτι ταῦτα μέλοι</span> _may I_ <!-- page 491 --> _die when these things no longer delight me_ Mimnermus 1. 2, <span lang="grc">ὁ μὲν ἑκὼν πεινῶν φάγοι ἂν ὁπότε βούλοιτο</span> _he who starves of his own free will can eat whenever he wishes_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.1.18">X. M. 2.1.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ πάνυ σπουδάζοι φαγεῖν, εἴποιμ᾽ ἂν ὅτι παρὰ ταῖς γυναιξίν ἐστιν, ἕως παρατείναιμι τοῦτον κτλ</span>. _but if he was very desirous of eating_, _I would tell him that_ “_he was with the women_” _until I had tortured him_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.11">X. C. 1.3.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὄλοιο μήπω, πρὶν μάθοιμι</span> _perish not yet . . . until I learn_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 961">S. Ph. 961</cite>. But <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν ἀπέλθοιμι πρὶν ἂν παντάπᾱσιν ἡ ἀγορὰ̄ λυθῇ</span> _I shall not be leaving until the gathering in the market-place is quite dispersed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 12.1">X. O. 12.1</cite>.

**c.** Final and object clauses (rarely in prose, but occasionally after an optative of wish in poetry): <span lang="grc">πειρῴμην</span> (<span lang="grc">ἂν</span>) <span lang="grc">μὴ πρόσω ὑ̄μῶν εἶναι, ἵνα, εἴ που καιρὸς εἴη, ἐπιφανείην</span> _I will try to keep not far away from you, in order that, if there should be any occasion_, _I may show myself_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.4.17">X. C. 2.4.17</cite> (and five other cases in Xen.); <span lang="grc">ἔλθοι ὅπως γένοιτο τῶνδ᾽ ἐμοὶ λυτήριος</span> _may she come to prove my liberator from this affliction_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Eum. 297">A. Eum. 297</cite>. Ordinarily the subjunctive or future indicative is retained, as <span lang="grc">ὀκνοίην ἂν εἰς τὰ πλοῖα ἐμβαίνειν ἃ Κῦρος ἡμῖν δοίη μὴ ἡμᾶς . . . καταδύ̄σῃ</span> _I should hesitate to embark on the vessels which Cyrus might give us lest he sink us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.17">X. A. 1.3.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">τεθναίην, δίκην ἐπιθεὶς τῷ ἀδικοῦντι, ἵνα μὴ ἐνθάδε μένω καταγέλαστος</span> _let me die, when I have punished him who has done me wrong, that I may not remain here a laughing-stock_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 28d">P. A. 28d</cite>.

**d.** Indirect questions, when the direct question was a deliberative subjunctive: <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν ἔχοις ἐξελθὼν ὅ τι χρῷο σαυτῷ</span> _if you should escape, you would not know what to do with yourself_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 45b">P. Cr. 45b</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">τί χρῶμαι</span>;). But when a direct question or a direct quotation stood in the indicative, that mood is retained, as <span lang="grc">εἰ ἀποδειχθείη τίνας χρὴ ἡγεῖσθαι τοῦ πλαισίου</span> _if it should be settled who must lead the square_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.36">X. A. 3.2.36</cite>.

**e.** Very rarely in relative clauses of purpose (<cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 578e">P. R. 578e</cite> possibly); after <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> (<cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.5.30">X. C. 5.5.30</cite>), and in dependent statements with <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> or <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> (<cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.28">X. C. 3.1.28</cite>).

**f.** Assimilation and non-assimilation may occur in the same sentence (<cite data-ref="Eur. Ba. 1384">E. Bacch. 1384</cite> ff.)

##### <a id="s2187"></a> §2187

An optative referring to _general past_ time in a general supposition usually assimilates the mood of a conditional relative or temporal clause depending on that optative.

<span lang="grc">ἔχαιρεν ὁπότε τάχιστα τυχόντας ὧν δέοιντο ἀποπέμποι</span> _but he was wont to rejoice whenever he dismissed without delay his petitioners with their requests granted_ (lit. _obtaining what they wanted_) X. Ag. 9. 2. But the indicative may remain unassimilated, as <span lang="grc">ἐκάλει δὲ καὶ ἐτί̄μᾱ ὁπότε τινὰς ἴδοι τοιοῦτόν τι ποιήσαντας ὃ αὐτὸς ἐβούλετο ποιεῖν</span> _and he was wont to honour with an invitation any whom he saw practising anything that he himself wished them to do_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.1.30">X. C. 2.1.30</cite>.

So when the optative refers to past time through dependence on a verb of past time, as <span lang="grc">προσκαλῶν τοὺς φίλους ἐσπουδαιολογεῖτο ὡς δηλοίη οὓς τῑμᾷ</span> _summoning his friends he used to carry on a serious conversation with them in order to show whom he honoured_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.9.28">X. A. 1.9.28</cite> (here <span lang="grc">τῑμῴη</span> would be possible).

##### <a id="s2188"></a> §2188

**Assimilation to the Subjunctive**.—Conditional relative clauses and temporal clauses referring to _future_ or _general present_ time, if dependent on a subjunctive, take the subjunctive.

**a.** In reference to future time: <span lang="grc">τῶν πρᾱγμάτων τοὺς βουλευομένους</span> (<span lang="grc">ἡγεῖσθαι</span> <!-- page 492 --> <span lang="grc">δεῖ</span>), <span lang="grc">ἵν᾽ ἃ̄ν ἐκείνοις δοκῇ, ταῦτα πρά̄ττηται</span> _men of counsel must guide events in order that what they resolve shall be accomplished_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.39">D. 4.39</cite>.

**b.** In reference to general present time: <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽, ἐπειδὰν ὧν ἂν πρίηται κύ̄ριος γένηται, τῷ προδότῃ συμβούλῳ περὶ τῶν λοιπῶν ἔτι χρῆται</span> _nor when he has become master of what he purchases, does he any longer employ the traitor to advise him concerning his plans for the future_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.47">D. 18.47</cite>. But the indicative may occur (<cite data-ref="Dem. 22.22">D. 22.22</cite>).
