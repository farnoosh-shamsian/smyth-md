# ADJECTIVES

##### <a id="s1018"></a> §1018

Adjectives modify substantives (including words used substantively, [908](02-syntax-of-the-simple-sentence.md#s908)), and substantive pronouns. Adjectives are either _attributive_ ([912](02-syntax-of-the-simple-sentence.md#s912)) or _predicate_ ([910](02-syntax-of-the-simple-sentence.md#s910)).

##### <a id="s1019"></a> §1019

The equivalents of an adjective are: a participle (<span lang="grc">οἱ παρόντες πολῖται</span> _the citizens who are present_); a noun in apposition (<span lang="grc">Δημοσθένης ὁ ῥήτωρ</span> _Demosthenes the orator_, i.e. not <span lang="grc">Δημοσθένης ὁ στρατηγός, ὑ̄μεῖς οἱ Ἀθηναῖοι</span> _you Athenians_); an oblique case (<span lang="grc">στέφανος χρῡσοῦ</span> _a crown of gold_, <span lang="grc">τῆς αὐτῆς γνώμης ἐγώ</span> _I am likeminded_); an oblique case with a preposition (<span lang="grc">αἱ ἐν τῇ Ἀσίᾳ πόλεις</span> _the cities in Asia_); an adverb (<span lang="grc">οἱ πάλαι</span> _the ancients_). (Furthermore, a clause in a complex sentence: <span lang="grc">τὸ τείχισμα, ὃ ἦν αὐτόθι, αἱροῦσι</span> _they captured the fortress which was there;_ cp. [2542](30-adjective-clauses-relative-clauses-2488-2573.md#s2542).)

##### <a id="s1020"></a> §1020

**Concord**.—An adjective agrees with its substantive in gender, number, and case. This holds true also of the article, adjective pronouns, and participles: thus, A. _Attributive:_ <span lang="grc">ὁ δίκαιος ἀνήρ</span> _the just man_, <span lang="grc">τοῦ δικαίου ἀνδρός, τὼ δικαίω ἄνδρε, οἱ δίκαιοι ἄνδρες</span>, etc., <span lang="grc">οὗτος ὁ ἀνήρ</span> _this man_, <span lang="grc">τούτου τοῦ ἀνδρός</span>, etc., <span lang="grc">ἡ φιλοῦσα θυγάτηρ</span> _the loving daughter_. B. _Predicate:_ <span lang="grc">καλὸς ὁ ἀγών</span> _the prize is glorious_, <span lang="grc">ταῦτ᾽ ἐστὶν ἀληθῆ</span> _these things are true_, <span lang="grc">αἱ ἄρισται δοκοῦσαι εἶναι φύσεις</span> _the natures which seem to be best_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.1.3">X. M. 4.1.3</cite>.

On the agreement of demonstrative pronouns used adjectively with a predicate substantive, see [1239](17-pronouns.md#s1239). For relative pronouns, see [2501](30-adjective-clauses-relative-clauses-2488-2573.md#s2501).

## ATTRIBUTIVE ADJECTIVES

### ADJECTIVES USED SUBSTANTIVELY

##### <a id="s1021"></a> §1021

An attributive adjective (or participle) generally with the article, often dispenses with its substantive, and thus itself acquires the value of a substantive. <!-- page 273 -->

**a.** This occurs when the substantive may be supplied from the context; when it is a general notion; or when it is omitted in common expressions of a definite character, when the ellipsis is conscious.

##### <a id="s1022"></a> §1022

Masculine or feminine, when the substantive is a person: <span lang="grc">ὁ δίκαιος</span> _the just man_, <span lang="grc">δίκαιος</span> _a just man_, <span lang="grc">οἱ Ἀθηναῖοι</span> _the Athenians_, <span lang="grc">οἱ πολλοί</span> _the many_, _the rabble_, <span lang="grc">οἱ ὀλίγοι</span> _the oligarchical party_, <span lang="grc">οἱ βουλόμενοι</span> _all who will_, <span lang="grc">ἡ καλή</span> _the beautiful woman_, <span lang="grc">ἡ τεκοῦσα</span> _the mother_ (poet., <cite data-ref="Eur. Alc. 167">E. Alc. 167</cite>), <span lang="grc">ἐκκλησιάζουσαι</span> _women in assembly_.

##### <a id="s1023"></a> §1023

Neuter, when the substantive idea is _thing_ in general: <span lang="grc">τὸ ἀγαθόν</span> _the_ (highest) _good_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 506b">P. R. 506b</cite> (but <span lang="grc">τὰ ἀγαθά</span> _good things_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.33">L. 12.33</cite>), <span lang="grc">τὸ ἀληθές</span> _truth_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 473b">P. G. 473b</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ κοινόν</span> _the commonwealth_ Ant. 3. <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 2.3"><span lang="grc">β</span>. 3</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ ἐσόμενον</span> _the future_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.165">Aes. 3.165</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ λεγόμενον</span> _as the saying is_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.68">T. 7.68</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀμφὶ μέσον ἡμέρᾱς</span> _about mid-day_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.4.1">X. A. 4.4.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ πολύ</span> _over a wide space_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.18">T. 1.18</cite>.

##### <a id="s1024"></a> §1024

In words denoting a collection ([996](11-peculiarities-in-the-use-of-number.md#s996)) of persons or facts: <span lang="grc">τὸ ὑπήκοον</span> _the subjects_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.69">T. 6.69</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ βαρβαρικόν</span> _the barbarian force_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.1">X. A. 1.2.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ ξυμμαχικόν</span> _the allied forces_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.77">T. 4.77</cite> (and many words in <span lang="grc">-ικόν</span>), <span lang="grc">τὰ Ἑλληνικά</span> _Greek history_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.97">T. 1.97</cite>; and in words denoting _festivals_ (<span lang="grc">τὰ Ὀλύμπια</span> _the Olympian games_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 7.4.28">X. H. 7.4.28</cite>).

##### <a id="s1025"></a> §1025

With participles, especially in Thucydides: <span lang="grc">τὸ ὀργιζόμενον τῆς ὀργῆς</span> _their angry feelings_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.59">T. 2.59</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῆς πόλεως τὸ τῑμώμενον</span> _the dignity of the State_ 2. 63. The action of the verb is here represented as taking place under particular circumstances or at a particular time. These participles are not dead abstractions, but abstract qualities in action.

##### <a id="s1026"></a> §1026

A substantivized adjective may appear in the neuter plural as well as in the neuter singular: <span lang="grc">τὰ δεξιὰ τοῦ κέρᾱτος</span> _the right of the wing_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.4">X. A. 1.8.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῆς Σαλαμῖνος τὰ πολλά</span> _the greater part of Salamis_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.94">T. 2.94</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ πλεῖστον ἀνθρώπων</span> _to the greatest part of mankind_ 1. 1, <span lang="grc">ἐς τοῦτο δυστυχίᾱς</span> _to this degree of misfortune_ 7. 86 (cp. [1325](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1325)).

**a.** On the construction of <span lang="grc">τῆς γῆς ἡ πολλή</span> _the greater part of the land_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.56">T. 2.56</cite>, see [1313](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1313).

##### <a id="s1027"></a> §1027

In common expressions a definite noun is often implied (such as <span lang="grc">ἡμέρᾱ</span> _day_, <span lang="grc">ὁδός</span> _way_, <span lang="grc">χείρ</span> _hand_).

**a.** Masculine: <span lang="grc"> **κόλπος** </span> _gulf_, <span lang="grc">ὁ Ἰόνιος</span> _the Ionian_ gulf <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.34">T. 6.34</cite>, <span lang="grc"> **στρατός** </span> _force_, <span lang="grc">ὁ πεζός</span> _the land_ force 1. 47.

**b.** Feminine: <span lang="grc"> **γῆ** </span> _land_ (<span lang="grc"> **χώρᾱ** </span> _country_)—<span lang="grc">ἀπὸ τῆς ἑαυτῶν</span> _from their own_ country <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.15">T. 1.15</cite>; <span lang="grc">οὔθ᾽ ἡ Ἑλλὰς οὔθ᾽ ἡ βάρβαρος</span> _neither Greece nor barbaric_ land <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.27">D. 9.27</cite>; <span lang="grc"> **γνώμη** </span> _judgment:_ <span lang="grc">κατὰ τὴν ἐμήν</span> _according to my_ opinion <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Eccl. 153">Ar. Eccl. 153</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐκ τῆς νῑκώσης</span> _according to the prevailing_ opinion <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.1.18">X. A. 6.1.18</cite>; <span lang="grc"> **δίκη** </span> _suit:_ <span lang="grc">ἐρήμην κατηγοροῦντες</span> _bringing an accusation in a case where there is no defence_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 18c">P. A. 18c</cite>; <span lang="grc"> **ἡμέρᾱ** </span> _day:_ <span lang="grc">τὴν ὑστεραίᾱν</span> _the next day_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.2.11">X. C. 1.2.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῇ προτεραίᾳ</span> _the day before_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 19.22">L. 19.22</cite>; <span lang="grc"> **κέρας** </span> _wing:_ <span lang="grc">τὸ εὐώνυμον</span> _the left_ wing <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.96">T. 4.96</cite>; <span lang="grc"> **μερίς** </span> _part:_ <span lang="grc">εἰκοστή</span> _a twentieth_ 6. 54; <span lang="grc"> **μοῖρα** </span> _portion:_ <span lang="grc">ἡ πεπρωμένη</span> (<cite data-ref="Isoc. 10.61">I. 10.61</cite>) or <span lang="grc">ἡ εἱμαρμένη</span> (<cite data-ref="Dem. 18.205">D. 18.205</cite>) _the allotted portion, destiny;_ <span lang="grc"> **ναῦς** </span> _ship:_ <span lang="grc">ἡ τριήρης</span> the ship _with three banks of oars;_ <span lang="grc"> **ὁδός** </span> _way:_ <span lang="grc">εὐθείᾳ</span> _by the straight_ road <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 716a">P. L. 716a</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὴν ταχίστην</span> _by the_ <!-- page 274 --> _shortest_ way <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.14">X. A. 1.3.14</cite>; <span lang="grc"> **τέχνη** </span> _art:_ <span lang="grc">μουσική</span> the art of _music_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 668a">P. L. 668a</cite>; <span lang="grc"> **χείρ** </span> _hand:_ <span lang="grc">ἐν δεξιᾷ</span> _on the right_ hand <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.1">X. A. 1.5.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐξ ἀριστέρᾱς</span> _on the left_ 4. 8. 2; <span lang="grc"> **ψῆφος** </span> _vote:_ <span lang="grc">τὴν ἐναντίᾱν Νῑκίᾳ ἔθετο</span> _he voted in opposition to Nicias_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lach. 184d">P. Lach. 184d</cite>.

##### <a id="s1028"></a> §1028

The context often determines the substantive to be supplied: <span lang="grc">τοῦτον ἀνέκραγον ὡς ὀλίγᾱς</span> (<span lang="grc">πληγὰ̄ς</span>) <span lang="grc">παίσειεν</span> _they shouted that he had dealt him_ (too, [1063](#s1063)) _few_ blows <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.8.12">X. A. 5.8.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">τρία τάλαντα καὶ χῑλίᾱς</span> (<span lang="grc">δραχμά̄ς</span>) _three talents and a thousand_ drachmas <cite data-ref="Dem. 27.34">D. 27.34</cite>; cp. _a dollar and twenty_ (cents). Cp. [1572](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1572).

##### <a id="s1029"></a> §1029

From such substantivized adjectives arose many prepositional and adverbial expressions of whose source the Greeks themselves had probably lost sight. Many of these seem to be analogues of phrases once containing <span lang="grc">ὁδός</span>: <span lang="grc">τὴν ἄλλως ψηφίζεσθε</span> _you vote to no purpose_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.181">D. 19.181</cite> (i.e. _the way_ leading _elsewhere_ than the goal), <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ τῆς πρώτης</span> _at the very beginning_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.43">T. 7.43</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ τῆς ἴσης</span> _on an equality_ 1. 15, <span lang="grc">ἐξ ἐναντίᾱς</span> _from an opposite direction, facing_ 7. 44.

### AGREEMENT OF ATTRIBUTIVE ADJECTIVES

##### <a id="s1030"></a> §1030

An attributive adjective belonging to more than one substantive agrees with the nearest: <span lang="grc">τὸν καλὸν κἀ̄γαθὸν ἄνδρα καὶ γυναῖκα εὐδαίμονα εἶναί φημι</span> _the perfect man and woman are happy I maintain_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 470e">P. G. 470e</cite>. In some cases it is repeated with each substantive (often for emphasis): <span lang="grc">ἓν σῶμ᾽ ἔχων καὶ ψῡχὴν μίαν</span> _having one body and one soul_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.227">D. 19.227</cite>.

##### <a id="s1031"></a> §1031

But occasionally the adjective agrees with the more important substantive: <span lang="grc">ὁ σίγλος δύναται ἑπτὰ ὀβολοὺς καὶ ἡμιωβόλιον Ἀττικούς</span> _the siglus is worth seven and a half Attic obols_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.6">X. A. 1.5.6</cite>.

##### <a id="s1032"></a> §1032

Of two adjectives with one substantive, one may stand in closer relation to the substantive, while the other qualifies the expression thus formed: <span lang="grc">πόλις ἐρήμη μεγάλη</span> _a large deserted-city_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.4">X. A. 1.5.4</cite>.

##### <a id="s1033"></a> §1033

If one substantive has several attributive adjectives, these are sometimes added without a conjunction (by _Asyndeton_): <span lang="grc">κρέᾱ ἄρνεια, ἐρίφεια, χοίρεια</span> _flesh of lambs, kids_, _swine_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.5.31">X. A. 4.5.31</cite>. This is commoner in poetry, especially when the adjectives are descriptive: <span lang="grc">ἔγχος βρῑθὺ μέγα στιβαρόν</span> _a spear heavy, huge_, _stout_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 16.141"><span lang="grc">Π</span> 141</cite>.

##### <a id="s1034"></a> §1034

Two adjectives joined by <span lang="grc">καί</span> may form one combined notion in English, which omits the conjunction. So often with <span lang="grc">πολύς</span> to emphasize the idea of plurality: <span lang="grc">πολλὰ κἀ̄γαθά</span> _many blessings_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.6.4">X. A. 5.6.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">πολλὰ καὶ δεινά</span> _many dreadful sufferings_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 37.57">D. 37.57</cite>.

**a.** <span lang="grc">καλὸς κἀ̄γαθός</span> means _an aristocrat_ (in the political sense), or is used of _a perfect quality_ or _action_ (in the moral sense) as <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.40">T. 4.40</cite>, <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 21d">P. A. 21d</cite>. <!-- page 275 -->

##### <a id="s1035"></a> §1035

An attributive adjective is often used in poetry instead of the attributive genitive: <span lang="grc">βίη Ἡρᾱκληείη</span> B 658 _the might of Heracles_ (cp. “a Niobean daughter” Tennyson); rarely in prose: <span lang="grc">ποταμός, εὖρος πλεθριαῖος</span> _a river, a plethron in width_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.6.4">X. A. 4.6.4</cite>.

##### <a id="s1036"></a> §1036

An attributive adjective belonging logically to a dependent genitive is often used in poetry with a governing substantive: <span lang="grc">νεῖκος ἀνδρῶν ξύναιμον</span> _kindred strife of men_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 793">S. A. 793</cite> (for _strife of kindred men_). Rarely in prose in the case of the possessive pronoun: <span lang="grc">ἐν τῷ ὑ̄μετέρῳ ἀσθενεῖ τῆς γνώμης</span> _in the weakness of your purpose_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.61">T. 2.61</cite>.

##### <a id="s1037"></a> §1037

An attributive adjective may dispense with its substantive when that substantive is expressed in the context: <span lang="grc">μετέχει τῆς καλλίστης</span> (<span lang="grc">τέχνης</span>) <span lang="grc">τῶν τεχνῶν</span> _he shares in the fairest of the arts_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 448c">P. G. 448c</cite>.

##### <a id="s1038"></a> §1038

A substantivized participle may take the genitive rather than the case proper to the verb whence it is derived: <span lang="grc">βασιλέως προσήκοντες</span> _relations of the king_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.128">T. 1.128</cite>; contrast <span lang="grc">Περικλῆς ὁ ἐμοὶ προσήκων</span> _Pericles my relation_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.7.21">X. H. 1.7.21</cite>.

##### <a id="s1039"></a> §1039

Adjectives used substantively may take an attributive: <span lang="grc">οἱ ὑ̄μέτεροι δυσμενεῖς</span> _your enemies_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 5.2.33">X. H. 5.2.33</cite>.

## PREDICATE ADJECTIVES

##### <a id="s1040"></a> §1040

The predicate adjective is employed

**a.** With intransitive verbs signifying _to be, become_, and the like ([917](02-syntax-of-the-simple-sentence.md#s917)): <span lang="grc">ἡ δὲ χάρις ἄδηλος γεγένηται</span> _the favour has been concealed_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.233">Aes. 3.233</cite>. So with active verbs which take a preposition: <span lang="grc">νόμους ἔθεσθε ἐπ᾽ ἀδήλοις τοῖς ἀδικήσουσι</span> _you have enacted laws with regard to offenders who are unknown_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.30">D. 21.30</cite>.

**b.** With transitive verbs: (1) to qualify the object of the verb directly and immediately: <span lang="grc">τοὺς κακοὺς χρηστοὺς νομίζειν</span> _to judge bad men good_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 609">S. O. T. 609</cite>, (2) to express the result of the action (the proleptic use, [1579](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1579)). So with <span lang="grc">αὔξειν</span> _grow_, <span lang="grc">αἴρειν</span> _raise_ with <span lang="grc">μέγας</span> _great_, <span lang="grc">μετέωρος</span> _on high_, <span lang="grc">ὑψηλός</span> _high_, <span lang="grc">μακρός</span> _large_.

##### <a id="s1041"></a> §1041

With _verbs of saying_ and _thinking_ the predicate adjective is usually connected with its noun by <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span>, with _verbs of perceiving_, _showing_, by <span lang="grc">ὤν</span> ([2106](22-the-participle.md#s2106)): <span lang="grc">οὐδένα γὰρ οἶμαι δαιμόνων εἶναι κακόν</span> _for I think no one of the gods is base_ <cite data-ref="Eur. IT 391">E. I. T. 391</cite>, <span lang="grc">δηλοῖ ψευδῆ τὴν διαθήκην οὖσαν</span> _it shows that the will is false_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 45.34">D. 45.34</cite>. But <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span> is sometimes omitted ([945](09-the-predicate.md#s945)), as <span lang="grc">τὰ̄ς γὰρ καλὰ̄ς πρά̄ξεις ἁπά̄σᾱς ἀγαθὰ̄ς ὡμολογήσαμεν</span> _for we have agreed that all honourable actions are good_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 359e">P. Pr. 359e</cite>. On the omission of <span lang="grc">ὤν</span>, see [2117](22-the-participle.md#s2117). For <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span> with _verbs of naming_ and _calling_, see [1615](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1615).

##### <a id="s1042"></a> §1042

Several _adjectives of time_, _place_, _order of succession_, etc., are used as predicates where English employs an adverb or a preposition with its case: <span lang="grc">ἀφικνοῦνται τριταῖοι</span> _they arrive on the third day_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.3.2">X. A. 5.3.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">κατέβαινον σκοταῖοι</span> _they descended in the dark_ 4. 1. 10. In such cases the adjective is regarded as a _quality_ of the subject; whereas an adverb would regard the _manner_ of the action.

**a.** _Time_, _place:_ <span lang="grc">χρόνιος</span> _late_, <span lang="grc">ὄρθριος</span> _in the morning_, <span lang="grc">δευτεραῖος</span> _on the second day_, <span lang="grc">ποσταῖος</span> _how many days?_ <span lang="grc">ὑπαίθριος</span> _in the open air_. <!-- page 276 -->

**b.** _Order of succession:_ <span lang="grc">πρῶτος, πρότερος</span> _first_, <span lang="grc">ὕστερος</span> _later_, <span lang="grc">μέσος</span> _in the midst_, <span lang="grc">τελευταῖος</span> _last_, <span lang="grc">ὕστατος</span> _last_.

N.—When one action is opposed to another in order of sequence, the adverbs <span lang="grc">πρῶτον, πρότερον, ὕστατον</span>, etc., not the adjectives <span lang="grc">πρῶτος</span>, etc.. must be used: <span lang="grc">πρῶτον μὲν ἐδάκρῡε πολὺν χρόνον . . . εἶτα δὲ ἔλεξε τοιάδε</span> _first he wept for a long time, then he spoke as follows_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.2">X. A. 1.3.2</cite>. Hence distinguish

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="grc">πρῶτος τῇ πόλει προσέβαλε</span> | _he was the first to attack the city_. |
| <span lang="grc">πρώτῃ τῇ πόλει προσέβαλε</span> | _the city was the first place he attacked_. |
| <span lang="grc">πρῶτον τῇ πόλει προσέβαλε</span> | _his first act was to attack the city_. |

The same rule applies in the case of <span lang="grc">μόνος, μόνον</span>, as <span lang="grc">μόνην τὴν ἐπιστολη&lt;\*&gt; ἔγραψα</span> _this is the only letter I wrote_, <span lang="grc">μόνον ἔγραψα τὴν ἐπιστολήν</span> _I only wrote_ (but did not send) _the letter_. But this distinction is not always observed (<cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.69">Aes. 3.69</cite>).

##### <a id="s1043"></a> §1043

So also with _adjectives of degree_, _mental attitude, manner_, etc.: <span lang="grc">φέρονται οἱ λίθοι πολλοί</span> _the stones are thrown in great numbers_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.7.7">X. A. 4.7.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοὺς νεκροὺς ὑποσπόνδους ἀπέδοσαν</span> _they restored the dead under a truce_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.63">T. 1.63</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ θεοὶ εὐμενεῖς πέμπουσί σε</span> _the gods send you forth favourably_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.6.2">X. C. 1.6.2</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">μέγας</span> _high_, <span lang="grc">ἄσμενος</span> _gladly_, <span lang="grc">ἑκούσιος, ἑκών</span> _willingly_, <span lang="grc">ὅρκιος</span> _under oath_, <span lang="grc">αἰφνίδιος</span> _suddenly_. On <span lang="grc">ἄλλος</span>, see [1272](17-pronouns.md#s1272).

### AGREEMENT OF PREDICATE ADJECTIVES (AND PARTICIPLES)

#### WITH ONE SUBJECT

##### <a id="s1044"></a> §1044

A circumstantial participle ([2054](22-the-participle.md#s2054)) referring to a collective noun ([996](11-peculiarities-in-the-use-of-number.md#s996)) may be plural: <span lang="grc">τὸ στράτευμα ἐπορίζετο σῖτον κόπτοντες τοὺς βοῦς</span> _the army provided itself with provisions by killing the cattle_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.6">X. A. 2.1.6</cite>. So after <span lang="grc">οὐδείς</span>, as <span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς ἐκοιμήθη</span> ( = <span lang="grc">πάντες ἐν ἀγρυπνίᾳ ἦσαν</span>) <span lang="grc">τοὺς ἀπολωλότας πενθοῦντες</span> _no one slept because they were all bewailing the dead_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.2.3">X. H. 2.2.3</cite>. Cp. [950](10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s950).

##### <a id="s1045"></a> §1045

A plural participle may be used with a dual verb: <span lang="grc">ἐγελασάτην ἄμφω βλέψαντες εἰς ἀλλήλους</span> _both looked at each other and burst out laughing_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyd. 273d">P. Eu. 273d</cite>. A dual participle may be used with a plural verb: <span lang="grc">ποῦ ποτ᾽ ὄνθ᾽ ηὑρήμεθα</span>; _where in the world are we?_ <cite data-ref="Eur. IT 777">E. I. T. 777</cite>.

##### <a id="s1046"></a> §1046

A dual subject may be followed by a plural predicate adjective or participle: <span lang="grc">εἰ γάρ τις φαίη τὼ πόλει τούτω πλείστων ἀγαθῶν αἰτίᾱς γεγενῆσθαι</span> _if any one should assert that these two cities have been the cause of very many blessings_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 12.156">I. 12.156</cite>.

##### <a id="s1047"></a> §1047

A predicate adjective is neuter singular when the subject is an infinitive, a sentence, or a general thought: <span lang="grc">ἡδὺ πολλοὺς ἐχθροὺς ἔχειν</span> _; is it pleasant to have many enemies?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.221">D. 19.221</cite>, <span lang="grc">δῆλον δ᾽ ὅτι ταῦτ᾽ ἐστὶν ἀληθῆ</span> _it is clear that these things are true_ 2. 19.

##### <a id="s1048"></a> §1048

A predicate adjective referring to a masculine or feminine singular subject is often neuter singular and equivalent to a substantive. This occurs chiefly in statements of a general truth, where the subject refers to a whole class, not to an individual thing. Thus, <span lang="grc">καλὸν εἰρήνη</span> _peace is a fine thing_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.336">D. 19.336</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄπιστον ταῖς πολῑ-</span> <!-- page 277 --> <span lang="grc">τείαις ἡ τυραννίς</span> _despotism is an object of mistrust to free states_ 1. 5, <span lang="grc">μεῖζον πόλις ἑνὸς ἀνδρός</span> _the state is larger than the individual_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 368e">P. R. 368e</cite>. So also in the plural ([1056](#s1056)).

##### <a id="s1049"></a> §1049

So with names of places: <span lang="grc">ἔστι δὲ ἡ Χαιρώνεια ἔσχατον τὴς Βοιωτίᾱς</span> _Chaeronea is on the frontier of Boeotia_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.76">T. 4.76</cite>.

##### <a id="s1050"></a> §1050

A predicate superlative agrees in gender either with the subject or (usually) with a dependent genitive: <span lang="grc">νόσων χαλεπώτατος φθόνος</span> _envy_ is _the most fell of diseases_ Men. fr. 535, <span lang="grc">σύμβουλος ἀγαθὸς χρησιμώτατον ἁπάντων τῶν κτημάτων</span> _a good counsellor_ is _the most useful of all possessions_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 2.53">I. 2.53</cite>.

##### <a id="s1051"></a> §1051

For a predicate adjective used where English has an adverb, cp. [1042](#s1042).

##### <a id="s1052"></a> §1052

A predicate adjective is often used in the neuter plural (especially with verbal adjectives in <span lang="grc">-τός</span> and <span lang="grc">-τέος</span> in Thucydides and the poets): <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὴ ἑτοῖμα ἦν, ἀνήγετο</span> _when_ (all) _was ready, he put out to sea_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.56">T. 2.56</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀδύνατα ἦν τοὺς Λοκροὺς ἀμύ̄νεσθαι</span> _it was impossible to resist the Locrians_ 4. 1, <span lang="grc">ἐδόκει ἐπιχειρητέα εἶναι</span> _they decided to make the attempt_ 2. 3. Cp. [1003 a](11-peculiarities-in-the-use-of-number.md#s1003).

#### WITH TWO OR MORE SUBJECTS

##### <a id="s1053"></a> §1053

With two or more substantives a predicate adjective is plural, except when it agrees with the nearer subject: <span lang="grc">φόβος καὶ νόμος ἱκανὸς ἔρωτα κωλύ̄ειν</span> _fear and the law are capable of restraining love_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.1.10">X. C. 5.1.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">πολλῶν δὲ λόγων καὶ θορύβου γιγνομένου</span> _there arising much discussion and confusion_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 3.4">D. 3.4</cite>. See [968](10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s968).

##### <a id="s1054"></a> §1054

With substantives denoting persons of like gender, a predicate adjective is of the same gender: <span lang="grc">Ἀγάθων καὶ Σωκράτης λοιποί</span> _Agathon and Socrates_ are _left_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 193c">P. S. 193c</cite>.

##### <a id="s1055"></a> §1055

When the persons are of different gender, the masculine prevails: <span lang="grc">ὡς εἶδε πατέρα τε καὶ μητέρα καὶ ἀδελφοὺς καὶ τὴν ἑαυτοῦ γυναῖκα αἰχμαλώτους γεγενημένους, ἐδάκρῡσε</span> _when he saw that his father and mother and brothers and wife had been made prisoners of war, he burst into tears_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.7">X. C. 3.1.7</cite>.

**a.** But persons are sometimes regarded as things: <span lang="grc">ἔχω αὐτῶν καὶ τέκνα καὶ γυναῖκας φρουρούμενα</span> _I have their children and wives under guard_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.8">X. A. 1.4.8</cite>.

##### <a id="s1056"></a> §1056

With substantives denoting things of like gender a predicate adjective is of the same gender and plural. A neuter plural with the singular verb is often preferred: <span lang="grc">εὐγένειαί τε και` δυνάμεις καὶ τῑμαὶ δῆλά ἐστιν ἀγαθὰ ὄντα</span> _noble birth and power and honour are clearly good things_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyd. 279b">P. Eu. 279b</cite>.

##### <a id="s1057"></a> §1057

When the things are of different gender, a predicate adjective is neuter plural with singular verb: <span lang="grc">λίθοι τε καὶ πλίνθοι καὶ ξύλα καὶ κέραμος ἀτάκτως ἐρρῑμμένα οὐδὲν χρήσιμά ἐστιν</span> _stones and bricks and pieces of wood and tiles thrown together at random are useless_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.1.7">X. M. 3.1.7</cite>.

##### <a id="s1058"></a> §1058

When the substantives denote both persons and things, a predicate adjective is—**a.** plural, and follows the gender of the person, if the person is more important, or if the thing is treated as a person: <span lang="grc">γρᾴδια καὶ γερόντια καὶ</span> <!-- page 278 --> <span lang="grc">πρόβατα ὀλίγα καὶ βοῦς καταλελειμμένους</span> _old women and old men and a few sheep and oxen that had been left behind_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.3.22">X. A. 6.3.22</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ τύχη καὶ Φίλιππος ἦσαν τῶν ἔργων κύ̄ριοι</span> _Fortune and Philip were masters of the situation_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 2.118">Aes. 2.118</cite>,

**b.** or is neuter plural if the person is treated like a thing: <span lang="grc">ἡ καλλίστη πολῑτείᾱ τε καὶ ὁ κάλλιστος ἀνὴρ λοιπὰ ἂν ἡμῖν εἴη διελθεῖν</span> _we should still have to treat of the noblest polity and the noblest man_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 562a">P. R. 562a</cite>.

##### <a id="s1059"></a> §1059

The verbal and the adjective predicate may agree with the first of two subjects as the more important: <span lang="grc">Βρᾱσίδᾱς καὶ τὸ πλῆθος ἐπὶ τὰ μετέωρα τῆς πόλεως ἐτράπετο βουλόμενος κατ᾽ ἄκρᾱς ἑλεῖν αὐτήν</span> _Brasidas with the bulk of his troops turned to the upper part of the city wishing to capture it completely_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.112">T. 4.112</cite>.

For further uses of predicate adjectives, see [1150](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1150) ff., 1168 ff., 2647.

#### ATTRACTION OF PREDICATE NOUNS WITH THE INFINITIVE TO THE CASE OF THE OBJECT OF THE GOVERNING VERB

##### <a id="s1060"></a> §1060

When the subject of the infinitive is the same as a genitive or dative depending on the governing verb, it is often omitted.

##### <a id="s1061"></a> §1061

A predicate adjective referring to a _genitive_ regularly stands in the genitive, but a predicate substantive or participle generally stands in the accusative in agreement with the unexpressed subject of the infinitive: <span lang="grc">Κύ̄ρου ἐδέοντο ώς προθῡμοτάτου γενέσθαι</span> _they entreated Cyrus to show himself as zealous as possible_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.5.2">X. H. 1.5.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ τῶν δεομένων μου προστάτην γενέσθαι</span> _by those who begged me to become their chief_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.2.23">X. C. 7.2.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">δέομαι ὑ̄μῶν ἐθελῆσαί μου ἀκοῦσαι, ὑπολογιζομένους τὸ πλῆθος τῶν αἰτιῶν</span> _I beg of you that you be willing to listen to me_, _paying heed to the number of charges_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 2.1">Aes. 2.1</cite>.

##### <a id="s1062"></a> §1062

A predicate substantive, adjective, or participle referring to a _dative_ stands in the dative or in the accusative in agreement with the unexpressed subject of the infinitive: <span lang="grc">νῦν σοι ἔξεστιν ἀνδρὶ γενέσθαι</span> _now it is in your power to prove yourself a man_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.1.21">X. A. 7.1.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">Λακεδαιμονίοις ἔξεστιν ὑ̄μῖν φίλους γενέσθαι</span> _it is in your power to become friends to the Lacedaemonians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.29">T. 4.29</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔδοξεν αὐτοῖς . . . ἐξοπλισαμένοις προϊ̄έναι</span> _they decided to arm themselves fully and to advance_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.2">X. A. 2.1.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔδοξεν αὐτοῖς προφυλακὰ̄ς καταστήσαντας συγκαλεῖν τοὺς στρατιώτᾱς</span> _they decided to station pickets and to assemble the soldiers_ 3. 2. 1, <span lang="grc">συμφέρει αὐτοῖς φίλους εἶναι μᾶλλον ἢ πολεμίους</span> _it is for their interest to be friends rather than enemies_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 11.23">X. O. 11.23</cite>.

For predicate nouns in the nominative or accusative in agreement with omitted _subject_ of the infinitive, see [1973](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1973)-1975.

## **COMPARISON OF ADJECTIVES** (**AND ADVERBS**)

### POSITIVE

##### <a id="s1063"></a> §1063

The positive, used to imply that something is not suited or inadequate for the purpose in question, is especially common before an infinitive with or without <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> (<span lang="grc">ὡς</span>): (<span lang="grc">τὸ ὕδωρ</span>) <span lang="grc">ψῡχρόν</span> <!-- page 279 --> <span lang="grc">ἐστιν ὥστε λούσασθαι</span> _the water is_ too _cold for bathing_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.13.3">X. M. 3.13.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">νῆες ὀλίγαι ἀμύ̄νειν</span> _ships_ too _few to defend_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.50">T. 1.50</cite>, <span lang="grc">μακρὸν ἂν εἴη μοι λέγειν</span> _it would take_ too _long for me to state_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 2.15">And. 2.15</cite>.

##### <a id="s1064"></a> §1064

A positive adjective followed by the genitive of the same adjective has, in poetry, the force of a superlative: <span lang="grc">κακὰ κακῶν</span> _woe of woe_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 1238">S. O. C. 1238</cite>.

##### <a id="s1065"></a> §1065

<span lang="grc">μᾶλλον ἤ</span> _rather than, more . . . than_ may be used after a positive: <span lang="grc">προθύ̄μως μᾶλλον ἢ φίλως</span> _more prompt than kindly_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 1591">A. Ag. 1591</cite>.

### COMPARATIVE

##### <a id="s1066"></a> §1066

The comparative expresses contrast or comparison. Thus, <span lang="grc">δεξίτερος</span> is _right_ in contrast to its opposite, <span lang="grc">ἀριστερός</span> _left_. Cp. [1082 b](#s1082). Usually comparison is expressed, as <span lang="grc">εὖ τε καὶ χεῖρον</span> _well or ill_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.35">T. 2.35</cite>.

**a.** When the positive precedes, <span lang="grc">μα῀λλον</span> alone may stand for the comparative; as in <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνοί τε ἄξιοι ἐπαίνου καὶ ἔτι μᾶλλον</span> (i.e. <span lang="grc">ἀξιώτεροι</span>) <span lang="grc">οἱ πατέρες</span> _they are worthy of praise and still more worthy are our fathers_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.36">T. 2.36</cite>.

**b.** The persons or things with which comparison is made may include _all_ others of the same class: <span lang="grc">ἡμῶν ὁ γεραίτερος</span> _the elder_ (= eldest) _of us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.1.6">X. C. 5.1.6</cite>.

##### <a id="s1067"></a> §1067

The comparative is sometimes used merely as an intensive and does not differ essentially from the positive: <span lang="grc">τούτων καταδεέστερος</span> _at a disadvantage with_ (inferior to) _these men_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 27.2">D. 27.2</cite>.

##### <a id="s1068"></a> §1068

For the use of <span lang="grc">μᾶλλον</span> instead of the comparative, and <span lang="grc">μάλιστα</span> instead of the superlative, see [323](../part-2-inflection/05-comparison-of-adjectives.md#s323). When either form can be used, that with <span lang="grc">μᾶλλον</span> or <span lang="grc">μάλιστα</span> is more emphatic. Thucydides sometimes uses <span lang="grc">πλέον</span> (<span lang="grc">τι</span>), <span lang="grc">τὸ πλέον</span> instead of <span lang="grc">μᾶλλον</span>.

##### <a id="s1069"></a> §1069

The comparative degree may be followed by the genitive ([1431](18-the-cases/05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1431)) or by <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> _than:_ <span lang="grc">σοφώτερος ἐμοῦ</span> or <span lang="grc">σοφώτερος ἢ ἐγώ</span> _wiser than I_. The genitive may precede or follow the comparative. With <span lang="grc">ἤ</span>, the persons or things compared usually stand in the same case, and always so when they are connected by the same verb: <span lang="grc">φιλῶ γὰρ οὐ σὲ μᾶλλον ἢ δόμους ἐμούς</span> _for I do not love thee more than my own house_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 327">E. Med. 327</cite>.

**a.** The genitive is usual if two subjects would have the same verb in common; as <span lang="grc">οἱ Κρῆτες βραχύτερα τῶν Περσῶν ἐτόξευον</span> _the Cretans shot a shorter distance than the Persians_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἢ οἱ Πέρσαι</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.3.7">X. A. 3.3.7</cite>.

**b.** When two objects have the same verb in common: if the object stands (1) in the _accusative_, the genitive is preferred, as <span lang="grc">ἐμοὶ δοκεῖ Κῦρος, οὕστινας ἂν ὁρᾷ ἀγαθούς, φιλεῖν οὐδὲν ἧττον ἑαυτοῦ</span> _Cyrus seems to me to love all whom he finds excellent quite as much as he loves himself_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.3.12">X. C. 2.3.12</cite>, but the accusative is not uncommon, as <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 327">E. Med. 327</cite> quoted above; (2) in the _dative_, the genitive is frequent, as <span lang="grc">προσήκει μοι μᾶλλον ἑτέρων . . . ἄρχειν</span> _it behooves me rather than others to rule_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.16">T. 6.16</cite>; (3) in the _genitive_, the genitive is very rare (<cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.3.10">X. M. 4.3.10</cite>). Here <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> is preferred to the genitive for the sake of euphony: <span lang="grc">οἱ γὰρ πονηροὶ πολὺ πλειόνων εὐεργεσιῶν ἢ οἱ χρηστοὶ</span> (not <span lang="grc">τῶν χρηστῶν</span>) <span lang="grc">δέονται</span> _for the wicked need more favours than the good_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.6.27">X. M. 2.6.27</cite>. <!-- page 280 -->

**c.** The genitive is often used where <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> would be followed by some other case than nominative or accusative, or by a preposition: <span lang="grc">ταῦτα τοῖς ὁπλί̄ταις οὐχ ἧσσον τῶν ναυτῶν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἢ τοῖς ναύταις</span>) <span lang="grc">παρακελεύομαι</span> _I address these exhortations to the hoplites not less than to the sailors_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.63">T. 7.63</cite>, (<span lang="grc">δεῖ βλέπειν</span>) <span lang="grc">εἰς τὴν ἐμπειρίᾱν μᾶλλον τῆς ἀρετῆς</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἢ εἰς τὴν ἀρετήν</span>) _we must look at skill more than_ (at) _courage_ Aristotle, Politics 1309 b 5.

**d.** <span lang="grc">ἐλά̄ττων</span> (<span lang="grc">χείρων, ἐνδεέστερος, ὕστερος</span>, etc.) <span lang="grc">οὐδενός</span> _inferior to none, greater than all_; here <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> is not used). Thus, <span lang="grc">δουλεύειν δουλείᾱν οὐδεμιᾶς ἧττον αἰσχρά̄ν</span> _to endure a most disgraceful slavery_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.5.6">X. M. 1.5.6</cite>.

##### <a id="s1070"></a> §1070

The word following <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> may be the subject of a new verb (expressed or understood): <span lang="grc">ἡμεῖς ὑπὸ κρείττονος διδασκάλου πεπαιδεύμεθα ἢ οὗτοι</span> _we have been educated by a better teacher than they_ (have been) <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.3.13">X. C. 2.3.13</cite>; but this word is more often attracted into the case of the preceding word: <span lang="grc">τινὲς καὶ ἐκ δεινοτέρων ἢ τοιῶνδε</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἢ τοιάδε ἐστίν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐσώθησαν</span> _some have been rescued from dangers even greater than these_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.77">T. 7.77</cite>. The genitive is also common without <span lang="grc">ἤ</span>: <span lang="grc">λέγων ὅτι οὔπω . . . τούτου ἡδί̄ονι οἴνῳ ἐπιτύχοι</span> _saying that he had never met with sweeter wine than this_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.9.25">X. A. 1.9.25</cite>.

##### <a id="s1071"></a> §1071

<span lang="grc">ὡς</span> for <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> is rare, and suspected by some. But cp. <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 629">A. Pr. 629</cite>, <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 30b">P. A. 30b</cite>, 36 d, R. 526 c.

##### <a id="s1072"></a> §1072

<span lang="grc">μᾶλλον ἤ</span> may be used though a comparative precedes: <span lang="grc">αἱρετώτερόν ἐστι μαχομένους ἀποθνῄσκειν μᾶλλον ἤ φεύγοντας σῴζεσθαι</span> _it is more desirable for men to die fighting_ (rather) _than to save themselves by running away_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.51">X. C. 3.3.51</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">μᾶλλον ἤ</span> is to be taken with the verb.

##### <a id="s1073"></a> §1073

Instead of the genitive or <span lang="grc">ἤ</span>, the prepositions <span lang="grc">ἀντί, πρό</span> (w. gen.) or <span lang="grc">πρός, παρά</span> (w. accus.) are sometimes used with the comparative: <span lang="grc">κατεργάσασθαι αἱρετώτερον εἶναι τὸν καλὸν θάνατον ἀντὶ τοῦ αἰσχροῦ βίου</span> _to make a noble death more desirable than_ (instead of) _a shameful life_ X. R. <cite data-ref="Lys. 9.1">L. 9.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">μὴ παῖδας περὶ πλείονος ποιοῦ πρὸ τοῦ δικαίου</span> _do not consider children of more account than_ (before) _justice_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 54b">P. Cr. 54b</cite>, <span lang="grc">χειμὼν μείζων παρὰ τὴν καθεστηκυῖαν ὥρᾱν</span> _a cold too severe for_ (in comparison with) _the actual time of year_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.6">T. 4.6</cite>.

##### <a id="s1074"></a> §1074

In statements of number and measure <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> may be omitted after the adverbial comparatives <span lang="grc">πλέον</span> (<span lang="grc">πλεῖν</span>) _more_, <span lang="grc">ἔλᾱττον</span> (<span lang="grc">μεῖον</span>) _less_, which do not alter their case and number: <span lang="grc">πέμπει οὐκ ἔλᾱττον δέκα φέροντας πῦρ</span> _he sends not less_ than _ten men carrying fire_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.5.4">X. H. 4.5.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">πόλις πλέον πεντακισχῑλίων ἀνδρῶν</span> _a city of more_ than _5000 men_ 5. 3. 16. Even when <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> is kept, <span lang="grc">πλέον</span> (<span lang="grc">πλεῖν</span>), etc., remains unchanged: <span lang="grc">ἐν πλεῖν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">πλείοσιν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἢ διᾱκοσίοις ἔτεσιν</span> _in more than 200 years_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 24.141">D. 24.141</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοξότᾱς πλεῖν ἢ εἴκοσι μῡριάδας</span> _more bowmen than 20 myriads_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.1.6">X. C. 2.1.6</cite>.

**a.** In place of the adverbial <span lang="grc">πλέον</span>, etc., we find also the adjectival forms with or without <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> or with the genitive: <span lang="grc">τοξότᾱς πλείους ἢ τετρακισχῑλίους</span> _more bowmen than 4000_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.1.5">X. C. 2.1.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔτη γεγονὼς πλείω ἑβδομήκοντα</span> _more_ than _70 years old_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 17d">P. A. 17d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ι·ππέᾱς πλείους τριᾱκοσίων</span> _more than 300 horse_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.3.10">X. H. 1.3.10</cite>.

##### <a id="s1075"></a> §1075

The genitive sometimes occurs together with <span lang="grc">ἤ</span>, and either when the genitive has a separate construction, or is a pronoun to which the <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> clause stands as an appositive, or of which it is explanatory. Thus, <span lang="grc">προῄει πλέον . . . ἢ δέκα σταδίων</span> _he advanced more than ten stades_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.6.5">X. H. 4.6.5</cite> (here <span lang="grc">πλέον</span> is treated as a <!-- page 281 --> substantive), <span lang="grc">τίς γὰρ ἂν γένοιτο ταύτης μανίᾱ μείζων ἢ . . . ἡμᾶς κακῶς ποιεῖν</span>; _for what madness could be greater than_ (_this_) . . . _to use us ill?_ <cite data-ref="Isaeus 1.20">Is. 1.20</cite>. Cp. [1070](#s1070).

##### <a id="s1076"></a> §1076

**Compendious Comparison**.—The possessor, rather than the object possessed, may be put in the genitive after a comparative: <span lang="grc">εἰ δ᾽ ἡμεῖς ἱππικὸν κτησαίμεθα μὴ χεῖρον τούτων</span> ( = <span lang="grc">τοῦ τούτων ἱππικοῦ</span>) _but if we should raise a cavalry-force not inferior to theirs_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.3.7">X. C. 4.3.7</cite>.

##### <a id="s1077"></a> §1077

**Comparison with a Noun representing a clause**.—When one person or thing is to be compared, not with another person or thing in regard to its quality, but with an entire idea expressed by a clause (e.g. <span lang="grc">ἢ ὥστε</span> with the infinitive, <span lang="grc">ἢ ὡς</span> with the potential optative, or <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> and a finite verb), this clause may be abridged into a substantive or a participle. Thus, <span lang="grc">πρᾶγμα ἐλπίδος κρεῖσσον</span> _an event beyond our expectations_ (too great to be expected) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.64">T. 2.64</cite>, <span lang="grc">προσωτέρω τοῦ καιροῦ προϊόντες</span> _advancing further than the proper measure_ (i.e. _further than they should have gone_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.3.34">X. A. 4.3.34</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡς τῶν γε παρόντων οὐκ ἂν πρά̄ξαντες χεῖρον</span> _in the belief that they could not fare worse than at present_ (<span lang="grc">ἢ τὰ παρόντα ἐστίν</span>) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.67">T. 7.67</cite>.

##### <a id="s1078"></a> §1078

**Reflexive Comparison**.—The comparative followed by the reflexive pronoun in the genitive is used to denote that an object displays a quality in a higher degree than usual. The degree of increase is measured by comparison with the subject itself. <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> is often added to the subject: <span lang="grc">αὐτοὶ αὑτῶν εὐμαθέστεροι γίγνονται</span> _they learn more easily than before_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 15.267">I. 15.267</cite>, <span lang="grc">πλουσιώτεροι ἑαυτῶν γιγνόμενοι</span> _becoming richer than they were before_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.8">T. 1.8</cite>. Cp. [1093](#s1093).

##### <a id="s1079"></a> §1079

**Proportional Comparison**.—After a comparative, <span lang="grc">ἢ κατά</span> with the accusative ([1690](19-prepositions.md#s1690). 2 c), or <span lang="grc">ἢ ὥστε, ἢ ὡς</span>, rarely <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> alone, with the infinitive (not with the indicative), denote too high or too low a degree: <span lang="grc">ὅπλα ἔτι πλείω ἢ κατὰ τοὺς νεκροὺς ἐλήφθη</span> _more arms were taken than there were men slain_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.45">T. 7.45</cite>, <span lang="grc">φοβοῦμαι μή τι μεῖζον ἢ ὥστε φέρειν δύνασθαι κακὸν τῇ πόλει συμβῇ</span> _I fear lest there should befall the State an evil too great for it to be able to bear_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.5.17">X. M. 3.5.17</cite> ([2264](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/06-omegasigmatauepsilon-rarely-omegasigma-with-the-infinitive.md#s2264)).

##### <a id="s1080"></a> §1080

**Double Comparison**.—Two adjectives (or adverbs) referring to the same subject, when compared with each other, are both put in the comparative; <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> is always used: <span lang="grc">ἡ εἰρήνη ἀναγκαιοτέρᾱ ἢ καλλί̄ων</span> _a peace inevitable rather than honourable_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.69">Aes. 3.69</cite>, <span lang="grc">συντομώτερον ἢ σαφέστερον διαλεχθῆναι</span> _to discourse briefly rather than clearly_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 6.24">I. 6.24</cite>.

**a.** <span lang="grc">μᾶλλον</span> may be used with the first adjective in the positive (cp. [1065](#s1065)), and <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> before the second: <span lang="grc">πρόθῡμος μᾶλλον ἢ σοφωτέρᾱ</span> _with more affection than prudence_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 485">E. Med. 485</cite>.

##### <a id="s1081"></a> §1081

A comparative may follow a positive to mark the contrast with it: <span lang="grc">καὶ μῑκρὰ καὶ μείζω</span> _both small and great_(er) <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.14">D. 21.14</cite>.

##### <a id="s1082"></a> §1082

The comparative may stand alone, the second part being implied.

**a.** That which is exceeded is indicated by the sense only: <span lang="grc">οἱ σοφώτεροι</span> _the wiser_ (those wiser than the rest); <span lang="grc">ἐν εἰρήνῃ αἱ πόλεις ἀμείνους τὰ̄ς γνώμᾱς ἔχουσιν</span> _in_ <!-- page 282 --> _time of peace States are actuated by higher convictions_ (than in time of war) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.82">T. 3.82</cite>. So <span lang="grc">τι νεώτερον</span> _something new_ (more recent than that already known) <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 310a">P. Pr. 310a</cite> (often = _a calamity_ or _a revolutionary movement_); <span lang="grc">ὕστερον ἧκον</span> _they came too late_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.27">T. 7.27</cite>; and often where we supply _is usual_ (_right_, _fitting_, etc.).

**b.** The Hom. <span lang="grc">θηλύτεραι γυναῖκες</span> implies a comparison with men. In <span lang="grc">Κῦρος . . . ἐγεγόνει μητρὸς ἀμείνονος, πατρὸς δὲ ὑποδεεστέρου</span> _Cyrus was born of a mother of superior_, _but of a father of inferior race_ (<cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.91">Hdt. 1.91</cite>) the comparison is between the qualities of mother and father respectively. Cp. [313 b](../part-2-inflection/05-comparison-of-adjectives.md#s313).

**c.** The comparative denotes excess: <span lang="grc">μείζοσιν ἔργοις ἐπιχειροῦντες οὐ μῑκροῖς κακοῖς περιπί̄πτουσι</span> _by entering upon undertakings too great they encounter no slight troubles_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.2.35">X. M. 4.2.35</cite>.

**d.** The comparative is used to soften an expression (_rather_, _somewhat_): <span lang="grc">ἀγροικότερον</span> _somewhat boorishly_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 486c">P. G. 486c</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀμελέστερον ἐπορεύετο</span> _he proceeded rather carelessly_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.8.36">X. H. 4.8.36</cite>. Here the quality is compared with its absence or with its opposite.

##### <a id="s1083"></a> §1083

The comparative is often used where English requires the positive: <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ χεῖρον πολλάκις ἀκούειν</span> _for 'tis not a bad thing to hear often_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 105a">P. Ph. 105a</cite>.

##### <a id="s1084"></a> §1084

**Strengthened forms**.—The comparative may be strengthened by <span lang="grc">ἔτι, πολλῷ, μακρῷ</span> ([1513](18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1513)), <span lang="grc">πολύ</span> ([1609](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1609)), <span lang="grc">πολὺ ἔτι</span>, etc. <span lang="grc">μᾶλλον</span> is sometimes used with the comparative: <span lang="grc">αἰσχυντηροτέρω μᾶλλον τοῦ δέοντος</span> _more bashful than they ought to be_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 487b">P. G. 487b</cite>. So the correlative <span lang="grc">ὅσῳ, ὅσον</span>: <span lang="grc">ὅσῳ μείζους εἰσὶ τὰ̄ς ὄψεις, τοσούτῳ μᾶλλον ὀργῆς ἄξιοί εἰσι</span> _the braver they are to appearances, the more they deserve our anger_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 10.29">L. 10.29</cite>.

### SUPERLATIVE

##### <a id="s1085"></a> §1085

The superlative expresses either the highest degree of a quality (the _relative_ superlative: <span lang="grc">ὁ σοφώτατος ἀνήρ</span> _the wisest man_) or a very high degree of a quality (the _absolute_ superlative, which does not take the article: <span lang="grc">ἀνὴρ σοφώτατος</span> _a very wise man_). The relative superlative is followed by the genitive of the person or thing surpassed ([1315](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1315), [1434](18-the-cases/05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1434)). On the agreement, see [1050](#s1050).

**a.** The class to which an individual, marked by the superlative, belongs, may be designated by a genitive of the divided whole ([1315](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1315)): <span lang="grc">ὁ σοφώτατος τῶν Ἑλλήνων</span> _the wisest of the Greeks_. So often by <span lang="grc">πάντων</span>: <span lang="grc">πάντων ἀνθρώπων ἀγνωμονέστατοι</span> _the most senseless of all men_ Lyc. 54. On the superlative with <span lang="grc">ἄλλων</span>, see [1434](18-the-cases/05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1434).

**b.** With _two_ the comparative exhausts all the degrees of comparison: hence <span lang="grc">πρότερος</span> and <span lang="grc">πρῶτος, ὕστερος</span> and <span lang="grc">ὕστατος, ἑκάτερος</span> _each of two_, and <span lang="grc">ἕκαστος</span> _each of several_, are carefully to be distinguished.

##### <a id="s1086"></a> §1086

**Strengthened Forms**.—The superlative may be strengthened by prefixing <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> or <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>, rarely <span lang="grc">ᾗ</span> (also <span lang="grc">ὅσον</span> or <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> in poetry): <span lang="grc">ὅτι πλεῖστοι</span> _as many men as possible_, <span lang="grc">ὅτι τάχιστα</span> _as quickly as possible_, <span lang="grc">ᾗ ἄριστον</span> _the very best way_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.5.82">X. C. 7.5.82</cite> (<span lang="grc">ὅπως ἄριστα</span> <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 600">A. Ag. 600</cite>). <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> or <span lang="grc">ὥς</span> is always added when a preposition precedes the superlative: <span lang="grc">ὡς εἰς στενώτατον</span> _into as narrow compass as possible_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 18.8">X. O. 18.8</cite>. <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> and <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> may be used together: <span lang="grc">ὡς ὅτι βέλτιστον ἐμέ γενέσθαι</span> _for me to become as good as may be_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 218d">P. S. 218d</cite>. <!-- page 283 -->

**a.**With <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> and <span lang="grc">ᾖ</span>, rarely with <span lang="grc">ὅπῃ</span> (not with <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span>), a form of <span lang="grc">δύναμαι</span> or <span lang="grc">οἷός τέ εἰμι</span>, may be employed: <span lang="grc">διηγήσομαι ὑ̄μῖν ὡς ἂν δύνωμαι διὰ βραχυτάτων</span> _I will relate to you in the briefest terms I can_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 21.2">I. 21.2</cite>.

##### <a id="s1087"></a> §1087

<span lang="grc">ὁ̂ιος</span> may strengthen the superlative: <span lang="grc">ὁρῶντες τὰ πρά̄γματα οὐχ οἷα βέλτιστα ἐν τῇ πόλει ὄντα</span> _observing that affairs are not in the very best state in the city_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 13.23">L. 13.23</cite>. If <span lang="grc">ὄσος</span> or <span lang="grc">ὁπόσος</span> take the place of <span lang="grc">οἷος</span>, a form, or a synonym, of <span lang="grc">δύναμαι</span> is usually added: <span lang="grc">ἤγαγον συμμάχους ὁπόσους πλείστους ἐδυνάμην</span> _I brought the very largest number of allies I could_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.5.29">X. C. 4.5.29</cite>. <span lang="grc">ὁποῖος</span> is rare (Thuc., Plato).

##### <a id="s1088"></a> §1088

<span lang="grc">εἷς ἀνήρ</span> in apposition to the person designated may be added to strengthen the superlative: <span lang="grc">Ἀντιφῶν πλεῖστα εἷς ἀνὴρ δυνάμενος ὠφελεῖν</span> _Antiphon being able to render_ (most aid as one man) _aid beyond any other man_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.68">T. 8.68</cite>.

##### <a id="s1089"></a> §1089

<span lang="grc">ἐν τοῖς</span> is used before the superlative in all genders and numbers (esp. in Hdt., Thuc., Plato): <span lang="grc">ὠμὴ ἡ στάσις . . . ἔδοξε μᾶλλον, διότι ἐν τοῖς πρώτη ἐγένετο</span> _the revolution seemed the more cruel since it was the first_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.81">T. 3.81</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν τοῖς πλεῖσται δη᾽ νῆες ἅμ᾽ αὐτοῖς ἐγένοντο</span> _they had the very largest number of ships_ 3. 17.

##### <a id="s1090"></a> §1090

<span lang="grc">μάλιστα</span>, or <span lang="grc">πλεῖστον, μέγιστον</span>, occurs with the superlative: <span lang="grc">οἱ μάλιστα ἀνοητότατοι</span> _the very stupidest_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Tim. 92a">P. Tim. 92a</cite>. In poetry <span lang="grc">βαθυ-</span> has the effect of a superlative: <span lang="grc">βαθύπλουτος</span> _exceeding rich_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Supp. 555">A. Supp. 555</cite>.

##### <a id="s1091"></a> §1091

<span lang="grc">καί</span> _even_, <span lang="grc">πολλῷ, μακρῷ</span> ([1513](18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1513)), <span lang="grc">πολύ</span> ([1609](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1609)), <span lang="grc">παρὰ πολύ, πάντα</span> (<span lang="grc">τὰ πάντα</span>), the correlative <span lang="grc">ὅσῳ</span> also strengthen the superlative.

##### <a id="s1092"></a> §1092

In poetry (rarely in prose) a superlative may be strengthened by the addition of the genitive of the same adjective in the positive: <span lang="grc">ὦ κακῶν κάκιστε</span> _oh_, _vilest of the vile_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 334">S. O. T. 334</cite>.

##### <a id="s1093"></a> §1093

Reflexive comparison (cp. [1078](#s1078)) occurs with the superlative: <span lang="grc">ἀμβλύτατα αὐτὸς αὑτοῦ ὁρᾷ</span> _his sight is at its dullest_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 715d">P. L. 715d</cite>.
