# ADVERBS

##### <a id="s1094"></a> §1094

Adverbs are of two kinds

**a. Ordinary adverbs**, denoting manner, degree, time, place, etc. Ordinary adverbs qualify verbs, adjectives, other adverbs, and (rarely) substantives: <span lang="grc">ὄπισθεν γενόμενος</span> _getting behind_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.24">X. A. 1.8.24</cite>, <span lang="grc">εὐθὺς ἐβόᾱ</span> _straightway he shouted_ 1. 8. 1, <span lang="grc">φανερὸν ἤδη</span> _already clear_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 4.6">L. 4.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">πολὺ θᾶττον</span> _much more quickly_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.2">X. A. 1.5.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">εὖ μάλα</span> _very easily_ 6. 1. 1, <span lang="grc">εἰκότως τρόπον τινά</span> _in a way reasonably_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.41">D. 8.41</cite>, <span lang="grc">μάλα συμφορά̄</span> _a great misfortune_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.2.5">X. C. 4.2.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">μάλα στρατηγός</span> _an excellent general_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.2.39">X. H. 6.2.39</cite>.

**b. Sentence adverbs** (or _particles_) are adverbs that affect the sentence as a whole or give emphasis to particular words of any kind. Greek has many sentence adverbs, some of which are treated more fully under Particles.

Such are words of interrogation (<span lang="grc">ἦ, ἆρα, μῶν</span>); of affirmation and confidence (<span lang="grc">δή</span> _now_, _indeed_, <span lang="grc">δῆτα</span> _surely_, <span lang="grc">γέ</span> _at least, even_, <span lang="grc">ἦ</span> _really_, <span lang="grc">μήν</span> _in truth_, <span lang="grc">νή</span> _surely_, <!-- page 284 --> <span lang="grc">τοί</span> _surely_); of uncertainty (<span lang="grc">ἴσως, πού, τάχα</span> _perhaps_); of negation (<span lang="grc">οὐ, μή, οὔτοι, μήτοι</span>, etc.); of limitation (<span lang="grc">ἄν</span> 1761 ff.).

##### <a id="s1095"></a> §1095

The equivalents of an ordinary adverb are: an oblique case (<span lang="grc">ἐβασίλευεν εἴκοσιν ἔτη</span> _he reigned for twenty years_, 1581, 1582; <span lang="grc">ἀκούειν σπουδῇ</span> _to listen attentively_, <span lang="grc">τῇ ὑστεραίᾳ ἐπορεύοντο</span> _they proceeded on the next day_, and many other datives, 1527 b; <span lang="grc">ἧκε τὴν ταχίστην</span> _he came in the quickest way_, and many other accusatives, 1606-[1611](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1611)); an oblique case with a preposition (<span lang="grc">διὰ τάχους ἦλθε</span> _he came quickly_ = <span lang="grc">ταχέως, ἀπ᾽ οἴκου ὁρμῶμαι</span> _I start from home_ = <span lang="grc">οἴκοθεν, ἐν τῷ ἐμφανεῖ</span> _clearly_, <span lang="grc">ἐδίδου πρὸς τὴν ἀξίᾱν</span> _he gave according to merit_ = <span lang="grc">ἀξίως, πρὸς βίᾱν</span> _forcibly_ = <span lang="grc">βιαίως</span>); a participle (<span lang="grc">γελῶν εἶπε</span> _he said with a laugh, laughingly_). (Furthermore, a clause in a complex sentence, as <span lang="grc">εἰσπηδήσαντες . . . θᾶττον ἢ ὥς τις ἂν ᾤετο</span> _leaping in more quickly than one would have thought_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.8">X. A. 1.5.8</cite>; cp. [2189](28-classes-of-subordinate-clauses.md#s2189). 3.)

##### <a id="s1096"></a> §1096

In the attributive position an ordinary adverb may serve as an adjective: <span lang="grc">ἐν τῷ πλησίον παραδείσῳ</span> _in the neighbouring park_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.4.16">X. A. 2.4.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁ ἐκεῖθεν ἄγγελος</span> _the messenger from that quarter_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 619b">P. R. 619b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ταραχὴ ἡ τότε</span> _the confusion of that time_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 6.35">L. 6.35</cite>. See [1153 e](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1153). N.

##### <a id="s1097"></a> §1097

**a.** An ordinary adverb qualifying a verb is often so used that it may be referred to the subject or object of the sentence where an adjective could stand. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὥστε . . . ὑπολαμβάνεσθαι μειζόνως ἢ κατὰ τὴν ἀξίᾱν</span> _so as to be regarded as greater_ (lit. _in a greater way_) _than_ (_according to_) _their deserts_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 11.24">I. 11.24</cite>.

**b.** <span lang="grc">δίχα</span> and <span lang="grc">χωρίς</span> _apart_, <span lang="grc">ἑκάς</span> _far_, <span lang="grc">ἐγγύς</span> _near_ and some other ordinary adverbs supply, with <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span> or <span lang="grc">γίγνεσθαι</span>, the place of missing adjectives. Thus, <span lang="grc">χωρὶς σοφίᾱ ἐστὶν ἀνδρείᾱς</span> _wisdom is different from courage_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lach. 195a">P. Lach. 195a</cite>.
