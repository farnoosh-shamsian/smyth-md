# VERBAL ADJECTIVES IN -τέος

On verbal adjectives in <span lang="grc">-τός, -τή, -τόν</span>, see [425 c](../part-2-inflection/09-verbs/04-accent.md#s425), [472](../part-2-inflection/09-verbs/08-endings-of-the-verb-personal-endings.md#s472), 473.

##### <a id="s2149"></a> §2149

Verbal adjectives in <span lang="grc">-τέος</span> express necessity. They admit two constructions:

\1. The personal construction (<span lang="grc">-τέος, -τέᾱ, -τέον</span>), passive in meaning, and emphasizing the subject.

\2. The (more common) impersonal construction (<span lang="grc">-τέον, -τέᾰ</span>, [1052](14-adjectives.md#s1052)), practically active in meaning, and emphasizing the action.

Both constructions are used with the copula <span lang="grc">εἰμί</span>, which may be omitted. The agent—the person on whom the necessity rests— is expressed, if at all, by the dative (never by <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> and the genitive). <!-- page 480 -->

##### <a id="s2150"></a> §2150

Verbal adjectives from transitive verbs take the personal construction when the subject is emphasized; but the impersonal construction, when the emphasis falls on the verbal adjective itself. Verbal adjectives from intransitive verbs (that is, such as are followed by the genitive or dative) take only the impersonal construction.

**a.** Oblique cases of verbal adjectives are rare. Thus, <span lang="grc">περὶ τῶν ὑ̄μῖν πρᾱκτέων</span> _concerning what need be done by us_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 6.28">D. 6.28</cite>.

##### <a id="s2151"></a> §2151

**The Personal** (**Passive**) **Construction**.—The personal verbal in <span lang="grc">-τέος</span> is used only when the verb from which it is derived takes the accusative. The verbal agrees with the subject in gender, number, and case. The agent, if expressed, must always stand in the dative.

<span lang="grc">ποταμός τις ἡμῖν ἐστι διαβατέος</span> _a river must be crossed by us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.4.6">X. A. 2.4.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὠφελητέᾱ σοι ἡ πόλις ἐστί</span> _the State must be benefited by you_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.6.3">X. M. 3.6.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐμοὶ τοῦτο οὐ ποιητέον</span> _this must not be done by me_ (_I must not do this_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.15">X. A. 1.3.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ συμμαχεῖν ἐθέλοντες εὖ ποιητέοι</span> _those who would be allies must be well treated_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.6.27">X. M. 2.6.27</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ . . . τοσαῦτα ὄρη ὁρᾶτε ὑ̄μῖν ὄντα πορευτέα</span>; _do you not see such high mountains that must be traversed by you?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.18">X. A. 2.5.18</cite>.

##### <a id="s2152"></a> §2152

**The Impersonal** (**Active**) **Construction**.—The impersonal verbal stands in the neuter nominative, usually singular (<span lang="grc">-τέον</span>), rarely plural (<span lang="grc">-τέᾰ</span>). Its object stands in the case (genitive, dative, or accusative) required by the verb from which the verbal adjective is derived; verbs taking the genitive or dative have the impersonal construction only. The agent, if expressed, must always stand in the dative.

<span lang="grc">τῷ ἀδικοῦντι δοτέον δίκην</span> _the wrong-doer must suffer punishment_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyph. 8c">P. Euth. 8c</cite>, <span lang="grc">πιστὰ καὶ ὁμήρους δοτέον καὶ ληπτέον</span> _we must give and receive pledges and hostages_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 3.2.18">X. H. 3.2.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸν θάνατον ἡμῖν μετ᾽ εὐδοξίᾱς αἱρετέον ἐστίν</span> _we must prefer death with honour_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 6.91">I. 6.91</cite>, <span lang="grc">πειστέον πατρὸς λόγοις</span> _I must obey my father's commands_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hipp. 1182">E. Hipp. 1182</cite>, <span lang="grc">πειστέον τάδε</span> (<span lang="grc">σοί</span>) _thou must obey in this_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 994">S. Ph. 994</cite> (distinguish <span lang="grc">πειστέον ἐστί σε</span> _one must persuade thee_), <span lang="grc">φημὶ δὴ βοηθητέον εἶναι τοῖς πρά̄γμασιν ὑ̄μῖν</span> _I say that you must render assistance to the interests at stake_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 1.17">D. 1.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοὺς φίλους εὐεργετητέον, τὴν πόλιν ὠφελητέον . . ., τῶν βοσκημάτων ἐπιμελητέον</span> _you must do good to your friends, benefit your State_, _take care of your flocks_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.1.28">X. M. 2.1.28</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡμῖν ξύμμαχοι ἀγαθοί, οὓς οὐ παραδοτέα τοῖς Ἀθηναίοις ἐστίν</span> _we have serviceable allies, whom we must not abandon to the Athenians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.86">T. 1.86</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐψηφίσαντο . . . πολεμητέα εἶναι</span> _they voted that they must go to war_ 1. 88.

**a.** Since the impersonal construction is virtually active, and hence equivalent to <span lang="grc">δεῖ</span> with the accusative and infinitive (active or middle), the agent sometimes stands in the accusative, as if dependent on <span lang="grc">δεῖ</span>. The copula is (perhaps) always omitted when the agent is expressed by the accusative. Thus, <span lang="grc">τὸν βουλόμενον εὐδαίμονα εἶναι σωφροσύνην διωκτέον καὶ ἀσκητέον</span> (= <span lang="grc">δεῖ διώκειν καὶ ἀσκεῖν</span>) _it is necessary that the man who desires to be happy should pursue and practice temperance_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 507c">P. G. 507c</cite>.
