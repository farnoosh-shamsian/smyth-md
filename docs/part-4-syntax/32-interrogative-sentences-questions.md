# INTERROGATIVE SENTENCES (QUESTIONS)

<!-- page 596 -->

##### <a id="s2636"></a> §2636

Questions are either direct (independent) or indirect (dependent). Thus, <span lang="grc">τίς ἔλεξε ταῦτα</span>; _who said this?_ <span lang="grc">ἐρωτῶ ὅστις ἔλεξε ταῦτα</span> _I ask who said this_.

##### <a id="s2637"></a> §2637

Questions may have the assertive form with the interrogation expressed simply by the tone of the voice, or may be introduced by an interrogative pronoun, adjective, adverb, or particle.

**a.** A question gains in animation and has its emphatic part clearly marked if the interrogative word is placed late in the sentence. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἡδέα δὲ καλεῖς οὐ τὰ ἡδονῆς μετέχοντα</span>; _you call pleasant, do you not, that which participates in pleasure?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 351d">P. Pr. 351d</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ πεινῆν ἔλεγες πότερον ἡδὺ ἢ ἀνιᾱρὸν εἶναι</span>; _did you say that to be hungry was pleasant or painful?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 496c">P. G. 496c</cite>.

##### <a id="s2638"></a> §2638

**_Yes_ and _No_ Questions** (or sentence-questions) are asked by the verb (whether a given thing _is_ or _is done_). Such questions are commonly introduced by an interrogative particle. **Pronoun-questions** (or word-questions) are asked by an interrogative pronoun, adjective, or adverb (_who_, _what_, _where_, _when_, _how_).

**a.** A sentence-question may follow a word-question; as <span lang="grc">τί δοκοῦσιν ὑ̄μῖν, ὦ ἄνδρες; ἆρά γε ὁμοίως ὑ̄μῖν περὶ τῶν ἀδικούντων γιγνώσκειν κτλ</span>.; _what do you think of_ your ancestors, _gentlemen of the jury? Do they seem to entertain the same sentiments with yourselves about wrong-doers?_ Lyc. 119.

##### <a id="s2639"></a> §2639

**Deliberative Questions** ask what _is to be done_ or what _was to be done_. Questions asking what _is to be done_ in the present or future are expressed by the deliberative subjunctive (negative <span lang="grc">μή</span>, [1805](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1805)), by <span lang="grc">δεῖ</span> or <span lang="grc">χρή</span> and the infinitive, by the verbal in <span lang="grc">-τέον</span> with <span lang="grc">ἐστί</span> ([1808](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1808)) or by the deliberative future ([1916](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1916)). Questions asking what _was to be done_ are expressed by <span lang="grc">χρῆν</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐχρῆν</span>) or <span lang="grc">ἔδει</span> with the infinitive, or by the verbal in <span lang="grc">-τέον</span> with <span lang="grc">ἦν</span>. In _direct_ questions the optative is not used to denote what _was to be done_.

##### <a id="s2640"></a> §2640

**Rhetorical Questions** are questions asked for effect and not for information, since the speaker knows the answer in advance and either does not wait for, or himself gives, the answer. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἔστι ταῦτα· πόθεν</span>; _but this is not so. How can it be?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.47">D. 18.47</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί οὖν αἴτιον εἶναι ὑπολαμβάνω; ἐγὼ ὑ̄μῖν ἐρῶ</span> _what then do I regard as the explanation? I will tell you_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 40b">P. A. 40b</cite>. Such questions are often introduced by <span lang="grc">μή</span> ([2651 b](#s2651)). Other examples 2638 a, 2641. <!-- page 597 -->

**a.** Rhetorical questions awaken attention and express various shades of emotion; and are often used in passing to a new subject. Such questions are very rare in Lysias, somewhat frequent in Plato, common in Isaeus, highly developed in Demosthenes. The rhetorical question is much more favoured in Greek than in English.

## DIRECT (INDEPENDENT) QUESTIONS

##### <a id="s2641"></a> §2641

Any form of statement ([2153](24-summary-of-the-forms-of-simple-sentences.md#s2153)) may be used as a direct question. The interrogative meaning may be indicated only by the context, or it may be expressed by placing an emphatic word first or by the use of certain particles ([2650](#s2650), [2651](#s2651)).

<span lang="grc">ἐγὼ οὔ φημι</span>; _I say no?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 446e">P. G. 446e</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ ἀπεκρῑνάμην ὅτι εἴη ἡ καλλίστη</span>; _for did I not answer that it was the noblest_ art? 448 e, <span lang="grc">Ἕλληνες ὄντες βαρβάροις δουλεύσομεν</span>; _shall we, who are Greeks_, _be subject to barbarians?_ E. fr. 719, <span lang="grc">ἡγούμεθά τι τὸν θάνατον εἶναι</span>; _do we regard death as anything?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 64c">P. Ph. 64c</cite>. Cp. [1831](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1831), [1832](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1832).

##### <a id="s2642"></a> §2642

Questions which cannot be answered by _yes_ or _no_ are introduced by interrogative pronouns, adjectives or adverbs ([340](../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s340), [346](../part-2-inflection/07-adverbs.md#s346)), usually without any interrogative particle, and may have any form of the simple sentence.

<span lang="grc">τί οὖν κελεύω ποιῆσαι</span>; _what then do I urge_ you _to do?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.14">X. A. 1.4.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">πόσον . . . ἄπεστιν ἐνθένδε τὸ στράτευμα</span>; _how far distant from here is the army?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.3.10">X. C. 6.3.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">πῶς εἶπας</span>; _what_ (lit. _how_) _did you say?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 447d">P. G. 447d</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί ἂν αὐτῷ εἶπες</span>; _what would you have said to him?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 337c">P. R. 337c</cite>.

##### <a id="s2643"></a> §2643

An interrogative pronoun or adverb often depends on a participle and not on the main verb of the sentence. Thus, <span lang="grc">τί οὖν ποιήσαντος κατεχειροτονήσατε τοῦ Εὐάνδρου</span>; _for what act then did you condemn Evander?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.176">D. 21.176</cite>, (<span lang="grc">Ὀλύνθιοι</span>) <span lang="grc">οἳ τί πεποιηκότος αὐτοῖς Φιλίππου πῶς αὐτῷ χρῶνται</span>; _for whom what has Philip done and how do they treat him?_ 23. 107. Cp. [2147 d](22-the-participle.md#s2147). On <span lang="grc">τί παθών</span> see [2064 a](22-the-participle.md#s2064).

##### <a id="s2644"></a> §2644

A subordinate clause introduced by a conjunction or a relative pronoun may suddenly change into a direct pronoun-question, though the construction of the clause remains unaltered. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὴ περὶ τίνος Ἀθηναῖοι διανοοῦνται βουλεύεσθαι, ἀνίστασαι συμβουλεύων</span>; _when the Athenians are intending to deliberate_ (lit. _about what?_) _do you get up to give them advice?_ P. Alc. I. 106 c, <span lang="grc">πόθ᾽ ἃ χρὴ πρά̄ξετε; ἐπειδὰν τί γένηται</span>; _when will you do what you ought to do? in what event?_ (lit. _when what shall have happened?_) <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.10">D. 4.10</cite>.

**a.** Here belong the elliptical phrases <span lang="grc"> **ἵνα τί, ὡς τί** </span> (_scil_. <span lang="grc">γένηται</span>, [946](09-the-predicate.md#s946)), <span lang="grc"> **ὅτι τί** </span> (_scil_. <span lang="grc">γίγνεται</span>). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἵνα τί ταῦτα λέγεις</span>; _why_ (lit. _that what_ shall happen?) _do you say this?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 26d">P. A. 26d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔτι καὶ τοῦτ᾽ αὐτῷ προσθήσετε; ὅτι τί</span>; _will you give him this_ distinction _too in addition? for what reason?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.214">D. 23.214</cite>.

##### <a id="s2645"></a> §2645

Two questions may be condensed into one in an interrogative sentence by placing an interrogative between the article and its noun. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐγὼ οὖν τὸν ἐκ ποίᾱς πόλεως στρατηγὸν προσδοκῶ ταῦτα πρά̄ξειν</span>; _am I waiting for a general to do this? From what city?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.14">X. A. 3.1.14</cite>.

##### <a id="s2646"></a> §2646

Two or more interrogative pronouns, without a connective, may occur in the same sentence (question within a question). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ τούτων τίς τίνος αἴτιός ἐστι γενήσεται φανερόν</span> _from this it will become clear who is chargeable with_ <!-- page 598 --> _what_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.73">D. 18.73</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπειδάν τίς τινα φιλῇ, πότερος ποτέρου φίλος γίγνεται</span>; _whenever one person loves another, which one is the lover of which?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lysis 212a">P. Lys. 212a</cite>.

##### <a id="s2647"></a> §2647

With a substantive and the article or with a demonstrative pronoun an interrogative pronoun may be used as a predicate adjective. Here the interrogative sentence is equivalent to an interrogative clause with a dependent (relative) clause (cp. [1169](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1169)).

<span lang="grc">ποῖον τὸν μῦθον ἔειπες</span>; _what is the word that thou hast uttered?_ A 552 (lit. _the word_ being _what?_ In fuller form = <span lang="grc">ποῖός ἐστιν ὁ μῦθος δ̀ν ἔειπες</span>;), <span lang="grc">τίς ὁ πόθος αὐτοὺς ἵ̄κετο</span>; _what is this longing that has come upon them?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 601">S. Ph. 601</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὗτος δὲ τίς . . . κρατεῖ</span>; _who is this man who holds sway?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 68">S. O. C. 68</cite>, <span lang="grc">τίνας τούσδ᾽ εἰσορῶ</span>; _who are these I see?_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Orest. 1347">E. Or. 1347</cite>, <span lang="grc">διὰ σοφίᾱν τινὰ τοῦτο τὸ ὄνομα ἔσχηκα. ποίᾱν δὴ σοφίᾱν ταύτην</span>; _thanks to a kind of wisdom I obtained this name_. (_Thanks to_) _this wisdom being what?_ (that is, _what is this wisdom?_) <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 20d">P. A. 20d</cite>.

##### <a id="s2648"></a> §2648

<span lang="grc">τίς, τί, ποῖος</span> referring to something mentioned before may take the article; as A. <span lang="grc">πάσχει δὲ θαυμαστόν</span>. B. <span lang="grc">τὸ τί</span>; A. _A strange thing is happening to him_. B. (_The_) _what?_ Ar. Pax 696, A. <span lang="grc">νῦν δὴ ἐκεῖνα ἤδη . . . δυνάμεθα κρί̄νειν</span>. B. <span lang="grc">τὰ ποῖα</span>; A. _Now at last we are able to decide those matters_. B. (_The_) _what matters?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 277a">P. Phae. 277a</cite>.

##### <a id="s2649"></a> §2649

<span lang="grc">τίς, ποῖος</span> as adjective pronouns, and <span lang="grc">πῶς</span> etc., when followed by <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>, have the force of an affirmative assertion. Thus, <span lang="grc">ποίους λόγους οὐκ ἀνηλώσαμεν</span>; _what arguments did we not expend?_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 8.67">I. 8.67</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">πάντας</span>), <span lang="grc">τί κακὸν οὐχί; ῀ πᾶν κακόν</span> in 2147 d.

### QUESTIONS INTRODUCED BY INTERROGATIVE PARTICLES

##### <a id="s2650"></a> §2650

<span lang="grc">ἦ</span> and <span lang="grc">ἆρα</span> introduce questions asking merely for information and imply nothing as to the answer expected (neither _yes_ nor _no_).

<span lang="grc">ἦ τέθνηκεν Οἰδίπου πατήρ</span>; _is Oedipus' father_ really _dead?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 943">S. O. T. 943</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἦ λέγω</span> (delib. subj.); _shall I tell you?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.4.10">X. C. 8.4.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἦ καὶ οἴκοι τῶν πλουσίων ἦσθα</span>; _were you_ really _one of the rich men when you were at home?_ 8. 3. 36.

<span lang="grc">ἆρ᾽ εἰμὶ μάντις</span>; _am I a prophet?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 1212">S. Ant. 1212</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἆρ᾽ Ὀδυσσέως κλύω</span>; _can it be that I am listening to Odysseus?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 976">S. Ph. 976</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἆρα ἐθελήσειεν ἂν ἡμῖν διαλεχθῆναι</span>; _will he_ really _be willing to talk with us?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 447b">P. G. 447b</cite>.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ἆρα</span> is from <span lang="grc">ἦ ¨ ἄρα. ἦ</span> is chiefly poetic. Homer uses <span lang="grc">ἦ</span>, not <span lang="grc">ἆρα</span>. Both particles denote interest on the part of the questioner (often = _really? surely?_).

##### <a id="s2651"></a> §2651

<span lang="grc">οὐ, ἆρ᾽ ου᾽, οὐκοῦν</span> expect the answer _yes_ (_nonne_), <span lang="grc">μή, ἆρα μή, μῶν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">μὴ οὖν</span>) expect the answer _no_ (_num_).

**a.** <span lang="grc">οὐχ οὕτως ἔλεγες</span>; _did you not say so?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 334b">P. R. 334b</cite> (i.e. ‘I think you did, did you not?’), <span lang="grc">ἆρ᾽ οὐχ ὕβρις τάδε</span>; _is not this insolence?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 883">S. O. C. 883</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν . . . εὖ σοι δοκοῦσι βουλεύεσθαι</span>; _do they not then seem to you to plan well?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.1.8">X. C. 7.1.8</cite>. <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν οὐ</span> expects the answer _no_.

**b.** <span lang="grc">μή τι νεώτερον ἀγγέλλεις</span>; _no bad news_, _I hope?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 310b">P. Pr. 310b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἆρα μὴ αἰσχυνθῶμεν</span>; _surely we are not ashamed, are we?_ (or _can it be that we should be ashamed?_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 4.4">X. O. 4.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">μὴ ἀποκρί̄νωμαι</span>; _am I not to answer?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 337b">P. R. 337b</cite>, <span lang="grc">μῶν τί σε ἀδικεῖ</span>; _surely he has not wronged you, has he?_ (or _can it be that_, etc.) <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 310d">P. Pr. 310d</cite>. <span lang="grc">μῶν οὐ</span> expects the answer _yes_. <!-- page 599 -->

**c.** <span lang="grc">μῶν</span> is confined to Attic. Since the fact of its composition was lost, we find <span lang="grc">μῶν οὖν</span> (<cite data-ref="Aesch. Lib. 177">A. Ch. 177</cite>) and <span lang="grc">μῶν μή</span> (<cite data-ref="Plat. Lysis 208e">P. Lys. 208e</cite>).

**d.** <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> after <span lang="grc">μή</span> or <span lang="grc">ἆρα μή</span> belongs to a single word, not to the sentence (<cite data-ref="Plat. Meno 89c">P. Men. 89c</cite>, Lys. 213 d). On <span lang="grc">μή</span> or <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> with the subjunctive in half-questions, see [1801](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1801).

**e.** <span lang="grc">ἆρα</span> placed before <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> or <span lang="grc">μή</span> gives greater distinctness to the question. <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> questions ask concerning facts; <span lang="grc">μή</span> questions imply uncertainty or even apprehension, but sometimes are asked merely for effect.

**f.** <span lang="grc">οὔ που; οὔ τί που; οὐ δή; οὐ δή που</span> mean _surely it is not so?_ Here the negative belongs to the sentence.

##### <a id="s2652"></a> §2652

<span lang="grc"> **ἄλλο τι ἤ** </span>; _is it anything else than?_ and <span lang="grc"> **ἄλλο τι** </span>; _is it not?_ are used as direct interrogatives. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἄλλο τι ἢ οὐδὲν κωλύ̄ει παρεῖναι</span>; _there's nothing to hinder our passing, is there?_ (lit. _is there anything else_ the case _than_ this that _nothing prevents_, etc.) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.7.5">X. A. 4.7.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄλλο τι φιλεῖται ὑπὸ θεῶν</span>; _is it not loved by the gods?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyph. 10d">P. Euth. 10d</cite>. Cp. <span lang="grc">τί γὰρ ἄλλο ἢ κινδῡνεύσεις ἐπιδεῖξαι κτλ</span>.; _for what other risk will you run than that of showing_, etc.? ( = _for what else will_ you do _than that you will very likely show?_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.3.17">X. M. 2.3.17</cite>.

##### <a id="s2653"></a> §2653

<span lang="grc"> **εἶτα** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **ἔπειτα** </span> (more emphatic <span lang="grc">κᾆτα, κἄ̄πειτα</span>) introduce questions expressing surprise, indignation, irony, etc.; and often indicate a contrast between what a person has or has not done and what is or was to be expected of him. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἶτα πῶς οὐκ εὐθὺς ἐπήγειράς με</span>; _then why did you not rouse me at once?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 43b">P. Cr. 43b</cite>.

##### <a id="s2654"></a> §2654

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλά** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλ᾽ ἦ** </span>) introduces a question opposed to an expressed or implied thought of the speaker (especially an objection). Thus, <span lang="grc">ᾔτουν τί σε καὶ ἐπεί μοι οὐκ ἐδίδους ἔπαιον; ἀλλ᾽ ἀπῄτουν; ἀλλὰ περὶ παιδικῶν μαχόμενος; ἀλλὰ μεθύων ἐπαρῴνησα</span>; _did I ask anything of you and strike you when you would not give it to me? Or did I demand anything back? Or was I quarreling about an object of affection? Or was I the worse for liquor and did I treat you with drunken violence?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.8.4">X. A. 5.8.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἦ, τὸ λεγόμενον, κατόπιν ἑορτῆς ἥκομεν</span>; _but have we arrived_, _as the proverb says, late for a feast?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 447a">P. G. 447a</cite>. Cp. [2785](36-particles/02-alphalambdalambdaalpha.md#s2785).

##### <a id="s2655"></a> §2655

<span lang="grc"> **δέ** </span> sometimes introduces a suppressed thought, as an objection. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰπέ μοι, σὺ δὲ δὴ τί τὴν πόλιν ἡμῖν ἀγαθὸν πεποίηκας</span>; _tell me_, (_but_) _what good_, _pray_, _have you done the State?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.70">D. 8.70</cite>.

### DIRECT ALTERNATIVE QUESTIONS

##### <a id="s2656"></a> §2656

Direct alternative questions are usually introduced by <span lang="grc">πότερον</span> (<span lang="grc">πότερα</span>) . . . <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> _whether . . . or_ (Lat. _utrum . . . an_).

<span lang="grc">πότερον δέδρᾱκεν ἢ οὔ</span>; _has he done it or not?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.79">D. 23.79</cite>, <span lang="grc">πότερόν σέ τις, Αἰσχίνη, τῆς πόλεως ἐχθρὸν ἢ ἐμὸν εἶναι φῇ</span>; _shall I say_, _Aeschines_, _that you are the enemy of the State or mine?_ 18. 124 (<span lang="grc">τις φῇ ῀ φῶ</span>, 1805 c), <span lang="grc">πότερα δ᾽ ἡγεῖ . . . ἄμεινον εἶναι σὺν τῷ σῷ ἀγαθῷ τὰ̄ς τῑμωρίᾱς ποιεῖσθαι ἢ σὺν τῇ σῇ ζημίᾳ</span>; _do you think that it is better to inflict the_ proper _punishments in your own interest or to your own loss?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.15">X. C. 3.1.15</cite>.

##### <a id="s2657"></a> §2657

<span lang="grc">ἤ</span> often stands alone without <span lang="grc">πότερον</span> (as _an_ without _utrum_). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔλῡε τὴν εἰρήνην ἢ οὔ</span>; _did he break the peace or not?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.71">D. 18.71</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἢν χρήματα πολλὰ</span> <!-- page 600 --> <span lang="grc">ἔχῃ, ἐᾷς πλουτεῖν ἢ πένητα ποιεῖς</span>; _if he has great wealth, do you let him keep on being rich or do you make him poor?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.12">X. C. 3.1.12</cite>. So when the first question expresses uncertainty on the part of the questioner; as <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ τίς σοι διηγεῖτο; ἢ αὐτὸς Σωκράτης</span>; _but who told you the story?_ (was it some one else) _or was it Socrates himself?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 173a">P. S. 173a</cite>. Cp. [2860](36-particles/14-eta.md#s2860).

##### <a id="s2658"></a> §2658

An alternative question may follow upon a simple direct (or indirect) question. Thus, <span lang="grc">πόθεν πλεῖθ᾽ ὑγρὰ κέλευθα; ἤ τι κατὰ πρῆξιν ἤ μαψιδίως ἀλάλησθε</span>; _whence do ye sail over the watery ways? Or is it perchance on some enterprise or by way of rash adventure that ye rove?_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 9.252"><span lang="grc">ι</span> 252</cite>. Cp. E 85 (cited in [2660](#s2660)).

##### <a id="s2659"></a> §2659

<span lang="grc">πότερον</span> (<span lang="grc">πότερα</span>) may stand alone when the second member of the question is implicit in another sentence. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐννοήσατε δὲ κἀ̄κεῖνο, τίνα πρόφασιν ἔχοντες ἂν προσιοίμεθα κακί̄ονες ἢ πρόσθεν γενέσθαι. πότερον ὅτι ἄρχομεν; . . . ἀλλ᾽ ὅτι εὐδαιμονέστεροι δοκοῦμεν νῦν ἢ πρότερον εἶναι</span>; _and consider this too: what pretence should we have for allowing ourselves to become less deserving than heretofore? Is it because we are rulers? Or is it because we seem to be more prosperous than before?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.5.83">X. C. 7.5.83</cite>.

##### <a id="s2660"></a> §2660

<span lang="grc">πότερον</span> (<span lang="grc">πότερα</span>) was originally the neuter of <span lang="grc">πότερος</span> _which of the two?_ placed in front of a double question and later made a part of the first question. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐρωτῶ πότερον φιλεῖ ἢ μῑσεῖ σε</span> _I ask which of the two_ (is true): _does he love or does he hate you?_ Cp. <span lang="grc">Τῡδεΐδην δ᾽ οὐκ ἂν γνοίης ποτέροισι μετείη, ἠὲ μετὰ Τρώεσσιν ὁμῑλέοι ἦ μετ᾽ Ἀχαιοῖς</span> _you could not tell on which side Tydides was_, _whether he consorted with Trojans or with Achaeans_ E 85, <span lang="grc">τίνες κατῆρξαν, πότερον Ἕλληνες, μάχης, ἢ παῖς ἐμός</span>; _who began the battle—was it the Greeks or my son?_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Pers. 351">A. Pers. 351</cite>, cp. <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.2">X. C. 1.3.2</cite>.

##### <a id="s2661"></a> §2661

<span lang="grc"> **ἢ** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **ἠὲ** </span>) . . . <span lang="grc"> **ἦ** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **ἦε** </span>), or <span lang="grc">ἦ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἦε</span>) alone, occurs in Homer, who does not use <span lang="grc">πότερον</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἤ ῥά τι ἴδμεν ἐνὶ φρεσίν, ἦε καὶ οὐκί</span>; _do we know aught in heart_, _or do we not?_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 4.632"><span lang="grc">δ</span> 632</cite>, <span lang="grc">ψεύσομαι ἦ ἔτυμον ἐρέω</span>; _shall I speak falsehood or the truth?_ K 534.

**a.** All the ancient grammarians attest the accentuation of these particles as given above. Modern editors often adopt other accents. <span lang="grc">ἠέ</span> and <span lang="grc">ἦε</span> are derived from <span lang="grc">ἠ-ϝέ</span> and <span lang="grc">ἦ-ϝε</span> (whence <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> and <span lang="grc">ἦ</span>). With this enclitic <span lang="grc">ϝέ</span>, cp. Lat. <span lang="grc">-ϝε</span>.

### MOODS IN DIRECT QUESTIONS

##### <a id="s2662"></a> §2662

The moods used in direct questions are the same as those used in statements.

**a.** _Indicative_ (examples in [2642](#s2642)): sometimes in a past tense with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, as <span lang="grc">πῶς δὲ πάντες ἐζήλουν ἂν τοὺς τυράννους</span>; _but why should all men envy despotic rulers?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hiero 1.9">X. Hi. 1.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἴ τις ἕνα νόμον . . . ἐξαλείψειεν . . . , ἆρ᾽ οὐκ ἂν ἀπεκτείνατ᾽ αὐτόν</span>; _if any one should cancel a single law_ . . ., _would you not have put him to death?_ Lyc. 66. On <span lang="grc">τί οὐ</span> or <span lang="grc">τί οὖν οὐ</span> with the aorist, see [1936](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1936).

**b.** _Subjunctive:_ in deliberative questions ([2639](#s2639)). On the anticipatory subjunctive in <span lang="grc">τί πάθω</span>, see [1811](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1811).

**c.** _Optative_ (potential), as <span lang="grc">τίς φράσειεν ἄν</span>; _who can tell?_ <cite data-ref="Eur. IT 577">E. I. T. 577</cite>. Without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> this optative is rare, as <span lang="grc">τίς λέγοι</span>; _who can tell?_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Lib. 595">A. Ch. 595</cite>. Cp. [1821 a](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1821).
