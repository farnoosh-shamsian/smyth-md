# INDIRECT (DEPENDENT) QUESTIONS

<!-- page 601 -->

##### <a id="s2663"></a> §2663

Indirect questions are introduced by interrogative pronouns, adjectives, and adverbs, indefinite relative pronouns and adverbs ([340](../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s340), [346](../part-2-inflection/07-adverbs.md#s346)), or by certain interrogative particles ([2671](#s2671), [2675](#s2675)).

##### <a id="s2664"></a> §2664

The interrogatives of the direct question may be retained in an indirect question. But it is more common to use the indefinite relatives which (in interrogative sentences) are employed only in indirect questions.

<span lang="grc">ἠρώτων αὐτοὺς τίνες εἶεν</span> _they asked them who they were_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.5.10">X. A. 4.5.10</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">τίνες ἐστέ</span>;), <span lang="grc">ἠρώτων ὅ τι ἐστὶ τὸ πρᾶγμα</span> _I asked what the matter was_ 5. 7. 23 ( = <span lang="grc">τι´ ἐστι</span>;), <span lang="grc">ἠρώτᾱ αὐτόν πόσον χρῡσίον ἔχοι</span> _he asked him how much money he had_ 7. 8. 1 ( = <span lang="grc">πόσον ἔχεις</span>;), <span lang="grc">ἠρώτων αὐτὸν τὸ στράτευμα ὁπόσον εἴη</span> _they asked him how large the army was_ 4. 4. 17 ( = <span lang="grc">πόσον ἐστί</span>;), <span lang="grc">ἀπορῶν ποῖ τράποιτο ἐπὶ λόφον τινὰ καταφεύγει</span> _being in doubt whither he should turn, he fled to a hill_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.4">X. C. 3.1.4</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">ποῖ τράπωμαι</span>;), <span lang="grc">ᾔδει δὲ οὐδεὶς ὅποι στρατεύουσιν</span> _but no one knew where they were going to march_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.54">T. 5.54</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">ποῖ στρατεύομεν</span>;).

##### <a id="s2665"></a> §2665

The use of the direct interrogatives is a relic of original juxtaposition, e.g. <span lang="grc">εἰπέ μοι, ποῖόν τι νομίζεις εὐσέβειαν εἶναι</span>; _tell me, what sort of a thing do you think holiness is?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.6.2">X. M. 4.6.2</cite>. The interrogative force of the indefinite relatives is derived, not from any interrogative idea in these words, but from the connection in which they stand.

##### <a id="s2666"></a> §2666

An indirect interrogative is often used in the same sentence after a direct interrogative. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐκ οἶδα οὔτ᾽ ἀπὸ ποίου ἂν τάχους οὔτε ὅποι ἄν τις φεύγων ἀποφύγοι οὔτ᾽ εἰς ποῖον ἂν σκότος ἀποδραίη οὔθ᾽ ὅπως ἂν εἰς ἐχυρὸν χωρίον ἀποσταίη</span> _I do not know with what swiftness of foot nor by fleeing to what quarter a man might escape nor into what darkness he might run away nor how he could withdraw into any stronghold_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.7">X. A. 2.5.7</cite>. The direct form precedes less often, as <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ αἰσθάνομαί σου ὁποῖον νόμιμον ἢ ποῖον δίκαιον λέγεις</span> _for I do not perceive what you mean by_ ‘_conformable to law_’ _or what you mean by_ ‘_just_’ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.4.13">X. M. 4.4.13</cite>.

##### <a id="s2667"></a> §2667

Two interrogatives may occur in the same sentence without a connective; as <span lang="grc">πῶς οἶδεν ὁποῖα ὀποίοις δυνατὰ κοινωνεῖν</span>; _how does he know what_ letters _are able to unite with what?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Soph. 253a">P. Soph. 253a</cite>. Cp. [2646](32-interrogative-sentences-questions.md#s2646).

##### <a id="s2668"></a> §2668

After _verbs of saying_, _knowing_, _seeing_, _making known, perceiving_, etc. (but not after verbs of _asking_) the simple relatives are found where the indefinite relatives (or the interrogatives) might stand in an indirect question. Where <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> is so used, it has the force of <span lang="grc">οἷος</span> (cp. _qualis_ in such questions); and rarely follows a negative clause, because verbs denoting lack of knowledge are allied in meaning to _verbs of asking_. The usual forms are e.g. <span lang="grc">οἶδά σε ὃς εἶ</span> and <span lang="grc">οὐκ οἶδά σε ὅστις εἶ</span>. But we find <span lang="grc">οἶδά σε ὅστις εἶ</span> and <span lang="grc">οὐκ οἶδά σε ὃς εἶ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">πέμπει . . . εἰπὼν ὃς ἦν</span> _he sends . . . telling who he was_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.1.46">X. C. 6.1.46</cite> (here <span lang="grc">ἦν</span> represents the point of view of the _writer_), <span lang="grc">ἐκέλευσε . . . δεῖξαι ὃς εἴη</span> _he ordered him to explain who he was_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 52.7">D. 52.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">μήποτε γνοίης ὃς εἶ</span> _mayest thou never come to know who thou_ <!-- page 602 --> _art_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 1068">S. O. T. 1068</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁρᾷς ἡμᾶς, ὅσοι ἐσμέν</span>; _do you see how many there are of us?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 327c">P. R. 327c</cite>. So with the adverbs <span lang="grc">ἔνθα, οὗ, ᾗ, ὡς, ὅθεν</span>; as <span lang="grc">τὴν ὁδὸν ὁδὸν ἔφραζεν ᾗ εἴη</span> _he told where the road was_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.5.34">X. A. 4.5.34</cite>. In some cases these sentences may be exclamatory ([2685](34-exclamatory-sentences.md#s2685)).

**a.** That the simple relatives are never thus used after _verbs of asking_ indicates that such clauses are not true indirect questions (as in Latin), and that the pronouns have their value as _relatives_. But some scholars allow an indirect question after all these words except <span lang="grc">ὅς</span>; and others admit no such limitation.

**b.** Only in late Greek are the pronouns or adverbs of the indirect form used in direct questions.

**c.** <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> is used occasionally (often in poetry) in the sense of <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">μή μοι φράζ᾽ ὅπως οὐκ εἶ κακός</span> _tell me not that_ (lit. _how_) _thou art not vile_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 548">S. O. T. 548</cite>.

**d.** The context must sometimes determine whether a sentence is an indirect question or a relative clause. Thus, without the context, <span lang="grc">οὐκ εἶχον ὅποι ἀποσταῖεν</span> (<cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 3.5.10">X. H. 3.5.10</cite>) might mean _they did not know to whom_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἠγνόουν πρὸς τίνας</span>) _to revolt_ or _they had no_ allies _to whom_ ( = <span lang="grc">πρὸς οὕς</span>) _to revolt_. But the present or aorist optative in relative final clauses is rare; cp. [2554 c](30-adjective-clauses-relative-clauses-2488-2573.md#s2554).

##### <a id="s2669"></a> §2669

An indirect question may depend (especially in poetry) on an ide&lt;\*&gt; involved in the principal verb; or may depend on a verb to be supplied. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὥστε μ᾽ ὠδί̄νειν τί φῄς</span> _so that I am in travail to know thy meaning_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 794">S. Aj. 794</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁποτέρως οὖν σοι . . . ἀρέσκει</span> _in whatever way it pleases you_ (_scil._ <span lang="grc">ἡδέως ἂν ἀκούσαιμι</span>) <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 348b">P. R. 348b</cite>.

##### <a id="s2670"></a> §2670

The indefinite relative is commonly used when a question is repeated by the respondent before his reply. Here _you ask?_ is supplied in thought. Thus, A. <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ τίς γὰρ εἷ</span>; B. <span lang="grc">ὅστις; πολί̄της χρηστός</span> A. _But who are you, pray?_ B. _Who am I? an honest burgher_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Ach. 594">Ar. Ach. 594</cite>, <span lang="grc">πῶς δή; φήσω ἐγώ. ὅπως; φήσει</span> _how are you? I will say; How am I? he will say_ Hippocrates 1. 292 c.

##### <a id="s2671"></a> §2671

Simple indirect questions are introduced by <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> _whether_, less often by <span lang="grc">ἆρα</span>.

<span lang="grc">ἐρωτῶντες εἰ λῃσταί εἰσιν</span> _asking whether they are pirates_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.5">T. 1.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦτον οἶσθ᾽ ει᾽ ζῶν κυρεῖ</span>; _dost thou know whether he is alive?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 444">S. Ph. 444</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἤρετο αὐτὸν εἰ βληθείη</span> _he asked him whether he had been struck_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.3.30">X. C. 8.3.30</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">ἐβλήθης</span>;), <span lang="grc">φόβος εἰ πείσω δέσποιναν ἐμήν</span> _I am afraid_ (about the question) _whether I can persuade my mistress_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 184">E. Med. 184</cite> ([2234](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/03-object-clauses.md#s2234)), <span lang="grc">ἴδωμεν ἆρ᾽ οὑτωσὶ̄ γίγνεται πάντα</span> _let us see whether everything is thus produced_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 70d">P. Ph. 70d</cite>. With the deliberative subjunctive: <span lang="grc">ἐπανερομένου Κτησιφῶντος εἰ καλέσῃ Δημοσθένην</span> _when Ctesiphon was asking if he was to call Demosthenes_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.202">Aes. 3.202</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">καλέσω</span>;).

**a.** <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> has an affirmative force (_whether_) or a negative force (_whether . . . not_). The latter is seen e.g. after verbs expressing uncertainty or doubt, as after <span lang="grc">οὐκ οἶδα</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν δὴ δίκαια ποιήσω, οὐκ οἶδα</span> _I don't know whether I shall do what is right_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.5">X. A. 1.3.5</cite> (i.e. I may possibly not do what is right). The assumption is affirmative in <span lang="grc">τὰ ἐκπώματα . . . οὐκ οἶδ᾽ ει᾽ Χρῡσάντᾳ τουτῳῒ δῶ</span> _I don't know whether I must not give the cups to Chrysantas here_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.4.16">X. C. 8.4.16</cite> (i.e. I think I shall give them).

**b.** The interrogative use of <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> is derived from the conditional meaning _if_, as <!-- page 603 --> in <span lang="grc">σὺ δὲ φράσαι εἴ με σαώσεις</span> _but do thou tell me whether thou wilt save me_ A 83 (i.e. ‘if thou wilt save me, tell me so’).

##### <a id="s2672"></a> §2672

<span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> rarely, if ever, means _whether_, even after _verbs of examining_, _considering_ (<span lang="grc">σκοπῶ ἐσκεψάμην, καθορῶ</span>), where its use is best explained by 2354. In form such conditional sentences often approach closely to indirect questions. Thus, cp. <span lang="grc">σκέψαι . . . ἐὰ̄ν ἄρα καὶ σοὶ συνδοκῇ ἅπερ ἐμοί</span> _consider if_ (in case that, on the chance that) _you too agree with me_ (<cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 64c">P. Ph. 64c</cite>) with <span lang="grc">σκέψασθε εἰ ἄρα τοῦτο . . . πεποιήκᾱσιν οἱ βάρβαροι</span> _consider whether the barbarians have_ (not) _done this_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.22">X. A. 3.2.22</cite>. Cp. <span lang="grc">ἀναμιμνήσκεσθαι ἐὰ̄ν ἀληθῆ λέγω</span> _to recall to your recollection if I speak the truth_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.37">And. 1.37</cite>.

**a.** Some scholars maintain that, in Greek, _if_ was at an early period confused with _whether_ in such sentences as <span lang="grc">εἶμι γὰρ ἐς Σπάρτην . . . νόστον πευσόμενος πατρὸς φίλου, ἤν που ἀκούσω</span> strictly _for I will go to Sparta to inquire about the return of my dear father, in the hope that I may hear of it_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 2.359"><span lang="grc">β</span> 359</cite>. When the conditional clause was attached to <span lang="grc">πευσόμενος, ἤν</span> acquired (it is claimed) the force of _whether_. Cp. <span lang="grc">ᾤχετο πευσόμενος . . . εἴ</span> (_v.l._ <span lang="grc">ἤ</span>) <span lang="grc">που ἔτ᾽ εἴης</span> _he had gone to inquire whether you were still living_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 13.415"><span lang="grc">ν</span> 415</cite>. Cp. German _ob_, once meaning _if_, now _whether_.

##### <a id="s2673"></a> §2673

Homer has <span lang="grc">ἤν, εἴ κε, αἴ κε</span> with the subjunctive after _verbs of knowing_, _seeing_, _saying_ (but not after verbs of _asking_). Such cases belong under 2354 b.

##### <a id="s2674"></a> §2674

<span lang="grc">μή</span> is sometimes translated by _whether_ after _verbs of fear_ and _caution;_ but such dependent clauses with <span lang="grc">μή</span> are not indirect questions ([2221 a](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/03-object-clauses.md#s2221)). After _verbs of seeing_, _considering_ and the like (<span lang="grc">ὁρῶ, ἐννοοῦμαι, ἐνθῡμοῦμαι, σκοπῶ</span>) <span lang="grc">μή</span> is properly a conjunction and not the interrogative particle. In such clauses there is an idea of purpose or desire to _prevent_ something or a notion of fear that something _is_ or _may be done_. Thus, <span lang="grc">φροντίζω μὴ κράτιστον ᾖ μοι σῑγᾶν</span> _I am considering whether it is not best for me to be silent_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.2.39">X. M. 4.2.39</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁρῶμεν μὴ Νῑκίᾱς οἴεταί τι λέγειν</span> _let us see whether Nicias is of the opinion that he is saying something important_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lach. 196c">P. Lach. 196c</cite>. That <span lang="grc">μή</span> does not properly mean _whether not_ (indirect question) is clear from the fact that, in these clauses, it is not used of something that is _hoped for_. Cp. [2676 b](#s2676).

## INDIRECT ALTERNATIVE QUESTIONS

##### <a id="s2675"></a> §2675

Indirect alternative questions are introduced by the particles signifying _whether . . . or:_ <span lang="grc">πότερον</span> (<span lang="grc">πότερα</span>) . . . <span lang="grc">ἤ, εἴτε . . . εἴτε, εἰ . . . ἤ, εἰ . . . εἴτε</span>. See also under Particles.

**a. <span lang="grc">πότερον</span>** (<span lang="grc"> **πότερα** </span>) . . . <span lang="grc"> **ἤ** </span>: Thus, <span lang="grc">διηρώτᾱ τὸν Κῦρον πότερον βούλοιτο μένειν ἢ ἀπιέναι</span> _she asked Cyrus whether he wanted to stay or go away_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.15">X. C. 1.3.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">θαυμάζω πότερα ὡς κρατῶν βασιλεὺς αἰτεῖ τὰ ὅπλα ἢ ὡς διὰ φιλίᾱν δῶρα</span> _I wonder whether the king asks for our arms as a conqueror or as gifts on the plea of friendship_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.10">X. A. 2.1.10</cite>.

N.—<span lang="grc">πότερον . . . ἤ</span> may denote that the second alternative is more important than the first. <span lang="grc">πότερον</span> is omitted when the introductory clause contains the adjective <span lang="grc">πότερος</span> (<cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.2">X. C. 1.3.2</cite>).

**b. <span lang="grc">εἴτε . . . εἴτε</span>** gives equal value to each alternative. Thus, <span lang="grc">τὴν σκέψιν ποιώμεθα εἴτε ὠφελίᾱν εἴτε βλάβην παρέχει</span> _let us make the inquiry whether it produces benefit or injury_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 237d">P. Phae. 237d</cite>. <!-- page 604 -->

N. 1.—In Homer <span lang="grc">εἴτε . . . εἴτε</span> (<span lang="grc">εἴ τε . . . εἴ τε</span>) almost always retains the meaning _either . . . or_ (A [65](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/02-euphony-of-vowels.md#s65)).

N. 2.—The first <span lang="grc">εἴτε</span> is rarely omitted in prose, as <span lang="grc">πόλις εἴτε ἰδιῶταί τινες</span> _a State or certain individuals_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 864a">P. L. 864a</cite>; more often in poetry, as <span lang="grc">λόγοισιν εἴτ᾽ ἔργοισιν</span> _by words or deeds_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 517">S. O. T. 517</cite>.

**c. <span lang="grc">εἰ</span>** . . . <span lang="grc"> **ἤ** </span> indicates that the second alternative is preferable or more probable. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἠρώτᾱ εἰ αὐτοῖς τοῖς ἀνδράσι σπένδοιτο τοῖς ἰοῦσι καὶ ἀπιοῦσιν, ἢ καὶ τοῖς ἄλλοις ἔσοιντο σπονδαί</span> _he asked whether he was making a truce merely with the individual men who were coming and going or_ whether _the truce would be with the rest as well_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.7">X. A. 2.3.7</cite>.

**d. <span lang="grc">εἰ</span>** . . . <span lang="grc"> **εἴτε** </span> is like <span lang="grc">εἴτε . . . εἴτε</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ δ᾽ ἔτ᾽ ἐστὶν ἔμψῡχος γυνὴ εἴτ᾽ οὖν ὄλωλεν, εἰδέναι βουλοίμεθ᾽ ἄν</span> _we should like to know whether the lady is still alive or dead_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Alc. 140">E. Alc. 140</cite>.

**e. <span lang="grc">ἢ</span>** (<span lang="grc"> **ἠὲ** </span>) . . . <span lang="grc"> **ἦ** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **ἦε** </span>) occurs in Homer, as <span lang="grc">ὄφρ᾽ ἐὺ εἰδῶ ἠὲ νέον μεθέπεις ἦ καὶ πατρώιός ἐσσι ξεῖνος</span> _that I may know well whether thou art newly a visitor or art actually an ancestral guest-friend_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 1.175"><span lang="grc">α</span> 175</cite>. Cp. [2661](32-interrogative-sentences-questions.md#s2661). <span lang="grc"> **ἢ . . . ἤ** </span> is doubtful in Attic.

## THE NEGATIVES IN INDIRECT QUESTIONS

##### <a id="s2676"></a> §2676

The negative of the direct form is usually preserved in indirect questions.

<span lang="grc">εἴσομαι . . . πότερον ὁ ἔχων αὐτὸ οὐκ εὐδαίμων ἐστὶν ἢ εὐδαίμων</span> _I shall know whether its possessor is happy or not_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 354c">P. R. 354c</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ οἶδ᾽ ὅπως φῶ τοῦτο καὶ μὴ φῶ</span> _I know not how I am to say this and not to say it_ <cite data-ref="Eur. IA 643">E. I. A. 643</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">πῶς μὴ φῶ</span>;).

**a.** Indirect single questions introduced by interrogative pronouns, adjectives, and adverbs, usually have <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>.

**b.** <span lang="grc">μή</span> appears after _verbs of seeing_, _considering_ and the like (<span lang="grc">σκοπῶ, ὁρῶ, ἐννοοῦμαι, ἐνθῡμοῦμαι</span>) when there is an idea of purpose or desire to prevent something. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁρᾶτε . . . ὅτῳ τρόπῳ κάλλιστα ἀμυνεῖσθε αὐτοὺς καὶ μήτε καταφρονήσαντες ἄφαρκτοι ληφθήσεσθε κτλ</span>. _consider how you may best defend yourselves and may neither be caught off your guard through contempt_, etc. <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.33">T. 6.33</cite>. So also with the potential optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>; as <span lang="grc">τί οὖν οὐ σκοποῦμεν πῶς ἂν αὐτῶν μὴ διαμαρτάνοιμεν</span>; _why then do we not consider how we may avoid mistaking them?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.1.10">X. M. 3.1.10</cite>. Indirect questions with <span lang="grc">μή</span> thus belong under <span lang="grc">μή</span> with verbs of fear and apprehension, where <span lang="grc">μή</span> is the negative of the _will_. Cp. [2674](#s2674).

**c.** Indirect questions introduced by <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> have <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> or <span lang="grc">μή</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἤρετο τὸν δῆμον εἰ οὐκ αἰσχύ̄νοιντο</span> _he asked the people whether they were not ashamed_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 1.84">Aes. 1.84</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἤρετό με . . . εἰ μὴ μέμνημαι</span> _he asked me whether I did not remember_ 2. 36.

**d.** In relative clauses joined by <span lang="grc">καί</span> and standing in an indirect question (_what . . . and what not_), <span lang="grc">μή</span> must be used when the verb is to be supplied with the second clause; but when the verb is repeated, either <span lang="grc">μή</span>, or <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> if the antecedent is definite, may be used. Thus, <span lang="grc">διαγιγνώσκουσιν ἅ τε δύνανται καὶ ἃ μή</span> _they distinguish between what they can do and what they cannot_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.2.26">X. M. 4.2.26</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἶσθα . . . ὁπόσοι τε φρουροὶ ἱκανοί εἰσι καὶ ὁπόσοι μή εἰσιν</span> _you know how many garrisons are advantageously situated and how many are not_ 3. 6. 10. The antecedent is definite in <span lang="grc">ἀπέδειξεν οὓς χρὴ δημηγορεῖν καὶ οὓς οὐ δεῖ λέγειν ἐν τῷ δήμῳ</span> _he showed who must speak in the assembly and who must not speak before the people_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 1.27">Aes. 1.27</cite>. <!-- page 605 -->

**e.** As the second member of an alternative question introduced by <span lang="grc">εἰ</span>, _or not_ is either <span lang="grc">ἢ οὐ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἢ μή</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">σκοπῶμεν εἰ ἡμῖν πρέπει ἢ οὔ</span> _let us consider whether it is proper for us or not_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 451d">P. R. 451d</cite>, <span lang="grc">νῦν ἔμαθον δ̀ λέγεις· εἰ δὲ ἀληθὲς ἢ μή, πειρά̄σομαι μαθεῖν</span> _now I have made out what you mean; and I will try to make out whether it is true or not_ 339 a.

**f.** A shift from <span lang="grc">μή</span> to <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> in sequent alternative indirect questions appears to be due to the desire to attain variety. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐ δεῖ ὑ̄μᾶς ἐκ τῶν τοῦ κατηγόρου λόγων τοὺς νόμους καταμανθάνειν, εἰ καλῶς ὑ̄μῖν κεῖνται ἢ μή, ἀλλ᾽ ἐκ τῶν νόμων τοὺς τοῦ κατηγόρου λόγους, εἰ ὀρθῶς καὶ νομίμως ὑ̄μᾶς διδάσκουσι τὸ πρᾶγμα ἢ οὔ</span> _you must not start from the pleas of the accuser to learn whether your laws have been established well or not, but you must start from the laws to learn whether his pleas set forth the case fairly and legally or not_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.14">Ant. 5.14</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Antiph. 6.2">Ant. 6.2</cite>, <cite data-ref="Isaeus 8.9">Is. 8.9</cite>, <cite data-ref="Dem. 20.83">D. 20.83</cite>. Some scholars hold that <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> here lays stress on a negative fact or on something conceived as a negative fact, and that <span lang="grc">μή</span> puts the question abstractly as a mere conception.

## MOODS IN INDIRECT QUESTIONS

##### <a id="s2677"></a> §2677

The moods and tenses of indirect questions follow the same rules as govern clauses in indirect discourse. The person may be changed.

After primary tenses, the mood and tense of the direct question are retained (indicative, past indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, deliberative subjunctive, potential optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>).

After secondary tenses, the mood and tense of the direct form may be retained or the optative may be used instead. The latter is more common. But a past indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> always remains unchanged.

**a. Direct Form Retained**.—<span lang="grc">πολλάκις ἐσκόπει τί διαφέρει μανίᾱς ἀμαθίᾱ</span> _he often considered in what respect ignorance differed from madness_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.2.50">X. M. 1.2.50</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἠπορεῖτο τι ποιήσει</span> _he was uncertain what to do_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.3.29">X. A. 7.3.29</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">τί ποιήσω</span>, deliberative future, [1916](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1916)), <span lang="grc">ἐβουλεύοντο εἴτε κατακαύσωσιν . . . εἴτε τι ἄλλο χρήσωνται</span> _they deliberated whether they should burn them or dispose of them in some other manner_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.4">T. 2.4</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">κατακαύσωμεν, χρησώμεθα</span>;), <span lang="grc">ἠρώτησε . . . ποῦ ἂν ἴδοι Πρόξενον</span> _he asked where he could see Proxenus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.4.15">X. A. 2.4.15</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">ποῦ ἂν ἴδοιμι</span>;).

**b. Optative**: <span lang="grc">ἤρετο εἴ τις ἐμοῦ εἴη σοφώτερος</span> _he asked whether any one was wiser than I_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 21a">P. A. 21a</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">ἐστί</span>;), <span lang="grc">ὅ τι δὲ ποιήσοι οὐ διεσήμηνε</span> _he did not announce publicly what he was going to do_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.23">X. A. 2.1.23</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">τί ποιήσω</span>;), <span lang="grc">τὸν θεὸν ἐπηρώτων εἰ παραδοῖεν Κορινθίοις τὴν πόλιν</span> _they questioned the god whether they should surrender the city to the Corinthians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.25">T. 1.25</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">παραδῶμεν</span>;). Here <span lang="grc">παραδοῖεν</span> might represent the aorist indicative, but that tense is usually retained to avoid confusion (exceptionally <span lang="grc">ἠρώτᾱ τι πάθοιεν</span> <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.3.19">X. C. 2.3.19</cite>; cp. <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.3.25">X. A. 6.3.25</cite>, <cite data-ref="Dem. 50.55">D. 50.55</cite>). An imperfect relatively anterior to the time of the main verb is retained in <cite data-ref="Dem. 30.19">D. 30.19</cite>.

**c.** A dubitative subjunctive in an indirect question, when dependent on an optative, may be attracted into the optative; as <span lang="grc">ἔλεγες . . . ὅτι οὐκ ἂν ἔχοις ἐξεθὼν ὅ τι χοῷο σαυτῷ</span> _you were saying that if you went out you would not know what to do with yourself_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 45b">P. Cr. 45b</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">τί χρῶμαι ἐμαυτῷ</span>;). <!-- page 606 -->

**d.** Homer has the optative for the indicative due to indirect discourse only in indirect questions; as <span lang="grc">εἴροντο τίς εἴη καὶ πόθεν ἔλθοι</span> _they asked who he was and whence he had come_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 17.368"><span lang="grc">ρ</span> 368</cite>. See [2624 c](31-dependent-substantive-clauses-2574-2635.md#s2624).

##### <a id="s2678"></a> §2678

After a secondary tense the mood of a direct question may be retained (usually for vividness) in the same sentence with the mood of an indirect question (cp. [2632](31-dependent-substantive-clauses-2574-2635.md#s2632)). Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁμοῖοι ἦσαν θαυμάζειν ὅποι ποτὲ τρέψονται οἱ Ἕλληνες καὶ τί ἐν νῷ ἔχοιεν</span> _they seemed to be wondering to what direction the Greeks would turn and what they had in mind_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.5.13">X. A. 3.5.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἤρετο ὅ τι θαυμάζοι καὶ ὁπόσοι αὐτῶν τεθνᾶσιν</span> _he asked what it was that he was astonished at and how many of them were dead_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.113">T. 3.113</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">τί θαυμάζεις, πόσοι τεθνᾶσιν</span>;).

**a.** In some cases there is no apparent reason (apart from desire for variety) for this use of the indicative and optative in the same sentence. Sometimes the indicative may ask for a statement of _fact_, the optative request an _opinion_ of the person questioned.

##### <a id="s2679"></a> §2679

Parallel to 2624 are cases like <span lang="grc">ᾔδει ὅπου ἔκειτο ἡ ἐπιστολή</span> _he knew where the letter had been put_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.2.9">X. C. 2.2.9</cite>.

## ANSWERS TO _YES_ AND _NO_ QUESTIONS

##### <a id="s2680"></a> §2680

_Yes_ and _No_ questions may be answered in various ways, _e.g.:_

**a.** By repeating the verb or another emphatic word with or without one or more confirmative adverbs. Thus, <span lang="grc">φῂς σὺ ἀμείνω πολί̄την εἶναι, ὃν σὺ ἐπαινεῖς, ἢ ὃν ἐγώ; φημὶ γὰρ οὖν</span> _do you assert that the citizen whom you approve is better than the one I approve? I do say so_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.6.14">X. M. 4.6.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἶσθ᾽ οὖν ἂ λέξαι σοι . . . θέλω; οὐκ οἶδα</span> _dost thou know what I fain would tell thee? No._ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hec. 999">E. Hec. 999</cite>.

**b.** By <span lang="grc">ἐγώ, ἔγωγε, οὐκ ἐγώ, οὐκ ἔγωγε</span>, sometimes with <span lang="grc">νὴ Δία</span> or <span lang="grc">μὰ Δία</span>.

**c.** _Yes_ may be expressed by <span lang="grc">ναί, ναὶ μὰ τὸν Δία, μάλιστα, φημί, πάνυ γε, πάνυ μὲν οὖν, εὖ γε, ἔστι ταῦτα, ἔστιν οὕτως, ἀληθῆ λέγεις, ἀληθέστατα, ὀρθῶς γε, κομιδῇ</span>, etc.

_No_ may be expressed by <span lang="grc">οὔ, οὐκ ἔστιν, οὐ δῆτα, οὐ μὰ Δία, οὐδαμῶς, οὔ φημι, μὴ γάρ, ἥκιστα, ἥκιστά γε</span>, etc.

**d.** In the form of a question: <span lang="grc">τί μήν; τί γάρ; ἀλλὰ τί; πῶς; πόθεν; πῶς γὰρ οὔ</span>;
