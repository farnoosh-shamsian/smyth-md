# CLASSES OF SUBORDINATE CLAUSES

##### <a id="s2189"></a> §2189

Subordinate clauses are of three classes:

\1. Substantival clauses: in which the subordinate clause plays the part of a substantive and is either the subject or the object: <span lang="grc">δῆλον ἦν ι ὅτι ἐγγύς που βασιλεὺς ἦν</span> _it was plain that the king was somewhere hard by_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.6">X. A. 2.3.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἴστε ι ὅ τι ποιεῖτε</span> _you do not know what you are doing_ 1. 5. 16.

\2. Adjectival (attributive) clauses: in which the subordinate clause plays the part of an adjective, and contains a relative whose antecedent (expressed or implied) stands in the principal clause: <span lang="grc">λέγε δὴ τὴν ἐπιστολὴν ι ἣν ἔπεμψε Φίλιππος</span> _come read the letter which Philip sent_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.39">D. 18.39</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">τὴν ὑπὸ Φιλίππου πεμφθεῖσαν</span>).

\3. Adverbial clauses: in which the subordinate clause plays the part of an adverb or adverbial expression modifying the principal clause in like manner as an adverb modifies a verb.

<span lang="grc">κραυγὴν πολλὴν ἐποίουν καλοῦντες ἀλλήλους, ὥστε καὶ τοὺς πολεμίους ἀκούειν</span> _they made a loud noise by calling each other so that even the enemy heard them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.2.17">X. A. 2.2.17</cite> (here <span lang="grc">ὥστε . . . ἀκούειν</span> may be regarded as having the force of an adverb: _and in a manner audible even to the enemy_); <span lang="grc">πῶς ἂν οὖν ὀρθῶς δικάσαιτε περὶ αὐτῶν; εἰ τούτους ἐά̄σετε τὸν νομιζόμενον ὅρκον διομοσαμένους κατηγορῆσαι κτλ</span>. _how then would you judge correctly about them? if you permit_ (i.e. by permitting) _them to make their accusations after having sworn the customary oath_, etc. <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.90">Ant. 5.90</cite>. Cp. [1095](15-adverbs.md#s1095) end.

##### <a id="s2190"></a> §2190

Accordingly all complex sentences may be classified as Substantival sentences, Adjectival sentences, and Adverbial sentences. This division is, in general, the basis of the treatment of complex sentences in this book, except when, for convenience, closely connected constructions are treated together; as in the case of (adverbial) pure final clauses and (substantival) object clauses after _verbs of effort_ and of _fearing_.

**a.** Some sentences may be classed both as substantival and adverbial, as clauses with <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> and <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span>. An adverbial or adjectival clause may assume a substantival character ([2247](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/04-causal-clauses.md#s2247), [2488](30-adjective-clauses-relative-clauses-2488-2573.md#s2488)).

Complex sentences are considered in the following order: Adverbial, Adjectival, Substantival.
