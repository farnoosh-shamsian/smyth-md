# THE PARTICIPLE

##### <a id="s2039"></a> §2039

The participle (<span lang="grc">μετοχή</span> _participation_) is a verbal adjective, in part a verb, in part an adjective.

##### <a id="s2040"></a> §2040

The participle is like a verb herein:

**a.** It shows the distinctions of voice and tense. Its tenses mark action simply occurring, continuing, and completed.

**b.** It can have an object in the same case (genitive, dative, accusative) as the finite forms.

**c.** It is modified by adverbs, not by adjectives.

**d.** It may take <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, and, with that particle, represents <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the indicative or <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the optative ([1845 f](20-the-verb-voices/09-infinitive-and-participle-with-alphanu.md#s1845)f.).

##### <a id="s2041"></a> §2041

The participle shows its adjectival nature by being inflected and by admitting the article before it, both of which characteristics give it the character of a noun. It follows the rules of agreement like other adjectives ([1020](14-adjectives.md#s1020)). Unlike the adjective, it represents a quality in action (cp. [1857](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1857)).

##### <a id="s2042"></a> §2042

The participle is always used in connection with a substantive or a substantive pronoun, which may be contained in a verbal form, as <span lang="grc">διάγουσι μανθάνοντες</span> _they spend their time in learning_.

##### <a id="s2043"></a> §2043

The tenses of the participle (except the future) not in indirect discourse are timeless, and denote only stage of action ([1872](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1872)). When they stand in indirect discourse and represent the indicative, they denote time relatively to that of the main verb.

##### <a id="s2044"></a> §2044

The future participle marks an action as in prospect at the time denoted by the leading verb. Since it expresses an idea of _will_, it shows that an action is purposed, intended, or expected. With the article it denotes the person or thing _likely_ (or _able_) _to do_ something ( = <span lang="grc">μέλλων</span> with inf. [1959](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1959)). The nearest approach to mere futurity appears in general only after _verbs of knowing_ and _perceiving_ ([2106](#s2106), cp. [2112](#s2112) b).

<span lang="grc">ὁ δ᾽ ἀνὴρ αὐτῆς λαγὼς ᾤχετο θηρά̄σων</span> _but her husband had gone to hunt hares_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.5.24">X. A. 4.5.24</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁ ἡγησόμενος οὐδεὶς ἔσται</span> _there will be no one to guide_ us 2. 4. 5, <span lang="grc">πολλὰ . . . δεῖ τὸν εὖ στρατηγήσοντα</span> ( = <span lang="grc">τὸν μέλλοντα εὖ στρατηγήσειν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἔχειν</span> _he who_ <!-- page 455 --> _intends to be a good general must have many qualifications_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.1.6">X. M. 3.1.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">θανουμένη γὰρ ἐξῄδη</span> _for I knew that I should_ (or _must_) _die_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 460">S. Ant. 460</cite> (cp. [2106](#s2106)).

##### <a id="s2045"></a> §2045

The negative of the participle is <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>, except when the participle has a general or conditional force, or occurs in a sentence which requires <span lang="grc">μή</span>. See [2728](35-negative-sentences.md#s2728).

##### <a id="s2046"></a> §2046

The participle has three main uses.

A. Attributive: as an attributive to a substantive.

B. Circumstantial (or Adverbial): denoting some attendant circumstance and qualifying the main verb like an adverbial phrase or clause.

C. Supplementary: as a supplement to a verbal predicate, which, without such a supplement, would be incomplete.

##### <a id="s2047"></a> §2047

The circumstantial and supplementary participles are predicate participles.

##### <a id="s2048"></a> §2048

The attributive and circumstantial participles are commonly not necessary to the _construction;_ but the removal of a supplementary participle may make the construction incomplete. The circumstantial participle is used by way of apposition to the subject of the verb and, though strictly predicative, may agree attributively with a noun or pronoun. An attributive participle may be circumstantial, as <span lang="grc">οἱ μὴ δυνάμενοι διατελέσαι τὴν ὁδὸν ἐνυκτέρευσαν ἄσῑτοι</span> _those who_ (i.e. _if any_) _were unable to complete the march passed the night without food_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.5.11">X. A. 4.5.11</cite>. A participle may be both circumstantial and supplementary, as <span lang="grc">ἀδικούμενοι ὀργίζονται</span> (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.77">T. 1.77</cite>) _they are enraged at being wronged_ or _because_ (_when_, _if_) _they are wronged_. Circumstantial and supplementary participles often cannot be sharply distinguished; as with verbs signifying _to be angry_, _ashamed_, _content_, _pleased_ ([2100](#s2100)), _inferior to, do wrong_ ([2101](#s2101)), _endure_ ([2098](#s2098)), _come_ and _go_ ([2099](#s2099)). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀδικῶ ταῦτα ποιῶν</span> _I do wrong in doing this_ or _I am guilty in doing this:_ in the first case <span lang="grc">ταῦτα ποιῶν</span> is appositive to the subject of the verb; in the second these words define the predicate adjective <span lang="grc">ἄδικος</span> contained in <span lang="grc">ἀδικῶ</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἄδικός εἰμι</span>).

## THE ATTRIBUTIVE PARTICIPLE

##### <a id="s2049"></a> §2049

The attributive participle (with any modifier), with or without the article, modifies a substantive like any other adjective.

<span lang="grc">ὁ ἐφεστηκὼς κίνδῡνος τῇ πόλει</span> _the danger impending over the State_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.176">D. 18.176</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ ὄντες ἐχθροί</span> _the existing enemies_ 6. 15, <span lang="grc">ὁ παρὼν καιρός</span> _the present crisis_ 3. 3, <span lang="grc">τὸ Κοτύλαιον ὀνομαζόμενον ὄρος</span> _the mountain called Cotylaeum_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.86">Aes. 3.86</cite>, <span lang="grc">αἱ Αἰόλου νῆσοι καλούμεναι</span> _the so-called islands of Aeolus_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.88">T. 3.88</cite> (cp. [1170](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1170)). For the position of an attributive participle with its modifiers, see [1166](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1166).

##### <a id="s2050"></a> §2050

The substantive with which the attributive participle (with the article) agrees directly, may be omitted, the participle thus becoming a substantive ([1153 b](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1153), and N. 1); as, <span lang="grc">ὁ οἴκαδε βουλόμενος ἀπιέναι</span> _whoever wants to go home_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.4">X. A. 1.7.4</cite>. Neuter participles are often substantival, as <span lang="grc">τὰ δέοντα</span> _duties_.

**a.** Substantives or relative clauses must often be used to translate such par<!-- page 456 --> ticiples, as <span lang="grc">ὁ φεύγων</span> _the exile_ or _the defendant_, <span lang="grc">τὸ μέλλον</span> _the future_, <span lang="grc">οἱ νῑκῶντες</span> _the victors_, <span lang="grc">ὁ κλέπτων</span> _the thief_, <span lang="grc">οἱ θανόντες</span> _the dead_, <span lang="grc">ὁ σωθείς</span> _the man who has been saved_, <span lang="grc">οἱ δεδιότες</span> _those who are afraid_, <span lang="grc">οἱ ἀδικούμενοι</span> _those who are_ (_being_) _wronged_, <span lang="grc">ὁ τὴν γνώμην ταύτην εἰπών</span> _the one who gave this opinion_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.68">T. 8.68</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁ ἐνταῦθ᾽ ἑαυτὸν τάξᾱς τῆς πολῑτείᾱς εἴμ᾽ ἐγώ</span> _the man who took this position in the State was I_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.62">D. 18.62</cite>. The participle with the article may represent a relative clause of purpose or result, as <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.4.5">X. A. 2.4.5</cite> cited in 2044.

##### <a id="s2051"></a> §2051

A participle may be modified by adjectives or take a genitive, when its verbal nature has ceased to be felt: <span lang="grc">τὰ μῑκρὰ συμφέροντα τῆς πόλεως</span> _the petty interests of the State_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.28">D. 18.28</cite>. Cp. <span lang="grc">συμφέρον ἦν τῇ πόλει</span> _it was advantageous to the State_ 19. 75 (here the participle is used like a predicate). Thucydides often uses in an abstract sense a substantival neuter participle where the infinitive would be more common, e.g., <span lang="grc">τὸ δεδιός</span> _fear_, <span lang="grc">τὸ θαρσοῦν</span> _courage_ (for <span lang="grc">τὸ δεδιέναι, τὸ θαρσεῖν</span>) 1. 36. See [1153 b](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1153), N. 2. In poetry many participles are used substantively, as <span lang="grc">ὁ τεκών</span> _father_, <span lang="grc">ἡ τεκοῦσα</span> _mother_, <span lang="grc">οἱ τεκόντες</span> _parents_.

##### <a id="s2052"></a> §2052

The article with the participle is either _generic_ or _particular_ ([1124](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1124)). Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁ λέγων</span> _the_ definite _speaker_ on a particular occasion, or _orator_ in general. So <span lang="grc">ὁ οὐ δρά̄σᾱς</span> the definite person who did not do something, <span lang="grc">ὁ μὴ δρά̄σᾱς</span> any one who did not do something (a supposed case), <span lang="grc">ὁ μὴ γαμῶν ἄνθρωπος οὐκ ἔχει κακά</span> _the unmarried man has no troubles_ Men. Sent. 437. Generic are <span lang="grc">ὁ τυχών, ὁ βουλόμενος</span>, 2050 a.

**a.** Participles having an indefinite force may, especially in the plural number, be used without the article. Thus, <span lang="grc">κατασκεψομένους ἔπεμπε</span> _he sent men to reconnoitre_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.2">X. C. 3.1.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀδικοῦντα πειρᾱσόμεθα . . . ἀμύ̄νασθαι</span> _we shall endeavour to avenge ourselves on any one who injures us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.23">X. A. 2.3.23</cite>.

##### <a id="s2053"></a> §2053

A participle and its substantive often correspond to a verbal noun with the genitive or to an articular infinitive. Cp. _post urbem conditam_ and Milton's “Since created man.”

<span lang="grc">τῷ σί̄τῳ ἐπιλείποντι ἐπιέζοντο</span> _they suffered from the failure of the crops_ ( = <span lang="grc">τῇ τοῦ σί̄του ἐπιλείψει</span>) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.20">T. 3.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">δι᾽ ὑ̄μᾶς μὴ ξυμμαχήσαντας</span> _by reason of your not joining the alliance_ ( = <span lang="grc">διὰ τὸ ὑ̄μᾶς μὴ ξυμμαχῆσαι</span>) 6. 80, <span lang="grc">μετὰ Συρᾱκούσᾱς οἰκισθείσᾱς</span> _after the foundation of Syracuse_ 6. 3, <span lang="grc">ἐλύ̄πει αὐτὸν ἡ χώρᾱ πορθουμένη</span> _the ravaging of the country grieved him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.7.12">X. A. 7.7.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ ὀργὴ σὺν τῷ φόβῳ λήγοντι ἄπεισι</span> _his wrath will disappear with the cessation of his fear_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.5.21">X. C. 4.5.21</cite>.

**a.** Except in expressions of time, such as <span lang="grc">ἅμα ἦρι ἀρχομένῳ</span> _at the beginning of spring_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.2">T. 2.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ Κόδρου βασιλεύοντος</span> _in the reign of Codrus_ Lyc. 84 (cp. [1689](19-prepositions.md#s1689) b), this construction is in place only when the part. is necessary to the sense. In poetry: <span lang="grc">Ζεὺς γελοῖος ὀμνύμενος</span> _swearing by Zeus is ridiculous_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 1241">Ar. Nub. 1241</cite>; in Hom. A 601, I 682.

## THE CIRCUMSTANTIAL PARTICIPLE

##### <a id="s2054"></a> §2054

The circumstantial participle is added, without the article, to a noun or pronoun to set forth some circumstance under which an action, generally the main action, takes place.

**a.** The circumstantial participle thus qualifies the principal verb of the sentence like an adverbial clause or supplementary predicate. Cp. <span lang="grc">μετὰ ταῦτα εἶπε</span> <!-- page 457 --> _afterwards he said_ with <span lang="grc">γελῶν εἶπε</span> _he said laughingly_. Such participles usually have the force of subordinate clauses added to the main verb by conjunctions denoting _time_, _condition_, _cause_, etc.; but may often be rendered by adverbial phrases or even by a separate finite verb, which brings out distinctly the idea latent in the participle.

**b.** The circumstantial participle has no article. In agreement with a noun and its article, it stands before the article or after the noun (i.e. in the predicate position). By the agreement of the participle with a noun or pronoun, the predicate of the sentence is more exactly defined.

##### <a id="s2055"></a> §2055

The circumstantial participle has two main constructions each equivalent in meaning to a clause of _time_, _condition_, _cause_, etc.

##### <a id="s2056"></a> §2056

(I) The subject of the participle is identical with the noun or pronoun subject or object of the leading verb, and agrees with it in gender, number, and case.

(<span lang="grc">οἱ ἄνθρωποι</span>) <span lang="grc">λιπόντες τὴν ὁδὸν φεύγοντες ὀλίγοι ἀπέθνῃσκον</span> _by leaving the road and making off_ only _a few were killed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.2.7">X. A. 4.2.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">προπέμψαντες κήρῡκα πόλεμον προεροῦντα</span> _having sent a herald in advance to proclaim war_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.29">T. 1.29</cite>.

##### <a id="s2057"></a> §2057

(II) Absolute participial clauses, in which a participle, and not a finite verb, forms the predicate. These are of two kinds.

##### <a id="s2058"></a> §2058

A. **Genitive Absolute**.—A participle agreeing in the genitive with its own subject, which is not identical with the subject of the leading verb, is said to stand in the genitive absolute. Cp. [2070](#s2070).

<span lang="grc">Κῦρος ἀνέβη ἐπὶ τὰ ὄρη οὐδενὸς κωλύ̄οντος</span> _Cyrus ascended the mountains without any one preventing him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.22">X. A. 1.2.22</cite>.

N.—The English nominative absolute is represented by the Greek genitive absolute. Cp. Tennyson: “we sitting, as I said, the cock crew loud” = <span lang="grc">ἡμῶν καθημένων, ὅπερ ἔλεγον, μέγα ᾖσεν ὁ ἀλεκτρυών</span>.

##### <a id="s2059"></a> §2059

B. **Accusative Absolute**.—When the participle has no definite subject (i.e. with impersonal verbs), the accusative absolute is used instead of the genitive absolute. Cp. [2076](#s2076).

<span lang="grc">συνδόξαν τῷ πατρὶ καὶ τῇ μητρὶ γαμεῖ τὴν Κυαξάρου θυγατέρα</span> _on the approval of_ (lit. _it seeming good to_) _his father and mother he married the daughter of Cyaxares_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.5.28">X. C. 8.5.28</cite>.

##### <a id="s2060"></a> §2060

The circumstantial participle expresses simply circumstance or manner in general. It may imply various other relations, such as _time_, _manner_, _means_, _cause_, _purpose_, _concession_, _condition_, etc. But it is often impossible to assign a participle exclusively to any one of these relations (which are purely logical), nor can all the delicate relations of the participle be set forth in systematic form.

##### <a id="s2061"></a> §2061

**Time.**—The time denoted by the participle is only relative to that of the governing verb, and is to be inferred from the context. Each participial form in itself expresses only stage of action ([1850](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1850)).

<span lang="grc">ἀκούσᾱσι τοῖς στρατηγοῖς ταῦτα ἔδοξε τὸ στράτευμα συναγαγεῖν</span> _on hearing this i&lt;\*&gt; seemed best to the generals to collect the troops_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.4.19">X. A. 4.4.19</cite>. <!-- page 458 -->

**a.** Several temporal participles have an adverbial force: <span lang="grc"> **ἀρχόμενος** </span> _in the beginning_, _at first_, <span lang="grc"> **τελευτῶν** </span> _at last, finally_, <span lang="grc"> **διαλιπὼν** </span> (or <span lang="grc">ἐπισχὼν</span>) <span lang="grc"> **χρόνον** </span> _after a while_, <span lang="grc"> **διαλείπων χρόνον** </span> _at intervals_, <span lang="grc"> **χρονίζων** </span> _for a long time_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἅπερ καὶ ἀρχόμενος εἶπον</span> _as I said at the outset_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.64">T. 4.64</cite>, <span lang="grc">τελευτῶν ἐχαλέπαινεν</span> _at last he became angry_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.5.16">X. A. 4.5.16</cite>. Note <span lang="grc"> **ἀρξάμενος ἀπό τινος** </span> _beginning with_ or _especially_.

##### <a id="s2062"></a> §2062

**Manner**.—<span lang="grc">παρήλαυνον τεταγμένοι</span> _they marched past in order_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.16">X. A. 1.2.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">κραυγὴν πολλὴν ἐποίουν καλοῦντες ἀλλήλους</span> _they made a loud noise by calling to each other_ 2. 2. 17, <span lang="grc">προείλετο μᾶλλον τοῖς νόμοις ἐμμένων ἀποθανεῖν ἢ παρανομῶν ζῆν</span> _he preferred rather to abide by the laws and die than to disobey them and live_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.4.4">X. M. 4.4.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">φατὲ μὲν εὐτυχεῖς εἶναι, ὡς καὶ ἐστὲ καλῶς ποιοῦντες</span> _you claim to be favoured by fortune as happily you are in fact_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.232">Aes. 3.232</cite>. To characterize a preceding statement with the participle in apposition to the subject of the preceding sentence; thus, <span lang="grc">ὀρθῶς γε ταῦτα λέγοντες</span> _yes_, _and saying this correctly_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 16.2">X. O. 16.2</cite>.

**a.** Several participles of manner have an idiomatic meaning, e.g. <span lang="grc"> **ἀνύσᾱς** </span> _quickly_ (lit. _having accomplished_), <span lang="grc"> **ἔχων** </span> _continually_, _persistently_ (lit. _holding on_), <span lang="grc"> **λαθών** </span> _secretly_, <span lang="grc"> **κλαίων** </span> _to one's sorrow_ (lit. _weeping_), <span lang="grc"> **χαίρων** </span> _with impunity_ (lit. _rejoicing_), <span lang="grc"> **φέρων** </span> _hastily_ (lit. _carrying off_), <span lang="grc"> **φθάσᾱς** </span> _before_ (lit. _anticipating_). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἄνοιγ᾽ ἀνύσᾱς</span> _hurry up and open_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 181">Ar. Nub. 181</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔκπλουν ποιεῖται λαθὼν τὴν φυλακήν</span> _he sailed out unobserved by the guard_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.65">T. 1.65</cite> (cp. [2096](#s2096) f), <span lang="grc">φλυᾱρεῖς ἔχων</span> _you keep trifling_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 490e">P. G. 490e</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦτον οὐδεὶς χαίρων ἀδικήσει</span> _no one will wrong him with impunity_ 510 d, <span lang="grc">ἀνέῳξάς με φθάσᾱς</span> _you opened_ the door _before_ I could knock <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Pl. 1102">Ar. Plut. 1102</cite> (cp. [2096](#s2096) e).

##### <a id="s2063"></a> §2063

**Means** (often the present participle).—<span lang="grc">λῃζόμενοι ξῶσι</span> _they live by pillaging_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.2.25">X. C. 3.2.25</cite>, <span lang="grc">μὴ κρῖν᾽ ὁρῶν τὸ κάλλος, ἀλλὰ τὸν τρόπον</span> _judge by regarding not beauty, but_ (_by regarding_) _character_ Men. Sent. 333.

##### <a id="s2064"></a> §2064

**Cause**.—<span lang="grc">Παρύσατις . . . ὑπῆρχε τῷ Κύ̄ρῳ, φιλοῦσα αὐτὸν μᾶλλον ἢ τὸν βασιλεύοντα Ἀρταξέρξην</span> _Parysatis favoured Cyrus because she loved him more than she did Artaxerxes the king_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.4">X. A. 1.1.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπείχοντο κερδῶν αἰσχρὰ νομίζοντες εἶναι</span> _they held aloof from gains because they thought them disgraceful_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.2.22">X. M. 1.2.22</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί γὰρ δεδιότες σφόδρα οὕτως ἐπείγεσθε</span>; _for what are you afraid of, that you are so desperately in haste?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.7.26">X. H. 1.7.26</cite>.

**a. <span lang="grc">τί μαθών</span>** _what induced him to_ (lit. _having learned what?_), <span lang="grc"> **τί παθών** </span> _what possessed him to_ (lit. _having experienced what?_) are used with the general sense of _wherefore?_ in direct (with <span lang="grc">ὅ τι</span> in indirect) questions expressing surprise or disapprobation; as <span lang="grc">τί μαθόντες ἐμαρτυρεῖτε ὑ̄μεῖς</span>; _what put it into your heads to give evidence?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 45.38">D. 45.38</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί παθόντε λελάσμεθα</span>; _what possessed us to forget?_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 11.313"><span lang="grc">Λ</span> 313</cite>. Cp. <span lang="grc">τί βουλόμενος</span>.

**b. <span lang="grc">τί ἔχων</span>**; _what's the matter with you?_ (lit. _having what?_)

##### <a id="s2065"></a> §2065

**Purpose or Object**.—The future (sometimes the present) participle is used to denote purpose, especially after verbs denoting _to come, go_, _send_, _summon_, etc. Thus, <span lang="grc">προπέμψαντες κήρῡκα πόλεμον προεροῦντα</span> _having sent a herald in advance to proclaim war_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.29">T. 1.29</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁ βάρβαρος ἐπὶ τὴν Ἑλλάδα δουλωσόμενος ἦλθεν</span> _the barbarians proceeded against Greece with the purpose of enslaving it_ 1. 18, <span lang="grc">συνεκάλεσαν ἀπὸ τῶν πόλεων ἁπᾱσῶν ἀκουσομένους</span> ([2052 a](#s2052)) <span lang="grc">τῆς παρὰ βασιλέως ἐπιστολῆς</span> _they summoned from all the cities men to listen to the letter from the king_ <!-- page 459 --> <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 7.1.39">X. H. 7.1.39</cite>. Present: <span lang="grc">ἔπεμπον . . . λέγοντας ὅτι κτλ</span>. _they sent men to say that_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.4.37">X. H. 2.4.37</cite>.

##### <a id="s2066"></a> §2066

**Opposition or Concession**.—<span lang="grc">οὐδὲν ἐρῶ πρὸς ταῦτα ἔχων εἰπεῖν</span> _I will make no reply to this though I might_ (speak) _do so_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lach. 197c">P. Lach. 197c</cite>, <span lang="grc">πολλοὶ γὰρ ὄντες εὐγενεῖς εἰσιν κακοί</span> _for many, albeit noble by birth, are ignoble_ <cite data-ref="Eur. El. 551">E. El. 551</cite>.

##### <a id="s2067"></a> §2067

**Condition** (negative always <span lang="grc">μή</span>).—<span lang="grc">σὺ δὲ κλύων</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν κλύῃς</span>) <span lang="grc">εἴσει τάχα</span> _but if you listen you shall soon know_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Birds 1390">Ar. Av. 1390</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν δύναιο μὴ καμὼν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ κάμοις</span>) <span lang="grc">εὐδαιμονεῖν</span> _you cannot be happy unless you work_ E. fr. 461.

##### <a id="s2068"></a> §2068

**Any Attendant Circumstance**.—<span lang="grc">συλλέξᾱς στράτευμα ἐπολιόρκει Μί̄λητον</span> _having collected an army he laid siege to Miletus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.7">X. A. 1.1.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">παραγγέλλει τῷ Κλεάρχῳ λαβόντι ἥκειν ὅσον ἦν αὐτῷ στράτευμα</span> _he gave orders to Clearchus to come with all the force he had_ 1. 2. 1.

**a. <span lang="grc">ἔχων</span>** _having_, <span lang="grc"> **ἄγων** </span> _leading_, <span lang="grc"> **φέρων** </span> _carrying_ (mostly of inanimate objects), <span lang="grc"> **χρώμενος** </span> _using_, <span lang="grc"> **λαβών** </span> _taking_ are used where English employs _with_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔχων στρατιὰ̄ν ἀφικνεῖται</span> _he arrives with an army_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.30">T. 4.30</cite>, <span lang="grc">βοῇ χρώμενοι</span> _with a shout_ 2. 84, <span lang="grc">ἐκέλευσε λαβόντα ἄνδρας ἐλθεῖν ὅτι πλείστους</span> _he ordered him to come with all the men he could_ (or to _take . . . and come_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.11">X. A. 1.1.11</cite>.

**b.** In poetry participles (especially) of verbs denoting motion are often added to _verbs of giving_, _setting_ to make the action more picturesque (H. 304, <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 854">S. Aj. 854</cite>).

##### <a id="s2069"></a> §2069

The force of these circumstantial participles does not lie in the participle itself, but is derived from the context. Unless attended by some modifying adverb, the context often does not decide whether the participle has a temporal, a causal, a conditional, a concessive force, etc.; and some participles may be referred to more than one of the above classes. Thus, <span lang="grc">πατὴρ δ᾽ ἀπειλῶν οὐκ ἔχει μέγαν φόβον</span> (Men. fr. [454](../part-2-inflection/09-verbs/06-reduplication.md#s454)) may mean: _a father by threatening_ ( = _when_ or _because_ or _if_ or _though_, _he threatens_) _does not excite much fear_.

### GENITIVE ABSOLUTE. ACCUSATIVE ABSOLUTE

##### <a id="s2070"></a> §2070

**Genitive Absolute**.—A circumstantial participle agreeing with a genitive noun or pronoun which is not in the main construction of the sentence, stands in the genitive absolute. Like other circumstantial participles, the genitive absolute expresses time, cause, condition, concession, or simply any attendant circumstance.

**a.** Time: <span lang="grc">ταῦτ᾽ ἐπρά̄χθη Κόνωνος στρατηγοῦντος</span> _these things were effected while Conon was in command_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 9.56">I. 9.56</cite>, <span lang="grc">τούτων λεχθέντων ἀνέστησαν</span> _this said, they rose_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.3.1">X. A. 3.3.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">Ἠϊόνα . . . Μήδων ἐχόντων πολιορκίᾳ εἷλον</span> _they blockaded and captured Eïon which was held by the Medes_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.98">T. 1.98</cite>.

**b.** Cause: <span lang="grc">τῶν σωμάτων θηλῡνομένων καὶ αἱ ψῡχαὶ ἀρρωστότεραι γίγνονται</span> _by the enfeebling of the body, the spirit too is made weaker_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 4.2">X. O. 4.2</cite>.

**c.** Opposition or Concession: <span lang="grc">καὶ μεταπεμπομένου αὐτοῦ οὐκ ἐθέλω ἐλθεῖν</span> _even though he is sending for me_, _I am unwilling to go_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.10">X. A. 1.3.10</cite>. <span lang="grc">καίπερ</span> is usually added ([2083](#s2083)).

**d.** Condition: <span lang="grc">οἴομαι καὶ νῦν ἔτι ἐπανορθωθῆναι ἂν τὰ πρά̄γματα τούτων γιγνομέ-</span> <!-- page 460 --> <span lang="grc">νων</span> _if these measures should be taken_, _I am of the opinion that even now our situation might be rectified_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.76">D. 9.76</cite>.

**e.** Attendant Circumstance: <span lang="grc">Κῦρος ἀνέβη ἐπὶ τὰ ὄρη οὐδενὸς κωλύ̄οντος</span> _Cyrus ascended the mountains without opposition_ (lit. _no one hindering_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.22">X. A. 1.2.22</cite> (or _since_ no one opposed him).

##### <a id="s2071"></a> §2071

<span lang="grc"> **ἑκών** </span> _willing_, <span lang="grc"> **ἄ̄κων** </span> _unwilling_ are properly participles and are treated as such (cp. [2117](#s2117) c). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐμοῦ οὐχ ἑκόντος</span> _without my consent_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 455">S. Aj. 455</cite>.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ἄ̄κων, ἀεκαξόμενος, ἀφρονέων, ἀελπτέων, ἀνάρμενος, ἀνομολογούμενος, ἀτίξων</span> are the only cases in Greek showing the earlier method of negativing the participle with _alpha privative_. Elsewhere <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> or <span lang="grc">μή</span> is used.

##### <a id="s2072"></a> §2072

The genitive of the participle may stand without its noun or pronoun

**a.** When the noun or pronoun may easily be supplied from the context. Thus, <span lang="grc">οἱ δὲ πολέμιοι, προσιόντων</span> (<span lang="grc">τῶν Ἑλλήνων</span>, previously mentioned), <span lang="grc">τέως μὲν ἡσυχίαζον</span> _the enemy, as they were approaching, for a while remained quiet_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.4.16">X. A. 5.4.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐρώτᾱ, ἔφη, ὦ Κῦρε, . . . ὡς</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐμοῦ</span>) <span lang="grc">τἀ̄ληθῆ ἐροῦντος</span> _put your question_ (_said he_), _Cyrus_, _on the supposition that I will speak the truth_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.9">X. C. 3.1.9</cite>.

**b.** When the noun or pronoun may easily be supplied otherwise; here, e.g., <span lang="grc">ἀνθρώπων</span> or <span lang="grc">πρᾱγμάτων</span> is said to be supplied grammatically. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἰόντων εἰς μάχην</span> _when_ (men) _are going into battle_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.54">X. C. 3.3.54</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦτον τὸν τρόπον πρᾱχθέντων τῆς πόλεως γίγνεται τὰ χρήματα</span> _when_ (things) _have happened in this way_, _the property belongs to the State_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 24.12">D. 24.12</cite>; and in <span lang="grc">ὕ̄οντος</span> (<span lang="grc">Διός</span>, 934 a) <span lang="grc">πολλῷ</span> _when it was raining hard_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.1.16">X. H. 1.1.16</cite>. Quasi-impersonal verbs ([933](07-omission-of-the-subject.md#s933)) thus take the genitive rather than the accusative absolute: <span lang="grc">οὕτως ἔχοντος</span> _in this state of things_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 381c">P. R. 381c</cite>, influenced by <span lang="grc">οὕτως ἐχόντων</span> <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.40">X. A. 3.1.40</cite>.

**c.** When a subordinate clause with <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> follows upon the participle in the passive. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐσαγγελθέντων ὅτι Φοίνισσαι νῆες ἐπ᾽ αὐτοὺς πλέουσιν</span> _it having been announced that Phoenician ships were sailing against them_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.116">T. 1.116</cite>, <span lang="grc">δηλωθέντος ὅτι ἐν ταῖς ναυσὶ τῶν Ἑλλήνων τὰ πρά̄γματα ἐγένετο</span> _it having been shown that the salvation of the Greeks depended on their navy_ 1. 74. The plural is used when the subject of the subordinate clause is plural, or when several circumstances are mentioned.

##### <a id="s2073"></a> §2073

Exceptionally, the subject of the genitive absolute is the same as that of the main clause. The effect of this irregular construction is to emphasize the idea contained in the genitive absolute. Thus, <span lang="grc">βοηθησάντων ὑ̄μῶν προθύ̄μως πόλιν προσλήψεσθε ναυτικὸν ἔχουσαν μέγα</span> _if you assist us heartily, you will gain to your cause a State having a large navy_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.13">T. 3.13</cite>. The genitive absolute usually precedes the main verb.

**a.** The genitive absolute may be used where the grammatical construction demands the dative. Thus, <span lang="grc">διαβεβηκότος Περικλέους . . . ἠγγέλθη αὐτῷ ὅτι Μέγαρα ἀφέστηκε</span> _when Pericles had already crossed over, news was brought to him that Megara had revolted_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.114">T. 1.114</cite> (in Latin: _Pericli iam transgresso nuntiatum est_).

**b.** The subject of the genitive absolute may be identical with the object of the leading verb: <span lang="grc">ἧλθον ἐπὶ τὴν Ἐπίδαυρον ὡς ἐρήμου οὔσης . . . αἱρήσοντες</span> _they came against Epidaurus expecting to capture it undefended_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.56">T. 5.56</cite>.

##### <a id="s2074"></a> §2074

Observe that the genitive absolute differs from the Latin ablative abso<!-- page 461 --> lute herein: 1. The subject need not be expressed ([2072](#s2072)). 2. The subject _may_ appear in the leading clause ([2073 a](#s2073)). 3. With a substantive the participle <span lang="grc">ὤν</span> is always added in prose, whereas Latin has to omit the participle. Thus, <span lang="grc">παίδων ὄντων ἡμῶν</span> _nobis pueris_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 173a">P. S. 173a</cite>. On <span lang="grc">ἐμοῦ ἄ̄κοντος</span> _me invito_, see [2071](#s2071). 4. Because it has a present participle passive and an aorist and perfect participle active, Greek can use the genitive absolute where Latin, through lack of a past participle active, has to use a clause with _dum_, _cum_, etc. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὅλης τῆς πόλεως ἐν τοῖς πολεμικοῖς κινδύ̄νοις ἐπιτρεπομένης τῷ στρατηγῷ</span> _cum bellicis in periculis universa respublica imperatori committatur_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.1.3">X. M. 3.1.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦ παιδὸς γελάσαντος</span> _cum puer risisset_. Latin uses the absolute case more frequently than Greek because it employs the perfect participle passive where Greek uses the aorist participle active. Thus, <span lang="grc">Κῦρος συγκαλέσᾱς τοὺς στρατηγοὺς εἶπεν</span> _Cyrus_, _convocatis ducibus, dixit_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.8">X. A. 1.4.8</cite>.

##### <a id="s2075"></a> §2075

The genitive absolute took its rise from such cases as <span lang="grc">Σαρπήδοντι δ᾽ ἄχος γένετο Γλαύκου ἀπιόντος</span> _but sorrow came on Sarpedon for Glaucus—departing_ M 392. The genitive, here properly dependent on <span lang="grc">ἄχος γένετο</span>, ceased to be felt as dependent on the governing expression, and was extended, as a distinct construction, to cases in which the governing expression did not take the genitive. Cp. the development of the accusative with the infinitive ([1981](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1981)).

##### <a id="s2076"></a> §2076

**Accusative Absolute**.—A participle stands in the accusative absolute, instead of the genitive, when it is impersonal, or has an infinitive as its subject (as under C). When impersonal, such participles have no apparent grammatical connection with the rest of the sentence.

A. Impersonal verbs: <span lang="grc">δέον, ἐξόν, μετόν, παρόν, προσῆκον, μέλον, μεταμέλον, παρέχον, παρασχόν, τυχόν, δοκοῦν, δόξαν</span>, or <span lang="grc">δόξαντα</span> (<span lang="grc">ταῦτα</span>), <span lang="grc">γενόμενον ἐπ᾽ ἐμοί</span> _as it was in my power_.

<span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς τὸ μεῖζον κακὸν αἱρήσεται ἐξὸν τὸ ἔλᾱττον</span> (<span lang="grc">αἱρεῖσθαι</span>) _no one will choose the greater evil when it is possible to choose the less_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 358d">P. Pr. 358d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἧς</span> (<span lang="grc">βουλῆς</span>) <span lang="grc">νῦν ἀξιοῖ τυχεῖν οὐ μετὸν αὐτῷ</span> _to which he now claims admission though he has no right_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 31.32">L. 31.32</cite>, <span lang="grc">δῆλον γὰρ ὅτι οἶσθα μέλον γέ σοι</span> _for of course you know because it concerns you_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 24d">P. A. 24d</cite>, <span lang="grc">μετεμέλοντο ὅτι μετὰ τὰ ἐν Πύλῳ, καλῶς παρασχόν, οὐ ξυνέβησαν</span> _they repented that after what had occurred at Pylos_, _although a favourable occasion had presented itself, they had not come to terms_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.14">T. 5.14</cite>. Cp. [2086 d](#s2086), [2087](#s2087).

N.—Apart from <span lang="grc">δόξαν, τυχόν</span>, the accusative absolute of the aorist participle of impersonal verbs is very rare.

B. Passive participles used impersonally: <span lang="grc">γεγραμμένον, δεδογμένον, εἰρημένον, προσταχθέν, προστεταγμένον</span>. Cp. Eng. _granted this is so, this done_, _which said_.

<span lang="grc">εἰρημένον δ᾽ αὐταῖς ἀπαντᾶν ἐνθάδε . . . εὕδουσι κοὐχ ἥκουσιν</span> _though it was told them to meet here, they sleep and have not come_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Lys. 13">Ar. Lys. 13</cite>, <span lang="grc">προσταχθέν μοι ὑπὸ τοῦ δήμου Μένωνα ἄγειν εἰς Ἑλλήσποντον</span> _a command having been given_ (_it having been commanded_) _me by the people to convey Menon to the Hellespont_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 50.12">D. 50.12</cite>. <!-- page 462 -->

N.—The aorist participle passive is rarely used absolutely: <span lang="grc">ἀμεληθέν, ἀπορρηθέν, καταχειροτονηθέν, κῡρωθέν, ὁρισθέν, περανθέν, προσταχθέν, χρησθέν</span>.

C. Adjectives with <span lang="grc">ὄν</span>: <span lang="grc">ἄδηλον ὄν, δυνατὸν ὄν, ἀδύνατον ὄν, αἰσχρὸν ὄν, καλὸν ὄν, χρεών</span> (<span lang="grc">χρεώ ¨ ὄν</span>), etc.

<span lang="grc">σὲ οὐχὶ ἐσώσαμεν . . . οἷόν τε ὂν καὶ δυνατόν</span> _we did not rescue you although it was both feasible and possible_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 46a">P. Cr. 46a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡς οὐκ ἀναγκαῖον</span> (<span lang="grc">ὄν</span>) <span lang="grc">τὸ κλέπτειν, αἰτιᾷ τὸν κλέπτοντα</span> _on the ground that stealing is not necessary you accuse the thief_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.1.13">X. C. 5.1.13</cite>.

##### <a id="s2077"></a> §2077

The impersonal character of the above expressions would not be shown by the genitive since the participle in that case marks a distinction between masculine (neuter) and feminine. The accusative absolute, which occurs first in Herodotus and the Attic prose writers of the fifth century, is probably in its origin an internal accusative, developed, at least in part, by way of apposition ([991](10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s991)-[994](10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s994)), the neuter of a participle or of an adjective standing in apposition to an idea in the leading clause. Thus, <span lang="grc">προσταχθὲν αὐτοῖς οὐκ ἐτόλμησαν εἰσαγαγεῖν</span> (<cite data-ref="Isaeus 1.22">Is. 1.22</cite>) _they did not dare to bring_ him _in_—a duty _that was enjoined_ (_although it was enjoined_) _upon them_. Cp. <span lang="grc">πείθει δ᾽ Ὀρέστην μητέρα . . . κτεῖναι, πρὸς οὐχ ἅπαντας εὔκλειαν φέρον</span> _he persuaded Orestes to slay his mother_, a deed _that brings not glory in the eyes of all_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Orest. 30">E. Or. 30</cite>.

##### <a id="s2078"></a> §2078

The participle of a _personal_ verb may be used absolutely if it is preceded by <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> or <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ηὔχετο πρὸς τοὺς θεοὺς τἀ̄γαθὰ διδόναι, ὡς τοὺς θεοὺς κάλλιστα εἰδότας ὁποῖα ἀγαθά ἐστι</span> (Socrates) _prayed to the gods that they would give him good things, in the belief that the gods know best what sort of things are good_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.3.2">X. M. 1.3.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">σιωπῇ ἐδείπνουν, ὤσπερ τοῦτο προστεταγμένον αὐτοῖς</span> _they were supping in silence just as if this had been enjoined upon them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 1">X. S. 1</cite>. 11.

**a.** Cases without <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> or <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ</span> are rare. Thus, <span lang="grc">δόξαντα ὑ̄μῖν ταῦτα εἵλεσθε ἄνδρας εἴκοσι</span> _on reaching this conclusion you chose twenty men_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.81">And. 1.81</cite>; cp. <span lang="grc">δόξαν ταῦτα</span> <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.1.13">X. A. 4.1.13</cite> (by analogy to <span lang="grc">ἔδοξε ταῦτα</span>) and <span lang="grc">δοξάντων τούτων</span> <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.7.30">X. H. 1.7.30</cite>. Neuter participles so used come chiefly from impersonal verbs, but <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.125">T. 4.125</cite> has <span lang="grc">κῡρωθὲν οὐδὲν οἱ Μακεδόνες ἐχώρουν ἐπ᾽ οἴκου</span> _the Macedonians proceeded homewards, nothing having been accomplished_. The neuter subject is a pronoun, very rarely a substantive (<cite data-ref="Isoc. 5.12">I. 5.12</cite>).

### ADVERBS USED IN CONNECTION WITH CIRCUMSTANTIAL PARTICIPLES

##### <a id="s2079"></a> §2079

Adverbs are often used to set forth clearly the relations of time, manner, cause, concession, etc., that are implied in the participle. They occur also with the genitive and accusative absolute. These adverbs modify either the principal verb or the participle itself.

#### ADVERBIAL ADJUNCTS OF THE PRINCIPAL VERB

##### <a id="s2080"></a> §2080

The adverbs <span lang="grc"> **ἔπειτα** </span> _thereupon_, <span lang="grc">**τότε**, **εἶτα**</span> (less often <span lang="grc">ἐνταῦθα</span>) _then_, <span lang="grc"> **ἤδη** </span> _already_, <span lang="grc"> **οὕτω** </span> _so_, when used with the verb of the sentence which contains a temporal participle, emphasize the temporal relation: (<span lang="grc">ὑ̄μῶν δέομαι</span>) <span lang="grc">ἀκροᾱσαμένους διὰ τέλους τῆς ἀπολογίᾱς τότε ἤδη ψηφίζεσθαι κτλ</span>. (_I beg you_) _when you have heard my defence to the end, then and not till then to vote_, etc. <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.9">And. 1.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπὲρ μεγίστων</span> <!-- page 463 --> <span lang="grc">καὶ καλλίστων κινδῡνεύσαντες οὕτω τὸν βίον ἐτελεύτησαν</span> _they incurred danger for a great and noble cause, and so ended their lives_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 2.79">L. 2.79</cite>.

##### <a id="s2081"></a> §2081

<span lang="grc"> **ἅμα** </span> _at the same time_, <span lang="grc"> **αὐτίκα** </span> _immediately_, <span lang="grc"> **εὐθύς** </span> _straightway_, <span lang="grc"> **μεταξύ** </span> _between_, _in the midst_, though strictly modifying the main verb, are often placed close to a temporal participle which they modify _in sense:_ <span lang="grc">ἅμα ταῦτ᾽ εἰπὼν ἀνέστη</span> _saying this, he rose_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.47">X. A. 3.1.47</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῷ δεξιῷ κέρᾳ τῶν Ἀθηναίων εὐθὺς ἀποβεβηκότι . . . ἐπέκειντο</span> _they fell upon the right wing of the Athenians as soon as it had disembarked_ (lit. _upon the right wing when it had disembarked_) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.43">T. 4.43</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐξαναστάντες μεταξὺ δειπνοῦντες</span> _getting up in the middle of supper_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.169">D. 18.169</cite>, <span lang="grc">πολλαχοῦ με ἐπέσχε λέγοντα μεταξύ</span> _it often checked me when the words were on my lips_ (in the very act of speaking) <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 40b">P. A. 40b</cite>.

##### <a id="s2082"></a> §2082

A participle implying opposition or concession ([2066](#s2066)) may have its meaning rendered explicit by <span lang="grc"> **ὅμως** </span> _yet_, _nevertheless_ (with or without <span lang="grc">καίπερ</span>, [2083](#s2083)), <span lang="grc"> **εἶτα** </span> _then_ or <span lang="grc"> **ἔπειτα** </span> _afterwards_ to express censure or surprise (_then_, _for all that_): <span lang="grc">σὺν σοὶ ὅμως καὶ ἐν τῇ πολεμίᾳ ὄντες θαρροῦμεν</span> _with you, though we are in the enemies' country_, _nevertheless we have no fear_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.1.26">X. C. 5.1.26</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔπειτ᾽ ἀπολιπὼν τοὺς θεοὺς ἐνθάδε μενεῖς</span>; _and then, though you desert the gods, will you remain here?_ Ar. Pl. 1148. <span lang="grc">ὅμως</span> may attach itself more closely to the participle, though belonging with the principal verb: <span lang="grc">πείθου γυναιξί, καίπερ οὐ στέργων ὅμως</span> _take the advice of women none the less though thou likest it not_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Seven 712">A. Sept. 712</cite>.

##### <a id="s2083"></a> §2083

With participles of opposition or concession ([2066](#s2066)): <span lang="grc"> **καίπερ** </span> _although_, <span lang="grc"> **καί** </span> (infrequent), _although_ <span lang="grc"> **καὶ ταῦτα** </span> ([947](09-the-predicate.md#s947)) _and that too_. Thus, <span lang="grc">συμβουλεύω σοι καίπερ νεώτερος ὤν</span> _I give you advice though I am your junior_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.5.32">X. C. 4.5.32</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀποπλεῖ οἴκαδε καίπερ μέσου χειμῶνος ὄντος</span> _he sailed off home though it was midwinter_ X. Ag. 2. 31, <span lang="grc">Κλέωνος καίπερ μανιώδης οὖσα ἡ ὑπόσχεσις ἀπέβη</span> _Cleon's promise, insane though it was, was fulfilled_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.39">T. 4.39</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ δοῦλος ὤν γὰρ τί̄μιος πλουτῶν ἀνήρ</span> _for_, _slave though he be, the man of wealth is held in esteem_ E. fr. 142, <span lang="grc">ἀδικεῖς ὅτι ἄνδρα ἡμῖν τὸν σπουδαιότατον διαφθείρεις γελᾶν ἀναπείθων, καὶ ταῦτα οὕτω πολέμιον ὄντα τῷ γέλωτι</span> _you do wrong in that you corrupt the most earnest man we have by tempting him to laugh, and that though he is such an enemy to laughter_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.2.16">X. C. 2.2.16</cite>. On <span lang="grc">καίτοι</span> see [2893 b](36-particles/19-kappaalphaiotatauomicroniota.md#s2893).

**a.** In Homer the parts of <span lang="grc">καίπερ</span> are often separated by the participle or an emphatic word connected with it: <span lang="grc">καὶ ἀχνύμενοί περ</span> _although distressed_ M 178. <span lang="grc">πέρ</span> may stand alone without <span lang="grc">καί</span>: <span lang="grc">ἀνάσχεο κηδομένη περ</span> _bear up, though vexed_ A 586. Both uses occur in tragedy. The part. with <span lang="grc">πέρ</span> is not always concessive.

**b.** In a negative sentence, <span lang="grc"> **οὐδέ** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **μηδέ** </span>), with or without <span lang="grc">πέρ</span>, takes the place of <span lang="grc">καί</span>; as <span lang="grc">γυναικὶ πείθου μηδὲ τἀ̄ληθῆ κλύων</span> _listen to a woman, though thou hearest not the truth_ E. fr. 440.

##### <a id="s2084"></a> §2084

With participles of cause ([2064](#s2064)): <span lang="grc">**οὕτως**, **διὰ τοῦτο**</span> (<span lang="grc"> **ταῦτα** </span>), <span lang="grc"> **ἐκ τούτου** </span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀνελόμενοι τὰ ναυά̄για . . . καὶ ὅτι αὐτοῖς . . . οὐκ ἀντεπέπλεον, διὰ ταῦτα τροπαῖον ἔστησαν</span> _because they had picked up the wrecks and because they_ (the enemy) _did not sail against them_, (_for this reason_) _they set up a trophy_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.54">T. 1.54</cite>.

##### <a id="s2085"></a> §2085

With participles of cause ([2064](#s2064)): <span lang="grc"> **ἅτε** </span> (<span lang="grc">ἅτε δη</span>), <span lang="grc"> **οἷα** </span> or <span lang="grc"> **οἷον** </span> (<span lang="grc">οἷον δή</span>) _inasmuch as_, state the cause as a fact on the authority of the speaker or writer. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁ Κῦρος, ἅτε παῖς ὤν, . . . ἥδετο τῇ στολῇ</span> _Cyrus_, _inasmuch as he was a child_, _was pleased with the robe_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.3">X. C. 1.3.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἥκομεν ἑσπέρᾱς ἀπὸ τοῦ στρατοπέδου</span>, <!-- page 464 --> <span lang="grc">οἷον δὲ διὰ χρόνου ἀφῑγμένος ᾖ ἐπὶ τὰ̄ς συνήθεις διατριβά̄ς</span> _I returned in the evening from the camp, and_, _as I arrived after a long absence_, _I proceeded to my accustomed haunts_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Charm. 153a">P. Charm. 153a</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἷα δὴ ἀπιόντων πρὸς δεῖπνον . . . τῶν πελταστῶν, . . . ἐπελαύνουσι</span> _inasmuch as the peltasts were going off to supper, they rode against them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 5.4.39">X. H. 5.4.39</cite>. <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> has the same force in Hdt.

##### <a id="s2086"></a> §2086

With participles of cause or purpose, etc. ([2064](#s2064), [2065](#s2065)): <span lang="grc"> **ὡς** </span>. This particle sets forth the ground of belief on which the agent acts, and denotes the thought, assertion, real or presumed intention, in the mind of the subject of the principal verb or of some other person mentioned prominently in the sentence, without implicating the speaker or writer.

**a.** Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀπῆλθον ὡς νῑκήσαντες</span> may mean either _they departed under the impression that they had been victorious_ (though as a matter of fact they may have been defeated) or _pretending that they had been victorious_ (when they knew they had been defeated). The use of <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> implies nothing as to the opinion of the speaker or writer. On the other hand <span lang="grc">ἀπῆλθον νῑκήσαντες</span> means that, as a matter of fact, and on the authority of the writer, they _had_ been victorious.

**b.** <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> may be rendered _as if_ (though there is nothing conditional in the Greek use, as is shown by the negative <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>, not <span lang="grc">μή</span>), by _in the opinion_ (_belief_) _that_, _on the ground that, under pretence of, under the impression that, because as he said_ (or _thought_)_; in the hope of, with the_ (_avowed_) _intention of_ (with the future participle).

**c.** <span lang="grc">ἐνταῦθ᾽ ἔμενον ὡς τὸ ἄκρον κατέχοντες · οἱ δ᾽ ου᾽ κατεῖχον, ἀλλὰ μαστὸς ἦν ὑπὲρ αὐτῶν</span> _there they remained in the belief that they were occupying the summit; but in fact they were not occupying it, since there was a hill above them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.2.5">X. A. 4.2.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ταύτην τὴν χώρᾱν ἐπέτρεψε διαρπάσαι τοῖς Ἕλλησιν ὡς πολεμίᾱν οὖσαν</span> _he turned this country over to the Greeks to ravage on the ground that it was hostile_ 1. 2. 19, <span lang="grc">τὴν πρόφασιν ἐποιεῖτο ὡς Πῑσίδᾱς βουλόμενος ἐκβαλεῖν</span> _he made his pretence as if he wished_ (i.e. _he gave as his pretext his desire_) _to expel the Pisidians_ 1. 2. 1, <span lang="grc">παρεσκευάζοντο ὡς πολεμήσοντες</span> _they made preparations to go to war_ (_with the avowed intention of going to war_) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.7">T. 2.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">συλλαμβάνει Κῦρον ὡς ἀποκτενῶν</span> _he seized Cyrus for the purpose_ (_as he declared_) _of putting him to death_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.3">X. A. 1.1.3</cite>, and often with the future participle. After _verbs of motion_ <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> is rarely used.

**d.** <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> with the absolute participle: <span lang="grc">οὐ δεῖ ἀθῡμεῖν ὡς οὐκ εὐτάκτων ὄντων Ἀθηναίων</span> _we must not be discouraged on the ground that the Athenians are not well disciplined_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.5.20">X. M. 3.5.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔλεγε θαρρεῖν ὡς καταστησομένων τούτων ἐς τὸ δέον</span> _he bade him be of good cheer in the assurance that this would arrange itself in the right way_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.8">X. A. 1.3.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡς ἐξὸν ἤδη ποιεῖν αὐτοῖς ὅ τι βούλοιντο, πολλοὺς ἀπέκτεινον</span> _in the belief that it was already in their power to do what they pleased, they put many to death_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.3.21">X. H. 2.3.21</cite>. Cp. also 2078, and 2122.

##### <a id="s2087"></a> §2087

<span lang="grc"> **ὥσπερ** </span> _as_, _just as, as it were_, an adverb of comparison, denotes that the action of the main verb is compared with an assumed case. Thus, <span lang="grc">κατακείμεθ᾽ ὥσπερ ἐξὸν ἡσυχίᾱν ἄγειν</span> _we lie inactive just as if it were possible to take one's ease_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.3">X. A. 3.1.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὠρχοῦντο . . . ὥσπερ ἐπιδεικνύμενοι</span> _they danced as it were making an exhibition_ 5. 4. 34, <span lang="grc">οἱ δὲ ὡς ἤκουσαν, ὥσπερ συὸς ἀγρίου φανέντος, ἵ̄ενται ἐπ᾽ αὐτόν</span> _but when they heard him, just as though a wild boar had appeared_, _they rushed against him_ 5. 7. 24. Cp. [2078](#s2078). <!-- page 465 -->

**a.** Where a condition is meant, we have <span lang="grc"> **ὥσπερ ἂν εἰ** </span> (<span lang="grc">ὡσπερανεί</span>). Cp. [2480 a](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/13-clauses-of-comparison.md#s2480).

**b.** Hom. uses <span lang="grc">ὥς τε, ὡς εἰ, ὡς εἴ τε</span> like <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ</span> or <span lang="grc">ὡς. ὡς εἰ, ὡς εἴ τε</span> occur also in tragedy, and do not have a conditional force. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὀλοφῡρόμενοι ὡς εἰ θανατόνδε κιόντα</span> _bewailing him as if he were going to death_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 24.328"><span lang="grc">Ω</span> 328</cite>. Cp. [2481](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/13-clauses-of-comparison.md#s2481).

## THE SUPPLEMENTARY PARTICIPLE

##### <a id="s2088"></a> §2088

The supplementary participle completes the idea of the verb by showing that to which its action relates.

##### <a id="s2089"></a> §2089

The supplementary participle agrees either with the subject or with the object of the main verb; with the subject when the verb is intransitive or passive, with the object when the verb is transitive.

<span lang="grc">οὔποτ᾽ ἐπαυόμην ἡμᾶς οἰκτί̄ρων</span> _I never ceased pitying ourselves_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.19">X. A. 3.1.19</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοὺς πένητας ἔπαυσ᾽ ἀδικουμένους</span> _I put a stop to the poor being wronged_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.102">D. 18.102</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἑώρων οὐ κατορθοῦντες καὶ τοὺς στρατιώτᾱς ἀχθομένους</span> _they saw that they_ (_themselves_) _were not succeeding and that the soldiers were indignant_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.47">T. 7.47</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀδικοῦντα φίλιππον ἐξήλεγξα</span> _I proved that Philip was acting unjustly_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.136">D. 18.136</cite>, <span lang="grc">εὐθὺς ἐλεγχθήσεται γελοῖος ὤν</span> _he will straightway be proved to be ridiculous_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.7.2">X. M. 1.7.2</cite>.

**a.** When the object is the same as the subject, it is commonly suppressed, and the participle agrees with the subject. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁρῶ ἐξαμαρτάνων</span> _I see that I err_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 350">E. Med. 350</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἴσθι ἀνόητος ὤν</span> _know that you are a fool_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.13">X. A. 2.1.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ αἰσθάνεσθε ἐξαπατώμενοι</span>; _do you not perceive that you are being deceived?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 7.1.12">X. H. 7.1.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐδήλωσε τῶν νόμων καταφρονῶν</span> _he showed that he despised the laws_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 4.14">And. 4.14</cite>.

**b.** For the sake of emphasis or contrast (and to secure greater symmetry) the object may be expressed by the reflexive pronoun. Thus, <span lang="grc">οἶδα ἐμαυτὸν δικαίως κεχρημένον αὐτοῖς</span> _I know that I have presented my case honestly_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 15.321">I. 15.321</cite>, <span lang="grc">δεῖξον οὐ πεποιηκότα ταῦτα σαυτόν</span> _show that you did not do this yourself_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 22.29">D. 22.29</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀμφότερ᾽ οὖν οἶδε, καὶ αὑτὸν ὑ̄μῖν ἐπιβουλεύοντα, καὶ ὑ̄μᾶς αἰσθανομένους</span> _now he knows both—that he is himself plotting against you and that you are aware of it_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 6.18">D. 6.18</cite>. Observe <span lang="grc">ἐλάνθανον αὑτοὺς ἐπὶ τῷ λόφῳ γενόμενοι</span> (agreeing with the subject) _without knowing it they found themselves on the hill_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.3.22">X. A. 6.3.22</cite>. On the use with <span lang="grc">σύνοιδα</span>, see [2108](#s2108).

**c.** <span lang="grc"> **ἔοικα** </span> (the personal use for the impersonal <span lang="grc">ἔοικε</span>, [1983](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1983)) usually takes the participle in the dative; as, <span lang="grc">ἔοικας ὀκνοῦντι λέγειν</span> _you seem reluctant to speak_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 414c">P. R. 414c</cite>; but also in the nominative (see [2133](#s2133)).

##### <a id="s2090"></a> §2090

Many verbs supplementing their meaning by the participle admit of the construction with the infinitive (often with a difference of meaning; see [2123](#s2123) ff.) or with a substantive clause with <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> or <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>.

##### <a id="s2091"></a> §2091

The present or perfect participle is often used as a simple predicate adjective, especially with <span lang="grc">εἰμί</span> and <span lang="grc">γίγνομαι</span>. The aorist participle is chiefly poetic.

<span lang="grc">ἦσαν ἀπιστοῦντές τινες φιλίππῳ</span> _there were some who distrusted Philip_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.53">D. 19.53</cite>, (<span lang="grc">Κλέαρχος</span>) <span lang="grc">φιλοκίνδῡνός τ᾽ ἦν καὶ ἡμέρᾱς καὶ νυκτὸς ἄγων ἐπὶ τοὺς πολεμίους</span> Clearchus _was both fond of danger and by day and by night led_ his men _against the enemy_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.6.7">X. A. 2.6.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐγὼ τὸ πρᾶγμ᾽ εἰμὶ τοῦθ᾽ ὁ δεδρᾱκώς</span> _I am the one who has done_ <!-- page 466 --> _this deed_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.104">D. 21.104</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἢ τοῦτο οὐκ ἔστι γιγνόμενον παρ᾽ ἡμῖν</span>; _or is not this something that takes place in us?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phileb. 39c">P. Phil. 39c</cite>. So with adjectivized participles ([1857](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1857)), as <span lang="grc">συμφέρον ἦν τῇ πόλει</span> _it was advantageous to the State_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.75">D. 19.75</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">ὑπάρχω</span> _am_, _am assumed_ (<cite data-ref="Dem. 18.228">D. 18.228</cite>).

**a.** Here the participle has the article when it designates the subject itself (third example; cp. [1152](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1152)). But the article is not used when the participle marks a class in which the subject is included.

##### <a id="s2092"></a> §2092

The supplementary participle after certain verbs represents a dependent statement.

In <span lang="grc">ἤκουσε Κῦρον ἐν Κιλικίᾳ ὄντα</span> _he heard that Cyrus was in Cilicia_ <span lang="grc">ὄντα</span> stands for <span lang="grc">ἐστί</span>, what was heard being “<span lang="grc">Κῦρος ἐν Κιλικίᾳ ἐστί</span>.” This is shown by the fact that the sentence might have been, according to the principles of _indirect discourse_, <span lang="grc">ἤκουσεν ὅτι Κῦρος ἐν Κιλικίᾳ εἴη</span> (or <span lang="grc">ἐστί</span>, [2615](31-dependent-substantive-clauses-2574-2635.md#s2615)). With verbs not introducing indirect discourse, however, there is no such indirect statement; as in <span lang="grc">ἐπαύσαντο μαχόμενοι</span> _they ceased fighting_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 23.9">L. 23.9</cite>.

##### <a id="s2093"></a> §2093

Accordingly, from this point of view, the uses of the supplementary participle are two: (1) not in indirect discourse, and (2) in indirect discourse.

**a.** Some verbs take the participle _either_ in indirect discourse _or_ not in indirect discourse ([2112](#s2112)). It is sometimes impossible to decide whether a participle stands in indirect discourse or not ([2113](#s2113)); and the difference, especially after _verbs of perceiving_ ([2112 a](#s2112), b), may be of no great importance to the sense.

### THE SUPPLEMENTARY PARTICIPLE NOT IN INDIRECT DISCOURSE

##### <a id="s2094"></a> §2094

The supplementary participle not in indirect discourse is often like an object infinitive, the tenses denoting only stage of action and not difference of time (cp. [1850](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1850)). Thus, compare <span lang="grc">παύομέν σε λέγοντα</span> _we stop you from speaking_ (of continued action) with <span lang="grc">κωλύ̄ομέν σε λέγειν</span> _we prevent you from speaking_ (also of continued action).

##### <a id="s2095"></a> §2095

With verbs denoting _being_ in some modified way ([2096-2097](#s2096)).

##### <a id="s2096"></a> §2096

<span lang="grc"> **τυγχάνω** </span> (poet. <span lang="grc">κυρῶ</span>) _happen_, _am just now_, <span lang="grc"> **λανθάνω** </span> _escape the notice of_, _am secretly_, <span lang="grc"> **φθάνω** </span> _anticipate_, _am beforehand_.

**a.** With these verbs the participle contains the main idea, and is often represented in translation by the finite verb with an adverbial phrase; thus, <span lang="grc">παρὼν ἐτύγχανε</span> _he happened to be there_, or _he was there by chance_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.2">X. A. 1.1.2</cite>.

**b.** The action of <span lang="grc">φθάνω</span> and <span lang="grc">λανθάνω</span> usually coincides with that of the supplementary participle (present with present, aorist with aorist). But the aorist of a finite verb is occasionally followed by the present participle when it is necessary to mark an action or a state as continuing. <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔλαθον</span> is like an imperfect and may take the present participle. The aorist of <span lang="grc">τυγχάνω</span> very often takes the present participle. With a present or imperfect of <span lang="grc">τυγχάνω, λανθάνω, φθάνω</span>, the (rare) aorist participle refers to an action or state anterior to that of the present or imperfect. Many of the cases of the present of <span lang="grc">τυγχάνω</span> with the <!-- page 467 --> aorist participle are historical presents; and in some cases the aorist participle is used for the perfect. With other tenses than present or imperfect, an aorist participle with these verbs refers to an action or state coincident in time (cp. [1873](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1873)).

**c.** <span lang="grc">τυγχάνω</span> often loses the idea of _chance_, and denotes mere coincidence in time (_I am just now_, _I was just then_) or simply _I am_ (_was_).

**d.** Examples. <span lang="grc"> **τυγχάνω** </span>: <span lang="grc">προξενῶν τυγχάνω</span> _I happen to be proxenus_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 52.5">D. 52.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄριστα τυγχάνουσι πρά̄ξαντες</span> _they happen to have fared the best_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.103">I. 4.103</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐτύγχανον λέγων</span> _I was just saying_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.10">X. A. 3.2.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅστις ἀντειπών γε ἐτύγχανε καὶ γνώμην ἀποδεδειγμένος</span> _who happened to have spoken in opposition and to have declared his opinion_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.27">L. 12.27</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔτυχον καθήμενος ἐντ**α**ῦθα</span> _I was_, _by chance_, _sitting there_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyd. 272e">P. Eu. 272e</cite>. <span lang="grc"> **λανθάνω** </span>: <span lang="grc">φονέα τοῦ παιδὸς ἐλάνθανε βόσκων</span> _he entertained the murderer of his son without knowing it_ (_it escaped his notice that he was_, etc.) <cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.44">Hdt. 1.44</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔλαθον ἐσελθόντες</span> _they got in secretly_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.2">T. 2.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔλαθες ἀποδιδρά̄σκων</span> _you did not escape notice in attempting to escape_ (_your attempt at escape did not escape notice_) <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 457e">P. R. 457e</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔλαθεν ἀποδρά̄ς</span> _he escaped without being noticed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.3.22">X. H. 1.3.22</cite>, <span lang="grc">λήσετε πάνθ᾽ ὑπομείναντες</span> _you will submit to every possible calamity ere you are aware_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 6.27">D. 6.27</cite>. <span lang="grc"> **φθάνω** </span>: <span lang="grc">οὐ φθάνει ἐξαγόμενος ὁ ἵππος κτλ</span>. _the horse is no sooner led out_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Horse. 5.10">X. Eq. 5.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">φθάνουσιν</span> (hist. pres.) <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ τῷ ἄκρῳ γενόμενοι τοὺς πολεμίους</span> _they anticipated the enemy in getting upon the summit_ (_they got to the summit before the enemy_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Ph. 107c</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀδικοῦντα</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἀδικεῖ</span>) <span lang="grc">φίλιππον ἐξήλεγξα</span> _I convicted Philip of acting unjustly_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.136">D. 18.136</cite>, <span lang="grc">ῥᾳδίως ἐλεγχθήσεται ψευδόμενος</span> (= <span lang="grc">ψεύδεται</span>) _he will easily be convicted of lying_ 27. 19, <span lang="grc">ὁμολογούμεθα ἐλθόντες</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἤλθομεν</span>) _I acknowledge that I came_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 4.7">L. 4.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">αὐτῷ Κῦρον ἐπιστρατεύοντα</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἐπιστρατεύει</span>) <span lang="grc">πρῶτος ἤγγειλα</span> _I was the first to announce that Cyrus was taking the field against him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.19">X. A. 2.3.19</cite>.

**a.** Except with <span lang="grc">ἀγγέλλω</span> _announce_ (_what is certain_), _verbs of saying_ or _thinking_ rarely take the participle in prose, e.g. <span lang="grc">πᾶσι ταῦτα δεδογμένα ἡμῖν νόμιζε</span> (= <span lang="grc">εὖ ἴσθι</span>) _think that this is our unanimous opinion_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 450a">P. R. 450a</cite>.

##### <a id="s2107"></a> §2107

The personal constructions <span lang="grc">**δῆλός εἰμι**, **φανερός εἰμι**</span> _I am plainly_ (impersonal <span lang="grc">δῆλόν</span> and <span lang="grc">φανερόν ἐστιν ὅτι</span>) are followed by a dependent statement in the participle. Thus, <span lang="grc">δῆλος ἦν οἰόμενος</span> (= <span lang="grc">δῆλον ἦν ὅτι οἴοιτο</span>) _it was clear that he thought_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.27">X. A. 2.5.27</cite>, <span lang="grc">θύ̄ων φανερὸς ἦν πολλάκις</span> (= <span lang="grc">φανερὸν ἦν ὅτι θύ̄οι</span>) _it was_ <!-- page 471 --> _evident that he often sacrificed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.1.2">X. M. 1.1.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀνιᾱθεὶς δῆλος ἦν</span> (= <span lang="grc">δῆλον ἦν ὅτι ἀνιᾱθείη</span>) _he showed his dissatisfaction_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.2.3">X. C. 2.2.3</cite>.

##### <a id="s2108"></a> §2108

The participle with <span lang="grc"> **σύνοιδα** </span> or <span lang="grc"> **συγγιγνώσκω** </span> _am conscious_, accompanied by the dative of the reflexive pronoun, may stand either in the nominative agreeing with the subject, or in the dative agreeing with the reflexive. Thus, <span lang="grc">συνειδὼς αὐτὸς αὑτῷ ἔργον εἰργασμένος</span> _conscious_ (to himself) _that he had done the deed_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 6.5">Ant. 6.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐμαυτῷ ξυνῄδη οὐδὲν ἐπισταμένῳ</span> _I was conscious of knowing nothing_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 22c">P. A. 22c</cite>.

**a.** When the subject is not the same as the object, the latter, with the participle, may stand in the dative, or (rarely) in the accusative. Thus, <span lang="grc">ξυνίσᾱσι Μελήτῳ μὲν ψευδομένῳ, ἐμοὶ δὲ ἀληθεύοντι</span> _they know as well as Meletus that he is lying_, _and_ (as well as I do) _that I am speaking the truth_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 34b">P. A. 34b</cite>, <span lang="grc">συνειδὼς τῶν ἀ̄θλημάτων δούλους μετέχοντας</span> _knowing that slaves participate in the contests_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 61.23">D. 61.23</cite>. (The force of <span lang="grc">σύν</span> at times almost disappears.)

##### <a id="s2109"></a> §2109

The use of the participle to represent a dependent statement comes from its circumstantial use. Thus, in <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ ᾔδεσαν αὐτὸν τεθνηκότα</span> ([2106](#s2106)), <span lang="grc">τεθνηκότα</span> agrees with the object of <span lang="grc">ᾔδεσαν</span>; and from _they did not know him as dead_ the thought passes into _they did not know_ (the fact) _that he was dead_.

#### CONSTRUCTION OF VERBS OF PERCEIVING AND OF FINDING

##### <a id="s2110"></a> §2110

**Verbs of Perception**.—Verbs signifying _to see, perceive_, _hear_, _learn_ (i.e. _learn by inquiry, hear of_), when they denote physical (actual) perception take the participle. When they denote intellectual perception they may take the participle or <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> or <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> with a finite verb. (The Homeric usage is less strict.)

##### <a id="s2111"></a> §2111

Such verbs are, in Attic, <span lang="grc"> **ὁρῶ** </span> _see_, <span lang="grc"> **αἰσθάνομαι** </span> _perceive_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀκούω** </span> _hear_, <span lang="grc"> **πυνθάνομαι** </span> _learn_.

##### <a id="s2112"></a> §2112

The participle may stand either not in indirect discourse or in indirect discourse.

**a. Not in Indirect Discourse**.—Here _verbs of perceiving_ denote _physical_ perception—the act perceived or heard of. With <span lang="grc">ἀκου´ω</span> and <span lang="grc">πυνθάνομαι</span> the participle stands in the genitive; with <span lang="grc">αἰσθάνομαι</span> it usually stands in the accusative (as with <span lang="grc">ὁρῶ</span>), but sometimes in the genitive. (See [1361](18-the-cases/04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1361), [1367](18-the-cases/04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1367).)

<span lang="grc">εἶδε Κλέαρχον διελαύνοντα</span> _he saw Clearchus riding through_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.12">X. A. 1.5.12</cite>; <span lang="grc">αἰσθόμενος Λαμπροκλέᾱ πρὸς τὴν μητέρα χαλεπαίνοντα</span> _perceiving Lamprocles angry with his mother_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.2.1">X. M. 2.2.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">ᾔσθησαι πώποτέ μου ἢ ψευδομαρτυροῦντος ἢ σῡκοφαντοῦντος</span>; _have you ever noticed me either bearing false witness or playing the part of an informer?_ 4. 4. 11; <span lang="grc">ἤκουσαν αὐτοῦ φωνήσαντος</span> _they heard him speaking_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 3">X. S. 3</cite>. 13; <span lang="grc">ὡς ἐπύθοντο τῆς Πύλου κατειλημμένης</span> _when they learned of the capture of Pylos_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.6">T. 4.6</cite>.

N. Verbs of physical perception, <span lang="grc">ὁρῶ</span> (especially) and <span lang="grc">ἀκούω</span>, regularly take the present participle in Attic prose, which usually refuses to distinguish between _I see a house burning_ and _I see a house burn_. The complexive aorist, summing up the action, does however occur, as <span lang="grc">ὡς εἶδεν ἐλαφον ἐκπηδήσᾱσαν . . . ἐδίωκεν</span> _when he saw a hind break cover he gave chase_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.8">X. C. 1.4.8</cite>. Cp. <span lang="grc">πεσόντα εἶδον</span> <cite data-ref="Hdt. 9.22">Hdt. 9.22</cite>.

**b. In Indirect Discourse**.—Here _verbs of perceiving_ denote _intellectual_ <!-- page 472 --> perception—the fact that something is perceived or heard of. With <span lang="grc">ἀκούω</span> and <span lang="grc">πυνθάνομαι</span> the participle stands in the accusative (as with <span lang="grc">ὁρῶ, αἰσθάνομαι</span>). Cp. [1363](18-the-cases/04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1363), [1365](18-the-cases/04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1365), [2144](#s2144), [2145](#s2145).

<span lang="grc">ὁρῶμεν πάντα ἀληθῆ ὄντα ἃ λέγετε</span> _we see that everything you say is true_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.5.24">X. A. 5.5.24</cite>, <span lang="grc">αἰσθάνομαι ταῦτα οὕτως ἔχοντα</span> _I perceive that this is so_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.5.5">X. M. 3.5.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἤκουσε Κῦρον ἐν Κιλικίᾳ ὄντα</span> _he heard that Cyrus was in Cilicia_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.5">X. A. 1.4.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅταν κλύῃ τινὸς ἥξοντ᾽ Ὀρέστην</span> _when she hears from any one that Orestes will return_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 293">S. El. 293</cite>, <span lang="grc">πυθόμενοι Ἀρταξέρξην τεθνηκότα</span> _having learned that Artaxerxes was dead_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.50">T. 4.50</cite>.

##### <a id="s2113"></a> §2113

**Verbs of Finding**.—_Verbs of finding_ and _detecting_ (<span lang="grc">εὑρίσκω</span>, (<span lang="grc">κατα</span>)<span lang="grc">λαμβάνω</span>; pass. <span lang="grc">ἁλίσκομαι</span>) in their capacity as _verbs of perceiving_ take the participle (_a_) not in indirect discourse, of the act or state in which a person or thing is found; or (_b_) in indirect discourse, of the fact that a person or thing is found in an act or state.

**a.** <span lang="grc">κῆρυξ ἀφικόμενος ηὗρε τοὺς ἄνδρας διεφθαρμένους</span> _the herald, on his arrival_, _found the men already put to death_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.6">T. 2.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">εὕρηται πιστῶς πρά̄ττων</span> _he has been found to have dealt faithfully_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.332">D. 19.332</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἂ̄ν ἄρ᾽ ἄλλον τινὰ λαμβάνῃ ψευδόμενον</span> _if then he catch anybody else lying_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 389d">P. R. 389d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἢν ἐπιβουλεύων ἁλίσκηται</span> _if he be detected in plotting_ X. Ag. 8. 3.

**b.** <span lang="grc">διὰ τὴν Ἰ̄λίου ἅλωσιν εὑρίσκουσι σφίσι ἐοῦσαν τὴν ἀρχὴν τῆς ἔχθρης</span> _they conclude that the beginning of their enmity was on account of the capture of Ilium_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.5">Hdt. 1.5</cite>.

##### <a id="s2114"></a> §2114

It is often difficult to distinguish the two constructions of 2113. Thus, <span lang="grc">καταλαμβάνουσι νεωστὶ στάσει τοὺς τῶν Ἀθηναίων ἐναντίους ἐκπεπτωκότας</span> (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.33">T. 7.33</cite>) may mean _they found that the anti-Athenian party had been recently expelled by a revolution_ (ind. disc.) or _them recently expelled_ (not in ind. disc.). So <span lang="grc">καταλαμβάνουσι . . . τἆλλα ἀφεστηκότα</span> _they found the other cities in a state of revolt_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.59">T. 1.59</cite> (_that they had revolted_ would be possible). In the meaning _discover_, _find_ <span lang="grc">καταλαμβάνω</span> does not take the aorist participle.

##### <a id="s2115"></a> §2115

<span lang="grc">ποιῶ</span> meaning _represent_ has the construction of the verbs of 2113. Thus, <span lang="grc">πλησιάζοντας τοὺς θεοὺς τοῖς ἀνθρώποις οἷόν τ᾽ αὐτοῖς ποιῆσαι</span> _it is possible for them_ (poets) _to represent the gods as drawing nigh to men_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 9.9">I. 9.9</cite>. Cp. [2142](#s2142).

## OMISSION OF <span lang="grc">ὤν</span>

##### <a id="s2116"></a> §2116

The participle <span lang="grc">ὤν</span> is often omitted.

##### <a id="s2117"></a> §2117

After <span lang="grc">ἅτε, οἷα, ὡς</span>, or <span lang="grc">καίπερ, ὤν</span> is often omitted in prose with predicate adjectives: <span lang="grc">συνδείπνους ἔλαβεν ἀμφοτέρους πρὸς ἑαυτὸν ὡς φίλους ἤδη</span> (<span lang="grc">ὄντας</span>) _he took both to supper with him since they were now friends_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.2.25">X. C. 3.2.25</cite>. Such omission is rare in prose except after these particles: <span lang="grc">εἰ ἥττους</span> (<span lang="grc">ὄντες</span>) <span lang="grc">τῶν πολεμίων ληφθησόμεθα</span> _if we shall be caught at the mercy of our enemies_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.6.13">X. A. 5.6.13</cite>. With predicate substantives, even after these particles, <span lang="grc">ὤν</span> is very rarely omitted (<cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 568b">P. R. 568b</cite>).

**a.** In the genitive and accusative absolute the particles of 2117 usually precede when <span lang="grc">ὤν</span> is omitted. With the genitive absolute the omission is very rare in prose: <span lang="grc">ὡς ἑτοίμων</span> (<span lang="grc">ὄντων</span>) <span lang="grc">χρημάτων</span> _just as though the property was at their_ <!-- page 473 --> _disposal_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.8.11">X. A. 7.8.11</cite>; but <span lang="grc">ἡμέρᾱς ἤδη</span> (<span lang="grc">οὔσης</span>) _it being already day_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.59">T. 5.59</cite>. In poetry the substantive usually suggests the verb: <span lang="grc">ὑφηγητῆρος οὐδενὸς</span> (<span lang="grc">ὄντος</span>) <span lang="grc">φίλων</span> _with no friend to guide_ him <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 1588">S. O. C. 1588</cite>. Accusative absolute: <span lang="grc">ὡς καλὸν</span> (<span lang="grc">ὂν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἀγορεύεσθαι αὐτόν</span> _on the ground that it is admirable for it_ (the speech) _to be delivered_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.35">T. 2.35</cite>. Without the particles of 2117, the omission of <span lang="grc">ὄν</span> is poetical (<cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 44">S. Ant. 44</cite>). The omission of <span lang="grc">ὄν</span> with adjectives ending in <span lang="grc">-ον</span> aids euphony.

**b.** <span lang="grc">ἑκών</span> _willing_, <span lang="grc">ἄ̄κων</span> _unwilling_ are treated like participles ([2071](#s2071)): <span lang="grc">ἐμοῦ μὲν οὐχ ἑκόντος</span> _against my will_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 455">S. Aj. 455</cite>.

**c.** <span lang="grc">ὤν</span> must be used when it has the force of _in the capacity of_.

##### <a id="s2118"></a> §2118

A predicate substantive or adjective, coördinated with a participle in the same construction, may omit <span lang="grc">ὤν</span>; as <span lang="grc">οὐ ῥᾴδιον ἦν μὴ ἁθρόοις καὶ ἀλλήλους περιμείνᾱσι διελθεῖν τὴν πολεμίᾱν</span> _it was not easy for them to pass through the enemy's country except in a body and after having waited for one another_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.64">T. 5.64</cite>.

##### <a id="s2119"></a> §2119

<span lang="grc">ὤν</span> may be omitted with verbs taking a supplementary participle; so with verbs meaning _to perceive_ ([2111 f](#s2111)f.), _know_, _show_, _announce_, _find_, _discover_, etc.; especially with <span lang="grc">φαίνομαι, τυγχάνω</span> (poet. <span lang="grc">κυρῶ</span>), <span lang="grc">διατελῶ, διαγίγνομαι</span>, rarely with <span lang="grc">περιορῶ</span> and <span lang="grc">συμβαίνω</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁρῶ μέγαν</span> (<span lang="grc">ὄντα</span>) <span lang="grc">τὸν ἀγῶνα</span> _I see that the contest is important_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.45">T. 2.45</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἂ̄ν ἐν Χερρονήσῳ πύθησθε Φίλιππον</span> (<span lang="grc">ὄντα</span>) _if you learn that Philip is in Chersonesus_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.41">D. 4.41</cite>, <span lang="grc">εὶ ψευδὴς φαίνοιτο</span> (<span lang="grc">ὤν</span>) <span lang="grc">ὁ Λωβρύᾱς</span> _if Gobryas seem to be false_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.2.4">X. C. 5.2.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἴ τις εὔνους</span> (<span lang="grc">ὤν</span>) <span lang="grc">τυγχάνει</span> _if any one happens to be friendly_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Eccl. 1141">Ar. Eccl. 1141</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀχίτων</span> (<span lang="grc">ὤν</span>) <span lang="grc">διατελεῖς</span> _you are continually without a tunic_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.6.2">X. M. 1.6.2</cite>.

## <span lang="grc">Ὡς</span> WITH A PARTICIPLE IN INDIRECT DISCOURSE

##### <a id="s2120"></a> §2120

<span lang="grc">ὡς</span> is often used with a participle in indirect discourse to mark the mental attitude of the subject of the main verb or of some other person mentioned prominently in the sentence (cp. [2086](#s2086)); sometimes, to denote emphasis, when that mental attitude is already clearly marked.

<span lang="grc">ὡς μηδὲν εἰδότ᾽ ἴσθι με</span> _be assured that I know nothing_ (lit. _understand that you are to assume that I know nothing_) <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 253">S. Ph. 253</cite>, <span lang="grc">δῆλος ἦν Κῦρος ὡς σπεύδων</span> _Cyrus was plainly bent on haste_ (Cyrus showed that it was his intention to make haste) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.9">X. A. 1.5.9</cite>.

##### <a id="s2121"></a> §2121

A participle with <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> may follow a _verb of thinking_ or _saying_ though the verb in question does not take the participle in indirect discourse without <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὡς τὰ βέλτιστα βουλεύοντες ἰ̄σχῡρίζοντο</span> _they kept insisting in the belief that they were recommending the best course_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.68">T. 4.68</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡς στρατηγήσοντ᾽ ἐμὲ ταύτην τὴν στρατηγίᾱν μηδεὶς ὑ̄μῶν λεγέτω</span> _let no one of you say_ (i.e. _speak of me in the belief_) _that I will assume this command_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.15">X. A. 1.3.15</cite>.

##### <a id="s2122"></a> §2122

So after verbs admitting the supplementary participle in indirect discourse we may have the genitive or accusative absolute with <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> instead of the participle or a clause with <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> or <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὡς πολέμου ὄντος παρ᾽ ὑ̄μῶν ἀπαγγελῶ</span>; _shall I report from you_ (_on the assumption_) _that there is war?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.21">X. A. 2.1.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡς ἐμοῦ οὖν ἰόντος, ὅπῃ καὶ ὑ̄μεῖς, οὕτω τὴν γνώμην ἔχετε</span> _make up your minds_ (_on the assumption_) _that I am going wherever you go_ (= _be sure that I am going_, <!-- page 474 --> etc.) 1. 3. 6 (here <span lang="grc">τὴν γνώμην ἔχετε</span> could not take the participle without <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>); <span lang="grc">ὡς πάνυ μοι δοκοῦν, οὕτως ἴσθι</span> _rest assured that it is my decided opinion_ (lit. _on the assumption that this seems so to me, understand accordingly_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.2.30">X. M. 4.2.30</cite>. For <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> with the absolute participle not in indirect discourse, see [2086 d](#s2086).

## VERBS TAKING EITHER THE PARTICIPLE OR THE INFINITIVE

##### <a id="s2123"></a> §2123

Some verbs admit either the supplementary participle or the infinitive, sometimes with only a slight difference in meaning. Cases where the difference is marked are given below. (Most of the verbs in question admit also a substantive clause with <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> or <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>, [2577](31-dependent-substantive-clauses-2574-2635.md#s2577)).

##### <a id="s2124"></a> §2124

Infinitive and participle here differ greatly when the infinitive expresses purpose or result. Where the infinitive shows only its abstract verbal meaning it differs but little from the participle (cp. [2144](#s2144)).

##### <a id="s2125"></a> §2125

A participle or infinitive standing in indirect discourse is indicated in 2126-2143 by O (ratio) O (bliqua); when not standing in O. O. this fact is ordinarily not indicated.

##### <a id="s2126"></a> §2126

<span lang="grc"> **αἰσχύ̄νομαι** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **αἰδοῦμαι** </span> with part. ([2100](#s2100)) = _I am ashamed of_ doing something which I do; with inf. = _I am ashamed to_ do something which I have refrained from doing up to the present time and may never do. Thus, <span lang="grc">τοῦτο μὲν οὐκ αἰσχύ̄νομαι λέγων· τὸ δὲ . . . αἰσχῡνοίμην ἂν λέγειν</span> _I am not ashamed of saying this; but the following I should be ashamed to say_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.1.21">X. C. 5.1.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">αἰσχύ̄νομαι οὖν ὑ̄μῖν εἰπεῖν τἀ̄ληθῆ, ὅμως δὲ ῥητέον</span> _I am ashamed to speak the truth to you; nevertheless it must be spoken_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 22b">P. A. 22b</cite>. With a negative the distinction may disappear: <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽ αἰσχύ̄νει φθόνου δίκην εἰσάγειν</span> (_v.l._ <span lang="grc">εἰσάγων</span>), <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀδικήματος οὐδενός, καὶ νόμους μεταποιῶν</span>; _are you not ashamed to bring a cause into court out of envy—not for any offence—and to alter laws?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.121">D. 18.121</cite>.

##### <a id="s2127"></a> §2127

<span lang="grc"> **ἀνέχομαι** </span> ([2098](#s2098); rarely with the inf.), \*<span lang="grc"> **τλάω** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **τολμῶ** </span> (both rarely with the part. in poetry), <span lang="grc"> **ὑπομένω** </span>: with part. = _endure_, _submit to_ something that is present or past; with inf. _venture_ or _have the courage to_ do something in the future. Thus, <span lang="grc">πάσχοντες ἠνείχοντο</span> _they submitted to suffer_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.77">T. 1.77</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀνέσχοντο τὸν ἐπιόντα ἐπὶ τὴν χώρᾱν δέξασθαι</span> _they had the courage to receive the invader of their country_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 7.139">Hdt. 7.139</cite>; <span lang="grc">παῖδα . . . φᾱσὶν Ἀλκμήνης πρᾱθέντα τλῆναι</span> _they say that Alcmene's son bore up in bondage_ (lit. _having been sold_) <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 1041">A. Ag. 1041</cite>; <span lang="grc">ἐτόλμᾱ βαλλόμενος</span> _he submitted to be struck_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 24.161"><span lang="grc">ω</span> 161</cite>, <span lang="grc">τόλμησον ὀρθῶς φρονεῖν</span> _sapere aude_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 1000">A. Pr. 1000</cite>; <span lang="grc">οὐχ ὑπομένει ὠφελούμενος</span> _he cannot stand being improved_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 505c">P. G. 505c</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ ὑμομενέουσι χεῖρας ἐμοὶ ἀνταειρόμενοι</span> _if they shall dare to raise their hands against me_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 7.101">Hdt. 7.101</cite>.

##### <a id="s2128"></a> §2128

<span lang="grc"> **ἄρχομαι** </span>, cp. [1734](20-the-verb-voices/03-middle-voice.md#s1734) (Hom. <span lang="grc">ἄρχω</span>) with part. ([2098](#s2098)), _begin to do something_ and continue with something else; with inf. (usually present, cp. [1865](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1865) b) _begin to do something_ and continue with the same thing. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἄρξομαι διδάσκων ἐκ τῶν θείων</span> _I will begin my instruction with things divine_ (later the subject is the desire for wealth) <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.8.2">X. C. 8.8.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">πόθεν ἤρξατό σε διδάσκειν τὴν στρατηγίᾱν</span>; _at what point did he begin to teach you generalship?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.1.5">X. M. 3.1.5</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἄρχομαι</span> with the participle occurs only in Xenophon and Plato. <!-- page 475 -->

##### <a id="s2129"></a> §2129

<span lang="grc"> **γιγνώσκω** </span> with part. in O. O. ([2106](#s2106)) = _recognize that something is;_ with inf. in three uses: (1) in O. O. = _judge_ (_decide_) _that something is_ (a verb of _will_), as <span lang="grc">ἔγνωσαν κερδαλεώτερον εἶναι</span> _they judged that it was more profitable_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.9.17">X. A. 1.9.17</cite>; (2) not in O. O. = _resolve_, _determine to do something_, as <span lang="grc">ἔγνω διώκειν τοὺς ἐκ τῶν εὐωνύμων προσκειμένους</span> _he resolved to pursue those who were hanging on his left_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.6.9">X. H. 4.6.9</cite>; (3) not in O. O. = _learn how to do something_ (rarely), as <span lang="grc">γίγνωσκε τῆς ὀργῆς κρατεῖν</span> _learn to control thy temper_ Men. Sent. 20.

##### <a id="s2130"></a> §2130

<span lang="grc"> **δείκνῡμι** </span> with part. in O. O. ([2106](#s2106)) = _show that something is;_ with inf. (<span lang="grc">ἀποδείκνῡμι</span>) not in O. O. = _show how to do something, instruct_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀπέδειξαν οἱ ἡγεμόνες λαμβάνειν τὰ ἐπιτήδεια</span> _the guides directed them to take provisions_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.14">X. A. 2.3.14</cite>.

##### <a id="s2131"></a> §2131

<span lang="grc"> **δηλῶ** </span> with part. (and inf.) in O. O. ([2106](#s2106)) = _show that sometking is_, _indicate;_ with inf. not in O. O. = _command_, _make known, signify;_ as in <span lang="grc">κηρύ̄γματι ἐδήλου τοὺς ἐλευθερίᾱς δεομένους ὡς πρὸς σύμμαχον αὐτὸν παρεῖναι</span> _he made known by proclamation that those who wanted freedom should come to him as an ally_ X. Ag. 1. 33.

##### <a id="s2132"></a> §2132

<span lang="grc"> **δοκιμάζω** </span> with part. in O. O. ([2106](#s2106)) = _prove to be_, as <span lang="grc">ὁποῖοί τινες ὄντες αὐτοὶ περὶ τὴν πόλιν ἐδοκιμάσθητε</span> _what sort of persons you proved yourselves to be in regard to the city_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 31.34">L. 31.34</cite>; with inf. in O. O. = _pronounce an opinion to be correct_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐδοκιμάσαμεν ἀνδρὶ καλῷ τε κἀ̄γαθῷ ἐργασίᾱν εἶναι . . . κρατίστην γεωργίᾱν</span> _we approved the idea that tilling of the soil is the best occupation for a gentleman_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 6.8">X. O. 6.8</cite>.

##### <a id="s2133"></a> §2133

<span lang="grc"> **ἔοικα** </span> ([1983](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1983), [2089](#s2089) c) with nom. part. = _appear_, oftener with dat. part. (strictly = _am like_), _appear;_ with inf. = _seem_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐοίκατε τυραννίσι μᾶλλον ἢ πολῑτείαις ἡδόμενοι</span> _you appear to take delight in despotisms rather than in constitutional governments_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.3.8">X. H. 6.3.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔοικας δεδιότι τοὺς πολλούς</span> strictly _you are like one who fears_ (i.e. _you appear to fear_) _the multitude_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 527d">P. R. 527d</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔοικεν εἰδέναι</span> _he seems not to know_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Apol. 29">X. ##### <a id="s2134"></a> §2134

<span lang="grc"> **ἐπιλανθάνομαι** </span> with part. in O. O. ([2106](#s2106)) = _forget that something is;_ with inf. not in O. O. = _forget_ (_how_) _to do something_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὀλίγου ἐπελαθόμεθ᾽ εἰπεῖν</span> _I have almost forgotten to mention_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 563b">P. R. 563b</cite>.

<span lang="grc"> **εὑρίσκω** </span> with part. in O. O. = _judge_ and not in O. O. ([2113](#s2113)) = _find that something is;_ less often with inf. in O. O. = _judge_, as <span lang="grc">εὕρισκε ταῦτα καιριώτατα εἶναι</span> _he found_ (_judged_) _that this was the most opportune way_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.125">Hdt. 1.125</cite>. <span lang="grc">ευ&lt;\*&gt;ρίσκομαι</span> rarely with inf. = _find how to_ (<cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 196">E. Med. 196</cite>), _procure_ by asking (<cite data-ref="Hdt. 9.28">Hdt. 9.28</cite>).

##### <a id="s2136"></a> §2136

<span lang="grc"> **μανθάνω** </span> with part. in O. O. ([2106](#s2106)) = _learn that something is;_ with inf. not in O. O. = _learn_ (_how_) _to do something_. Thus, <span lang="grc">διαβεβλημένος οὐ μανθάνεις</span> _you do not perceive that you have been calumniated_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 3.1">Hdt. 3.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἂ̄ν ἅπαξ μάθωμεν ἀ̄ργοὶ ζῆν</span> _if we once learn to live in idleness_ <cite data-ref="Xen. <span lang="grc"> **μεθί̄ημι** </span> (_let go_), etc., with part. = _leave off;_ with inf. = _neglect_, _permit_. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ ἀνίει ἐπιών</span> _for he did not stop coming after them_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 4.125">Hdt. 4.125</cite>, <span lang="grc">μεθῑᾶσι τὰ δέοντα πρά̄ττειν</span> _they neglect to perform their duties_ <cite data-ref="Xen. <span lang="grc"> **μέμνημαι** </span> with part. in O. O. ([2106](#s2106)) = _remember that something is;_ with inf. not in O. O. = _remember to do something_. Thus, <span lang="grc">μεμνήσθω ἀνὴρ ἀγαθὸς εἶναι</span> _let in him be mindful to be a brave man_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.39">X. A. 3.2.39</cite>.

<span lang="grc"> **οἶδα** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **ἐπίσταμαι** </span> with part. in O. O. ([2106](#s2106)) = _know that something is;_ with inf. not in O. O. = _know how to do something_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐπιστάμενος νεῖν</span> _knowing how to swim_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.7.25">X. A. 5.7.25</cite>. In poetry (very rarely in prose, except with <span lang="grc">ἐπίσταμαι</span> in Hdt.) these verbs take also the inf. (in O. O.) in the meaning _know_ or _believe:_ <span lang="grc">ἐπιστάμεθα μή πώ ποτ᾽ αὐτὸν ψεῦδος λακεῖν</span> _we know that he has never yet spoken falsehood_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 1094">S. Ant. 1094</cite>.

##### <a id="s2140"></a> §2140

<span lang="grc"> **παύω** </span> with part. ([2098](#s2098)) = _stop_ what is taking place; with inf. = _prevent_ something from taking place. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔπαυσαν φοβουμένους πλῆθος νεῶν</span> _they stopped their terror at the number of ships_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Menex. 241b">P. Menex. 241b</cite>, <span lang="grc">παύσαντες τὸ μὴ προσελθεῖν ἐγγὺς τὴν ὁλκάδα</span> _preventing the merchantman from drawing near_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.53">T. 7.53</cite>.

<span lang="grc"> **περιορῶ** </span>, etc. ([2103](#s2103)) with pres. part. = _view with indifference_, with aor. part. = _shut one's eyes to;_ with inf. = _let something happen through negligence_, or simply _permit_ (<span lang="grc">ἐᾶν</span>). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀνωνύμους τοὺς ἄλλους εἶναι ποιεῖ</span> _causes the others to lose their names_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 7.129">Hdt. 7.129</cite>, <span lang="grc">ποιώμεθα</span> (conj. <span lang="grc">τί οἰώμεθα</span>) <span lang="grc">τὸν φιλόσοφον νομίζειν κτλ</span>. _let us assume that the philosopher holds_, etc. <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 581d">P. R. 581d</cite>.

##### <a id="s2143"></a> §2143

<span lang="grc"> **φαίνομαι** </span> with part in O. O. ([2106](#s2106)) = _I am plainly;_ with inf. in O. ## THE PARTICIPLE WITH <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>

<!-- page 477 -->

##### <a id="s2146"></a> §2146

The participle with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> represents the indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> ([1784 f](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1784)f.) or the potential optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> ([1824](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1824)). The present participle with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> thus represents either the imperfect indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or the present optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>; the aorist participle with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> represents either the aorist indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or the aorist optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>. Cp. [1845](20-the-verb-voices/09-infinitive-and-participle-with-alphanu.md#s1845) ff.

## REMARKS ON SOME USES OF PARTICIPLES

##### <a id="s2147"></a> §2147

The abundance of its participles is one of the characteristic features of Greek. Their use gives brevity to the sentence (cp. [2050](#s2050)), enabling the writer to set forth in a word modifications and amplifications of the main thought for which we require cumbersome relative clauses. But an excessive use of participles, especially in close conjunction, marked a careless style.

**a.** The participle may contain the leading thought, the finite verb the subordinate thought, of a sentence. Thus, <span lang="grc">τὸ ψήφισμα τοῦτο γράφω . . . τοὺς ὅρκους τὴν ταχίστην ἀπολαμβάνειν, ἵν᾽ ἐχόντων τῶν Θρᾳκῶν . . . ταῦτα τὰ χωρία, ἃ νῦν οὖτος διέσῡρε . . ., οὕτω γίγνοινθ᾽ οἱ ὅρκοι</span> _I moved this bill that_ the envoys _should with all speed receive_ Philip's _oaths in order that when the oaths were taken the Thracians might be in possession of the places which the plaintiff has just now been ridiculing_ (lit. _while the Thracians were in possession_, etc. . . . _the oaths might under these circumstances be ratified_) <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.27">D. 18.27</cite>, <span lang="grc">βούλομαι ὀλίγα ἑκατέρους ἀναμνήσᾱς καταβαίνειν</span> _I wish to recall a few things to the memory of each party and then sit down_ (_descend_ from the bema) <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.92">L. 12.92</cite>. Cp. also 2096, 2099.

**b.** The participle may repeat the stem and meaning of the finite verb. Thus, <span lang="grc">καὶ εὐχόμενος ἄν τις ταῦτα εὔξαιτο</span> _and some one might_ (_praying_) _utter this prayer_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 6.1">Ant. 6.1</cite>.

**c.** A participial construction may pass over into a construction with a finite verb. Thus, <span lang="grc">μάρτυρα μὲν . . . οὐδένα παρασχόμενος . . . παρεκελεύετο δέ κτλ</span>. lit. _producing on the one hand no witness . . . on the other hand he exhorted_, etc. <cite data-ref="Dem. 57.11">D. 57.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">προσέβαλον τῷ τειχίσματι, ἄλλῳ τε τρόπῳ πειρά̄σαντες καὶ μηχανὴν προσήγαγον</span> lit. _they attacked the rampart both making trial in other ways, and they brought up an engine_ (i.e. _and after trying other devices brought up an engine_) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.100">T. 4.100</cite>.

**d.** A participle may be used in close connection with a relative or interrogative pronoun. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽ ὑπὲρ οἷα πεποιηκότων ἀνθρώπων κινδῡνεύσετε διαλογισάμενοι</span> _not even calculating what had been the conduct of the men for whom you were going to risk your lives_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.98">D. 18.98</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐλαυνομένων καὶ ὑβριζομένων καὶ τί κακὸν οὐχὶ πασχόντων πᾶσ᾽ ἡ οἰκουμένη μεστὴ γέγονε</span> _the whole civilized world is filled with men who are harried to and fro and insulted, nay_, _what misery is there which they do not suffer?_ 18. 48.

**e.** In contrasts, two subjects may, by anacoluthon, belong to one participle in the nominative, though the participle belongs to only one subject (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.34">T. 3.34</cite>. 3).

**f.** Two or more participles may be coördinated without any connective. <!-- page 478 --> This is common in Homer when one participle forms a contrast to, or intensifies, another participle. Cp. <span lang="grc">ἦ καὶ ἐπῶρτ᾽ Ἀχιλῆι κυκώμενος ὑψόσε θύ̄ων, μορμύ̄ρων ἀφρῷ κτλ</span>. _he spake, and swelling in tumult rushed upon Achilles_, _raging on high_, _roaring with foam_, etc. <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 21.324"><span lang="grc">Φ</span> 324</cite>. This is very rare in prose (<cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.94">Aes. 3.94</cite>).

**g.** In prose such coördination without any connective is incomplete, one participle, e.g., often defining another, as in <span lang="grc">ὁ Κῦρος ὑπολαβὼν τοὺς φεύγοντας συλλέξᾱς στράτευμα ἐπολιόρκει Μί̄λητον</span> _taking the exiles under his protection_, _Cyrus collected an army, and laid siege to Miletus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.7">X. A. 1.1.7</cite>. So even when the participles are connected, as <span lang="grc">ξηρά̄νᾱς τὴν διώρυχα καὶ παρατρέψᾱς ἄλλῃ τὸ ὕδωρ</span> _by draining the canal and_ (i.e. in consequence of) _diverting the water elsewhere_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.109">T. 1.109</cite>. One participle may be appositive to another. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐξέτασιν ποιήσαντες ἐν τοῖς ἱππεῦσι, φάσκοντες εἰδέναι βούλεσθαι πόσοι εἶεν . . ., ἐκέλευον ἀπογράφεσθαι πάντας</span> _by making a review in the presence of the cavalry, alleging that they wished to find out how many they were, they ordered all to inscribe themselves_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.4.8">X. H. 2.4.8</cite>.

**h.** A participle with case absolute may be coördinated with a participle not in an absolute case. Thus, <span lang="grc">οἱ δὲ ἀφικομένης τῆς νεὼς καὶ ἀνέλπιστον τὴν εὐτυχίᾱν ἀκούσαντες . . . πολὺ ἐπερρώσθησαν</span> _they were much encouraged on the arrival of the ship and on hearing of the success which was unhoped for_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.106">T. 8.106</cite>, <span lang="grc">μεταπεμφθέντες ἤλθομεν ἢ οὐδενὸς καλέσαντος</span> _we came summoned or at no one's call_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 4.11">L. 4.11</cite>.

**i.** A finite verb may have two or more participles attached to it in different relations. Thus, <span lang="grc">οἱ πελτασταὶ προδραμόντες . . . διαβάντες τὴν χαράδρᾱν, ὁρῶντες πρόβατα πολλὰ . . . προσέβαλλον πρὸς τὸ χωρίον</span> _the light-armed troops after running forward and crossing the ravine, proceed to attack the stronghold on seeing quantities of sheep_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.2.4">X. A. 5.2.4</cite>. Of several aorist participles, one may be relatively earlier in time than another.

**j.** A participle may be added predicatively to another participle, and often follows the article belonging to the main participle. Thus, <span lang="grc">οἱ ζῶντες καταλειπόμενοι</span> _those who were being left behind alive_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.75">T. 7.75</cite>.

**k.** A participle is often omitted when it can be supplied from the context. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὡρμίσαντο καὶ αὐτοὶ . . . ἐπειδὴ καὶ τοὺς Ἀθηναίους</span> (<span lang="grc">ὁρμισαμένους</span>) <span lang="grc">εἶδον</span> _they too came to anchor when they saw that the Athenians had done so_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.86">T. 2.86</cite>.

##### <a id="s2148"></a> §2148

The participle often agrees with the logical, and not with the grammatical, subject. The participle thus often agrees with the subject of the finite verb which the writer had in mind when he began the sentence, but for which he later substitutes another verb; or the participle may later be used as if in agreement with the subject of another finite verb than the one actually employed.

**a.** A participle in the nominative may belong to a finite verb requiring an oblique case. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀποβλέψᾱς πρὸς τοῦτον τὸν στόλον . . ., ἔδοξέ μοι πάγκαλος εἶναι</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἡγησάμην πάγκαλον εἶναι</span>) _on looking at this expedition, it seemed to me to be very admirable_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 686d">P. L. 686d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔχοντες . . . ἀρχὴν μεγίστην . . ., ὅμως οὐδὲν τούτων ἡμᾶς ἐπῆρε</span> (= <span lang="grc">οὐδενὶ τούτων ἐπήρθημεν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐξαμαρτεῖν</span> _although we possessed the greatest empire . . . levertheless none of these reasons induced us to do wrong_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.108">I. 4.108</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔδοξεν αὐτοις</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἐβουλεύσαντο</span>) <span lang="grc">οὐ τοὺς παρόντας μόνον ἀποκτεῖναι ἀλλὰ καὶ τοὺς ἅπαντας Μυτιληναίους . . . ἐπικαλοῦντες τὴν ἀπόστασιν κτλ</span>. _they decided_ <!-- page 479 --> _to put to death not merely those who were there but also all the Mytilenaeans_, _urging against them their revolt_, etc. <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.36">T. 3.36</cite>.

**b.** Two or more substantives or pronouns with their participles may stand in partitive apposition ([981](10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s981)) to the logical subject. Thus, <span lang="grc">τὰ περὶ Πύλον ὑπ᾽ ἀμφοτέρων κατὰ κράτος ἐπολεμεῖτο</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἀμφότεροι ἐπολέμουν</span>), <span lang="grc">Ἀθηναῖοι μὲν . . . τὴν νῆσον περιπλέοντες . . ., Πελοποννήσιοι δὲ ἐν τῇ ἡπείρῳ στρατοπεδευόμενοι</span> _the war at Pylus was vigorously waged by both sides, the Athenians on their part by sailing around the island . . . the Peloponnesians by encamping on the mainland_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.23">T. 4.23</cite>. Cp. <span lang="grc">λόγοι δ᾽ ἐν ἀλλήλοισιν ἐρρόθουν κακοί, φύλαξ ἐλέγχων φύλακα</span> _bitter words flew loud from one to another, watchman accusing watchman_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 259">S. Ant. 259</cite>. As the sentence stands, we expect <span lang="grc">φύλακος ἐλέγχοντος φύλακα</span>, but the first clause is equivalent to <span lang="grc">κακοὺς λόγους εἴπομεν ἀλλήλους</span>. Cp. <span lang="grc">θαυμάζοντες ἄλλος ἄλλῳ ἔλεγεν</span> _one spoke to the other in astonishment_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 220c">P. S. 220c</cite>. Cp. [982](10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s982).

**c.** Without regard to the following construction, a participle may stand in the nominative. The use of the genitive absolute would here be proper, but would cause the main subject of the thought to occupy a subordinate position. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐπιπεσὼν τῇ Φαρναβάζου στρατοπεδείᾳ, τῆς μὲν προφυλακῆς αὐτοῦ Μῡσῶν ὄντων πολλοὶ ἔπεσον</span> _attacking the camp of Pharnabazus_, _he slew a large number_ (= <span lang="grc">πολλοὺς ἀπέκτεινε</span>) _of Mysians who constituted his advance guard_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.1.24">X. H. 4.1.24</cite>.

N. The nominative participle is sometimes found in clauses without a finite verb, but only when some finite verb is to be supplied (cp. <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 23.546"><span lang="grc">Ψ</span> 546</cite>), as with <span lang="grc">εἰ, ἐά̄ν, ὅταν</span> (<cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.1.23">X. M. 2.1.23</cite>); with <span lang="grc">ὅσα μή</span> _as far as is possible_ (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.111">T. 1.111</cite>); in replies in dialogue, where it stands in apposition to the subject of the preceding sentence (<cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 74b">P. Ph. 74b</cite>); or is interposed as a parenthesis (<span lang="grc">εὖ ποιοῦν</span> in <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.143">D. 23.143</cite>).

**d.** Likewise a participle may stand in the accusative or (rarely) in the dative when the construction demands another case. Thus, <span lang="grc">σοὶ δὲ συγγνώμη</span> (= <span lang="grc">συγγνώμη ἐστὶ σὲ</span>) <span lang="grc">λέγειν τάδ᾽ ἐστί, μὴ πάσχουσαν ὡς ἐγὼ κακῶς</span> _it is excusable for thee to speak thus, since thou dost not suffer cruelly as I do_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 814">E. Med. 814</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἦν ἡ γνώμη τοῦ Ἀριστέως</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἔδοξε τῷ Ἀριστεῖ</span>), <span lang="grc">τὸ μὲν μεθ᾽ ἑαυτοῦ στρατόπεδον ἔχοντι ἐν τῷ ἰσθμῷ ἐπιτηρεῖν τοὺς Ἀθηναίους</span> _Aristeus decided to keep his own forces at the Isthmus and watch for the Athenians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.62">T. 1.62</cite>.
