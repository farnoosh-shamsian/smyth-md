# THE VERB: VOICES: INFINITIVE AND PARTICIPLE WITH ἄν

##### <a id="s1845"></a> §1845

The infinitive or participle with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> represents either a past tense of the indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or the optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>. The context determines whether the indicative or the optative is meant. The participle with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is post-Homeric.

##### <a id="s1846"></a> §1846

The present infinitive or participle with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> represents the imperfect indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or the present optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>.

**a.** (_inf_.) <span lang="grc">ἀκούω Λακεδαιμονίους ἂν ἀναχωρεῖν ἐπ᾽ οἴκου</span> _I hear the Lacedaemonians used to return home_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἂν ἀνεχώρουν</span>, [1790](07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1790)) <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.48">D. 9.48</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἴεσθε γὰρ τὸν πατέρα οὐκ ἂν φυλάττειν</span>; _for do you think my father would not have taken care?_ ( = <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν ἐφύλαττεν</span>, [1786](07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1786)) <cite data-ref="Dem. 49.35">D. 49.35</cite>; <span lang="grc">νομίζοντες ἂν τῑμῆς τυγχάνειν</span> _in the belief that they would obtain reward_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἂν τυγχάνοιμεν</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.9.29">X. A. 1.9.29</cite>.

**b.** (_part_.) <span lang="grc">ὅπερ ἔσχε μὴ κατὰ πόλεις αὐτὸν ἐπιπλέοντα τὴν Πελοπόννησον πορθεῖν, ἀδυνάτων ἂν ὄντων . . . ἀλλήλοις ἐπιβοηθεῖν</span> _which prevented him from sailing against the Peloponnese and laying it waste city by city when_ the Peloponnesians _would have been unable to come to the rescue of one another_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἀδύνατοι ἂν ἦσαν</span>) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.73">T. 1.73</cite>, <span lang="grc">πόλλ᾽ ἂν ἔχων ἕτερ᾽ εἰπεῖν, παραλείπω</span> _though I might be able to say much else I pass it by_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἂν ἔχοιμι</span>, [1824](07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1824)) <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.258">D. 18.258</cite>, <span lang="grc">σοφίᾱ λεγομένη δικαιότατ᾽ ἄν</span> _that might most justly be called wisdom_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phileb. 30c">P. Phil. 30c</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">ἣ σοφίᾱ λέγοιτο ἄν</span>).

##### <a id="s1847"></a> §1847

The future infinitive and participle with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> are rare and suspected.

##### <a id="s1848"></a> §1848

The aorist infinitive or participle with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> represents the aorist indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or the aorist optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>.

**a.** (_inf_.) <span lang="grc">Κῦρός γε, εἰ ἐβίωσεν, ἄριστος ἂν δοκεῖ ἄρχων γενέσθαι</span> _it seems probable that Cyrus_, _if he had lived, would have proved himself a most excellent ruler_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἂν ἐγένετο</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 4.18">X. O. 4.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὥστε καὶ ἰδιώτην ἂν γνῶναι</span> _so that even a common man could have understood_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἂν ἔγνω</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.1.31">X. A. 6.1.31</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί ἂν οἰόμεθα παθεῖν</span>; _what do we think our fate would be?_ ( = <span lang="grc">τί ἂν πάθοιμεν</span>;) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.17">X. A. 3.1.17</cite>. <!-- page 412 -->

**b.** (_part_.) <span lang="grc">ὁρῶν τὸ παρατείχισμα ῥᾳδίως ἂν ληφθέν</span> _seeing that the counterwall could easily be captured_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἂν ληφθείη</span>) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.42">T. 7.42</cite>, <span lang="grc">Ποτείδαιαν ἑλὼν καὶ δυνηθεὶς ἂν αὐτὸς ἔχειν, εἰ ἐβουλήθη, παρέδωκεν</span> _after he had seized Potidaea and would have been able to keep it himself, had he wished, he gave it up to them_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἐδυνήθη ἄν</span>) <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.107">D. 23.107</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὔτε ὄντα οὔτε ἂν γενόμενα λογοποιοῦσιν</span> _they fabricate stories which neither are, nor could be, true_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.38">T. 6.38</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">ἃ οὔτε ἔστιν οὔτε ἂν γένοιτο</span>).

##### <a id="s1849"></a> §1849

The perfect infinitive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> represents the pluperfect indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or the perfect optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>: <span lang="grc">οἶδ᾽ ὅτι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἂν</span>) <span lang="grc">φήσειεν πάντα ταῦθ᾽ ὑπὸ τῶν βαρβάρων ἂν ἡλωκέναι</span> _I know that he would say that all this would have been captured by the barbarians_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἂν ἡλώκεσαν</span>) <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.312">D. 19.312</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡγεῖτο τοὺς ἀγνοοῦντας ἀνδραποδώδεις ἂν δικαίως κεκλῆσθαι</span> _he thought that those who did not know this might justly be deemed servile in nature_ ( = <span lang="grc">κεκλημένοι ἂν εἶεν</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.1.16">X. M. 1.1.16</cite>.

For the infinitive and participle without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> see [1865](10-the-tenses.md#s1865) ff., 1872 ff., and under Infinitive and Participle.
