# THE VERB: VOICES: THE PARTICLE ἄν

##### <a id="s1761"></a> §1761

The particle <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (Hom. <span lang="grc">κέν, κέ</span>) limits the meaning of the moods. It has two distinct uses:

**a.** In independent clauses: with the past tenses of the indicative and with the optative; also with the infinitive and participle representing the indicative or optative.

**b.** In dependent clauses: with the subjunctive. <!-- page 399 -->

##### <a id="s1762"></a> §1762

No separate word can be used to translate <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> by itself; its force varies as it modifies the meaning of the moods. In general <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> limits the force of the verb to particular conditions or circumstances (‘under the circumstances,’ ‘in that case,’ ‘then’).

##### <a id="s1763"></a> §1763

In Homer <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is preferred in negative, <span lang="grc">κέν, κέ</span> in relative, sentences.

##### <a id="s1764"></a> §1764

**Position of <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>**.—<span lang="grc">ἄν</span> does not begin a sentence or a clause, except after a weak mark of punctuation, as <span lang="grc">τί οὖν, ἄν τις εἴποι, ταῦτα λέγεις ἡμῖν νῦν</span>; _why then_ (_some one might say_) _do you tell us this now?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 1.14">D. 1.14</cite>. In independent sentences with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (indic. and opt.) the particle is often separated from its verb for emphasis, and is attached to negatives (<span lang="grc">οὐκ ἄν</span>), interrogatives (<span lang="grc">τίς ἄν, πῶς ἄν</span>), or to any emphatic modifier. It is commonly attached to _verbs of saying_ or _thinking:_ <span lang="grc">σὺν ὑ̄μῖν μὲν ἂν οἶμαι εἶναι τί̄μιος</span> if I should remain _with you_, _I think I should be esteemed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.6">X. A. 1.3.6</cite>.

**a.** So with <span lang="grc">οὐκ οἶδ᾽ ἂν εἰ</span> (or <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν οἶδα εἰ</span>) followed by a verb to which <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> belongs: <span lang="grc">οὐκ οἶδ᾽ ἂν εἰ πείσαιμι</span> _I do not know whether I could persuade_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 941">E. Med. 941</cite> (for <span lang="grc">πείσαιμι ἄν</span>).

##### <a id="s1765"></a> §1765

**Repetition of <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>**.—<span lang="grc">ἄν</span> may be repeated once or twice in the same sentence.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is placed early in a sentence which contains a subordinate clause, in order to direct attention to the character of the construction: <span lang="grc">δοκοῦμεν δ᾽ ἄν μοι ταύτῃ προσποιούμενοι προσβαλεῖν ἐρημοτέρῳ ἂν τῷ ὄρει χρῆσθαι</span> _if we should make a feint attack here it seems to me we should find the mountain to have fewer defenders_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.6.13">X. A. 4.6.13</cite>.

**b.** For rhetorical emphasis <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is added to give prominence to particular words: <span lang="grc">τίς γὰρ τοιαῦτ᾽ ἂν οὐκ ἂν ὀργίζοιτ᾽ ἔπη κλύων</span>; _and who would not be angered upon hearing such words?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 339">S. O. T. 339</cite>, <span lang="grc">πῶς ἂν οὐκ ἂν ἐν δίκῃ θάνοιμ᾽ ἄν</span>; _how should I not justly die?_ S. fr. 673.

##### <a id="s1766"></a> §1766

**<span lang="grc">ἄν</span> without a Verb**.—<span lang="grc">ἄν</span> sometimes stands without a verb, which is to be supplied from the context. So in the second member of a sentence with coördinate clauses: <span lang="grc">οἶδα ὅτι πολλοὺς μὲν ἡγεμόνας ἂν δοίη, πολλοὺς δ᾽ ἂν</span> (<span lang="grc">δοίη</span>) <span lang="grc">ὁμήρους</span> _I know that he would give many guides and many hostages_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.24">X. A. 3.2.24</cite>. Often with <span lang="grc">πῶς ἄν</span> (<span lang="grc">εἴη</span>); _how can_ (_could_) _it be?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 353c">P. R. 353c</cite>, <span lang="grc">τάχ᾽ ἄν</span> _perhaps_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Soph. 255c">P. Soph. 255c</cite>.

**a.** So with <span lang="grc">ὡς ἄν, ὥσπερ ἂν εἰ</span> ([2480](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/13-clauses-of-comparison.md#s2480)): <span lang="grc">παρῆν ὁ Γαδά̄τᾱς δῶρα πολλὰ φέρων, ὡς ἂν</span> (_scil._ <span lang="grc">φέροι τις</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐξ οἴκου μεγάλου</span> _Gadatas came with many gifts, such as one might offer from large means_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.4.29">X. C. 5.4.29</cite>, <span lang="grc">φοβούμενος ὥσπερ ἂν εἰ παῖς</span> _fearing like a child_ (<span lang="grc">ὥσπερ ἂν ἐφοβεῖτο, εἰ παῖς ἦν</span>) <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 479a">P. G. 479a</cite>.

**b.** <span lang="grc">κἂ̄ν εἰ</span> is often used for the simple <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ</span> ([2372](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/11-concessive-clauses.md#s2372)) and without regard to the mood of the following verb; sometimes there is no verb in the apodosis to which the <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> may be referred, as <span lang="grc">ἔστιν ἄρα τῇ ἀληθείᾳ, κἂ̄ν εἰ μή τῳ δοκεῖ, ὁ τῷ ὄντι τύραννος τῷ ὄντι δοῦλος</span> _the very tyrant is then in truth a very slave even if he does not seem so to any one_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 579d">P. R. 579d</cite> (here <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ μὴ δοκεῖ, εἴη ἄν</span> is implied). <span lang="grc">κἂ̄ν εἰ</span> may be also so used that <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> belongs to the apodosis, while <span lang="grc">καί</span>, though going with <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> in translation (_even if_), affects the whole conditional sentence. Thus, <span lang="grc">νῦν δέ μοι δοκεῖ, κἂν ὰσέβειαν εἰ</span> (<span lang="grc">τις</span>) <span lang="grc">καταγιγνώσκοι, τὰ προσήκοντα ποιεῖν</span> _but as it is, it seems to me that_, _even if any one should condemn his wanton assault, he would be acting properly_ <!-- page 400 --> <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.51">D. 21.51</cite> (here <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> goes with <span lang="grc">ποιεῖν</span>, i.e. <span lang="grc">ποιοίη ἄν</span>). <span lang="grc">κἄ̄ν</span> _if only_, followed by a limiting expression, may generally be regarded as <span lang="grc">καὶ ἄ̄ν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span>) with a subjunctive understood; as <span lang="grc">ἀλλά μοι πάρες κἂ̄ν σμῑκρὸν εἰπεῖν</span> _yet permit me to say but a word_ ( = <span lang="grc">καὶ ἐὰ̄ν παρῇς</span>) <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 1482">S. El. 1482</cite>.

##### <a id="s1767"></a> §1767

**Omission of <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>**.—<span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is sometimes omitted when it may be supplied from the preceding sentence or clause. So often with the second of two verbs that are connected or opposed: <span lang="grc">τί ἐποίησεν ἄν; ἢ δῆλον ὅτι ὤμοσε</span> (<span lang="grc">ἄν</span>); _what would he have done? is it not clear that he would have taken an oath?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 31.9">D. 31.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὔτ᾽ ἂν οὗτος ἔχοι λέγειν οὔθ᾽ ὑ̄μεῖς πεισθείητε</span> _neither can he assert nor can you be made to believe_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 22.17">D. 22.17</cite>. By retention of earlier usage the subjunctive is sometimes used without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> where it is commonly employed in the later language ([2327](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2327), [2339](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2339), 2565 b, 2567 b). Here the difference is scarcely appreciable except that the omission gives an archaic tone.

## DEPENDENT CLAUSES WITH <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>

##### <a id="s1768"></a> §1768

**Subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>**.—Conditional, relative, and temporal clauses requiring the subjunctive must have <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, which is more closely attached to the conditional, relative, and temporal words than it is to the subjunctive.

**a.** Hence the combinations <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> (<span lang="grc">ἤν, ἄ̄ν</span>) on which cp. [2283](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/09-conditional-clauses.md#s2283); <span lang="grc">ὅταν, ὁπόταν, ἐπήν</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐπά̄ν</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐπειδάν</span> from <span lang="grc">εἰ, ὅτε, ὁπότε, ἐπεί, ἐπειδή ¨ ἄν</span>. When the particle does not thus coalesce, it is usually separated only by such words as <span lang="grc">μέν, δέ, τέ, γάρ</span>.

**b.** The force of <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the subjunctive cannot usually be expressed in English. For <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> in final clauses with <span lang="grc">ὡς, ὅπως</span>, and <span lang="grc">ὄφρα</span>, see [2201](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/02-purpose-clauses-final-clauses.md#s2201). In Hom. <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (<span lang="grc">κέν</span>) is found in dependent clauses, 2334 c.
