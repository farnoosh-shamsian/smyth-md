# THE VERB: VOICES: PASSIVE VOICE

##### <a id="s1735"></a> §1735

The passive voice represents the subject as acted on: <span lang="grc">ἐώθουν, ἐωθοῦντο, ἔπαιον, ἐπαίοντο</span> _they pushed, were pushed, they struck_, _were struck_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.1.38">X. C. 7.1.38</cite>.

**a.** The passive has been developed from the middle. With the exception of some futures and the aorist, the middle forms do duty as passives: <span lang="grc">αἱρεῖται</span> _takes for himself_, i.e. _chooses_, and _is chosen_. (For this development of the passive, cp. the reflexive use in _se trouver, sich finden_.) So <span lang="grc">κέχυται</span> _has poured itself, has been poured_. In Homer there are more perfect middles used passively than any other middle tenses. Cp. [802](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s802).

**b.** Uncompounded <span lang="grc">ἐσχόμην</span> sometimes retained its use as a passive. <span lang="grc">ἐσχέθην</span> is late.

##### <a id="s1736"></a> §1736

The passive may have the sense _allow oneself to be, get oneself:_ <span lang="grc">ἐξάγοντές τε καὶ ἐξαγόυενοι</span> _carrying and allowing ourselves to be carried across the border_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 48d">P. Cr. 48d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπεχθήσει Γοργίᾳ</span> _you will incur the hatred of Gorgias_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phileb. 58c">P. Phil. 58c</cite>.

##### <a id="s1737"></a> §1737

Many future middle forms are used passively ([807 f](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s807)f.). <!-- page 395 -->

##### <a id="s1738"></a> §1738

The future middle forms in <span lang="grc">-σομαι</span> are developed from the present stem, and express durative action; the (later) future passives in <span lang="grc">-ήσομαι, -θήσομαι</span> are developed from the aorists in <span lang="grc">-ην</span> and <span lang="grc">-θην</span>, and are aoristic. This difference in kind of action is most marked when the future middle forms are used passively, but it is not always found. <span lang="grc">τοῖς ἄλλοις ξυμμάχοις παράδειγμα σαφὲς καταστήσατε, ὃς ἂν ἀφίστηται, θανάτῳ ζημιωσόμενον</span> _give to the rest of the allies a plain example that whoever revolts shall be punished_ (in each case) _with death_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.40">T. 3.40</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν ἁλῷ, θανάτῳ ζημιωθήσεται</span> _if he is convicted, he will be punished_ (a single occurrence) _with death_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.80">D. 23.80</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁ δίκαιος μαστῑγώσεται, στρεβλώσεται, δεδήσεται, ἐκκαυθήσεται τὠφθαλμώ</span> _the just man will be scourged, racked_, _fettered_, _will have his eyes burnt out_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 361e">P. R. 361e</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῑμήσομαι</span> _I shall enjoy honour_, <span lang="grc">τῑμηθήσομαι</span> _I shall be honoured_ (on a definite occasion), <span lang="grc">ὠφελήσομαι</span> _I shall receive lasting benefit_, <span lang="grc">ὠφεληθήσομαι</span> _I shall be benefited_ (on a definite occasion). Cp. [808](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s808), [809](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s809), 1911.

##### <a id="s1739"></a> §1739

The second aorist passive was originally a second aorist active (of the <span lang="grc">-μι</span> form) that was used intransitively to distinguish it from the transitive first aorist, as <span lang="grc">ἔφηνα</span> _showed_, <span lang="grc">ἐφάνην</span> _appeared;_ <span lang="grc">ἔφθειρα</span> _destroyed_, <span lang="grc">ἐφθάρην</span> _am destroyed;_ <span lang="grc">ἐξέπληξα</span> _was terrified_, <span lang="grc">ἐξεπλάγην</span> _was alarmed_. So <span lang="grc">ἐδάην</span> _learned_, <span lang="grc">ἐρρύην</span> _flowed_. Cp. <span lang="grc">ἔστησα</span> _placed_, <span lang="grc">ἔστην</span> _stood_ ([819](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s819)).

##### <a id="s1740"></a> §1740

In Hom. all the second aorist forms in <span lang="grc">-ην</span> are intransitive except <span lang="grc">ἐπλήγην</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐτύπην</span> _was struck_. Most of the forms in <span lang="grc">-θην</span> are likewise intransitive in Hom., as <span lang="grc">ἐφάνθην</span> _appeared_ (in Attic _was shown_).

##### <a id="s1741"></a> §1741

The perfect passive in the third singular with the dative of the agent ([1488](../18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1488)) is often preferred to the perfect active of the first person. Thus <span lang="grc">πέπρᾱκταί μοι</span> _it has been done by me_ is more common than <span lang="grc">πέπρᾱγα</span> or <span lang="grc">πέπρᾱχα</span> _I have done_.

##### <a id="s1742"></a> §1742

The passive may be passive of the middle as well as passive of the active: <span lang="grc">αἱρεῖται</span> _is taken_ or _is chosen_, <span lang="grc">βιάζεται</span> _does violence_ or _suffers violence_ (_is forced_), <span lang="grc">ᾑρέθη</span> _was taken_ or _was chosen_, <span lang="grc">ἐγράφη</span> _was written_ or _was indicted_ (<span lang="grc">γέγραμμαι</span> is commonly middle). The use of the passive as passive of the middle is post-Homeric.

**a.** When deponent verbs have a passive force, the future and aorist have the passive form: <span lang="grc">ἐβιάσθην</span> _I suffered violence_ (_was forced_), but <span lang="grc">ἐβιασάμην</span> _I did violence_. This holds when there was once an active form. Cp. also <span lang="grc">τῑμωρεῖσθαι, μεταπέμπεσθαι, ψηφίζεσθαι, κυκλεῖσθαι</span>.

**b.** The aorist passive may have a middle sense ([814](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s814)).

##### <a id="s1743"></a> §1743

The direct object of an active verb becomes the subject of the passive: <span lang="grc">ἡ ἐπιστολὴ ὑπὸ τοῦ διδασκάλου γράφεται</span> _the letter is written by the teacher_ (active <span lang="grc">ὁ διδάσκαλος γράφει τὴν ἐπιστολήν</span>).

##### <a id="s1744"></a> §1744

The cognate accusative may become the subject of the passive: <span lang="grc">πόλεμος ἐπολεμήθη</span> _war was waged_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Menex. 243e">P. Menex. 243e</cite> (<span lang="grc">πόλεμον πολεμεῖν</span>, [1564](../18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1564)).

##### <a id="s1745"></a> §1745

Active or middle verbs governing the genitive or dative may form (unlike the Latin use) a personal passive, the genitive or dative (especially if either denotes a person) becoming the subject of the passive. <!-- page 396 -->

**a.** With the genitive: <span lang="grc">ἄρχειν, ἡγεμονεύειν, καταφρονεῖν, καταγελᾶν, καταψηφίζειν</span> (<span lang="grc">καταψηφίζεσθαι</span>), <span lang="grc">ἀμελεῖν</span>.

**b.** With the dative: <span lang="grc">ἀπειλεῖν, ἀπιστεῖν, ἐγκαλεῖν, ἐπιβουλεύειν, ἐπιτῑμᾶν, ὀνειδίζειν, πιστεύειν, πολεμεῖν, φθονεῖν</span>.

**c.** Examples: <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἠξίουν οὗτοι ἡγεμονεύεσθαι ὑφ᾽ ἡμῶν</span> _they did not think it right to be governed by us_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.61">T. 3.61</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος κατεψηφίσθη</span> _he was condemned_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 5.2.36">X. H. 5.2.36</cite>, but <span lang="grc">θάνατος αὐτῶν κατεγνώσθη</span> _the penalty of death was pronounced against them_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 13.39">L. 13.39</cite> (pass. of <span lang="grc">καταγνῶναι θάνατον αὐτῶν</span>), <span lang="grc">ὥρᾱ ἡμῖν βουλεύεσθαι ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν αὐτῶν μὴ καταφρονηθῶμεν</span> _it is time for us to take counsel for ourselves that we may not be brought into contempt_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.7.12">X. A. 5.7.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">πολεμοῦνται μὲν ὑπὸ τῶν τὴν χώρᾱν αὐτῶν περιοικούντων, ἀπιστοῦνται δ᾽ ὑφ᾽ ἁπάντων</span> _they are warred against by those who dwell around their country, and are distrusted by all_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 5.49">I. 5.49</cite>, <span lang="grc">πῶς ἂν ἐπεβούλευσά τι αὐτῷ, ὅ τι μὴ καὶ ἐπεβουλεύθην ὑπ᾽ αὐτοῦ</span>; _how could I have plotted against him, unless I had been plotted against by him?_ Ant. 4. <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 2.5"><span lang="grc">β</span>. 5</cite>, <span lang="grc">φθονηθεὶς ὑπὸ τοὖ Οδυσσέως</span> _envied by Odysseus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.2.33">X. M. 4.2.33</cite> (contrast Lat. _invidetur mihi ab aliquo_).

N.—The above principle does not hold when the accusative of an external object intervenes between the verb and the dative.

##### <a id="s1746"></a> §1746

A verb governing an oblique case rarely forms in Greek (unlike Latin) an impersonal passive: <span lang="grc">ἐμοὶ βεβοήθηται τῷ τε τεθνεῶτι καὶ τῷ νόμῳ</span> _my aid has been given to the deceased and to the law_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 1.31">Ant. 1.31</cite>. The tense used is one from the perfect stem.

##### <a id="s1747"></a> §1747

An active verb followed by two accusatives, one of a person, the other of a thing, retains, when transferred to the passive, the accusative of the thing, while the accusative of the person becomes the nominative subject of the passive. Examples 1621, 1625, 1627, 1632.

##### <a id="s1748"></a> §1748

An active verb followed by an accusative of the direct object (a thing) and an oblique case of a person, retains, when transferred to the passive, the accusative of the direct object, while the indirect object becomes the nominative subject of the passive. Cp. _I have been willed a large estate_.

**a.** With verbs signifying _to enjoin, entrust:_ <span lang="grc">οἱ Βοιωτοὶ ταῦτα ἐπεσταλμένοι ἀνεχώρουν</span> _the Boeotians having received these instructions withdrew_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.37">T. 5.37</cite> (pass. of <span lang="grc">ἐπιστέλλειν ταῦτα τοῖς Βοιωτοῖς</span>), <span lang="grc">ἄλλο τι μεῖζον ἐπιταχθήσεσθε</span> _you will have some greater command laid upon you_ 1. 140 (pass. of <span lang="grc">ἐπιτάττειν ἄλλο τι μεῖζον ὑ̄μῖν</span>). Both accusatives are internal; and so, in <span lang="grc">οἱ τῶν Ἀθηναίων ἐπιτετραμμένοι τὴν φυλακήν</span> _those of the Athenians who had been entrusted with the watch_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.126">T. 1.126</cite>, <span lang="grc">φυλακήν</span> is equivalent to an internal accusative. The nominative of the thing and the dative of the person sometimes occur (<span lang="grc">Ἴωνες, τοῖσι ἐπετέτραπτο ἡ φυλακή</span> _the Ionians to whom the guard had been entrusted_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 7.10">Hdt. 7.10</cite>). The dative is common when an inf. is used with the pass. verb: <span lang="grc">ἐπετέτακτο τοῖς σκευοφόροις ἰέναι</span> _the baggage-carriers had been commanded to go_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.3.3">X. C. 6.3.3</cite>. <!-- page 397 -->

**b.** With other verbs: <span lang="grc">ἀποτμηθέντες τὰ̄ς κεφαλά̄ς</span> _having been decapitated_ (had their heads cut off) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.6.1">X. A. 2.6.1</cite> (pass. of <span lang="grc">ἀποτέμνειν τὰ̄ς κεφαλά̄ς τισι</span> or <span lang="grc">τινων</span>).

##### <a id="s1749"></a> §1749

A passive may be formed in the case of verbs ordinarily intransitive but allowing a cognate accusative in the active: <span lang="grc">ἱκανὰ τοῖς πολεμίοις ηὐτύχηται</span> _the enemy has had enough good fortune_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.77">T. 7.77</cite> (<span lang="grc">εὐτυχεῖν ἱκανά</span>, [1573](../18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1573)), <span lang="grc">κεκινδῡνεύσεται</span> _the risk will have been run_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.75">Ant. 5.75</cite>. See [1746](#s1746). This is common with neuter passive participles: <span lang="grc">τὰ ἠσεβημένα αὐτῷ</span> _the impious acts committed by him_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 6.5">L. 6.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ σοὶ κἀ̄μοὶ βεβιωμένα</span> _the life led by you and by me_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.265">D. 18.265</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ πεπολῑτευμένα αὐτοῖς</span> _their political acts_ 1. 28, <span lang="grc">ἁμαρτηθέντα</span> _errors committed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.8.20">X. A. 5.8.20</cite>.

**a.** Some verbs describing the action of the weather may be used in the passive: <span lang="grc">νειφόμενοι ἀπῆλθον εἰς τὸ ἄστυ</span> _they returned to the city covered with snow_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.4.3">X. H. 2.4.3</cite>.

##### <a id="s1750"></a> §1750

The cognate subject may be implied, as in the case of impersonal passives, in the perfect and tenses derived from the perfect. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὴ αὐτοῖς παρεσκεύαστο</span> _when their preparations were complete_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.46">T. 1.46</cite>. <span lang="grc">λέγεται</span> _it is said_, <span lang="grc">ἐδηλώθη</span> _it was made known_, followed by the logical subject are not impersonal: <span lang="grc">ἐδηλώθη τῷ τρόπῳ ἀπωλώλει τὰ χρήματα</span> _it was shown how the money had been lost_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.70">Ant. 5.70</cite>. See [935](../07-omission-of-the-subject.md#s935).

##### <a id="s1751"></a> §1751

Greek uses impersonals from intransitives (corresponding to Lat. _ambulatur_, _itur_, _curritur_) only when the active is itself intransitive; as <span lang="grc">δέδοκται</span> _it has seemed good_ (cp. <span lang="grc">δοκεῖ</span>).

##### <a id="s1752"></a> §1752

The active or the middle deponent of a transitive verb used transitively or of an intransitive verb may replace the passive of a transitive verb.

<span lang="grc"> **ἀκούειν** </span> (poet. <span lang="grc">κλύειν</span>) _be called; be well_ (<span lang="grc">εὖ, καλῶς</span>) or _ill_ (<span lang="grc">κακῶς</span>) _spoken of_, = pass. of <span lang="grc">λέγειν</span>: <span lang="grc">νῦν κόλακες ἀκούουσιν</span> _now they are called flatterers_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.46">D. 18.46</cite>, <span lang="grc">τίς ὑπ᾽ ἐμοῦ κακῶς ἀκήκοεν ἢ πέπονθε</span>; _who has been ill spoken of or suffered at my hands?_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 8.3">L. 8.3</cite>. Cp. _bene_, _male audire;_ Milton: “England hears ill abroad.”

<span lang="grc"> **ἁλίσκεσθαι** </span> _be caught_ = pass. of <span lang="grc">αἱρεῖν</span>, as <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν ἁλῷς τοῦτο πρά̄ττων</span> _if you are caught doing this_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 29c">P. A. 29c</cite>.

<span lang="grc"> **ἀποθνήσκειν** </span> (_die_) _be killed_ = pass. of <span lang="grc">ἀποκτείνειν</span>, as <span lang="grc">ἀπέθνῃσκον ὑπὸ ἱππέων</span> _they were killed by the cavalry_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.1.48">X. C. 7.1.48</cite>. But not in the perfect, where the uncompounded <span lang="grc">τέθνηκα</span> is used.

<span lang="grc"> **γίγνεσθαι** </span> _be born_ = pass. of <span lang="grc">τίκτειν</span> _beget_, _bring forth:_ <span lang="grc">παῖδες αὐτῷ οὐκ ἐγίγνοντο ἐκ ταύτης</span> _he had no children by her_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.4.37">X. H. 6.4.37</cite>.

<span lang="grc"> **δίκην δοῦναι** </span> _be punished_ = pass. of <span lang="grc">ζημιοῦν</span>, as <span lang="grc">ὑπ᾽ αὐτῶν τούτων δίκην ἔδοσαν</span> _they were punished by these very men_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.6.45">X. C. 1.6.45</cite>.

<span lang="grc"> **ἡττᾶσθαι** </span> _be defeated_ = pass. of <span lang="grc">νῑκᾶν</span> _conquer_, as <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ τῶν συμμάχων ἡττώμενοι</span> _worsted by their allies_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 4.28">And. 4.28</cite>.

<span lang="grc"> **κατιέναι** </span> (<span lang="grc">κατέρχεσθαι</span>) _return from exile_ = pass. of <span lang="grc">κατάγειν</span> _restore from exile_, as <span lang="grc">ὑπ ὀλιγαρχίᾱς κατελθεῖν</span> _to be restored by an oligarchy_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.68">T. 8.68</cite>.

<span lang="grc"> **κεῖσθαι** </span> (_lie_) _be placed_ = pass. of the perfect of <span lang="grc">τιθέναι</span>: <span lang="grc">πείθου τοῖς νόμοις τοῖς ὑπὸ τῶν βασιλέων κειμένοις</span> _obey the laws established by kings_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.36">I. 1.36</cite>.

<span lang="grc"> **λαγχάνειν** </span> (_obtain by lot_) _be drawn by lot_ = pass. of <span lang="grc">κληροῦν</span>: <span lang="grc">ἔλαχον ἱερεύς</span> _I became priest by lot_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 57.47">D. 57.47</cite>.

<span lang="grc"> **πάσχειν** </span> (_suffer_) _be treated well_ (<span lang="grc">εὖ</span>) or _ill_ (<span lang="grc">κακῶς</span>) = pass. of <span lang="grc">ποιεῖν</span> (<span lang="grc">εὖ, κακῶς</span>): <span lang="grc">εὖ παθόντες ὑπ᾽ αὐτῶν</span> _well treated by them_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 519c">P. G. 519c</cite>. <!-- page 398 -->

<span lang="grc"> **πί̄πτειν** </span> in <span lang="grc">ἐκπί̄πτειν</span> (_fall out_) _be expelled_ = pass. of <span lang="grc">ἐκβάλλειν</span>: <span lang="grc">οἱ ἐκπεπτωκότες &lt;\*&gt; τοῦ δήμου</span> _those who had been expelled by the people_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.8.20">X. H. 4.8.20</cite>.

<span lang="grc"> **φεύγειν** </span> (_flee_) _be prosecuted_ = pass. of <span lang="grc">διώκειν</span> (_be indicted_ = <span lang="grc">γράφεσθαι</span> passive); _&lt;\*&gt;iled_ = pass. of <span lang="grc">ἐκβάλλειν</span>. So <span lang="grc">ἀποφεύγειν</span> _be acquitted_ = pass. of <span lang="grc">ἀπολύ̄ειν</span>. Thus. <span lang="grc">ἀσεβείᾱς φεύγων ὑπὸ Μελήτου</span> _prosecuted for impiety by Meletus_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 35d">P. A. 35d</cite>.

##### <a id="s1753"></a> §1753

Other equivalents of passive forms are <span lang="grc">ἔχειν, τυγχάνειν, λαμβάνειν</span>, used with a substantive of like meaning with the active verb: <span lang="grc">ὄνομα ἔχειν ῀ ὀνο&lt;\*&gt;σθαι, συγγνώμην ἔχειν</span> or <span lang="grc">συγγνώμης τυγχάνειν ῀ συγγιγνώσκεσθαι, ἔπαινον λαμβά&lt;\*&gt;</span> or <span lang="grc">ἐπαίνου τυγχάνειν ῀ ἐπαινεῖσθαι</span>. So with middle deponents: <span lang="grc">αἰτίᾱν ἔχειν ῀ &lt;\*&gt;τιᾶσθαι</span>.

##### <a id="s1754"></a> §1754

The passive of the periphrasis with <span lang="grc">ποιεῖσθαι</span> ([1722](03-middle-voice.md#s1722)) is made with <span lang="grc">&lt;\*&gt;εσθαι</span>: so <span lang="grc">εἰρήνη γίγνεται</span> _peace is made_.

##### <a id="s1755"></a> §1755

The agent of the passive is regularly expressed by <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> and the genitive; sometimes by <span lang="grc">ἀπό, διά, ἐκ, παρά, πρός</span> with the genitive, or by <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> with the dative (in poetry). See [1678](../19-prepositions.md#s1678).

##### <a id="s1756"></a> §1756

The instrument of an action, when regarded as the agent, is personified, and may be expressed by <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> with the genitive: <span lang="grc">α&lt;\*&gt;λίσκεται ὑπὸ τριήρους</span> _he is captured by a trireme_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 53.6">D. 53.6</cite>.

##### <a id="s1757"></a> §1757

The dative, or a prepositional phrase, is regularly used with the passive to denote the instrument, means, or cause ([1506](../18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1506)). The agent may be viewed as the instrument: in prose, when persons are regarded as instruments, the dative is usually that of military accompaniment ([1526](../18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1526)).

##### <a id="s1758"></a> §1758

The dative of the agent used with the perfect passive and verbal adjective is a dative of interest ([1488](../18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1488)); on <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> with the genitive used instead of the dative, see [1493](../18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1493), [1494](../18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1494).
