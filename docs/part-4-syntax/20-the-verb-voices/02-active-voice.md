# THE VERB: VOICES: ACTIVE VOICE

##### <a id="s1703"></a> §1703

The active voice represents the subject as performing the action of the verb: <span lang="grc">λούω</span> _I wash_.

**a.** Under _action_ is included _being_, as <span lang="grc">ἡ ὁδὸς μακρά̄ ἐστι</span> _the way is long_.

##### <a id="s1704"></a> §1704

Active verbs are _transitive_ or _intransitive_ ([920](../02-syntax-of-the-simple-sentence.md#s920)).

##### <a id="s1705"></a> §1705

The action of a transitive verb is directed immediately upon an object, as <span lang="grc">τύπτω τὸν παῖδα</span> _I strike the boy_.

##### <a id="s1706"></a> §1706

The object of a transitive verb is always put in the accusative ([1553](../18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1553)).

##### <a id="s1707"></a> §1707

The action of an intransitive verb is not directed immedi-ately upon an object. The action may be restricted to the subject, as <span lang="grc">ἀλγῶ</span> _I am in pain_, or it may be defined by an oblique case or by a preposition with its case, as <span lang="grc">ἀλγῶ τοὺς πόδας</span> _I have a pain in my feet_, <span lang="grc">ἀφί̄κετο εἰς τὴν πόλιν</span> _he arrived at the city_.

##### <a id="s1708"></a> §1708

Many verbs are used in the active voice both transitively and intransitively. So, in English, _turn_, _move_, _change_. Cp. [1557](../18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1557) ff.

**a.** The distinction between transitive and intransitive verbs is a grammatical convenience, and is not founded on an essential difference of nature.

##### <a id="s1709"></a> §1709

Active verbs ordinarily transitive are often used intransitively:

**a.** By the ellipsis of a definite external object, which in some cases may be employed, as <span lang="grc">ἄγειν</span> (<span lang="grc">τὸ στράτευμα</span>) _march_, <span lang="grc">αἴρειν</span> (<span lang="grc">τὴν ἄγκῡραν</span>) _hoist the anchor_, (<span lang="grc">τὰ̄ς ναῦς</span>) _get under sail, start_, <span lang="grc">ἀπαίρειν</span> (<span lang="grc">τὰ̄ς ναῦς, τὸν στρατόν</span>) _sail away, march away_, <span lang="grc">διάγειν</span> (<span lang="grc">τὸν βίον</span>) _live_, <span lang="grc">ἐλαύνειν</span> (<span lang="grc">τὸν ἵππον</span>) _ride_, (<span lang="grc">τὸ ἅρμα</span>) _drive_, (<span lang="grc">τὸν στρατόν</span>) _march_, <span lang="grc">καταλύ̄ειν</span> (<span lang="grc">τοὺς ἵππους, τὰ ὑποζύγια</span>) _halt_, <span lang="grc">κατέχειν</span> (<span lang="grc">τῆν ναῦν</span>) _put in shore_, <span lang="grc">προσέχειν</span> (<span lang="grc">τὸν νοῦν</span>) _pay attention_, <span lang="grc">τελευτᾶν</span> (<span lang="grc">τὸν βίον</span>) _die_. The original sense has often been so completely forgotten that it becomes possible to say <span lang="grc">αἴρειν τῷ στρατῷ</span> _set out with the army_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.12">T. 2.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐλαύνων ἱδροῦντι τῷ ἵππῳ</span> _riding with his horse in a sweat_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.1">X. A. 1.8.1</cite>.

**b.** <span lang="grc">πρά̄ττειν, ἔχειν</span> with adverbs often mean _to keep, to be:_ <span lang="grc">εὖ πρά̄ττειν</span> _fare well_, <span lang="grc">καλῶς ἔχειν</span> _be well_ (_bene se habere_), <span lang="grc">ἔχειν οὕτως</span> _be so_. So when a reflexive pronoun is apparently omitted: <span lang="grc">ἔχ᾽ αὐτοῦ</span> _stop there!_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 45.26">D. 45.26</cite>.

**c.** Many other transitive verbs may be used absolutely, i.e. with no definite object omitted, as <span lang="grc">νῑκᾶν</span> _be a victor_, <span lang="grc">ἀδικεῖν</span> _be guilty_. Cp. ‘amare’ _be in love_, ‘drink’ _be a drunkard_. This is especially the case in compounds, e.g. of <span lang="grc">ἀλλάττειν, ἀνύειν, διδόναι, κλί̄νειν, λαμβάνειν, λείπειν, μειγνύναι</span>.

**d.** In poetry many uncompounded transitive verbs are used intransitively. Many intransitive verbs become transitive when compounded with a prep., especially when the compound has a transferred sense, 1559. In some verbs 1st aorist and 1st perfect are transitive, 2d aorist and 2d perfect are intransitive. <!-- page 390 -->

##### <a id="s1710"></a> §1710

Instead of the active, a periphrasis with <span lang="grc">γίγνεσθαι</span> may be used, often to express solemnity. <span lang="grc">μηνῡταὶ γίγνονται</span> _they turned informers_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.2">T. 3.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">μὴ ὑβριστὴς γέϝῃ</span> ‘_do not be guilty of outrage_’ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 1092">S. Aj. 1092</cite>.

##### <a id="s1711"></a> §1711

**Causative Active**.—The active may be used of an action performed at the bidding of the subject: <span lang="grc">Κῦρος τὰ βασίλεια κατέκαυσεν</span> _Cyrus burnt down the palace_ (i.e. had it burnt down) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.10">X. A. 1.4.10</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">ἀποκτείνειν</span> _put to death_, <span lang="grc">θάπτειν</span> _bury_, <span lang="grc">οἰκοδομεῖν</span> _build_, <span lang="grc">παιδεύειν</span> _instruct_, <span lang="grc">ἀνακηρΰττειν</span> _publicly proclaim_.

##### <a id="s1712"></a> §1712

An infinitive limiting the meaning of an adjective is usually active where English employs the passive (cp. [2006](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s2006)).
