# THE VERB: VOICES: MIDDLE VOICE

##### <a id="s1713"></a> §1713

The middle voice shows that the action is performed with special reference to the subject: <span lang="grc">λοῦμαι</span> _I wash myself_.

##### <a id="s1714"></a> §1714

The middle represents the subject as doing something in which he is interested. He may do something _to himself, for himself_, or he may act with something _belonging to himself_.

##### <a id="s1715"></a> §1715

The future middle is often ([807](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s807)), the first aorist middle is almost never, used passively.

##### <a id="s1716"></a> §1716

The object of the middle (1) may belong in the sphere of the subject, as his property, etc.: <span lang="grc">λούομαι τὰ̄ς χεῖρας</span> _I wash my hands_, or (2) it may be brought into the sphere of the subject: <span lang="grc">τοὺς ὁπλί̄τᾱς μετεπέμψαντο</span> _they sent for the hoplites_, or (3) it may be removed from the sphere of the subject: <span lang="grc">ἀποδίδομὰι τὴν οἰκίᾱν</span> _I sell my house_ (lit. _give away_). Here the object is also the property of the subject.

##### <a id="s1717"></a> §1717

The **Direct Reflexive Middle** represents the subject as acting directly _on himself_. _Self_ is here the direct object. So with verbs expressing external and natural acts, as the verbs of the toilet: <span lang="grc">ἀλείφεσθαι</span> _anoint oneself_, <span lang="grc">λοῦσθαι</span> _wash oneself;_ and <span lang="grc">κοσμεῖσθαι</span> _adorn oneself_, <span lang="grc">στεφανοῦσθαι</span> _crown oneself;_ <span lang="grc">γυμνάζεσθαι</span> _exercise oneself_.

**a.** The direct reflexive idea is far more frequently conveyed by the active and a reflexive pronoun, 1723.

**b.** The part affected may be added in the accusative: <span lang="grc">ἐπαίσατο τὸν μηρόν</span> _he smote his thigh_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.3.6">X. C. 7.3.6</cite>.

##### <a id="s1718"></a> §1718

So with many other verbs, as <span lang="grc">ἵστασθαι</span> _stand_ (_place oneself_), <span lang="grc">τρέπεσθαι</span> _turn_ (lit. _turn oneself_), <span lang="grc">δηλοῦσθαι</span> _show oneself_, <span lang="grc">τάττεσθαι</span> _post oneself_, <span lang="grc">ἀπολογεῖσθαι</span> _defend oneself_ (_argue oneself off_), <span lang="grc">φαίνεσθαι</span> _show oneself, appear_, <span lang="grc">παρασκευάζεσθαι</span> _prepare onself_, <span lang="grc">ἀπόλλυσθαι</span> _destroy oneself, perish_.

##### <a id="s1719"></a> §1719

The **Indirect Reflexive Middle** represents the subject as acting _for himself, with reference to himself_, or _with something belonging to himself_. _Self_ is often here the indirect object. So <span lang="grc">πορίζεσθαι</span> _provide for oneself_ (<span lang="grc">πορίζειν</span> _provide_), <span lang="grc">φυλάττεσθαι</span> _guard against_ (<span lang="grc">φυλάττειν</span> <!-- page 391 --> _keep guard_), <span lang="grc">αἱρεῖσθαι</span> _choose_ (_take for oneself_), <span lang="grc">παρέχεσθαι</span> _furnish_ (<span lang="grc">παρέχειν</span> _offer_, _present_).

##### <a id="s1720"></a> §1720

Cases in which the object is to be removed from the sphere of the subject may be resolved into the dative _for oneself_ ([1483](../18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1483))_:_ <span lang="grc">τὴν ῥᾳθῡμίᾱν ἀποθέσθαι</span> _to lay aside your indolence_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.46">D. 8.46</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐτρέψαντο τοὺς ἱππέᾱς</span> _they routed the cavalry_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.98">T. 6.98</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοὺς ἐχθροὺς ἀμύ̄νεσθαι</span> _to ward off the enemy for themselves_, i.e. _to defend themselves against the enemy_ 1. 144.

##### <a id="s1721"></a> §1721

The middle often denotes that the subject acts with something belonging to himself (material objects, means, powers). It is often used of acts done willingly. Thus, <span lang="grc">παρέχεσθαι</span> _furnish from one's own resources_, <span lang="grc">ἐπαγγέλλεσθαι</span> _promise_, _make profession of_, <span lang="grc">τίθεσθαι τὴν ψῆφον</span> _give one's vote_, <span lang="grc">τίθεσθαι τὰ ὅπλα</span> _ground arms_, <span lang="grc">ἀποδείξασθαι γνώμην</span> _set forth one's opinion_, <span lang="grc">λαμβάνεσθαί τινος</span> _put one's hand on_ (seize) _something_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐσπασμένοι τὰ ξίφη</span> _having drawn their swords_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.4.16">X. A. 7.4.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">παῖδας ἐκκεκομισμένοι ἦσαν</span> _they had removed their children_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.78">T. 2.78</cite>, <span lang="grc">τροπαῖον στησάμενοι</span> _having set up a trophy_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.4.7">X. H. 2.4.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅπλα πορίσασθαι</span> _to procure arms for themselves_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.9">T. 4.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁπλί̄τᾱς μετεπέμψατο</span> _he sent for hoplites_ 7. 31, <span lang="grc">γυναῖκα ἠγαγόμην</span> _I married_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 1.6">L. 1.6</cite>.

##### <a id="s1722"></a> §1722

Under the indirect middle belong the periphrases of <span lang="grc">ποιεῖσθαι</span> with verbal nouns instead of the simple verb (cp. [1754](04-passive-voice.md#s1754)). <span lang="grc">ποιεῖν</span> with the same nouns means _to bring about, effect_, _fashion_, etc.

<span lang="grc">εἰρήνην ποιεῖσθαι</span> _make peace_ (of one nation at war with another).

<span lang="grc">εἰρήνην ποιεῖν</span> _bring about a peace_ (between opponents, nations at war: of an individual).

<span lang="grc">θήρᾱν ποιεῖσθαι</span> ( = <span lang="grc">θηρᾶν</span> _hunt_, <span lang="grc">θήρᾱν ποιεῖν</span> _arrange a hunt_.

<span lang="grc">λόγον ποιεῖσθαι</span> ( = <span lang="grc">λέγειν</span>) _deliver a speech_, <span lang="grc">λόγον ποιεῖν</span> _compose a speech_.

<span lang="grc">ναυμαχίᾱν ποιεῖσθαι</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ναυμαχεῖν</span>) _fight a naval battle_.

<span lang="grc">ναυμαχίᾱν ποιεῖν</span> _bring on a naval battle_ (of the commander).

<span lang="grc">ὁδὸν ποιεῖσθαι</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ὁδεύειν</span>) _make a journey_, <span lang="grc">ὁδὸν ποιεῖν</span> _build a road_.

<span lang="grc">πόλεμον ποιεῖσθαι</span> _wage war_, <span lang="grc">πόλεμον ποιεῖν</span> _bring about a war_.

<span lang="grc">σπονδὰ̄ς ποιεῖσθαι</span> _conclude_ (_make_) _a treaty_ , or _truce_.

<span lang="grc">σπονδὰ̄ς ποιεῖν</span> _bring about a treaty_ , or _truce_.

##### <a id="s1723"></a> §1723

**Active and Reflexive**.—Instead of the direct middle the active voice with the reflexive pronoun is usually employed; often of difficult and unnatural actions (especially with <span lang="grc">αὐτὸς ἑαυτόν</span>, etc.).

<span lang="grc">τὰ ὅπλα παρέδοσαν καὶ σφᾶς αὐτούς</span> _they surrendered their arms and themselves_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.38">T. 4.38</cite>, <span lang="grc">μισθώσᾱς αὑτόν</span> _hiring himself out_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.29">D. 19.29</cite> (not <span lang="grc">μισθωσάμενος</span>, which means _hiring for himself_), <span lang="grc">καταλέλυκε τὴν αὐτὸς αὑτοῦ δυναστείᾱν</span> _he himself has put an end to his own sovereignty_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.233">Aes. 3.233</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἠτί̄μωκεν ἑαυτόν</span> _he has dishonoured himself_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.103">D. 21.103</cite>. But regularly <span lang="grc">ἀπάγχεσθαι</span> _hang oneself_ ([1717](#s1717)).

**a.** The active and a reflexive pronoun in the gen. or dat. may be used for the simple middle when the reflexive notion is emphatic: <span lang="grc">καταλείπειν συγγράμματα ἑαυτῶν</span> _to leave behind them their written compositions_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 257d">P. Phae. 257d</cite>.

##### <a id="s1724"></a> §1724

**Middle and Reflexive**.—The reflexive pronoun may be used with the middle: <span lang="grc">ἑαυτὸν ἀποκρύπτεσθαι</span> _to hide himself_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 393c">P. R. 393c</cite>; often for emphasis, as in contrasts: <span lang="grc">οἱ μέν φᾱσι βασιλέᾱ κελεῦσαί τινα ἐπισφάξαι αὐτὸν Κύ̄ρῳ, οἱ δ᾽</span> <!-- page 392 --> <span lang="grc">ἑαυτὸν ἐπισφάξασθαι</span> _some say that the king issued orders for some one to slay him_ (Artapates) _over_ (the body of) _Cyrus_, _while others say that he slew himself with his own hand_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.29">X. A. 1.8.29</cite>, cp. also <span lang="grc">τί τὴν πόλιν προσῆκε ποιεῖν, ἀρχὴν καὶ τυραννίδα τῶν Ἑλλήνων ὀρῶσαν ἑαυτῷ κατασκευαξόμενον Φίλιππον</span>; _what did it beseem the city to do when it saw Philip compassing for himself dominion and despotic sway over the Greeks?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.66">D. 18.66</cite>.

##### <a id="s1725"></a> §1725

**The Causative Middle** denotes that the subject has something done by another for himself: <span lang="grc">ἐγὼ γάρ σε ταῦτα ἐδιδαξάμην</span> _for I had you taught this_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.6.2">X. C. 1.6.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">παρατίθεσθαι σῖτον</span> _to have food served up_ 8. 6. 12, <span lang="grc">ὅσοι ὅπλα ἀφῄρηνται, ταχὺ ἄλλα ποιήσονται</span> _all who have had their arms taken from them will soon get others made_ 6. 1. 12, <span lang="grc">ἑαυτῷ σκηνὴν κατεσκευάσατο</span> _he had a tent prepared for himself_ 2. 1. 30.

**a.** This force does not belong exclusively to the middle; cp. [1711](02-active-voice.md#s1711).

##### <a id="s1726"></a> §1726

**Reciprocal Middle**.—With a dual or plural subject the middle may indicate a reciprocal relation. So with _verbs of contending_, _conversing_ (_questioning_, _replying_), _greeting_, _embracing_, etc. The reciprocal middle is often found with compounds of <span lang="grc">διά</span>.

<span lang="grc">οἱ ἀ̄θληταὶ ἠγωνίζοντο</span> _the athletes contended_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.6">T. 1.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">καταστάντες ἐμάχοντο</span> _when they had got into position they fought_ 1. 49, <span lang="grc">ἀνὴρ ἀνδρὶ διελέγοντο</span> _they conversed man with man_ 8. 93, <span lang="grc">ἐπιμείγνυσθαι ἀλλήλοις</span> _to have friendly intercourse with one another_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.4.5">X. C. 7.4.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ταῦτα διανεμοῦνται</span> _they will divide this up among themselves_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 21.14">L. 21.14</cite>. So <span lang="grc">αἰτιᾶσθαι</span> _accuse_, <span lang="grc">λῡμαίνεσθαι</span> _maltreat_, <span lang="grc">μέμφεσθαι</span> _blame_, <span lang="grc">ἁμιλλᾶσθαι</span> _vie_, <span lang="grc">παρακελεύεσθαι</span> _encourage one another_.

**a.** The active may also be employed, as <span lang="grc">πολεμεῖν</span> _wage war_.

**b.** Some of these verbs have a passive aorist form, as <span lang="grc">διελέχθην</span> ([812](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s812)).

##### <a id="s1727"></a> §1727

The reciprocal relation may also be expressed (1) by the use of the reflexive pronoun (cp. [1724](#s1724)) with the active: <span lang="grc">φθονοῦσιν ἑαυτοῖς</span> _they are mutually envious_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.5.16">X. M. 3.5.16</cite>; (2) by the use of <span lang="grc">ἀλλήλων</span>, etc., with the active: <span lang="grc">ἀμφισβητοῦμεν ἀλλήλοις</span> _we are at variance with one another_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 263a">P. Phae. 263a</cite>; (3) by repetition of the noun: <span lang="grc">πτωχὸς πτωχῷ φθονέει</span> = _beggars envy each other_ Hesiod W. D. 26. The reflexive pronouns and <span lang="grc">ἀλλήλων</span>, etc., may also be added to the middle.

##### <a id="s1728"></a> §1728

**Differences between Active and Middle**.—As contrasted with the active, the middle lays stress on the conscious activity, bodily or mental participation, of the agent.

In verbs that possess both active and middle: <span lang="grc">βουλεύεσθαι</span> _deliberate_, <span lang="grc">βουλεύειν</span> _plan_, <span lang="grc">σταθμᾶν</span> _measure_, <span lang="grc">σταθμᾶσθαι</span> _calculate_, <span lang="grc">σκοπεῖν</span> _look at_, <span lang="grc">σκοπεῖσθαι</span> _consider_, <span lang="grc">ἔχεσθαι</span> _cling to_, <span lang="grc">παύεσθαι</span> _cease_ ([1734](#s1734). [14](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s14)). The force of the middle often cannot be reproduced in translation (<span lang="grc">ἀκούεσθαι, τῑμᾶσθαι, ἀριθμεῖσθαι, ἀπορεῖσθαι</span>), and in some other cases it may not have been felt, as in <span lang="grc">ὁρᾶσθαι</span> in poetry (<span lang="grc">προορᾶσθαι</span> occurs in prose).

**a.** Many such verbs form their futures from the middle: <span lang="grc">ἀκούσομαι, ᾁσομαι, ἁμαρτήσομαι</span>. See [805](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s805).

**b.** In verbs in <span lang="grc">-ευω</span>, the middle signifies that the subject is acting in a manner appropriate to his state or condition: <span lang="grc">πολῑτεύειν</span> _be a citizen_, <span lang="grc">πολῑτεύεσθαι</span> _act as_ <!-- page 393 --> _a citizen, perform one's civic duties;_ <span lang="grc">πρεσβεύειν</span> _be an envoy_, <span lang="grc">πρεσβεύεσθαι</span> _negotiate as envoy_ or _send envoys_ (of the State in its negotiations). But this force of the middle is not always apparent.

##### <a id="s1729"></a> §1729

**Middle Deponents** ([810](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s810)) often denote bodily or mental action (feeling and thinking): <span lang="grc">ἅλλεσθαι</span> _jump_, <span lang="grc">πέτεσθαι</span> _fly_, <span lang="grc">ὀρχεῖσθαι</span> _dance_, <span lang="grc">οἴχεσθαι</span> _be gone_, <span lang="grc">δέρκεσθαι</span> _look;_ <span lang="grc">βούλεσθαι</span> _wish_, <span lang="grc">αἰσθάνεσθαι</span> _perceive_, <span lang="grc">ἀκροᾶσθαι</span> _listen_, <span lang="grc">μέμφεσθαι</span> _blame_, <span lang="grc">οἴεσθαι</span> _conjecture_, _think_ (lit. _take omens for oneself_, from <span lang="grc">ὀϝις</span>, Lat. _avis_, _auspicium_), <span lang="grc">ἡγεῖσθαι</span> _consider;_ <span lang="grc">ὀλοφύ̄ρεσθαι</span> _lament_.

**a.** Some of the verbs denoting a functional state or process have the middle either in all forms or only in the future.

**b.** Verbs denoting bodily activity regularly have a middle future, 805-806.

##### <a id="s1730"></a> §1730

Deponent verbs are either direct or indirect middles; direct: <span lang="grc">ὑπισχνεῖσθαι</span> _undertake_, _promise_ (lit. _hold oneself under_); indirect: <span lang="grc">κτᾶσθαι</span> _acquire for oneself_, <span lang="grc">ἀγωνίζεσθαι</span> _contend_ (with one's own powers).

##### <a id="s1731"></a> §1731

The middle may denote more vigorous participation on the part of the subject than the active: <span lang="grc">σεύεσθαι</span> _dart_, but <span lang="grc">θέειν</span> _run_.

##### <a id="s1732"></a> §1732

The active is often used for the middle when it is not of practical importance to mark the interest of the subject in the action. The active implies what the middle expresses. So with <span lang="grc">μεταπέμπειν</span> _send for_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.15">T. 7.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">δηλώσαντες τὴν γνώμην</span> _setting forth their opinion_ 3. 37, <span lang="grc">τροπαῖον στήσαντες</span> _setting up a trophy_ 7. 5.

##### <a id="s1733"></a> §1733

The passive form may have reflexive force, as <span lang="grc">κῑνηθῆναι</span> _set oneself in motion_, <span lang="grc">ἀπαλλαγῆναι</span> _remove oneself_, <span lang="grc">ἐναντιωθῆναι</span> _oppose oneself_, <span lang="grc">σωθῆναι</span> _save oneself_ (<span lang="grc">σώθητι</span> _save yourself_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 44b">P. Cr. 44b</cite>). Some of these middle passives may take the accusative, as <span lang="grc">αἰσχυνθῆναι</span> _be ashamed before_, <span lang="grc">φοβηθῆναι</span> _be afraid of_, <span lang="grc">καταπληγῆναί τινα</span> _be amazed at some one_. See [814](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s814) ff.

##### <a id="s1734"></a> §1734

List of the chief verbs showing important differences of meaning between active and middle. It will be noted that the active is often transitive, the middle intransitive.

\1. <span lang="grc"> **αἱρεῖν** </span> _take;_ <span lang="grc">αἱρεῖσθαι</span> _choose_.

\2. <span lang="grc">**ἀμύ̄νειν** τί τινι</span> _ward off something from some one_, <span lang="grc">ἀμύ̄νειν τινί</span> _help some one;_ <span lang="grc">ἀμύ̄νεσθαί τι</span> _defend oneself against something_, <span lang="grc">ἀμύ̄νεσθαί τινα</span> _requite some one_.

\3. <span lang="grc"> **ἀποδοῦναι** </span> _give back;_ <span lang="grc">ἀποδόσθαι</span> _sell_ (give away for one's profit).

\4. <span lang="grc"> **ἅπτειν** </span> _attach;_ <span lang="grc">ἅπτεσθαί τινος</span> _touch_.

\5. <span lang="grc"> **ἄρχειν** </span> _begin_, contrasts one beginner of an action with another, as <span lang="grc">ἄρχειν πολέμου</span> _take the aggressive, strike the first blow_ (_bellum movere_), <span lang="grc">ἄρχειν λόγου</span> _be the first to speak_, <span lang="grc">ἦρχε χειρῶν ἀδίκων</span> _he began an unprovoked assault_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 4.11">L. 4.11</cite>; <span lang="grc">ἄρχεσθαι</span> make one's own beginning, as contrasted with the later stages, as <span lang="grc">ἄρχεσθαι πολέμου</span> _begin warlike operations_ (_bellum incipere_), <span lang="grc">ἄρχεσθαι τοῦ λόγου</span> _begin one's speech._ <span lang="grc">πολέμου οὐκ ἄρξομεν, ἀρχομένους δὲ ἀμῡνούμεθα</span> _we shall not take the initiative in the war, but upon those who take it up we shall retaliate_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.144">T. 1.144</cite>.

\6. <span lang="grc"> **γαμεῖν** </span> _marry_ (of the man, _ducere_); <span lang="grc">γαμεῖσθαι</span> _marry_ (of the woman, _nubere_).

\7. <span lang="grc">**γράφειν** νόμον</span> _propose a law_ (said of the maker of a law whether or not he is himself subject to it); <span lang="grc">γράφεσθαι γραφήν</span> _draw up an indictment_ for a public <!-- page 394 --> offence, <span lang="grc">γράφεσθαί τινα</span> _bring suit against some one_ (have him written down in the magistrates' records).

\8. <span lang="grc"> **δανείζειν** </span> (make of anything a <span lang="grc">δάνος</span> _loan_) i.e. _put out at interest, lend;_ <span lang="grc">δανείζεσθαι</span> (have a <span lang="grc">δάνος</span> _made to oneself_) _have lent to one, borrow at interest_.

\9. <span lang="grc"> **δικάζειν** </span> _give judgment;_ <span lang="grc">δικάζεσθαι</span> (<span lang="grc">δίκην τινί</span>) _go to law with a person_, _conduct a case_ (properly _get some one to give judgment_).

\10. <span lang="grc"> **ἐπιψηφίζειν** </span> _put to vote_ (of the presiding officer); <span lang="grc">ἐπιψηφίζεσθαι</span> _vote_, _decree_ (of the people).

\11. <span lang="grc"> **ἔχειν** </span> _hold;_ <span lang="grc">ἔχεσθαί τ&lt;\*&gt;νος</span> _hold on to, be close to_.

\12. <span lang="grc"> **θύ̄ειν** </span> _sacrifice;_ <span lang="grc">θύ̄εσθαι</span> _take auspices_ (of a general, etc.).

\13. <span lang="grc"> **μισθοῦν** </span> (put a <span lang="grc">μισθός</span>, _rent_, on anything) i.e. _let for hire_ (_locare_); <span lang="grc">μισθοῦσθαι</span> (lay a <span lang="grc">μισθός</span> upon oneself) i.e. _hire_ (_conducere_). Cp. [1723](#s1723).

\14. <span lang="grc"> **παύειν** </span> _make to cease, stop_ (trans.); <span lang="grc">παύεσθαι</span> _cease_ (intr.). But <span lang="grc">παῦε λέγων</span> _stop talking_.

\15. <span lang="grc"> **πείθειν** </span> _persuade;_ <span lang="grc">πείθεσθαι</span> _obey_ (_persuade oneself_); <span lang="grc">πέποιθα</span> _I trust_.

\16. <span lang="grc">**τιθέναι** νόμον</span> _frame_ or _propose a law_ for others (said of the lawgiver, _legem ferre_ or _rogare_); <span lang="grc">τίθεσθαι νόμον</span> _make a law_ for one's own interest, for one's own State (said of the State legislating, _legem sciscere_ or _iubere_). <span lang="grc">αὐτοὺς</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀγράφους νόμους</span>) <span lang="grc">οἱ ἄνθρωποι ἔθεντο . . . θεοὺς οἶμαι τοὺς νόμους τούτους τοῖς ἀνθρώποις θεῖναι</span> _men did not make the unwritten laws for themselves, but I think the gods made these laws for men_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.4.19">X. M. 4.4.19</cite>.

\17. <span lang="grc">**τῑμωρεῖν** τινι</span> _avenge some one_, <span lang="grc">τῑμωρεῖν τινά τινι</span> _punish A for B's satisfaction;_ <span lang="grc">τῑμωρεῖσθαί τινα</span> _avenge oneself on_ (punish) _some one_.

\18. <span lang="grc">**τίνειν** δίκην</span> _pay a penalty_ (_poenas dare_); <span lang="grc">τίνεσθαι δίκην</span> _exact a penalty_ (_poenas sumere_).

\19. <span lang="grc">**φυλάττειν** τινά</span> _watch some one;_ <span lang="grc">φυλάττεσθαί τινα</span> _be on one's guard against some one_.

\20. <span lang="grc"> **χρᾶν** </span> _give an oracle_ , and _lend;_ <span lang="grc">χρᾶσθαι</span> _consult an oracle_ , and _use_.
