# THE VERB: VOICES: THE TENSES

##### <a id="s1850"></a> §1850

By the tenses (‘tense’ from _tempus_) are denoted:

\1. The time of an action: present, past, future.

\2. The stage of an action: action continued or repeated (in process of development), action simply brought to pass (simple occurrence), action completed with a permanent result.

**a.** The time of an action is either _absolute_ or _relative_. Time that is absolutely present, past, or future is reckoned from the time of the speaker or writer. Time that is relatively present, past, or future in dependent clauses is reckoned from the time of some verb in the same sentence. In dependent clauses Greek has no special forms to denote the temporal relation of one action to another (antecedent, coincident, subsequent), but leaves the reader to infer whether one action happened _before_, _at the same time as_, or _after_ another action. The aorist is thus often used where English has the pluperfect ([1943](#s1943)). See [1888](#s1888), [1944](#s1944). Unless special reference is made to relative time, the expressions “kind of time,” “time of an action,” in this book are used of absolute time.

**b.** In independent clauses only the tenses of the indicative denote absolute time; in dependent clauses they express relative time. The tenses of the subjunctive, optative, imperative, infinitive and participle do not refer to the differences in kind of time. Thus <span lang="grc">γράφειν</span> and <span lang="grc">γράψαι</span> _to write_, <span lang="grc">γεγραφέναι</span> _to finish writing_, may be used of the present, the past, or the future according to the context. On the tenses of the optative, infinitive, and participle in indirect discourse see [1862](#s1862), [1866](#s1866), 1874. The future infinitive may be used, outside of indirect discourse, to lay stress on the idea of futurity ([1865 d](#s1865)).

**c.** Even in the indicative the actual time may be different from that which would seem to be denoted by the tense employed. Thus the speaker or writer may imagine the past as present, and use the present in setting forth an event that happened before his time ([1883](#s1883)); or may use the aorist or perfect of an event that has not yet occurred ([1934](#s1934), [1950](#s1950)). <!-- page 413 -->

**d.** In the subjunctive, optative (except in indirect discourse), and imperative the kind of time is implied only by the mood-forms, not by the tenses. The relation of the time of one action to the time of another usually has to be inferred in all the moods.

**e.** The stage of an action is expressed by all the tenses of all the different moods (including the participle and infinitive).

**f.** The action of the verb of a subordinate clause may _overlap_ with that of the verb of the main clause. See [2388](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/12-temporal-clauses.md#s2388).

## KIND OF TIME

##### <a id="s1851"></a> §1851

Only in the indicative do the tenses show time absolutely present, past, or future.

a. Present time is denoted by

\1. The Present: <span lang="grc">γράφω</span> _I write_, _am writing_.

\2. The Perfect: <span lang="grc">γέγραφα</span> _I have written_.

b. Past time is denoted by

\1. The Imperfect: <span lang="grc">ἔγραφον</span> _I wrote_, _was writing_.

\2. The Aorist: <span lang="grc">ἔγραψα</span> _I wrote_.

\3. The Pluperfect: <span lang="grc">ἐγεγράφη</span> _I had written_.

N.—The only past tenses are the augmented tenses.

c. Future time is denoted by

\1. The Future: <span lang="grc">γράψω</span> _I shall write_.

\2. The Future Perfect: <span lang="grc">γεγράψεται</span> _it will have been written_, <span lang="grc">τεθνήξω</span> _I shall be dead_ (_shall have died_).

## STAGE OF ACTION

##### <a id="s1852"></a> §1852

Every form of the verb denotes the stage of the action.

a. Continued action is denoted by the present stem:

\1. Present: <span lang="grc">γράφω</span> _I am writing_, <span lang="grc">πείθω</span> _I am persuading_ (_trying to persuade_), <span lang="grc">ἀνθεῖ</span> _is in bloom_.

\2. Imperfect: <span lang="grc">ἔγραφον</span> _I was writing_, <span lang="grc">ἔπειθον</span> _I was persuading_ (_trying to persuade_), <span lang="grc">ἤνθει</span> _was in bloom_.

\3. Future: <span lang="grc">γράψω</span> _I shall write_ (_shall be writing_), <span lang="grc">βασιλεύσει</span> _he will reign_.

N.—Continued action is incomplete: hence nothing is stated as to the conclusion. Thus <span lang="grc">φεύγει</span> _he flees_ does not state whether or not the subject succeeded in escaping.

b. Completed action with permanent result is denoted by the perfect stem:

\1. Perfect: <span lang="grc">γέγραφα ἐπιστολήν</span> _I have written a letter_ (and it is _now_ finished), <span lang="grc">ἤνθηκε</span> _has bloomed_ (and is in flower).

\2. Pluperfect: <span lang="grc">ἐγεγράφη ἐπιστολήν</span> _I had written a letter_ (and it was _then_ finished), <span lang="grc">ἠνθήκει</span> _had bloomed_ (and was in flower). <!-- page 414 -->

\3. Future Perfect: <span lang="grc">γεγράψεται</span> _it will have been written_, <span lang="grc">τεθνήξει</span> _he will be dead_.

c. Action simply brought to pass (simple attainment) is denoted by the

\1. Aorist: <span lang="grc">ἔγραψα</span> _I wrote_, <span lang="grc">ἔπεισα</span> _I persuaded_ (_succeeded in persuading_), <span lang="grc">ἐβασίλευσε</span> _he became king_ or _he was king_, <span lang="grc">ἤνθησε</span> _burst into flower_ or _was in flower_.

\2. Future: <span lang="grc">γράψω</span> _I shall write_, <span lang="grc">βασιλεύσει</span> _he will become king_.

N.—The aorist tense (<span lang="grc">ἀόριστος χρόνος</span> from <span lang="grc">ὁρίζω</span> _define;_ unlimited, indefinite, or undefined time) is so named because it does not show the limitation (<span lang="grc">ὅρος</span>) of continuance (expressed by the imperfect) or of completion with permanent result (expressed by the perfect).

##### <a id="s1853"></a> §1853

The present stem may denote the simple action of the verb in present time without regard to its continuance; as <span lang="grc">θαυμάζω</span> _I am seized with astonishment_, <span lang="grc">ἀστράπτει</span> _it lightens_ (once or continually), <span lang="grc">δίδωμι</span> _I make a present_. This is called the _aoristic present_. On inceptive verbs, see [526](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/02-1-present-system.md#s526).

##### <a id="s1854"></a> §1854

The future stem may denote either continued action (as in the present) or simple occurrence of the action of the verb (as in the aorist). Thus <span lang="grc">γράψω</span> _I shall be writing_ or _I shall write_. See [1910 b](#s1910).

##### <a id="s1855"></a> §1855

Some verbs are, by their meaning, restricted to the tenses of continued action, as <span lang="grc">ὁρᾶν</span> _behold_, <span lang="grc">φέρειν</span> _carry;_ others are exclusively aoristic, as <span lang="grc">ἰδεῖν</span> properly _glance at_, <span lang="grc">ἐνεγκεῖν</span> _bring_. Verbs expressing different kinds of action in their several tenses (as <span lang="grc">ὁρᾶν, ἰδεῖν</span>) unite to form a verbal system.

##### <a id="s1856"></a> §1856

The difference between the present stem (present and imperfect) and the aorist stem may be compared to the difference between a _line_ and a _point_ (both starting point and end). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔρχεσθαι</span> _go_, <span lang="grc">ἐλθεῖν</span> _come_, _arrive;_ <span lang="grc">φέρειν</span> _carry_, <span lang="grc">ἐνεγκεῖν</span> _bring;_ <span lang="grc">ἄγειν</span> _accompany_, _lead_, <span lang="grc">ἀγαγεῖν</span> _bring to a goal_.

##### <a id="s1857"></a> §1857

For the ‘progressive’ tenses of English (_is walking, has been giving_, etc.) Greek has no exact equivalent. The periphrasis of the present participle with <span lang="grc">ἐστί</span>, etc. is employed to adjectivize the participle or to describe or characterize the subject like an adjective, i.e. the subject has a quality which it may display in action. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀρέσκοντές ἐσμεν</span> _we are acceptable_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.38">T. 1.38</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ πάντ᾽ ἀναδεχόμενος καὶ εἰς αὑτὸν ποιούμενος τὰ τούτων ἁμαρτήματ᾽ ἐστίν</span> _and he takes upon himself and adopts all their misdeeds_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.36">D. 19.36</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἐστί</span> may be emphatic: <span lang="grc">ἔστι που δίχα διαιρούμενον</span> _there exists a twofold division_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 895d">P. L. 895d</cite>. Some participles have become completely adjectivized: <span lang="grc">συμφέρων</span> _useful_, <span lang="grc">διαφέρων</span> _superior_. Cp. [1961](#s1961).

##### <a id="s1858"></a> §1858

**Primary and Secondary Tenses**.—The primary tenses refer to present and future time (present, future, perfect, and future perfect), the secondary or historical tenses refer to past time (imperfect, aorist, pluperfect).

**a.** The gnomic aorist ([1931 b](#s1931)) is regarded as a primary tense, as is the aorist when used for the perfect ([1940](#s1940)), and the imperfect indicative referring to present time ([1788](07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1788)); the historical present ([1883](#s1883)), as a secondary tense. The subjunctive, optative, and imperative moods in their independent uses point to the future, and all their tenses therefore count as primary.

## THE TENSES OUTSIDE OF THE INDICATIVE

<!-- page 415 -->

##### <a id="s1859"></a> §1859

The tenses of the moods except the indicative do not express time in independent sentences.

##### <a id="s1860"></a> §1860

**Subjunctive**.—The subjunctive mood as such refers to the future. The tenses do not refer to differences of time, and denote only the stage of the action (continuance, simple occurrence, completion with permanent result).

Present (continuance): <span lang="grc">τὰ αὑτῶν ἅμα ἐκποριζώμεθα</span> _let us at the same time keep developing our resources_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.82">T. 1.82</cite>; Aorist (simple occurrence): <span lang="grc">πορισώμεθα οὖν πρῶτον τὴν δαπάνην</span> _let us procure the money first_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.83">T. 1.83</cite>; Perfect (completion with permanent result): <span lang="grc">ἵνα, ἢν μὴ ὑπακούωσι, τεθνήκωσιν</span> _that_, _in case they do not submit, they may be put to death_ (lit. _may be dead_ at once) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.74">T. 8.74</cite>. The aorist commonly replaces the more exact perfect because the perfect is rarely used.

**a.** The future time denoted by present or aorist (<span lang="grc">τί ποιῶμεν</span>; or <span lang="grc">τί ποιήσωμεν</span>; _what shall we do?_) may refer, according to the sense, either to the next moment or to some later time. Greek has no subjunctive form denoting an _intention_ to do this or that. In dependent constructions (including general conditions) the action of the present is generally coincident (rarely subsequent), that of the aorist is generally anterior (rarely coincident), to the action of the leading verb: <span lang="grc">χαλεπαίνουσι, ἐπειδὰν αὐτοῖς παραγγέλλω πί̄νειν τὸ φάρμακον</span> _they are angry whenever I bid them drink the poison_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 116c">P. Ph. 116c</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὰν ἅπαντ᾽ ἀκούσητε, κρί̄νατε</span> _when you_ (shall) _have heard everything, decide_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.14">D. 4.14</cite>. The use of the aorist of time relatively anterior to the action of the leading verb ( = Lat. future perfect) is, like its other references to relative time, only an inference from the connection of the thought ([1850 a](#s1850)).

**b.** Present and aorist subjunctive are occasionally used in the same sentence without any great difference in sense (<cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.2.6">X. C. 1.2.6</cite>-7, 5. 5. [13](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s13)).

**c.** An independent or dependent subjunctive may be ingressive ([1924](#s1924)): <span lang="grc">ἢν γὰρ ὁ Πλοῦτος νυνὶ βλέψῃ</span> _for if now Plutus recovers his sight_ Ar. Pl. 494.

**d.** In general conditions ([2336](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2336)) the subjunctive refers to general time, denoting what holds true now and at all times.

##### <a id="s1861"></a> §1861

**Optative** (not in indirect discourse).—The reference is always to future time. The tenses do not refer to differences of time, and denote only the stage of the action.

Present (continuance): <span lang="grc">πλούσιον δὲ νομίζοιμι τὸν σοφόν</span> _may I_ (always) _count the wise man wealthy_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 279b">P. Phae. 279b</cite>; Aorist (simple occurrence): <span lang="grc">εἰ γὰρ γένοιτο</span> _would that it might happen_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.1.38">X. C. 6.1.38</cite>; Perfect (completion with permanent result): <span lang="grc">τεθναίης</span> _die_ (lit. _may you be dead_) <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 6.164"><span lang="grc">Ζ</span> 164</cite>.

**a.** In general conditions ([2336](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2336)) the optative is used of past time.

**b.** In dependent constructions (including general conditions) the action of the present is generally coincident (rarely anterior), that of the aorist generally anterior (rarely coincident), to the action of the leading verb: <span lang="grc">εἴ τις τάδε παραβαίνοι, ἐναγὴς ἔστω τοὖ Απόλλωνος</span> _if any one violates this, let him be accurst of_ <!-- page 416 --> _Apollo_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.110">Aes. 3.110</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὴ δὲ ἀνοιχθείη</span> (<span lang="grc">τὸ δεσμωτήριον</span>), <span lang="grc">εἰσῇμεν παρὰ τὸν Σωκράτη</span> _whenever the prison was opened, we_ (always) _went in to Socrates_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 59d">P. Ph. 59d</cite>. The aorist is often preferred to the more exact perfect because the perfect was rarely used.

**c.** An independent or dependent optative may be ingressive ([1924](#s1924)): <span lang="grc">εἰ πολεμήσαιμεν δι᾽ Ὠρωπόν, οὐδὲν ἂν ἡμᾶς παθεῖν ἡγοῦμαι</span> _if we should enter upon a war on account of Oropus_, _I think we should suffer nothing_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 5.16">D. 5.16</cite>.

##### <a id="s1862"></a> §1862

**Optative** (in indirect discourse).—When the optative in indirect discourse represents the indicative after a past tense of a _verb of saying_ or _thinking_, each tense does denote time (as well as stage of action) relatively to that of the leading verb.

**a.** The present optative represents the imperfect as well as the present indicative.

**b.** The future optative (first in Pindar) occurs only in indirect discourse after _verbs of saying_ and _thinking_, in object clauses after <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span>, 2212, and in other indirect expressions of thought.

**c.** When the optative in indirect discourse represents the subjunctive ([2619 b](../31-dependent-substantive-clauses-2574-2635.md#s2619)), its tenses denote only stage of action.

##### <a id="s1863"></a> §1863

**a.** Present opt. = present indic.: <span lang="grc">ἀνηρώτᾱ τί βούλοιντο</span> _he demanded what they wanted_ ( = <span lang="grc">τί βούλεσθε</span>;) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.4">X. A. 2.3.4</cite>.

**b.** Present opt. = imperf. indic.: <span lang="grc">διηγοῦντο ὅτι ἐπὶ τοὺς πολεμίους πλέοιεν</span> _they explained that they kept sailing against the enemy_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἐπλέομεν</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.7.5">X. H. 1.7.5</cite>.

**c.** Future opt. = future indic.: <span lang="grc">ὅ τι ποιήσοι οὐδὲ τούτοις εἶπε</span> _he did not tell even these what he would do_ ( = <span lang="grc">ποιήσω</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.2.2">X. A. 2.2.2</cite>.

**d.** Aorist opt. = aorist indic.: <span lang="grc">ἠρώτᾱ τί πάθοιεν</span> _he asked what had happened to them_ ( = <span lang="grc">τί ἐπάθετε</span>;) <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.3.19">X. C. 2.3.19</cite>.

**e.** Perfect opt. = perfect indic.: <span lang="grc">ἔλεγον ὅτι οἱ μετὰ Δημοσθένους παραδεδώκοιεν σφᾶς αὐτούς</span> _they said that the troops of Demosthenes had surrendered_ ( = <span lang="grc">παραδεδώκᾱσι</span>) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.83">T. 7.83</cite>.

##### <a id="s1864"></a> §1864

**Imperative**.—The imperative always implies future time. The tenses do not refer to differences of time, and denote only the stage of the action.

**a. Present** (continuance): <span lang="grc">τοὺς γονεῖς τἱ̄μᾱ</span> _honour thy parents_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.16">I. 1.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">πάντα τἀ̄ληθῆ λέγε</span> _tell_ (go on and tell in detail) _the whole truth_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 1.18">L. 1.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοὺς ἵππους ἐκείνοις δίδοτε</span> _offer the horses to them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.5.47">X. C. 4.5.47</cite>.

**b. Aorist** (simple occurrence): <span lang="grc">βλέψον πρὸς τὰ ὄρη</span> _look_ (_cast a glance_) _toward the mountains_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.1.20">X. A. 4.1.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰπέ</span> _state_ (in a word) <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 24d">P. A. 24d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡμῖν τοὺς ἵππους δότε</span> _give the horses to us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.5.47">X. C. 4.5.47</cite>.

**c. Perfect** (completion with permanent result): <span lang="grc">τετάχθω</span> _let him take his place_ (and stay there) <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 562a">P. R. 562a</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰρήσθω</span> _let it have been said_ (once for all) 503 b.

N.—The perfect active and middle are generally used as presents (<span lang="grc">τεθνάτω</span> _let him be put to death_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 938c">P. L. 938c</cite>, <span lang="grc">μέμνησθε</span> _remember_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 40.30">D. 40.30</cite>). The perfect passive (in the third person) is used of a fixed decision concerning what is to be done or has been done. <!-- page 417 -->

##### <a id="s1865"></a> §1865

**Infinitive** (not in indirect discourse).—The tenses of the infinitive (without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>) not in indirect discourse have no time of themselves and express only the stage of the action; their (relative) time depends on the context and is that of the leading verb (present, past, or future). The infinitive may have the article ([2025 f](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s2025)f.).

**a. Present** (continuance): <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ βουλεύεσθαι ἔτι ὥρᾱ, ἀλλὰ βεβουλεῦσθαι</span> _it is time no longer to be making up one's mind, but to have it made up_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 46a">P. Cr. 46a</cite>.

**b. Aorist** (simple occurrence): <span lang="grc">τοῦ πιεῖν ἐπιθῡμίᾱ</span> _the desire of obtaining drink_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.84">T. 7.84</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἤρξατο γενέσθαι</span> _began to be_ 1. 103, but <span lang="grc">ἤρχετο γίγνεσθαι</span> 3. 18 (the tense of <span lang="grc">γίγνομαι</span> depends on that of <span lang="grc">ἄρχομαι</span>; _not_ <span lang="grc">ἤρξατο γίγνεσθαι</span>), <span lang="grc">δεῖ τοὺς ὑπὲρ αὐτοῦ λέγοντας μῑσῆσαι</span> (ingressive) _one must conceive an aversion for those who speak in his behalf_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.53">D. 9.53</cite>.

**c. Perfect** (completion with permanent result): see **a.** Often of certainty of action.

**d. Future**.—When the context shows that stress is laid on the idea of futurity, the future infinitive, referring to future time relative to the main verb, is sometimes used instead of the present or aorist: <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀποκωλύ̄σειν δυνατοὶ ὄντες</span> _not being able to prevent_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.28">T. 3.28</cite>, <span lang="grc">πολλοῦ δέω κατ᾽ ἐμαυτοῦ ἐρεῖν</span> _I am far from intending to speak to my own disadvantage_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 37b">P. A. 37b</cite>. On the future infinitive with <span lang="grc">μέλλω</span> see [1959](#s1959).

N. 1.—The action set forth by a dependent present or aorist infinitive (without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>) not in indirect discourse has no time except that which is implied by the context. With verbs signifying _to advise_ or _to command_, and when the infinitive expresses purpose, the reference is to future time. Usually the action of the present and aorist is coincident with or antecedent to that of the main verb. The action of an aorist infinitive with the article and a subject is _not_ always relatively past. The perfect (without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>) has no time apart from the context; its action is usually antecedent.

N. 2.—On the use of the present and aorist with _verbs of promising_, etc., see [1868](#s1868); with <span lang="grc">μέλλω</span>, see [1959](#s1959).

N. 3.—Observe that verbs denoting continuance (as <span lang="grc">μένω</span> _remain_) often appear in the aorist, while verbs of transitory action (as <span lang="grc">ἱ̄έναι</span> _send_, _hurl_) often appear in the present.

N. 4.—Present and aorist occasionally occur in close conjunction without any great difference in meaning, as <span lang="grc">προσήκει ὑ̄μῖν τούτου καταψηφίζεσθαι . . ., δεῖ ὑ̄μᾶς θάνατον αὐτοῦ καταψηφίσασθαι</span> _it is fitting that you vote against him, it is necessary that you pass a vote of death against him_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 13.69">L. 13.69</cite>; cp. <span lang="grc">ναυμαχῆσαι</span> and <span lang="grc">ναυμαχεῖν</span> <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.83">T. 2.83</cite>, <span lang="grc">βασανιστὴς γίγνεσθαι</span> and <span lang="grc">γενέσθαι</span> <cite data-ref="Antiph. 1.10">Ant. 1.10</cite>, 1. 11.

##### <a id="s1866"></a> §1866

**Infinitive** (in indirect discourse).—The tenses of the infinitive in indirect discourse denote the same time relative to that of the leading verb (present, past, or future) as was denoted by the corresponding tenses of the indicative in direct discourse which they represent.

**a.** The present infinitive represents also the imperfect, the perfect infinitive represents also the pluperfect indicative. <!-- page 418 -->

**b.** The action of the present is usually coincident, that of the aorist anterior, to the action of the leading verb.

**c.** The future infinitive is found chiefly in indirect discourse and in analogous constructions. With <span lang="grc">μέλλω</span>, see [1959](#s1959). It may have the article ([2026](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s2026)).

##### <a id="s1867"></a> §1867

**a.** Present = pres. indic.: <span lang="grc">φημὶ ταῦτα μὲν φλυᾱρίᾱς εἶναι</span> _I say this is nonsense_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἐστί</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.18">X. A. 1.3.18</cite>.

**b.** Present = imperf. indic: <span lang="grc">Κτησίᾱς ἰ̄ᾶσθαι αὐτὸς τὸ τραῦμά φησι</span> _Ktesias asserts that he himself cured the wound_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἰ̄ώμην</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.26">X. A. 1.8.26</cite>. With <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, 1846 a.

**c.** Future = fut. indic.: <span lang="grc">ἔφη ἢ ἄξειν Λακεδαιμονίους ἢ αὐτοῦ ἀποκτενεῖν</span> _he said that he would either bring the Lacedaemonians or kill them on the spot_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἄξω, ἀποκτενῶ</span>) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.28">T. 4.28</cite>.

**d.** Aorist = aor. indic.: <span lang="grc">ἐνταῦθα λέγεται Ἀπόλλων ἐκδεῖραι Μαρσύᾱν</span> _there Apollo is said to have flayed Marsyas_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἐξέδειρε</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.8">X. A. 1.2.8</cite>. With <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, 1848 a.

**e.** Perfect = perf. ind.: <span lang="grc">φησὶ ἐγκώμιον γεγραφέναι</span> _he says that he has written an encomium_ ( = <span lang="grc">γέγραφα</span>) <cite data-ref="Isoc. 10.14">I. 10.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔφασαν τεθνάναι τὸν ἄνδρα</span> _they said the man was dead_ ( = <span lang="grc">τέθνηκε</span>) <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.29">Ant. 5.29</cite>.

**f.** Perfect = pluperf. ind.: <span lang="grc">λέγεται ἄνδρα τινὰ ἐκπεπλῆχθαι</span> _it is said that a certain man had been fascinated_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἐξεπέπληκτο</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.27">X. C. 1.4.27</cite>. With <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, 1849.

##### <a id="s1868"></a> §1868

**The construction of verbs of hoping, etc.**—Verbs signifying _to hope_, _expect_, _promise_, _threaten_, _swear_, with some others of like meaning, when they refer to a future event, take either the future infinitive (in indirect discourse), or the aorist, less often the present, infinitive (not in indirect discourse). The use of the aorist and present is due to the analogy of _verbs of will_ or _desire_ ([1991](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s1991)) which take an object infinitive not in indirect discourse. The same analogy accounts for the use of <span lang="grc">μή</span> instead of <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> ([2725](../35-negative-sentences.md#s2725)). The present or aorist infinitive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, representing the potential optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, occurs occasionally.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ἐν ἐλπίδι ὢν τὰ τείχη τῶν Ἀθηναίων αἱρήσειν</span> _hoping that he would capture the walls of the Athenians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.46">T. 7.46</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐλπὶς . . . ἐκτραφῆναι</span> _hope of being brought up_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 19.8">L. 19.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐλπίζει δυνατὸς εἶναι ἄρχειν</span> _he expects to be able to rule_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 573c">P. R. 573c</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔχεις τινὰ ἐλπίδα μὴ ἂν . . . τὴν ναῦν ἀπολέσαι</span>; _have you any expectation that you would not shipwreck the vessel?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.6.38">X. M. 2.6.38</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἐλπίζω</span> with the present infinitive may mean _I feel sure that I am_.

**b.** <span lang="grc">τάχιστα οὐδένα εἰκὸς σὺν αὐτῷ βουλήσεσθαι εἶναι</span> _it is probable that very soon no one will wish to be with him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.3.30">X. C. 5.3.30</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡμᾶς εἰκὸς ἐπικρατῆσαι</span> _it is likely that we shall succeed_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.121">T. 1.121</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ εἰκὸς αὐτοὺς περιουσίᾱν νεῶν ἔχειν</span> _it is not likely that they will continue to have ships to spare_ 3. 13. With <span lang="grc">εἰκός</span> the aorist is preferred.

**c.** <span lang="grc">ὑπέσχετο ταῦτα ποιήσειν</span> _he promised that he would do this_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.14">L. 12.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπέσχετο βουλεύσασθαι</span> (most Mss.) _he promised to deliberate_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.20">X. A. 2.3.20</cite>. The aorist infinitive is especially common with _verbs of promising_ and must refer to the future. With the present infinitive <span lang="grc">ὑπισχνοῦμαι</span> means _I assure_, _profess_, _pledge my word that I am_.

**d.** <span lang="grc">ἀπείλει ἐκτρί̄ψειν</span> _he threatened that he would destroy_ them <cite data-ref="Hdt. 6.37">Hdt. 6.37</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἠπείλησαν ἀποκτεῖναι ἅπαντας</span> _they threatened to kill everybody_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 5.4.7">X. H. 5.4.7</cite>.

**e.** <span lang="grc">δικάσειν ὀμωμόκατε</span> _you have sworn that you will give judgment_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 39.40">D. 39.40</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀναγκάζει τὸν Κερσοβλέπτην ὀμόσαι . . . εἶναι μὲν τὴν ἀρχὴν κοινὴν . . ., πάντας δ᾽ ὑ̄μῖν ἀποδοῦναι τὴν χώρᾱν</span> _he compelled Cersobleptes to swear that the kingdom_ <!-- page 419 --> _should be in common and that they should all restore to you the territory_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.170">D. 23.170</cite>.

**f.** With <span lang="grc">ὄμνῡμι</span> a dependent infinitive may refer to the present, past, or future (e). Thus, <span lang="grc">ὀμνύντες βλέπειν . . . Ἀχιλλέᾱ πάλιν</span> _swearing that they see Achilles again_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 357">S. Ph. 357</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὀμνύουσι μὴ ᾿κπιεῖν</span> _they swear they did not drink_ Pherecrates 143 (Com. fr. I. [187](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s187)), <span lang="grc">ὤμνυε μηδὲν εἰρηκέναι</span> _he swore that he had said nothing_ (direct = <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν εἴρηκα</span>) <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.119">D. 21.119</cite>.

##### <a id="s1869"></a> §1869

_Verbs of will_ or _desire_ ([1991](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s1991)) regularly take the present or aorist infinitive not in indirect discourse; but in some cases we find the future infinitive by assimilation to indirect discourse through the analogy of _verbs of promising_, etc. ([1868](#s1868)). So with <span lang="grc">βούλομαι, ἐθέλω</span> _wish_, <span lang="grc">λέγω</span> meaning _command_, <span lang="grc">δέομαι</span> _ask_, <span lang="grc">ἐφί̄εμαι</span> _desire_ and some others (even <span lang="grc">δύναμαι</span> _am able_) that have a future action as their object. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐφῑέμενοι ἄρξειν</span> _being desirous to gain control_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.6">T. 6.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀδύνατοι ἐπιμελεῖς ἔσεσθαι</span> _unable to be careful_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 12.12">X. O. 12.12</cite>. <span lang="grc">διανοοῦμαι</span> may follow the analogy of <span lang="grc">μέλλω</span> ([1959](#s1959)): <span lang="grc">τὸν πόλεμον διενοοῦντο προθύ̄μως οἴσειν</span> _they intended to carry on the war with zeal_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.121">T. 4.121</cite>. In these and similar cases the future is employed to stress the future character of the action. Some editors would emend many of these futures.

##### <a id="s1870"></a> §1870

Verbs signifying _to foretell by oracle_ usually take the present or aorist infinitive like verbs signifying _to command_.

##### <a id="s1871"></a> §1871

A few cases stand in our texts of an aorist infinitive referring to the future after a _verb of saying_ or _thinking_, e.g. <span lang="grc">ἐνόμισαν ῥᾳδίως κρατῆσαι</span> _they thought they would easily master_ them <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.3">T. 2.3</cite>. Many editors change to the future or insert <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>.

##### <a id="s1872"></a> §1872

**Participle** (not in indirect discourse).—The participle, as a verbal adjective, is timeless. The tenses of the participle express only continuance, simple occurrence, and completion with permanent result. Whether the action expressed by the participle is antecedent, coincident, or subsequent to that of the leading verb (in any tense) depends on the context. The future participle has a temporal force only because its voluntative force points to the future.

**a. Present** (continuative). The action set forth by the present participle is generally coincident (rarely antecedent or subsequent) to that of the leading verb: <span lang="grc">ἐργαζόμεναι μὲν ἠρίστων, ἐργασάμεναι δὲ ἐδείπνουν</span> _the women took their noonday meal while they continued their work, but took their supper when they had stopped work_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.7.12">X. M. 2.7.12</cite>.

\1. Antecedent action ( = imperf.): <span lang="grc">οἱ Κύ̄ρειοι πρόσθεν σὺν ἡμῖν ταττόμενοι νῦν ἀφεστήκᾱσιν</span> _the forces of Cyrus that were formerly marshalled with us have now deserted_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.17">X. A. 3.2.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοὺς τότε παρόντας αἰτιά̄σονται συμβούλους</span> _they will accuse those who were their counsellors at that time_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 519a">P. G. 519a</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ Κορίνθιοι μέχρι τούτου προθύ̄μως πρά̄σσοντες ἀνεῖσαν τῆς φιλονεικίᾱς</span> _the Corinthians_, _who up to that time had been acting zealously, now slackened in their vehemence_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.32">T. 5.32</cite>. An adverb (<span lang="grc">πρότερον, πρόσθεν, τότε, ποτέ</span>) often accompanies the participle, which is sometimes called the _participle of the imperfect_.

\2. Subsequent action (especially when the leading verb denotes motion): <span lang="grc">ἔπεμψαν πρέσβεις ἀγγέλλοντας τὴν τοῦ Πλημυρίου λῆψιν</span> _they despatched messengers_ <!-- page 420 --> _to announce the capture of Plemyrium_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.25">T. 7.25</cite>. An attributive present part. w. <span lang="grc">νῦν</span> may refer to the absolute present, though the main verb is past: <span lang="grc">τὴν νῦν Βοιωτίᾱν καλουμένην ᾤκησαν</span> _they settled in the country now called Boeotia_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.12">T. 1.12</cite>.

\3. The present participle denotes that an action is in process, is attempted, or is repeated.

**b. Future** (chiefly voluntative): <span lang="grc">οὐ συνήλθομεν ὡς βασιλεῖ πολεμήσοντες</span> _we have not come together for the purpose of waging war with the king_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.21">X. A. 2.3.21</cite>.

**c. Aorist** (simple occurrence). The action set forth by the aorist participle is generally antecedent to that of the leading verb; but it is sometimes coincident or nearly so, when it defines, or is identical with, that of the leading verb, and the subordinate action is only a modification of the main action.

\1. Antecedent: <span lang="grc">δειπνήσᾱς ἐχώρει</span> _after supper he advanced_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.112">T. 3.112</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοὺς ἐλευθέρους ἀποκτείναντες ἀνεχώρησαν</span> _after killing the free men they withdrew_ 5. 83. <span lang="grc">ἐπομόσᾱς ἔφη</span> _he took an oath and said_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.1.23">X. C. 4.1.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἤδη δ᾽ ἐπὶ ταῦτα πορεύσομαι τοσοῦτον αὐτὸν ἐρωτήσᾱς</span> _I shall at once proceed to this matter after having put to him certain questions_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.124">D. 18.124</cite>. The aorist participle is often thus used when it takes up the preceding verb: <span lang="grc">νῦν μὲν δειπνεῖτε· δειπνήσαντες δὲ ἀπελαύνετε</span> _take your supper now, and when you have done so, depart_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.37">X. C. 3.1.37</cite>.

\2. Coincident: <span lang="grc">μή τι ἐξαμάρτητε ἐμοῦ καταψηφισάμενοι</span> _do not commit the error of condemning me_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 30d">P. A. 30d</cite>, <span lang="grc">εὖ γ᾽ ἐποίησας ἀναμνήσᾱς με</span> _you did well in reminding me_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 60c">P. Ph. 60c</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">ἀνέμνησάς με εὖ ποιῶν</span>). So also when an aorist participle is used with a future finite verb, as <span lang="grc">ἀπαλλαχθήσομαι βίου θανοῦσα</span> _by dying I shall be delivered from life_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hipp. 356">E. Hipp. 356</cite>. See also [2103](../22-the-participle.md#s2103).

\3. The action of an attributive aorist participle is rarely subsequent to that of the leading verb. When this is the case, the action of the participle is marked as past from the point of view of the present (like the aor. indic.): <span lang="grc">οἱ Ἕλληνες ὕστερον κληθέντες οὐδὲν πρὸ τῶν Τρωϊκῶν ἁθρόοι ἔπρᾱξαν</span> _the people later called Hellenes carried out no joint enterprise prior to the Trojan war_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.3">T. 1.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">Σάτυρος καὶ Χρέμων, οἱ τῶν τριά̄κοντα γενόμενοι, Κλεοφῶντος κατηγόρουν</span> _Satyrus and Chremon_, _who_ (afterwards) _became members of the Thirty_, _accused Cleophon_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 30.12">L. 30.12</cite>; cp. <span lang="grc">γενόμενος</span> <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.49">T. 2.49</cite>, 4. 81.

\4. The aorist participle is often ingressive or complexive ([1924](#s1924), [1927](#s1927)).

**d. Perfect** (completion with permanent result): <span lang="grc">καταλαμβάνουσι Βρᾱσίδᾱν ἐπεληλυθότα</span> _they found_ (historical present) _that Brasidas had arrived_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.69">T. 3.69</cite>. A perfect participle may have the force of a pluperfect if accompanied by an adverb like <span lang="grc">πρόσθεν</span> (cp. [1872 a](#s1872). 1): <span lang="grc">ὁ πρόσθε κεκτημένος</span> _he who possessed it before_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 778">S. Ph. 778</cite>.

##### <a id="s1873"></a> §1873

**Construction of <span lang="grc">λανθάνω, φθάνω, τυγχάνω</span>**.—A supplementary aorist participle with any tense, except the present or imperfect, of <span lang="grc">λανθάνω</span> _escape the notice of_, <span lang="grc">φθάνω</span> _anticipate_, <span lang="grc">τυγχάνω</span> _happen_ usually coincides in time with the leading verb: <span lang="grc">ἔλαθον ἐμαυτὸν οὐδὲν εἰπών</span> _I was unconsciously talking nonsense_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 76d">P. Ph. 76d</cite>, <span lang="grc">λήσομεν ἐπιπεσόντες</span> _we shall fall on them unawares_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.3.43">X. A. 7.3.43</cite>. But the action of an aorist participle with the present or imperfect is generally prior to that of the leading verb: <span lang="grc">ὅστις ἀντειπών γε ἐτύγχανε</span> _who chanced to have spoken in opposition_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.27">L. 12.27</cite>. See [2096](../22-the-participle.md#s2096).

##### <a id="s1874"></a> §1874

**Participle** (in indirect discourse). The tenses of the participle in indirect discourse after verbs of intellectual perception <!-- page 421 --> denote the same time relative to that of the leading verb (present, past, or future) as was denoted by the corresponding tenses of the indicative in direct discourse which they represent. See [2106](../22-the-participle.md#s2106), [2112](../22-the-participle.md#s2112) b.

**a.** Present = pres. indic.: the action is generally coincident: <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὰν γνῶσιν ἀπιστούμενοι</span> _when they find out that they are distrusted_ ( = <span lang="grc">ὅτι ἀπιστούμεθα</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.2.17">X. C. 7.2.17</cite>; rarely antecedent (when the present = the imperf. ind.): <span lang="grc">οἶδά σε λέγοντα ἀεί</span> _I know that you always used to say_ ( = <span lang="grc">ὅτι ἔλεγες</span>) 1. 6. 6.

**b.** Future = fut. indic.: <span lang="grc">ἀγνοεῖ τὸν πόλεμον δεῦρ᾽ ἥξοντα</span> _he is ignorant that the war will come here_ ( = <span lang="grc">ὅτι ὁ πόλεμος ἥξει</span>) <cite data-ref="Dem. 1.15">D. 1.15</cite>.

**c.** Aorist = aor. indic.: <span lang="grc">τὸν Μῆδον ἴσμεν ἐπὶ τὴν Πελοπόννησον ἐλθόντα</span> _we know that the Mede came against the Peloponnese_ ( = <span lang="grc">ὅτι ὁ Μῆδος ἦλθε</span>) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.69">T. 1.69</cite>.

**d.** Perfect = perf. indic.: <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ ᾔδεσαν αὐτὸν τεθνηκότα</span> _for they did not know that he was dead_ ( = <span lang="grc">ὅτι τέθνηκε</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.10.16">X. A. 1.10.16</cite>. The perfect may also represent the pluperfect (cp. [1872](#s1872) d).

## TENSES OF THE INDICATIVE

### PRESENT INDICATIVE

##### <a id="s1875"></a> §1875

The present represents a present state, or an action going on at the present time: <span lang="grc">ἀληθῆ λέγω</span> _I am telling the truth_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 13.72">L. 13.72</cite>.

**a.** On the present without any idea of duration, see [1853](#s1853).

##### <a id="s1876"></a> §1876

**Present of Customary Action**.—The present is used to express a customary or repeated action: <span lang="grc">οὗτος μὲν γὰρ ὕδωρ, ἐγὼ δ᾽ οἶνον πί̄νω</span> _for this man drinks water, whereas I drink wine_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.46">D. 19.46</cite>.

##### <a id="s1877"></a> §1877

**Present of General Truth**.—The present is used to express an action that is true for all time: <span lang="grc">ἄγει δὲ πρὸς φῶς τὴν ἀλήθειαν χρόνος</span> _time brings the truth to light_ Men. Sent. 11.

**a.** The present is an _absolute_ tense in such sentences. The future, aorist, and perfect may also express a general truth.

##### <a id="s1878"></a> §1878

**Conative Present**.—The present may express an action begun, attempted, or intended.

<span lang="grc">τὴν δόξαν ταύτην πείθουσιν ὑ̄μᾶς ἀποβαλεῖν</span> _they are trying to persuade you to throw away this renown_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 6.12">I. 6.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">δίδωμί σοι αὐτὴν ταύτην γυναῖκα</span> _I offer you this woman herself as a wife_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.5.19">X. C. 8.5.19</cite>, <span lang="grc">προδίδοτον τὴν Ἑλλάδα</span> _they are trying to betray Greece_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Peace 408">Ar. P. 408</cite>.

**a.** This use is found also in the infinitive and participle: <span lang="grc">Φιλίππου ἐπὶ Βυζάντιον παριόντος</span> _when Philip is preparing to advance against Byzantium_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.66">D. 8.66</cite>.

**b.** The idea of attempt or intention is an inference from the context and lies in the present only so far as the present does not denote completion.

##### <a id="s1879"></a> §1879

**Present for the Future** (**Present of Anticipation**).—The present is used instead of the future in statements of what is immediate, likely, certain, or threatening.

<span lang="grc">μεταξὺ τὸν λόγον καταλύ̄ομεν</span>; _shall we break off in the middle?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 505c">P. G. 505c</cite>, <!-- page 422 --> <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ βούλει, παραχωρῶ σοι τοῦ βήματος, ἕως ἂν εἴπῃς</span> _and if you wish_, _I will yield you the floor until you tell us_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.165">Aes. 3.165</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπόλλυμαι</span> _I am on the verge of ruin_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.35">Ant. 5.35</cite> (so <span lang="grc">ἀπώλλυτο</span> 5. 37 of past time), <span lang="grc">εἰ αὕτη ἡ πόλις ληφθήσεται, ἔχεται καὶ ἡ πᾶσα Σικελίᾱ</span> _if this city is taken, the whole of Sicily as well is in their power_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.91">T. 6.91</cite>.

**a.** Sometimes in questions to indicate that the decision must be made on the spot: <span lang="grc">ἢ πῶς λέγομεν</span>; _or how shall we say?_ (what must we say?) <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 480b">P. G. 480b</cite>.

##### <a id="s1880"></a> §1880

<span lang="grc">εἶμι</span> is regularly future (_I shall go_) in the indicative present. In the subjunctive it is always future; in the optative, infinitive, and participle it may be either future or present. Cp. [774](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s774). In <span lang="grc">ἰὼν ταῦτα λέγε</span> _go and say this_ (<cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.5.17">X. C. 4.5.17</cite>) <span lang="grc">ἰών</span> is used of time relatively past. In Hom. <span lang="grc">εἶμι</span> means both _I go_ and _I shall go_.

##### <a id="s1881"></a> §1881

<span lang="grc">ἔρχομαι, πορεύομαι, νέομαι</span> (poet.) may be used in a future sense. <span lang="grc">χέω</span> means either _I pour_ or _I shall pour_. <span lang="grc">ἔδομαι</span> _I shall eat_, <span lang="grc">πί̄ομαι</span> _I shall drink_, are present in form. Cp. [541](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/03-ii-future-system.md#s541).

##### <a id="s1882"></a> §1882

**Oracular Present**.—In prophecies a future event may be regarded as present: <span lang="grc">χρόνῳ ἀγρεῖ Πριάμου πόλιν ἅ̄δε κέλευθος</span> _in time this expedition will capture Priam_'_s city_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 126">A. Ag. 126</cite>.

##### <a id="s1883"></a> §1883

**Historical Present**.—In lively or dramatic narration the present may be used to represent a past action as going on at the moment of speaking or writing. This use does not occur in Homer.

<span lang="grc">ὁ δὲ Θεμιστοκλῆς φεύγει ἐς Κέρκῡραν . . . διακομίζεται ἐς τὴν ἤπειρον</span> _Themistocles fled_ (_flees_) _to Corcyra . . . was_ (_is_) _transported to the mainland_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.136">T. 1.136</cite>.

**a.** The historical present may represent either the descriptive imperfect or the narrative aorist.

**b.** The historical present may be coördinated with past tenses, which may precede or follow it: <span lang="grc">ἅμα δὲ τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῇ πόλει προσέκειτο καὶ αἱρεῖ</span> _at daybreak he assaulted the town and took it_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.29">T. 7.29</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὕτω δὴ ἀπογράφονται πάντες ἀνέλαβόν τε τὰ ὅπλα</span> _accordingly they all enrolled themselves and took the arms_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.1.19">X. C. 2.1.19</cite>.

**c.** The historical present is less frequent in subordinate clauses (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.91">T. 2.91</cite>. 3).

##### <a id="s1884"></a> §1884

**Annalistic Present**.—Closely connected with the historical present is the annalistic present, which is used to register historical facts or to note incidents.

<span lang="grc">Δᾱρείου καὶ Παρυσάτιδος γίγνονται παῖδες δύο</span> _of Darius and Parysatis were_ (_are_) _born two sons_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.1">X. A. 1.1.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρὸ Αευτυχίδεω γὰρ</span> (<span lang="grc">Ζευξίδημος</span>) <span lang="grc">τελευτᾷ . . . Αευτυχίδης γαμέει Εὐρυδάμην, ἐκ τῆς οἱ . . . γί̄νεται θυγάτηρ</span> _for Zeuxidemus died before Leutychides . . . L. married Eurydame_, _from her was born to him a daughter_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 6.71">Hdt. 6.71</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ ὁ ἐνιαυτὸς ἔληγεν, ἐν ᾧ Καρχηδόνιοι αἱροῦσι δύο πόλεις Ἑλληνίδας</span> _and the year came to an end in which the Carthaginians captured two Greek cities_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.1.37">X. H. 1.1.37</cite>.

##### <a id="s1885"></a> §1885

**Present of Past and Present Combined**.—The present, when accompanied by a definite or indefinite expression of past time, is used to express an action begun in the past and continued in the present. The ‘progressive perfect’ is often used in translation. <!-- page 423 --> Thus, <span lang="grc">πάλαι θαυμάζω</span> _I have been long_ (and am still) _wondering_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 43b">P. Cr. 43b</cite>. Cp. _iamdudum loquor_. So with <span lang="grc">πάρος, ποτέ</span>. This use appears also in the other moods.

**a.** So with _verbs of hearing_, _saying_, _learning_, whose action commenced in the past, but whose effect continues into the present: <span lang="grc">ἐξ ὧν ἀκούω</span> _from what I hear_ (_have heard_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.9.28">X. A. 1.9.28</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅπερ λέγω</span> _as I said_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 21a">P. A. 21a</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">αἰσθάνομαι, γιγνώσκω, μανθάνω, πυνθάνομαι. ἄρτι</span> _just_ is often found with these verbs.

**b.** The perfect is used instead of the present when the action is completed in the present.

##### <a id="s1886"></a> §1886

**Present for Perfect**.—<span lang="grc">ἥκω</span> _I am come_, _I have arrived_, <span lang="grc">οἴχομαι</span> _I am gone_, have a perfect sense; as also <span lang="grc">ἔρχομαι, ἀφικνοῦμαι</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">Θεμιστοκλῆς ἥκω παρὰ σέ</span> _I Themistocles have come to you_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.137">T. 1.137</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἶδα ὅπῃ οἴχονται</span> _I know where they have gone_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.8">X. A. 1.4.8</cite>.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ἥκω</span> may be used in connection with the gnomic aorist (<cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 188a">P. S. 188a</cite>).

##### <a id="s1887"></a> §1887

The present of certain verbs often expresses an enduring result, and may be translated by the perfect: <span lang="grc">ἀδικῶ</span> _I am guilty_ (<span lang="grc">ἄδικός εἰμι</span>), _I have done wrong_, <span lang="grc">νῑκῶ, κρατῶ</span>, _I am victorious_, _I have conquered_, <span lang="grc">ἡττῶμαι</span> _I am conquered_, <span lang="grc">φεύγω</span> _I am the defendant_ or _I am an exile_ (<span lang="grc">οἱ φεύγοντες</span> _the fugitives_ and _the exiles_), <span lang="grc">προδίδωμι</span> _I am a traitor_, <span lang="grc">ἁλίσκομαι</span> _I am captured_, <span lang="grc">στέρομαι</span> _I am deprived_, <span lang="grc">γίγνομαι</span> _I am a descendant_.

<span lang="grc">ἥκω εἰς τὴν σὴν οἰκίᾱν, ἀδικῶ δ᾽ οὐδέν</span> _I am come to thy house, but have done no wrong_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.14">L. 12.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπαγγέλλετε Ἀριαίῳ ὅτι ἡμεῖς γε νῑκῶμεν βασιλέᾱ</span> _report to Ariaeus that we at least have conquered the king_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.4">X. A. 2.1.4</cite>.

**a.** So, in poetry, <span lang="grc">γεννῶ, φύ̄ω, τίκτω, θνῄσκω, ὄλλυμαι</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἥδε τίκτει σε</span> _this woman_ (_has born thee_ = ) _is thy mother_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Ion 1560">E. Ion 1560</cite>.

##### <a id="s1888"></a> §1888

In subordinate clauses, the action expressed by the present may be (a) contemporaneous, (b) antecedent, or (c) subsequent to that set forth by the main verb. The context alone decides in which sense the present is to be taken: (a) <span lang="grc">ἔλεγεν ὅτι ἕτοιμος εἴη ἡγεῖσθαι αὐτοῖς</span> _he said that he was ready to lead them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.1.33">X. A. 6.1.33</cite>; (b) when the present states an action begun in the past and continued in the present: <span lang="grc">ἐπείτε δὲ Πέρσαι ἔχουσι τὸ κράτος</span>, (<span lang="grc">τὸ πεδίον</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐστὶ τοῦ βασιλέος</span> _from the time that the Persians began to hold sway, it belongs to the king_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 3.117">Hdt. 3.117</cite>; and with the historical present: <span lang="grc">ὡς δὲ γίγνονται ἐπ᾽ αὐτῷ, ἐσπί̄πτουσιν</span> _when they came to it, they rushed in_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.84">T. 7.84</cite>; (c) <span lang="grc">ἐγένετο ῥήτρᾱ . . . εἰ παρὰ ταῦτα ποιοῖεν, κολάζειν</span> _an ordinance was passed . . if they act contrary to this, to punish them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.6.33">X. C. 1.6.33</cite>.

### IMPERFECT

##### <a id="s1889"></a> §1889

The imperfect represents an action as still going on, or a state as still existing, in the past: <span lang="grc">Κῦρος οὔπω ἧκεν, ἀλλ᾽ ἔτι προσήλαυνε</span> _Cyrus had not yet arrived_ ([1886](#s1886)), _but was still marching on_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.12">X. A. 1.5.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐβασίλευεν Ἀντίοχος</span> _Antiochus was reigning_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.80">T. 2.80</cite>. The conclusion of the action is usually to be inferred from the context.

##### <a id="s1890"></a> §1890

**Imperfect of Continuance**.—The imperfect thus represents an action as continuing in the past: <span lang="grc">διέφθειραν Ἀθηναίων πίντε καὶ εἴκοσι</span>, <!-- page 424 --> <span lang="grc">οἳ ξυνεπολιορκοῦντο</span> _they put to death twenty-five of the Athenians who were besieged_ (i.e. from the beginning to the end of the siege) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.68">T. 3.68</cite>.

##### <a id="s1891"></a> §1891

The imperfect of _verbs of sending_, _going_, _saying_, _exhorting_, etc., which imply continuous action, is often used where we might expect the aorist of concluded action. Thus, in <span lang="grc">ἔπεμπον</span>, the action is regarded as unfinished since the goal is not reached: <span lang="grc">ἄγγελον ἔπεμπον καὶ τοὺς νεκροὺς ὑποσπόνδους ἀπέδοσαν</span> _they sent a messenger and surrendered the dead under a truce_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.6">T. 2.6</cite>. In <span lang="grc">ἐκέλευον</span> _gave orders, urged_, _requested_ the command, etc., is regarded as not yet executed. In <span lang="grc">ἔλεγεν αὐτοῖς τοιάδε</span> _he spoke to them as follows_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.6.4">X. H. 1.6.4</cite> (followed by the speech and <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ δὲ ταῦτ᾽ εἶπεν</span> 1. 6. [12](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s12)) the speech is not thought of as a finished whole, but as developed point by point, as in <span lang="grc">ἐπἐιδὴ δὲ οὗτος ταῦτα ἔλεγεν, ἔλεξα</span> _but when he had said this_, _I said_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 6.21">Ant. 6.21</cite>.

**a.** In messenger's speeches the speaker may go back to the time of receiving a command: <span lang="grc">ἰέναι σ᾽ ἐκέλευον οἱ στρατηγοὶ τήμερον</span> _the generals order you to depart to-day_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Ach. 1073">Ar. Ach. 1073</cite>.

##### <a id="s1892"></a> §1892

The imperfect, when accompanied by an expression of past time, is used of actions which had been in progress for some time and were still in progress (cp. [1885](#s1885)): <span lang="grc">τὸ Π̔ήγιον ἐπὶ πολὺν χρόνον ἐστασίαζε</span> _Rhegium had been for a long time in a state of faction_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.1">T. 4.1</cite>. If the action is regarded as completed the pluperfect is used.

##### <a id="s1893"></a> §1893

**Imperfect of Customary Action**.—The imperfect is used to express frequently repeated or customary past actions: <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ εἶδον αὐτὸν οἵπερ πρόσθεν προσεκύνουν, καὶ τότε προσεκύνησαν</span> _when they caught sight of him, the very men who before this were wont to prostrate themselves before him, prostrated themselves on this occasion also_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.6.10">X. A. 1.6.10</cite>, (<span lang="grc">Σωκράτης</span>) <span lang="grc">τοὺς ἑαυτοῦ ἐπιθῡμοῦντας οὐκ ἐπρά̄ττετο χρήματα</span> Socrates _was not in the habit of demanding money from those who were passionately attached to him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.2.5">X. M. 1.2.5</cite>. See also [2340](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2340).

**a.** The repetition of a simple act in the past is expressed by <span lang="grc">πολλάκις</span> with the aorist ([1930](#s1930)).

##### <a id="s1894"></a> §1894

**Iterative Imperfect**.—<span lang="grc">ἄν</span> may be used with this imperfect ([1790](07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1790)): <span lang="grc">ἐπεθύ̄μει ἄν τις ἔτι πλείω αὐτοῦ ἀκούειν</span> _people would_ (_used to_) _desire to hear still more from him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.3">X. C. 1.4.3</cite>.

##### <a id="s1895"></a> §1895

**Conative Imperfect**.—The imperfect may express an action attempted, intended, or expected, in the past.

<span lang="grc">ἔπειθον αὐτούς, καὶ οὓς ἔπεισα, τούτους ἔχων ἐπορευόμην</span> _I tried to persuade them_, _and I marched away with those whom I succeeded in persuading_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.5.22">X. C. 5.5.22</cite>, <span lang="grc">Ἁλόννησον ἐδίδου· ὁ δ᾽ ἀπηγόρευε μὴ λαμβάνειν</span> Philip _offered_ (proposed to give) _Halonnesus_, _but he_ (Demosthenes) _dissuaded_ them _from accepting it_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.83">Aes. 3.83</cite>, <span lang="grc">Θηβαῖοι κατεδουλοῦντ᾽ αὐτούς</span> _the Thebans tried to enslave them_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.74">D. 8.74</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἠπείγοντο ἐς τὴν Κέρκῡραν</span> _they were for pushing on to Corcyra_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.3">T. 4.3</cite>.

**a.** Here may be placed the imperfect equivalent in sense to <span lang="grc">ἔμελλον</span> with the infinitive. Thus, <span lang="grc">φονεὺς οὖν αὐτῶν ἐγιγνόμην ἐγὼ μὴ εἰπὼν ὑ̄υῖν ἃ ἤκουσα. ἔτι δὲ τριᾱκοσίους Ἀθηναίων ἀπώλλυον</span> _I was on the point of becoming their murderer_ <!-- page 425 --> (_interfecturus eram_) _had I not told you what I heard. And besides I threatened three hundred Athenians with death_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.58">And. 1.58</cite>. So <span lang="grc">ἀπωλλύμην</span> _I was threatened with death_.

##### <a id="s1896"></a> §1896

**Imperfect of Resistance or Refusal**.—With a negative, the imperfect often denotes resistance or refusal (_would not_ or _could not_). The aorist with a negative denotes unrestricted denial of a fact.

<span lang="grc">τὴν πρόκλησιν οὐκ ἐδέχεσθε</span> _you would not accept the proposal_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.64">T. 3.64</cite> (<span lang="grc">τὴν ἱκετείᾱν οὐκ ἐδέξαντο</span> _they did not receive the supplication_ 1. [24](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s24)), <span lang="grc">ὁ μὲν οὐκ ἐγάμει, ὁ δὲ ἔγημεν</span> _the one would not marry, the other did_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 44.17">D. 44.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ φωνὴν ἤκουον, εἴ τις ἄλλο τι βούλοιτο λέγειν</span> _they would not even listen to a syllable if ever any one wished to say anything to the contrary_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.43">D. 18.43</cite>. So <span lang="grc">οὐκ εἴᾱ</span> _he would not allow_ (_he was not for allowing_).

##### <a id="s1897"></a> §1897

If simple positive and negative are contrasted, the aorist is preferred with the latter: <span lang="grc">τὰ ὑπάρχοντά τε σῴζειν</span> (positive with present) <span lang="grc">καὶ ἐπιγνῶναι μηδέν</span> (negative with aorist) _to preserve what you have, and to form no new plans_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.70">T. 1.70</cite>. But where the verb itself contains or implies a negative idea, the present is used: <span lang="grc">παρεῖναι καὶ μὴ ἀποδημεῖν</span> _to be present and not to be abroad_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 2.59">Aes. 2.59</cite>.

##### <a id="s1898"></a> §1898

**Imperfect of Description**.—The imperfect describes manners and customs; the situation, circumstances, and details, of events; and the development of actions represented as continuing in past time.

<span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνός τε τοὺς ὑφ᾽ ἑαυτῷ ὥσπερ ἑαυτοῦ παῖδας ἐτί̄μᾱ, οἵ τε ἀρχόμενοι Κῦρον ὡς πατέρα ἐσέβοντο</span> _he_ (Cyrus) _treated his subjects with honour as if they were his own children, and his subjects reverenced Cyrus like a father_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.8.2">X. C. 8.8.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">εὐθὺς ἀνεβόησάν τε πάντες καὶ προσπεσόντες ἐμάχοντο, ἐώθουν, ἐωθοῦντο, ἔπαιον, ἐπαίοντο</span> _immediately all raised a shout and falling upon each other fought, pushed and were pushed, struck and were struck_ 7. 1. 38, <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ δὲ ταῦτα ἐρρήθη, ἐπορεύοντο· τῶν δὲ ἀπαντώντων οἱ μὲν ἀπέθνῃσκον, οἱ δὲ ἔφευγον πάλιν εἴσω, οἱ δὲ ἐβόων</span> _and when these words had been spoken, they proceeded to advance; and of those who met them some were killed, others fled back indoors, and others shouted_ 7. 5. 26, <span lang="grc">ἐστρατήγει δὲ αὐτῶν Ἀριστεύς</span> _Aristeus was their commander_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.60">T. 1.60</cite>; cp. <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.2.28">X. C. 4.2.28</cite>, X. Ag. 2. 12, <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.3.8">X. A. 4.3.8</cite>-25, Isocr. 1. 9, 7. 51-53, <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.169">D. 18.169</cite> ff., <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.192">Aes. 3.192</cite>.

N.—The imperfect often has a dramatic or panoramic force: it enables the reader to follow the course of events as they occurred, as if he were a spectator of the scene depicted.

##### <a id="s1899"></a> §1899

The imperfect is thus often used to explain, illustrate, offer reasons for an action, and to set forth accompanying and subordinate circumstances that explain or show the result of the main action. Descriptive adverbs are often used with the imperfect.

<span lang="grc">ἐνταῦθα ἔμεινεν ἡμέρᾱς πέντε· καὶ τοῖς στρατιώταις ὠφείλετο μισθὸς πλέον ἢ τριῶν μηνῶν, καὶ πολλάκις ἰόντες ἐπὶ τὰ̄ς θύρᾱς ἀπῄτουν· ὁ δὲ ἐλπίδας λέγων διῆγε καὶ δῆλος ἦν ἀνιώμενος</span> _there he remained for five days; and the soldiers whose pay was in arrears for more than three months kept going to headquarters and demanding their dues; but he kept expressing his expectation_ (of making payment) _and was plainly annoyed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.11">X. A. 1.2.11</cite>. See also [1907 a](#s1907). <!-- page 426 -->

##### <a id="s1900"></a> §1900

**Inchoative Imperfect**.—The imperfect may denote the beginning of an action or of a series of actions: <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὴ δὲ καιρὸς ἦν, προσέβαλλον</span> _but when the proper time arrived, they began an_ (proceeded to) _attack_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.51">T. 7.51</cite>.

##### <a id="s1901"></a> §1901

**Imperfect for Present**.—In descriptions of places and scenery and in other statements of existing facts the imperfect, instead of the present, is often used by assimilation to the time of the narrative (usually set forth in the main verb).

<span lang="grc">ἀφί̄κοντο ἐπὶ τὸν ποταμὸν ὃς ὥριζε τὴν τῶν Μακρώνων χώρᾱν καὶ τὴν τῶν Σκυθηνῶν</span> _they came to the river which divided the country of the Macrones from that of the Scytheni_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.8.1">X. A. 4.8.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐξελαύνει ἐπὶ ποταμὸν πλήρη ἰχθύων, οὓς οἱ Σύροι θεοὺς ἐνόμιζον</span> _he marched to a river full of fish, which the Syrians regarded as gods_ 1. 4. 9.

##### <a id="s1902"></a> §1902

—**Imperfect of a Truth Just Recognized**.—The imperfect, usually some form of <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span>, with <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span>, is often used to denote that a present fact or truth has just been recognized, although true before: <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν ἄρ᾽ ἦν πρᾶγμα</span> _it is, as it appears_, _no matter after all_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 198e">P. S. 198e</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦτ᾽ ἄρ᾽ ἦν ἀληθές</span> _this is true after all_ <cite data-ref="Eur. IT 351">E. I. T. 351</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄρα ἠπίστω</span> _you know, sure enough_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 3.4.9">X. H. 3.4.9</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> _sure enough_, _after all_ appears with other tenses (<cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 49a">P. Cr. 49a</cite>, <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 61a">P. Ph. 61a</cite>, <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.160">D. 19.160</cite>).

##### <a id="s1903"></a> §1903

The imperfect may refer to a topic previously discussed: <span lang="grc">ἦν ἡ μουσικὴ ἀντίστροφος τῆς γυμναστικῆς εἰ μέμνησαι</span> _music is_ (as we have seen) _the counterpart of gymnastics, if you remember the discussion_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 522a">P. R. 522a</cite>. This is called the _philosophical imperfect_.

##### <a id="s1904"></a> §1904

The epistolary imperfect is rare in Greek. See [1942 b](#s1942).

##### <a id="s1905"></a> §1905

<span lang="grc">**ἔδει**, **ἐχρῆν**</span>.—The imperfect of verbs expressing _obligation_ or _duty_ may refer to present time and imply that the obligation or duty is not fulfilled: <span lang="grc">σῑγήσᾱς ἡνίκ᾽ ἔδει λέγειν</span> _keeping silence when he ought to speak_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.189">D. 18.189</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">ἐχρῆν</span> _it were proper_, <span lang="grc">εἰκὸς ἦν</span> _it were fitting_ ([1774](07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1774)). But the imperfect may also express past obligation without denying the action of the infinitive, as <span lang="grc">ἔδει μένειν</span> _he was obliged to remain_ (and did remain) <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.124">D. 19.124</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅπερ ἔδει δεῖξαι</span> _quod erat demonstrandum_ Euclid 1. 5 ([1779](07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1779)).

##### <a id="s1906"></a> §1906

**Imperfect for Pluperfect**.—The imperfect has the force of the pluperfect in the case of verbs whose present is used in the sense of the perfect ([1886](#s1886)).

Thus, <span lang="grc">ἧκον</span> _I had come_ (rarely _I came_), <span lang="grc">ᾠχόμην</span> _I had departed_, as <span lang="grc">ἐνί̄κων</span> _I was victorious_, <span lang="grc">ἡττώμην</span> _I was defeated_ ([1752](04-passive-voice.md#s1752)). So (<span lang="grc">Ὀλύμπια</span>) <span lang="grc">οἷς Ἀνδροσθένης παγκράτιον ἐνί̄κᾱ</span> _the Olympic games, at which Androsthenes was the victor_ (= _had won_) _in the pancratium_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.49">T. 5.49</cite>.

##### <a id="s1907"></a> §1907

In subordinate clauses, the action expressed by the imperfect may be (a) contemporaneous with or (b) antecedent to that set forth by the main verb: (a) <span lang="grc">τοσοῦτοι ἦσαν οἱ ξύμπαντες ὅτε ἐς τὴν πολιορκίᾱν καθί̄σταντο</span> _this was their total number when they began to be besieged_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.78">T. 2.78</cite>; (b) <span lang="grc">τὸ πλοῖον ἧκεν, ἐν ᾧ ἐπλέομεν</span> _the vessel arrived in which we_ (_had_) _sailed_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.29">Ant. 5.29</cite>. Greek has no special form to express time that is anterior to the past. <!-- page 427 -->

##### <a id="s1908"></a> §1908

**Imperfect and Aorist**.—The imperfect and aorist often occur in the same passage; and the choice of the one or the other often depends upon the manner in which the writer may view a given action. The imperfect may be represented by a line, along which an action progresses; the aorist denotes a point on the line (either starting point or end), or surveys the whole line from beginning to end.

**a.** The imperfect of ‘continuance’ or ‘duration’ implies nothing as to the absolute length of the action; cp. <span lang="grc">πάλιν κατὰ τάχος ἐκόμιζε τὴν στρατιά̄ν</span> _he took the army back as quickly as possible_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.114">T. 1.114</cite> with <span lang="grc">κατὰ τάχος ἀνεχώρησε</span> _he retreated as quickly as possible_ 1. 73. The imperfect does not indicate ‘prolonged’ action in contrast to ‘momentary’ action of the aorist.

**b.** The imperfect puts the reader in the midst of the events as they were taking place, the aorist simply reports that an event took place: <span lang="grc">ἔπειτα ψῑλοὶ δώδεκα ἀνέβαινον, ὧν ἡγεῖτο Ἀμμέᾱς, καὶ πρῶτος ἀνέβη</span> _then twelve light-armed men proceeded to climb up under the leadership of Ammeas_, _who was the first to mount_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.22">T. 3.22</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.49">T. 2.49</cite>, 3. 15. 1-2, 4. 14, <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.4.1">X. H. 4.4.1</cite>, <cite data-ref="Isoc. 5.53">I. 5.53</cite>-54, 8. 99-100.

##### <a id="s1909"></a> §1909

The following statement presents the chief differences between imperfect and aorist as narrative tenses.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| _Imperfect_ | _Aorist_ |
| circumstances, details, course of | mere fact of occurrence, general state- |
| action | ment |
| progress, enduring condition, con- | consummation (culmination, final is- |
| tinued activity | sue, summary process) |
| general description | isolated points, characteristic examples |
| endeavour | attainment |
| actions subordinate to the main | main actions, without reference to |
| action | other actions |

Cp. <span lang="grc">ξυνεστράτευον</span> _they served with them in the war_, <span lang="grc">ξυνεστράτευσαν</span> _they took the field with them_ (both in <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.57">T. 7.57</cite>). <span lang="grc">ἔπειθον</span> _I tried to persuade_, <span lang="grc">ἔπεισα</span> _I succeeded in persuading_ (both in <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.5.22">X. C. 5.5.22</cite>).

### FUTURE INDICATIVE

##### <a id="s1910"></a> §1910

The future denotes an action that will take place at some future time: <span lang="grc">λήψεται μισθὸν τάλαντον</span> _he shall receive a talent as his reward_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.2.20">X. A. 2.2.20</cite>.

**a.** The action is future according to the opinion, expectation, hope, fear, or purpose of the speaker or the agent.

**b.** The action of the future is either continuative (like the present) or, like that of the aorist, expresses simple attainment. Thus <span lang="grc">πείσω</span> means _I shall try to persuade_ , or _I shall convince_ (resultative), <span lang="grc">βασιλεύσω</span> _I shall be king, shall reign_ or _I shall become king_ (ingressive).

##### <a id="s1911"></a> §1911

When a verb has two futures, that formed from the same stem as the present is properly continuative, that formed from the aorist stem marks simple attainment: thus, <span lang="grc">ἕξω</span> _I shall have_, <span lang="grc">σχήσω</span> _I shall get;_ as <span lang="grc">καὶ ταῦτ᾽ εἰκότως οὕτως</span> <!-- page 428 --> <span lang="grc">ὑπελάμβανον ἕξειν</span> _and I supposed with reason that this would continue so_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.153">D. 19.153</cite>, <span lang="grc">Θηβαῖοι ἔχουσι μὲν ἀπεχθῶς, ἔτι δ᾽ ἐχθροτέρως σχήσουσιν</span> _the Thebans are hostile and will become still more so_ 5. 18. (But <span lang="grc">ἕξω</span> usually does duty for <span lang="grc">σχήσω</span>.) So, <span lang="grc">ἀχθέσομαι</span> _shall be angry_, <span lang="grc">ἀχθεσθήσομαι</span> _shall get angry_, <span lang="grc">φοβήσομαι</span> _shall continue fearful_, <span lang="grc">φοβηθήσομαι</span> _shall be terrified_, <span lang="grc">αἰσχυνοῦμαι</span> _shall feel_ (continued) _shame_, <span lang="grc">αἰσχυνθήσομαι</span> _shall be ashamed_ (on a single occasion). Cp. [1738](04-passive-voice.md#s1738).

##### <a id="s1912"></a> §1912

The future represents both our _shall_ and _will_. When voluntative (_will_), the action of the subject may be (1) the result of his own decision, as <span lang="grc">οὐ δὴ ποιήσω τοῦτο</span> _that I never will do_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.11">D. 18.11</cite>, or (2) dependent on the will of another, as <span lang="grc">ἡ βουλὴ μέλλει αἱρεῖσθαι ὅστις ἐρεῖ ἐπὶ τοῖς ἀποθανοῦσι</span> _the Senate is about to choose some one to speak over the dead_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Menex. 234b">P. Menex. 234b</cite>. The use of the future is often similar to that of the subjunctive, especially in dependent clauses.

##### <a id="s1913"></a> §1913

_Verbs of wishing_, _asking_, and other voluntative verbs may appear in the future where English has the present: <span lang="grc">τοσοῦτον οὖν σου τυγχάνειν βουλήσομαι</span> _I_ (_shall_) _wish to obtain_ only _so much at thy hands_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 259">E. Med. 259</cite>, <span lang="grc">παραιτήσομαι δ᾽ ὑ̄μᾶς μηδὲν ἀχθεσθῆναί μοι</span> _I_ (_shall_) _beg you not to take any offence at me_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.58">D. 21.58</cite>. Cp. Lat. _censebo_.

**a.** In many cases the use of the future indicates that the wish remains unchanged; and there is no reference to a future _act_. Sometimes the future appears to be a more modest form of statement than the present.

##### <a id="s1914"></a> §1914

**Gnomic Future**.—The future may express a general truth: <span lang="grc">ἀνὴρ ἐπιεικὴς υἱὸν ἀπολέσᾱς ῥᾷστα οἴσει τῶν ἄλλων</span> _a reasonable man, if he loses a son, will_ (is expected to) _bear it more easily than other men_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 603e">P. R. 603e</cite> (cp. [1434](../18-the-cases/05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1434)).

**a.** Hdt. uses the future in descriptions of customs and in directions to travellers (1. 173, 2. [29](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s29)).

##### <a id="s1915"></a> §1915

**Future for Present**.—The future may be used instead of the present of that which is possible at the moment of speaking: <span lang="grc">εὑρήσομεν τοὺς φιλοτί̄μους τῶν ἀνδρῶν . . . ἀντὶ τοῦ ζῆν ἀποθνῄσκειν εὐκλεῶς αἱρουμένους</span> _we shall find that ambitious men choose a glorious death in preference to life_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 9.3">I. 9.3</cite>.

**a.** The future may denote present intention: <span lang="grc">αἶρε πλῆκτρον, εἰ μαχεῖ</span> _raise your spur if you mean to fight_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Birds 759">Ar. Av. 759</cite> (in this use <span lang="grc">μέλλω</span> is more common ([1959](#s1959))). So in the tragic <span lang="grc">τί λέξεις</span>; _what do you mean?_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 1310">E. Med. 1310</cite>.

##### <a id="s1916"></a> §1916

**Deliberative Future**.—The future is often used in deliberative questions: <span lang="grc">τί ἐροῦμεν ἢ τί φήσομεν</span>; _what shall we say or what shall we propose?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.37">D. 8.37</cite>.

**a.** The deliberative future may occur in connection with the deliberative subjunctive ([1805](07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1805)): <span lang="grc">εἴπωμεν ἢ σῑγῶμεν; ἢ τί δρά̄σομεν</span>; _shall we speak or keep silent? or what shall we do?_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Ion 758">E. Ion 758</cite>.

##### <a id="s1917"></a> §1917

**Jussive Future**.—The future may express a command, like the imperative; and, in the second person, may denote concession or <!-- page 429 --> permission. The negative is <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>. The tone of the jussive future (which is post-Homeric) is generally familiar.

<span lang="grc">ὣς οὖν ποιήσετε</span> _you will do thus_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 338a">P. Pr. 338a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀναγνώσεται τὸν νόμον—ἀναγίγνωσκε</span> the clerk _will read the law—read_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 24.39">D. 24.39</cite>, <span lang="grc">αὐτὸς γνώσει</span> _you will judge for yourself_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phileb. 12a">P. Phil. 12a</cite>, <span lang="grc">σπουδὴ ἔσται τῆς ὁδοῦ</span> _you will have to hurry on the march_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.77">T. 7.77</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑ̄μεῖς οὖν, ἐὰ̄ν σωφρονῆτε, οὐ τούτου ἀλλ᾽ ὑ̄μῶν φείσεσθε</span> _now_, _if you are wise, you will spare, not him, but yourselves_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.3.34">X. H. 2.3.34</cite>.

##### <a id="s1918"></a> §1918

The future with <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> interrogative is used in questions in an imperative sense to express urgency, warning, or irony: <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔξιμεν . . . οὐκ ἐπὶ τὴν ἐκείνου πλευσόμεθα</span>; _shall we not go forth . . . shall we not set sail against his_ country? <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.44">D. 4.44</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ φυλάξεσθε</span>; _will you not be on your guard?_ 6. 25. In exhortations addressed to oneself: <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀπαλλαχθήσομαι θῡμοῦ</span>; _shall I not cease from my passion?_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 878">E. Med. 878</cite>.

**a.** <span lang="grc">μή</span> with the future in a prohibitive sense is used in a few suspected passages (<cite data-ref="Lys. 29.13">L. 29.13</cite>, <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.117">D. 23.117</cite>).

##### <a id="s1919"></a> §1919

<span lang="grc">οὐ μή</span> with the second person singular of the future in the dramatic poets denotes a strong prohibition; as <span lang="grc">οὐ μὴ διατρί̄ψεις</span> _don_'_t dawdle_ (you _shall_ not dawdle) <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Frogs 462">Ar. Ran. 462</cite>. <span lang="grc">οὐ μή</span> with any person of the future indicative occasionally denotes an emphatic future denial; as <span lang="grc">τοὺς πονηροὺς οὐ μή ποτε βελτί̄ους ποιήσετε</span> _you will never make the bad better_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.177">Aes. 3.177</cite>.

##### <a id="s1920"></a> §1920

<span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> and <span lang="grc">ὅπως μή</span> are used with the future in urgent exhortations and prohibitions: <span lang="grc">ὅπως οὖν ἔσεσθε ἄξιοι τῆς ἐλευθερίᾱς</span> _prove yourselves then worthy of freedom_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.3">X. A. 1.7.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅπως τοίνυν περὶ τοῦ πολέμου μηδὲν ἐρεῖς</span> _say nothing therefore about the war_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.92">D. 19.92</cite>. For the fuller form of this use after <span lang="grc">σκόπει, σκοπεῖτε</span>. see [2213](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/03-object-clauses.md#s2213).

##### <a id="s1921"></a> §1921

<span lang="grc">ὅπως μή</span> (negative <span lang="grc">ὅπως μὴ οὐ</span>) may express the desire to avert something; as <span lang="grc">ὅπως μὴ αἰσχροὶ φαινούμεθα</span> _mind we don_'_t appear base_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.2.39">X. C. 4.2.39</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ὅπως μὴ οὐχ οἷός τ᾽ ἔσομαι</span> _but_ (I fear that) _I shall not be able_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 506d">P. R. 506d</cite>. Cp. [1802](07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1802), [1803](07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1803), 2229.

##### <a id="s1922"></a> §1922

On <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (<span lang="grc">κέ</span>) with the future indicative, see [1793](07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1793). On the periphrastic future see [1959](#s1959); on the future in dependent clauses, see [2203](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/02-purpose-clauses-final-clauses.md#s2203), [2211](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/03-object-clauses.md#s2211), 2220 a, 2229, 2231, 2328, 2549-2551, 2554, 2558, 2559, 2565 a, 2573 c.

### AORIST INDICATIVE

##### <a id="s1923"></a> §1923

The aorist expresses the mere occurrence of an action in the past. The action is regarded as an event or single fact without reference to the length of time it occupied.

<span lang="grc">ἐνί̄κησαν οἱ Κερκῡραῖοι καὶ ναῦς πέντε καὶ δέκα διέφθειραν</span> _the Corcyraeans were victorious and destroyed fifteen ships_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.29">T. 1.29</cite>, <span lang="grc">Παιώνιος ἐποίησε</span> _Paeonius fecit_ I.G.A. /lref&gt;, <span lang="grc">ἔδοξεν τῇ βουλῇ</span> _it was voted by_ (seemed good to) _the Senate_ C.I.A. /lref&gt;

**a.** The uses of the aorist may be explained by the figure of a point in time:

\1. The starting point (ingressive aorist, [1924](#s1924)); 2. The end point (resultative aorist, [1926](#s1926)); 3. The whole action (beginning to end) concentrated to a point (complexive aorist, [1927](#s1927)). <!-- page 430 -->

##### <a id="s1924"></a> §1924

**Ingressive Aorist**.—The aorist of verbs whose present denotes a state or a continued action, expresses the entrance into that state or the beginning of that action.

**a.** This holds true of the other moods. Greek has no special form to denote entrance into a state in present time ([1853](#s1853)).

##### <a id="s1925"></a> §1925

Most of the verbs in question are denominatives, and the forms are chiefly those of the first aorist:—

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="grc">ἄρχω</span> _rule_ | <span lang="grc">ἦρξα</span> _became ruler_ |
| <span lang="grc">βασιλεύω</span> _am king, rule_ | <span lang="grc">ἐβασίλευσα</span> _became king, ascended the throne_ |
| <span lang="grc">βλέπω</span> _look at_ | <span lang="grc">ἔβλεψα</span> _cast a glance_ |
| <span lang="grc">δακρύ̄ω</span> _weep_ | <span lang="grc">ἐδάκρῡσα</span> _burst into tears_ |
| <span lang="grc">δουλεύω</span> _am a slave_ | <span lang="grc">ἐδούλευσα</span> _became a slave_ |
| <span lang="grc">ἐρῶ</span> _love_ | <span lang="grc">ἠράσθην</span> _fell in love_ |
| <span lang="grc">θαρρῶ</span> _am courageous_ | <span lang="grc">ἐθάρρησα</span> _plucked up courage_ |
| <span lang="grc">νοσῶ</span> _am ill_ | <span lang="grc">ἐνόσησα</span> _fell ill_ |
| <span lang="grc">πλουτῶ</span> _am rich_ | <span lang="grc">ἐπλούτησα</span> _became rich_ |
| <span lang="grc">πολεμῶ</span> _make war_ | <span lang="grc">ἐπολέμησα</span> _began the war_ |
| <span lang="grc">σῑγῶ</span> _am silent_ | <span lang="grc">ἐσί̄γησα</span> _became silent_ |

**a.** Rarely with the second aorist: <span lang="grc">ἔσχον</span> _took hold, took possession of, got_, as <span lang="grc">Πεισιστράτου τελευτήσαντος Ἱππίᾱς ἔσχε τὴν ἀρχήν</span> _when Peisistratus died Hippias succeeded to his power_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.54">T. 6.54</cite>. So <span lang="grc">ᾐσθόμην</span> _became aware_, <span lang="grc">ἔστην</span> _took my stand_ (perfect <span lang="grc">ἕστηκα</span> _am standing_).

**b.** The aorist of these verbs denotes also a simple occurrence of the action as an historical fact: <span lang="grc">ἐβασίλευσα</span> _was king, ruled_, <span lang="grc">ἐνόσησα</span> _was ill_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνοι πέντε καὶ τετταράκοντα ἔτη τῶν Ἑλλήνων ἦρξαν</span> _they held the supremacy over Greece for forty-five years_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 3.24">D. 3.24</cite> (cp. [1927](#s1927) b).

##### <a id="s1926"></a> §1926

**Resultative Aorist**.—In contrast to the imperfect (and present) the aorist denotes the result, end, or effect of an action.

Thus, <span lang="grc">ἤγαγον</span> _I brought_, <span lang="grc">ἐβούλευσα</span> _I decided_ (<span lang="grc">ἐβούλευον</span> _I was deliberating_), <span lang="grc">ἔθηξα</span> _I sharpened_, <span lang="grc">ἔπεσον</span> _I struck in falling_ (<span lang="grc">ἔπῑπτον</span> _I was in the act of falling_), <span lang="grc">ἔπεισα</span> _I succeeded in persuading_ ([1895](#s1895)).

**a.** The same verb may be a resultative aorist or an ingressive aorist. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔβαλον</span> _I let fly_ a missile (ingressive), and _I hit_ (resultative); <span lang="grc">κατέσχον</span> _I got possession of_ (ingressive), and _I kept back_ (resultative).

**b.** <span lang="grc">ἔκτεινά σε</span> <cite data-ref="Eur. Ion 1291">E. Ion 1291</cite> means _I tried to kill you_, since <span lang="grc">κτείνω</span> denotes properly only the act of the agent, and does not, like _kill_, also connote the effect of the action upon another.

##### <a id="s1927"></a> §1927

**Complexive Aorist**.—The complexive aorist is used to survey at a glance the course of a past action from beginning to end: <span lang="grc">τούτῳ τῷ τρόπῳ τὴν πόλιν ἐτείχισαν</span> _it was in this manner that they fortified the city_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.93">T. 1.93</cite>. It may sum up the result of a preceding narrative (often containing imperfects, as <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.47">T. 2.47</cite>. 4; 3. [81](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s81)). The complexive aorist appears also in other moods than the indicative.

**a.** This is often called the ‘concentrative’ aorist, because it concentrates the <!-- page 431 --> entire course of an action to a single point. When used of rapid or instantaneous action this aorist is often called ‘momentary.’

**b.** The complexive aorist is used either of a long or of a short period of time: <span lang="grc">τέσσαρα καὶ δέκα ἔτη ἐνέμειναν αἱ σπονδαί</span> _the peace lasted fourteen years_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.2">T. 2.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὀλίγον χρόνον ξυνέμεινεν ἡ ὁμαιχμίᾱ</span> _the league lasted a short time_ 1. 18, <span lang="grc">ἦλθον, εἶδον, ἐνί̄κησα</span> _veni_, _vidi_, _vici_ (“Caesar's brag of came, and saw, and conquered”) Plutarch, Caes. 50.

##### <a id="s1928"></a> §1928

The aorist is commonly used with definite numbers. The imperfect is, however, often employed when an action is represented as interrupted or as proceeding from one stage to another. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐνταῦθα ἔμεινε Κῦρος ἡμέρᾱς τριά̄κοντα</span> _Cyrus remained thirty days there_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.9">X. A. 1.2.9</cite>; <span lang="grc">τέτταρας μῆνας ὅλους ἐσῴζοντο οἱ Φωκεῖς τοὺς ὕστερον, ἡ δὲ τούτου ψευδολογίᾱ μετὰ ταῦθ᾽ ὕστερον αὐτοὺς ἀπώλεσεν</span> _for the four whole ensuing months the Phocians remained safe, but the falsehood of this man afterwards effected their ruin_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.78">D. 19.78</cite>.

##### <a id="s1929"></a> §1929

The aorist enumerates and reports past events. It may be employed in brief continuous narration (<cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.9.6">X. A. 1.9.6</cite>). As a narrative tense it is often used to state the chief events and facts, while the other past tenses set forth subordinate actions and attendant circumstances.

##### <a id="s1930"></a> §1930

**Empiric Aorist**.—With adverbs signifying _often_, _always_, _sometimes_, _already_, _not yet, never_, etc., the aorist expressly denotes a fact of experience (<span lang="grc">ἐμπειρίᾱ</span>).

<span lang="grc">πολλοὶ πολλάκις μειζόνων ἐπιθῡμοῦντες τὰ παρόντ᾽ ἀπώλεσαν</span> _many men often lose what they have from a desire for greater possessions_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.113">D. 23.113</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀθῡμοῦντες ἄνδρες οὔπω τροπαῖον ἔστησαν</span> _faint heart never yet raised a trophy_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Criti. 108c">P. Criti. 108c</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">πολύς</span>: <span lang="grc">ἡ γλῶσσα πολλοὺς εἰς ὄλεθρον ἤγαγεν</span> _the tongue brings many a man to his ruin_ Men. Sent. 205. From this use proceeds 1931.

**a.** The empiric aorist is commonly to be translated by the present or perfect. The statement in the aorist is often based upon a concrete historical fact set forth in the context, and the reader is left to infer that the thought holds good for all time.

##### <a id="s1931"></a> §1931

**Gnomic Aorist** (<span lang="grc">γνώμη</span> _maxim_, _proverb_).—The aorist may express a general truth. The aorist simply states a past occurrence and leaves the reader to draw the inference from a concrete case that what has occurred once is typical of what often occurs: <span lang="grc">παθὼν δέ τε νήπιος ἔγνω</span> _a fool learns by experience_ Hesiod, Works and Days, 218, <span lang="grc">κάλλος μὲν γὰρ ἢ χρόνος ἀνήλωσεν ἢ νόσος ἐμάρᾱνε</span> _for beauty is either wasted by time or withered by disease_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.6">I. 1.6</cite>.

**a.** The gnomic aorist often alternates with the present of general truth ([1877](#s1877)): <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ ἡ πληγὴ παρέστησε τὴν ὀργήν, ἀλλ᾽ ἡ ἀτῑμίᾱ· οὐδὲ τὸ τύπτεσθαι τοῖς ἐλευθέροις ἐστὶ δεινόν . . . ἀλλὰ τὸ ἐφ᾽ ὕβρει</span> _for it is not the blow that causes anger_, _but the disgrace; nor is it the beating that is terrible to freemen, but the insult_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.72">D. 21.72</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 566e">P. R. 566e</cite>.

**b.** The gnomic aorist is regarded as a primary tense ([1858](#s1858)): <span lang="grc">οἱ τύραννοι πλούσιον ὃν ἂν βούλωνται παραχρῆμ᾽ ἐποίησαν</span> _tyrants make rich in a moment whomever they wish_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 20.15">D. 20.15</cite>. <!-- page 432 -->

##### <a id="s1932"></a> §1932

Akin to the gnomic aorist is the aorist employed in general descritions. So in imaginary scenes and in descriptions of manners and custom Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὰν ἀφίκωνται οἱ τετελευτηκότες εἰς τὸν τόπον, οἷ ὁ δαίμων ἕκαστον κομίζ πρῶτον μὲν διεδικάσαντο οἵ τε καλῶς καὶ ὁσίως βιώσαντες καὶ οἱ μή</span> _when the de&lt;\*&gt; reach the place whither each is severally conducted by his genius, first of all the have judgment pronounced upon them as they have lived well and devoutly not_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 113d">P. Ph. 113d</cite>, <span lang="grc">φᾶρος δὲ αὐτημερὸν ἐξυφήναντες οἱ ἱ̄ρέες κατ᾽ ὦν ἔδησαν ἑνὸς αὐτ&lt;\*&gt; μίτρῃ τοὺς ὀφθαλμούς</span> _after having woven a mantle on the same day the pries bind the eyes of one of their number with a snood_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 2.122">Hdt. 2.122</cite>.

##### <a id="s1933"></a> §1933

**Iterative Aorist**.—With <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> the aorist may denote repetition ([1790](07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1790)) <span lang="grc">εἶπεν ἄν</span> _he used to say_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.1.14">X. C. 7.1.14</cite>. Distinguish 2303.

##### <a id="s1934"></a> §1934

**Aorist for Future**.—The aorist may be substituted for the futur when a future event is vividly represented as having actually occurred: <span lang="grc">ἀπωλ&lt;\*&gt; μην ἄρ᾽, εἴ με δὴ λείψεις</span> _I am undone if thou dost leave me_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Alc. 386">E. Alc. 386</cite>.

##### <a id="s1935"></a> §1935

**Aorist in Similes**.—The aorist is used in similes in poetry, and usually contains the point of comparison. It may alternate with the present. Thus <span lang="grc">ἤριπε δ᾽ ὡς ὅτε τις δρῦς ἠ´ριπεν</span> _he fell as falls an oak_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 16.482"><span lang="grc">Π</span> 482</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἷος δ᾽ ἐκ νεφέων ἀνα φαίνεται οὔλιος ἀστὴρ ι παμφαίνων, τότε δ᾽ αὖτις ἔδῡ νέφεα σκιόεντα, ι ὣς Ἕκτωρ κτλ</span> _and as from out of the clouds all radiant appears a baneful star, and then again sinks within the shadowy clouds, so Hector_, etc. <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 1.62"><span lang="grc">Α</span> 62</cite>.

**a.** The aorist in 1931, 1935 is used of time past (in 1934 of the future), from the point of view of an assumed or ideal present.

##### <a id="s1936"></a> §1936

**Aorist for Present**.—The aorist is used in questions with <span lang="grc">τί οὖν ου</span> and <span lang="grc">τί οὐ</span> to express surprise that something has not been done. The question is here equivalent to a command or proposal: <span lang="grc">τί οὖν οὐχὶ καὶ σὺ ὑπέμνησάς με</span>, _why don_'_t you recall it to my mind?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hiero 1.3">X. Hi. 1.3</cite>. The (less lively) present, and the future, may also be used.

##### <a id="s1937"></a> §1937

**Dramatic Aorist**.—The first person singular of the aorist is used in the dialogue parts of tragedy and comedy to denote a state of mind or an act expressing a state of mind (especially approval or disapproval) occurring to the speaker in the moment just passed. This use is derived from familiar discourse, but is not found in good prose. In translation the present is employed. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἥσθην, ἐγέλασα</span> _I am delighted_, _I can_'_t help laughing_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Kn. 696">Ar. Eq. 696</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐδεξάμην τὸ ῥηθέν</span> _I welcome the omen_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 668">S. El. 668</cite> (prose <span lang="grc">δέχομαι τὸν οἰωνόν</span>). So <span lang="grc">ἐπῄνεσα</span> _I approve_, <span lang="grc">ξυνῆκα</span> _I understand_. Sometimes this use appears outside of dialogue (<span lang="grc">ἀπέπτυσα</span> _I spurn_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 1070">A. Pr. 1070</cite>, Ag. [1193](../17-pronouns.md#s1193)).

##### <a id="s1938"></a> §1938

With _verbs of swearing_, _commanding_, _saying_ , and _advising_ the aorist may denote a resolution that has already been formed by the speaker and remains unalterable: <span lang="grc">σὲ . . . εἶπον τῆσδε γῆς ἔξω περᾶν</span> _I command thee_ (once and for all) _to depart from out this land_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 272">E. Med. 272</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπώμοσα</span> _I swear_ ‘_nay_’ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 1289">S. Ph. 1289</cite>. This use is not confined to dialogue.

##### <a id="s1939"></a> §1939

So in other cases: <span lang="grc">πῶς τοῦτ᾽ ἔλεξας; οὐ κάτοιδ᾽ ὅπως λέγεις</span> _how saidst thou_ (_what dost thou mean_)_? I do not know how thou meanest_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 270">S. Aj. 270</cite>. Cp. <span lang="grc">νῦν</span> with the aorist (B 113, <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 3.439"><span lang="grc">Γ</span> 439</cite>).

##### <a id="s1940"></a> §1940

**Aorist for Perfect**.—In Greek the aorist, which simply states a past <!-- page 433 --> occurrence, is often employed where English uses the perfect denoting a present condition resulting from a past action. Thus, <span lang="grc">παρεκάλεσα ὑ̄μᾶς, ἄνδρες φίλοι</span> _I_ (have) _summoned you, my friends_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.6.6">X. A. 1.6.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁ μὲν τοίνυν πόλεμος ἁπάντων ἡμᾶς τῶν εἰρημένων ἀπεστέρηκεν· καὶ γὰρ πενεστέρους ἐποίησε καὶ πολλοὺς κινδύ̄νους ὑπομένειν ἠνάγκασε καὶ πρὸς τοὺς Ἕλληνας διαβέβληκε καὶ πάντας τρόπους τεταλαιπώρηκεν ἡμᾶς</span> _now the war has deprived us of all the blessings that have been mentioned; for it has made us poorer, compelled us to undergo many dangers, has brought us into reproach with the Greeks_, _and in every possible way has caused us suffering_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 8.19">I. 8.19</cite>. Sometimes the aorist is chosen because of its affinity to the negative, as <span lang="grc">τῶν οἰκετῶν οὐδένα κατέλιπεν ἀλλ᾽ ἅπαντας πέπρᾱκε</span> _he_ (has) _left not one of his servants, but has sold them all_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 1.99">Aes. 1.99</cite>. This aorist is sometimes regarded as a primary tense.

**a.** Where an active transitive perfect is not formed from a particular verb, or is rarely used, the aorist takes its place: <span lang="grc">Φεραίων μὲν ἀφῄρηται τὴν πόλιν καὶ φρουρὰ̄ν ἐν τῇ ἀκροπόλει κατέστησεν</span> _he has deprived the Pheraeans of their city and established a garrison in the acropolis_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 7.32">D. 7.32</cite> (<span lang="grc">καθέστᾰκε</span> transitive is not classic). So <span lang="grc">ἤγαγον</span> is used for <span lang="grc">ἦχα</span>.

**b.** In Greek of the classical period the aorist and perfect are not confused though the difference between the two tenses is often subtle. Cp. <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.72">D. 19.72</cite> with 19. 177.

##### <a id="s1941"></a> §1941

The aorist may be translated by the perfect when the perfect has the force of a present ([1946](#s1946), [1947](#s1947)): <span lang="grc">ἐκτησάμην</span> _I have acquired_ (<span lang="grc">κέτκημαι</span> _I possess_), <span lang="grc">ἐθαύμασα</span> _I have wondered_ (<span lang="grc">τεθαύμακα</span> _I admire_). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔκτησο αὐτὸς τά περ αὐτὸς ἐκτήσαο</span> _keep thyself what thyself hast gained_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 7.29">Hdt. 7.29</cite>.

##### <a id="s1942"></a> §1942

**Epistolary Tenses**.—The writer of a letter or book, the dedicator of an offering, may put himself in the position of the reader or beholder who views the action as past: <span lang="grc">μετ᾽ Ἀρταβάζου, ὅν σοι ἔπεμψα, πρᾶσσε</span> _negotiate with Artabazus whom I send_ (_sent_) _to you_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.129">T. 1.129</cite>, <span lang="grc">Τροίᾱν ἑλόντες Ἀργείων στόλος λάφῡρα ταῦτα . . . ἐπασσάλευσαν</span> _the Argive armament having captured Troy hang_ (_hung_) _up these spoils_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 577">A. Ag. 577</cite>. Cp. [1923](#s1923) (last two examples).

**a.** The perfect is also used: <span lang="grc">ἀπέσταλκά σοι τόνδε τὸν λόγον</span> _I send_ (_have sent_) _you this discourse_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.2">I. 1.2</cite>.

**b.** The imperfect (common in Latin) occurs rarely: <span lang="grc">Μνησίεργος ἐπέστειλε τοῖς οἴκοι χαίρειν καὶ ὑγιαίνειν καὶ αὐτὸς οὕτως ἔφασκε \[ἔχειν\]</span> _Mnesiergus sends greetings and wishes for good health to his friends at home and says that he himself is well_ Jahresheft des oesterreichischen Archaeol. Inst. 7 ([1904](#s1904)), p. 94, <span lang="grc">τῶν δὲ ταῦτα πρᾱξάντων ἄχρι οὗ ὅδε ὁ λόγος ἐγράφετο Τεισίφονος πρεσβύτατος ὢν τῶν ἀδελφῶν τὴν ἀρχὴν εἶχε</span> _up to the date of this portion of my work_, _Tisiphonus_, _as the eldest of the brothers who wrought this deed, maintained control of the government_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.4.37">X. H. 6.4.37</cite>.

##### <a id="s1943"></a> §1943

**Aorist for Pluperfect**.—The aorist with many temporal and causal conjunctions, and in relative clauses, has the force of the Eng. pluperfect. So with <span lang="grc">ἐπεί, ἐπειδή</span> _after that, since_, <span lang="grc">ὅτε, ὡς</span> _when_, <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> _because;_ regularly with <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> _before_, <span lang="grc">ἕως, μέχρι</span> _until:_ <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ ἐσάλπιγξε, ἐπῇσαν</span> _after the trumpeter had given the signal_, _they advanced_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.17">X. A. 1.2.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ δὲ συνῆλθον, ἔλεξε τοιάδε</span> _and when they had come together, he spoke as follows_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.1.19">X. C. 5.1.19</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐκέλευσέ με τὴν ἐπιστολὴν ἣν</span> <!-- page 434 --> <span lang="grc">ἔγραψα οἴκαδε δοῦναι</span> _he requested me to give him the letter which I had written home_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.2.9">X. C. 2.2.9</cite>. So often in other moods than the indicative.

##### <a id="s1944"></a> §1944

In subordinate clauses the action expressed by the aorist may be (a) contemporaneous, (b) antecedent, or (c) subsequent to that set forth by the main verb. The context alone decides in which sense the aorist is to be taken. (a) <span lang="grc">ἐν τῷ χρόνῳ ὃν ἐπέσχε ὅσα ἐδύνατο κατενόησε</span> _during the time he waited he learned all he could_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.138">T. 1.138</cite>; (b) <span lang="grc">ἐτράποντο ἐς τὸν Πάνορμον, ὅθενπερ ἀνηγάγοντο</span> _they turned toward Panormus_, _the very place from which they had put out_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.92">T. 2.92</cite> (see [1943](#s1943)); (c) <span lang="grc">ἐμάχοντο μέχρι οἱ Ἀθηναῖοι ἀπέπλευσαν</span> _they kept fighting until the Athenians had sailed away_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.1.3">X. H. 1.1.3</cite>.

### PERFECT INDICATIVE

##### <a id="s1945"></a> §1945

The perfect denotes a completed action the effects of which still continue in the present: <span lang="grc">τὰ οἰκήματα ᾠκοδόμηται</span> _the rooms have been constructed_ (their construction is finished) <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 9.2">X. O. 9.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ̄ς πόλεις αὐτῶν παρῄρηται</span> _he has taken away_ (and still holds) _their cities_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.26">D. 9.26</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπείληφα</span> _I have formed_ (_hold_) _the opinion_ 18. 123, <span lang="grc">βεβούλευμαι</span> _I have_ (_am_) _resolved_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 947">S. El. 947</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί βουλεύεσθον ποιεῖν; οὐδ&lt;\*&gt;ν, ἔφη ὁ Χαρμίδης, ἀλλὰ βεβουλεύμεθα</span> _what are you conspiring to do? Nothing_, _said Charmides; we have already conspired_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Charm. 176c">P. Charm. 176c</cite>.

**a.** The effects of a completed action are seen in the resulting present state. The state may be that of the subject or of the object: <span lang="grc">ἐφοβήθην, καὶ ἔτι καὶ νῦν τεθορύβημαι</span> _I was struck with fear, and even at the present moment am still in a state of agitation_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 2.4">Aes. 2.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ πολέμιοι τὰ̄ς σπονδὰ̄ς λελύκᾱσιν</span> _the enemy have broken the truce_ (which is now broken) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.10">X. A. 3.2.10</cite>.

##### <a id="s1946"></a> §1946

**Perfect with Present Meaning**.—When the perfect marks the enduring result rather than the completed act, it may often be translated by the present.

Thus, <span lang="grc">κέκλημαι</span> (have received a name) _am called, my name is_, <span lang="grc">κέκτημαι</span> (have acquired) _possess_, <span lang="grc">μέμνημαι</span> (have recalled) _remember_, <span lang="grc">τέθνηκα</span> (have passed away) _am dead_, <span lang="grc">εἴθισμαι</span> (have accustomed myself) _am accustomed_, <span lang="grc">ἠμφίεσμαι</span> (have clothed myself in) _have on_, <span lang="grc">πέποιθα</span> (have put confidence) _trust_, <span lang="grc">ἕστηκα</span> (have set myself) _stand_, <span lang="grc">βέβηκα</span> (have stepped) _stand_ and _am gone_, <span lang="grc">ἔγνωκα</span> (have recognized) _know_, <span lang="grc">πέφῡκα</span> (_natus sum_) _am by nature_, <span lang="grc">οἶδα</span> (have found out) _know_.

**a.** These perfecta praesentia do not in nature differ from other perfects.

##### <a id="s1947"></a> §1947

**‘Intensive’ Perfect**.—Many perfects seem to denote an action rather than a state resulting from an action, and to be equivalent to strengthened presents. These are often called _intensive_ perfects.

Such are: verbs of the _senses_ (<span lang="grc">δέδορκα</span> _gaze_, <span lang="grc">πέφρῑκα</span> _shudder_), of _sustained sound_ (<span lang="grc">κέκρᾱγα</span> _bawl_, <span lang="grc">λέληκα</span> _shout_, <span lang="grc">βέβρῡχα</span> _roar_), of _emotion_ (<span lang="grc">πεφόβημαι</span> _am filled with alarm_, <span lang="grc">γέγηθα</span> _am glad_, <span lang="grc">μέμηλε</span> _cares for_), of _gesture_ (<span lang="grc">κέχηνα</span> _keep the mouth agape_), and many others (<span lang="grc">σεσί̄γηκα</span> _am still_, etc.). <!-- page 435 -->

**a.** But most if not all of the verbs in question may be regarded as true perfects, i.e. they denote a mental or physical state resulting from the accomplishment of the action; thus, <span lang="grc">πέφρῑκα</span> _I have shuddered and am now in a state of shuddering_.

**b.** Certain verbs tend to appear in the perfect for emphasis: <span lang="grc">τέθνηκα</span> _am dead_, <span lang="grc">ἀπόλωλα</span> _perish_, <span lang="grc">πέπρᾱκα</span> _sell_ (_have sold_).

##### <a id="s1948"></a> §1948

**Empiric Perfect**.—The perfect may set forth a general truth expressly based on a fact of experience: <span lang="grc">ἡ ἀταξίᾱ πολλοὺς ἤδη ἀπολώλεκεν</span> _lack of discipline ere now has been the ruin of many_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.38">X. A. 3.1.38</cite>. Cp. [1930](#s1930).

##### <a id="s1949"></a> §1949

**Perfect of Dated Past Action**.—The perfect is sometimes used of a past action whose time is specifically stated: <span lang="grc">ὕβρισμαι τότε</span> _I was insulted on that occasion_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.7">D. 21.7</cite>. This use approaches that of the aorist.

##### <a id="s1950"></a> §1950

**Perfect for Future Perfect**.—The perfect may be used vividly for the future perfect to anticipate an action not yet done: <span lang="grc">κἂ̄ν τοῦτο νῑκῶμεν, πάνθ᾽ ἡμῖν πεποίηται</span> _and if we conquer in that quarter, everything has been_ (will have been) _accomplished by us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.12">X. A. 1.8.12</cite>.

**a.** Especially with the phrase <span lang="grc">τὸ ἐπί τινι</span>, the perfect anticipates the certain occurrence of an event: <span lang="grc">τὸ ἐπὶ τούτῳ ἀπολώλαμεν</span> _for all he could do, we had perished_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.6.23">X. A. 6.6.23</cite>.

##### <a id="s1951"></a> §1951

In subordinate clauses, the action of the perfect is usually (a) contemporaneous, but may be (b) antecedent to that of the main verb. The context alone decides in which sense the perfect is to be taken. (a) <span lang="grc">οἱ δὲ θεράποντες, ἐπειδὴ ἐς ἀντίπαλα καθεστήκαμεν, αὐτομολοῦσι</span> _while our attendants desert, now that we have been brought down to a level_ with the Syracusans <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.13">T. 7.13</cite>. (b) <span lang="grc">ἅ σοι τύχη κέχρηκε, ταῦτ᾽ ἀφείλετο</span> _Fortune has taken back what she has lent you_ Men. fr. 598.

On the epistolary perfect see [1942 a](#s1942).

### PLUPERFECT

##### <a id="s1952"></a> §1952

The pluperfect is the past of the perfect, hence it denotes a past fixed state resulting from a completed action: <span lang="grc">ἐβεβουλεύμην</span> _I had_ (_was_) _resolved_.

**a.** When the perfect is translated by the present, the pluperfect is rendered by the imperfect: <span lang="grc">ἐκεκτήμην</span> _was in possession_, <span lang="grc">ἐτεθνήκει</span> _he was dead_, <span lang="grc">ᾔδη</span> _knew_, <span lang="grc">ἐμεμνήμην</span> _remembered_. Cp. [1946](#s1946).

##### <a id="s1953"></a> §1953

**Pluperfect of Immediate Occurrence**.—The pluperfect may denote that a past action occurred so immediately or suddenly that it was accomplished almost at the same moment as another action: <span lang="grc">ὡς δὲ ἐλήφθησαν, ἐλέλυντο αἱ σπονδαί</span> _and when they were captured the truce was_ (already) _at an end_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.47">T. 4.47</cite> (the fact of their capture was equivalent to the immediate rupture of the truce).

##### <a id="s1954"></a> §1954

In subordinate clauses the pluperfect is rarely used to mark an action as anterior to an action already past: <span lang="grc">ἦλθον οἱ Ἰνδοὶ ἐκ τῶν πολεμίων οὓς ἐπεπόμφει Κῦρος ἐπὶ κατασκοπήν</span> _the Indians returned whom Cyrus had sent to get news of the enemy_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.2.9">X. C. 6.2.9</cite>. The aorist is usually employed ([1943](#s1943), [1944](#s1944) b).

### FUTURE PERFECT

<!-- page 436 -->

##### <a id="s1955"></a> §1955

The future perfect denotes a future state resulting from a completed action: <span lang="grc">ἀναγεγράψομαι</span> _I shall stand enrolled_, <span lang="grc">δεδήσεται</span> _he shall be kept in prison;_ <span lang="grc">ἡ θύρᾱ κεκλῄσεται</span> _the door will be kept shut_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Lys. 1071">Ar. Lys. 1071</cite>.

**a.** Most future perfects are middle in form, passive in meaning ([581](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/08-vii-perfect-middle-system.md#s581)).

**b.** The active future perfect is usually periphrastic ([600](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/11-periphrastic-forms.md#s600)): <span lang="grc">τὰ δέοντ᾽ ἐσόμεθα ἐγνωκότες</span> _we shall have determined on our duty_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.50">D. 4.50</cite>.

##### <a id="s1956"></a> §1956

When stress is laid upon complete fulfilment, the future perfect may _imply_ rapidity, immediate consequence, or certainty, of action accomplished in the future: <span lang="grc">φράζε, καὶ πεπρά̄ξεται</span> _speak_, _and it shall be done instanter_ Ar. Pl. 1027, <span lang="grc">εὐθὺς Ἀριαῖος ἀφεστήξει· ὥστε φίλος ἡμῖν οὐδεὶς λελείψεται</span> _Ariaeus will soon withdraw_, _so that we shall have no friend left_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.4.5">X. A. 2.4.5</cite>.

##### <a id="s1957"></a> §1957

The future perfect may have an imperative force ([1917](#s1917)): <span lang="grc">εἰρήσεται γὰρ τἀ̄ληθές</span> _for the truth shall_ (let it) _be spoken_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 7.76">I. 7.76</cite>.

##### <a id="s1958"></a> §1958

When the perfect has the force of a present, the future perfect is used like a simple future ([1946](#s1946)): <span lang="grc">κεκλήσομαι</span> _I shall bear the name_, <span lang="grc">μεμνήσομαι</span> _shall remember_, <span lang="grc">κεκτήσομαι</span> _shall possess_. So in the two active forms: <span lang="grc">τεθνήξω</span> _I shall be dead_, <span lang="grc">ἑστήξω</span> _I shall stand_.

**a.** The aorist subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> ([2324](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2324)), not the future perfect, is used to denote a past action in relation to an action still in the future.

### PERIPHRASTIC TENSES

On the periphrastic forms of perfect, pluperfect, and future perfect, see [599](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/11-periphrastic-forms.md#s599), [600](../../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/11-periphrastic-forms.md#s600).

##### <a id="s1959"></a> §1959

**Periphrastic Future**.—A periphrastic future is formed by <span lang="grc"> **μέλλω** </span> _I am about to, intend to, am_ (_destined_) _to_, _am likely to_ (strictly _think_) with the present or future (rarely the aorist) infinitive. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἃ μέλλω λέγειν σοὶ πάλαι δοκεῖ</span> _what I am going to say has long been your opinion_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.13">X. C. 3.3.13</cite> (cp. [1885](#s1885)), <span lang="grc">Κλέανδρος μέλλει ἥξειν</span> _Cleander is on the point of coming_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.4.18">X. A. 6.4.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">θήσειν ἔμελλεν ἄλγεα</span> _he purposed to inflict suffering_ B 39, <span lang="grc">ἔμελλον ὄλβιος εἶναι</span> _I was destined to be happy_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 18.138"><span lang="grc">ς</span> 138</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἴ ποτε πορεύοιτο καὶ πλεῖστοι μέλλοιεν ὄψεσθαι, προσκαλῶν τοὺς φίλους ἐσπουδαιολογεῖτο</span> _if ever_ Cyrus _was on the march and many were likely to catch sight of him, he summoned his friends and engaged them in earnest talk_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.9.28">X. A. 1.9.28</cite>.

**a.** The present infinitive usually occurs with <span lang="grc">μέλλω</span> as a _verb of will_, the future infinitive with <span lang="grc">μέλλω</span> as a _verb of thinking_.

**b.** The aorist is used when it is important to mark the action as ingressive, resultative, or complexive: <span lang="grc">ὅπερ μέλλω παθεῖν</span> _what I am doomed to suffer_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 625">A. Pr. 625</cite>.

**c.** <span lang="grc">μέλλω</span> _I delay_ usually takes the present, rarely the aorist, infinitive.

**d.** <span lang="grc">πῶς οὐ μέλλω</span> and <span lang="grc">τί οὐ μέλλω</span> mean _why should I not?_ Thus, <span lang="grc">τί δ᾽ ου᾽ μέλλει γελοῖον εἶναι</span>; _how should it not be ridiculous?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 530a">P. R. 530a</cite>. <!-- page 437 -->

##### <a id="s1960"></a> §1960

<span lang="grc">ἔμελλον</span> is used of past intention in <span lang="grc">ἔμελλε καταλύ̄ειν</span> _he was about to stop for the night_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.1">X. A. 1.8.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοὺς ἔσπλους κλῄσειν ἔμελλον</span> _they intended to close the entrances_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.8">T. 4.8</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἔμελλον</span> with the infinitive denoting an unfulfilled past intention is a periphrasis for an aorist indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐ συστρατεύειν ἔμελλον</span> _they would not have joined forces_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.159">D. 19.159</cite> (= <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν συνεστράτευσαν</span>). Cp. _recturus eram_, etc.

##### <a id="s1961"></a> §1961

**With <span lang="grc">εἰμί</span>**.—The present and perfect participle are freely used with the forms of <span lang="grc">εἰμί</span> to form a periphrasis, especially when the participle has an adjectival character ([1857](#s1857)): <span lang="grc">ἡγεῖ διαφθειρομένους τινὰς εἶναι</span>; _do you think that some are being ruined?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 492a">P. R. 492a</cite>, <span lang="grc">αἱ τέχναι διεφθαρμέναι ἔσονται</span> _the arts will be ruined_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.2.13">X. C. 7.2.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἦν τοῦτο συμφέρον</span> _this was advantageous_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.18">Ant. 5.18</cite>; <span lang="grc">ᾖ θέλουσα</span> is stronger than <span lang="grc">θέλῃ</span>, <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 580">S. O. T. 580</cite>.

##### <a id="s1962"></a> §1962

The aorist participle is rarely so used, since it denotes a single act, not a characteristic: <span lang="grc">ἦσαν δέ τινες καὶ γενόμενοι τῷ Νῑκίᾳ λόγοι πρότερον πρός τινας</span> _and communications between Nicias and some persons had actually been held before_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.54">T. 4.54</cite>.

**a.** With <span lang="grc">ἔσομαι</span> the aorist participle equals the future perfect: <span lang="grc">οὐ σιωπήσᾱς ἔσῃ</span>; _be silent, won_'_t you, once and for all?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 1146">S. O. T. 1146</cite>.

##### <a id="s1963"></a> §1963

**With <span lang="grc">ἔχω</span>**.—The periphrasis with <span lang="grc">ἔχω</span> and the aorist participle is analogous to the perfect in meaning, and emphasizes the permanence of the result attained (chiefly in Hdt. and the drama): <span lang="grc">κηρύ̄ξᾱς ἔχω</span> _I have proclaimed_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 192">S. Ant. 192</cite>.

**a.** In Attic prose <span lang="grc">ἔχω</span> usu. has a separate force: <span lang="grc">Φερὰ̄ς πρώην ἔχει καταλαβών</span> _he lately seized and now occupies Pherae_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.12">D. 9.12</cite>. So with the (rare) perfect: <span lang="grc">τὰ ἐπιτήδεια εἶχον ἀνακεκομισμένοι</span> _they had carried up_ to the forts _the provisions and kept them_ there <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.7.1">X. A. 4.7.1</cite>.

##### <a id="s1964"></a> §1964

**With <span lang="grc">γίγνομαι</span>**.—The forms of <span lang="grc">γίγνομαι</span> often combine with a participle to form periphrases. Thus, <span lang="grc">μὴ σαυτὸν . . . κτείνᾱς γένῃ</span> _lest thou destroy thyself_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 773">S. Ph. 773</cite>; in prose this periphrasis has the tone of tragedy. On <span lang="grc">γίγνομαι</span> with a substantive, see [1710](02-active-voice.md#s1710), [1754](04-passive-voice.md#s1754).

##### <a id="s1965"></a> §1965

**With <span lang="grc">φαίνομαι</span>**.—The aorist participle is used periphrastically with forms of <span lang="grc">φαίνομαι</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐχ ὑπὲρ ὑ̄μῶν οὐδὲ τῶν νόμων φροντίσᾱς οὐδ᾽ ἀγανακτήσᾱς φανήσεται</span> _it will appear that he took no heed, nor felt any resentment, concerning you or the laws_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.39">D. 21.39</cite>.
