# THE ARTICLE—ORIGIN AND DEVELOPMENT

##### <a id="s1099"></a> §1099

The article <span lang="grc">ὁ, ἡ, τό</span>, was originally a demonstrative pronoun, and as such supplied the place of the personal pronoun of the third person. By gradual weakening it became the definite article. It also served as a relative pronoun ([1105](#s1105)). (Cp. Germ. _der_, demonstrative article and relative; French _le_ from _ille_.) <span lang="grc">ὁ</span> as a demonstrative is still retained in part in Attic prose ([1106](#s1106)), while the beginnings of its use as the article are seen even in Homer ([1102](#s1102)).

## <span lang="grc">**ὁ**, **ἡ**, **τό**</span> IN HOMER

##### <a id="s1100"></a> §1100

In Homer <span lang="grc">ὁ, ἡ, τό</span> is usually a demonstrative pronoun and is used substantively or adjectively; it also serves as the personal pronoun of the third person: <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ τὸ θαυμάζω</span> _but I marvel at this_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 4.655"><span lang="grc">δ</span> 655</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸν λωβητῆρα ἐπεσβόλον</span> _this prating brawler_ B 275, <span lang="grc">τὴν δ᾽ ἐγὼ οὐ λύ̄σω</span> _but her I will not release_ A 29. <!-- page 285 -->

##### <a id="s1101"></a> §1101

In its _substantival_ use <span lang="grc">ὁ</span> either marks a contrast or recalls the subject (the anaphoric use). But with <span lang="grc">ἀλλά, δέ, αὐτάρ</span> the subject is generally changed. It often precedes an explanatory relative clause: <span lang="grc">τῶν οἳ νῦν βροτοί εἰσι</span> _of those who are now mortal men_ A 272.

##### <a id="s1102"></a> §1102

<span lang="grc">ὁ, ἡ, τό</span> often approaches to its later use as the definite article or is actually so used: <span lang="grc">τὸν μέν . . . τὸν δ᾽ ἕτερον</span> E 145 (cp. [1107](#s1107)). **a.** The substantive often stands in apposition, and is added, as an afterthought, to the demonstrative (especially <span lang="grc">ὁ δέ</span>) which is still an independent pronoun: <span lang="grc">αὐτὰρ ὁ τοῖσι γέρων ὁδὸν ἡγεμόνευεν</span> _but he, the old man, was leading the way for them_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 24.225"><span lang="grc">ω</span> 225</cite>. In some cases the appositive is needed to complete the sense: <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ τό γε καλὸν ἀκουέμεν ἐστὶν ἀοιδοῦ</span> _since this—to listen to a minstrel—is a good thing_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 1.370"><span lang="grc">α</span> 370</cite>. **b.** Often with adjectives and participles used substantively, with pronouns, and adverbs; especially when a contrast or distinction is implied: <span lang="grc">οἱ ἄλλοι</span> _the others_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 21.371"><span lang="grc">Φ</span> 371</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ ἐσσόμενα</span> _the things that are to be_ A 70, <span lang="grc">τὸ πάρος</span> _formerly_ N 228. The attributive adj. before the noun: <span lang="grc">τοὺς σούς</span> _thy_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 23.572"><span lang="grc">Ψ</span> 572</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ μέγιστα ἄεθλα</span> _the greatest prizes_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 23.640"><span lang="grc">Ψ</span> 640</cite>; and in apposition: <span lang="grc">Ἶρον τὸν ἀλήτην</span> _Irus_, _the beggar_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 18.333"><span lang="grc">ς</span> 333</cite>. Hom. has <span lang="grc">πατὴρ οὑμός Θ</span> 360 (but does not use <span lang="grc">ὁ πατὴρ ὁ ἐμός</span>).

##### <a id="s1103"></a> §1103

In Hom. <span lang="grc">ὁ</span> _contrasts_ two objects, indicates a change of person, or a change of action on the part of the same person. Attic <span lang="grc">ὁ</span> _defines_.

##### <a id="s1104"></a> §1104

The transition from the demonstrative to the article is so gradual that it is often impossible to distinguish between the two. Ordinarily Homer does not use the article where it is required in Attic prose. The Epic use is adopted in general by the lyric poets and in the lyric parts of tragedy. Even in tragic dialogue the article is less common than in prose. Hdt. has <span lang="grc">ὁ δέ</span> _and he_, <span lang="grc">ὁ γάρ</span> _for he_.

## <span lang="grc">**ὁ**, **ἡ**, **τό**</span> AS A RELATIVE

##### <a id="s1105"></a> §1105

The demonstrative <span lang="grc">ὁ, ἡ, τό</span> is used as a relative pronoun in Homer only when the antecedent is definite (cp. _that_): <span lang="grc">τεύχεα δ᾽ ἐξενάριξε, τά οἱ πόρε χάλκεος Ἄρης</span> _he stripped off the arms that brazen Ares had given him_ H 146. The tragic poets use only the forms in <span lang="grc">τ-</span>, and chiefly to avoid hiatus or to produce position: <span lang="grc">κτείνουσα τοὺς οὐ χρὴ κτανεῖν</span> _slaying those whom it is not right to slay_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Andr. 810">E. And. 810</cite>. (<span lang="grc">ὅ ῀ ὅς</span> <cite data-ref="Eur. Hipp. 525">E. Hipp. 525</cite>.) On the use in Herodotus, see [338 D](../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s338). 3.

## <span lang="grc">**ὁ**, **ἡ**, **τό**</span> AS A DEMONSTRATIVE IN ATTIC PROSE

##### <a id="s1106"></a> §1106

The demonstrative force of <span lang="grc">ὁ, ἡ, τό</span> survives chiefly in connection with particles (<span lang="grc">μέν, δέ, γέ, τοί</span>; and with <span lang="grc">καί</span> preceding <span lang="grc">ὁ</span>).

##### <a id="s1107"></a> §1107

<span lang="grc">ὁ</span> is a demonstrative commonly before <span lang="grc">μέν, δέ</span>, and especially in contrasted expressions: <span lang="grc">ὁ μέν . . . ὁ δέ</span> _the one, this . . . the other, that_, as in <span lang="grc">οἱ μὲν ἐπορεύοντο, οἱ δ᾽ εἵποντο</span> _the one party proceeded, the other followed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.4.16">X. A. 3.4.16</cite>.

##### <a id="s1108"></a> §1108

The reference may be indefinite; in which case <span lang="grc">τὶς</span> is often added: <span lang="grc">τοὺς μὲν ἀπέκτεινε, τοὺς δ᾽ ἐξέβαλεν</span> _some he put to death, and others he expelled_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.7">X. A. 1.1.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ μέν τινες ἀπέθνῃσκον, οἱ δ᾽ ἔφευγον</span> _some were killed, but others escaped_ C. 3. 2. 10. <!-- page 286 -->

##### <a id="s1109"></a> §1109

With prepositions the order is usually inverted: <span lang="grc">ἐκ μὲν τῶν, εἰς δὲ τά</span> ([1663 a](19-prepositions.md#s1663)).

##### <a id="s1110"></a> §1110

In late writers (but in Demosthenes) the relative is used as in 1107: <span lang="grc">πόλεις, ᾱ&lt;\*&gt;ς μὲν ἀναιρῶν, εἰς ᾱ&lt;\*&gt;ς δὲ τοὺς φυγάδας κατάγων</span> _destroying some cities, into others bringing back their exiles_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.71">D. 18.71</cite> (the first instance).

##### <a id="s1111"></a> §1111

Note the adverbial expressions: <span lang="grc">τὸ</span> (<span lang="grc">τὰ</span>) <span lang="grc">μέν . . . τὸ</span> (<span lang="grc">τὰ</span>) <span lang="grc">δέ</span> _on the one hand . . . on the other hand, partly . . . partly_ (so also <span lang="grc">τοῦτο μέν . . . τοῦτο δέ</span> [1256](17-pronouns.md#s1256)); <span lang="grc">τὸ δέ τι</span> _partly_, <span lang="grc">τῇ μέν . . . τῇ δέ</span> _in this way . . . in that way_, <span lang="grc">τὸ δέ</span> _whereas_ ([1112](#s1112)), <span lang="grc">τῷ τοι</span> _therefore_.

##### <a id="s1112"></a> §1112

<span lang="grc">ὁ δέ, ἡ δέ, τὸ δέ</span> (without a preceding <span lang="grc">μέν</span> clause) often mean _but_ (or _and_) _he_, _she_, _this_. In the nominative the person referred to is usually different from the subject of the main verb: <span lang="grc">Κῦρος δίδωσιν αὐτῷ μῡρίους δᾱρεικούς· ὁ δὲ λαβὼν τὸ χρῡσίον κ.τ.λ.</span> _Cyrus gives him_ (Clearchus) _10,000 darics; and he taking the money_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.9">X. A. 1.1.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">ταῦτα ἀπαγγέλλουσι τοῖς στρατιώταις· τοῖς δὲ ὑποψίᾱ ἦν ὅτι ἄγοι πρὸς βασιλέᾱ</span> _they report this to the soldiers; and they had a suspicion that he was leading_ (them) _against the king_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.21">X. A. 1.3.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ δ᾽ οὐκ ἔστι τοιοῦτον</span> _whereas this is not so_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 37a">P. A. 37a</cite>.

## **VARIOUS USES OF <span lang="grc">ὁ</span>** (<span lang="grc"> **ὅς** </span>), <span lang="grc"> **ἡ** </span> (<span lang="grc">ἥ</span>), **<span lang="grc">τό</span> DEMONSTRATIVE**

##### <a id="s1113"></a> §1113

As a personal pronoun, chiefly after <span lang="grc">καί</span>, and in the nominative: <span lang="grc">καὶ ὅς</span> (<span lang="grc">ἥ</span>) _and he_ (_she_): <span lang="grc">καὶ οἳ εἶπον</span> _and they said_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.6.4">X. A. 7.6.4</cite>. Also in <span lang="grc">ἦ δ᾽ ὅς</span> _and he said_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 327c">P. R. 327c</cite> ([792](../part-2-inflection/10-verb-stem-and-present-stem/16-irregular-mi-verbs.md#s792)). So <span lang="grc">καὶ τόν</span> (<span lang="grc">τήν</span>) used as the accusative of <span lang="grc">καὶ ὅς</span>, as subject of a following infinitive in indirect discourse: <span lang="grc">καὶ τὸν εἰπεῖν</span> _and_ (he said that) _he said_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 174a">P. S. 174a</cite>.

##### <a id="s1114"></a> §1114

In the nominative <span lang="grc">ὅς, ἥ</span>, are usually thus written. Some write <span lang="grc">ὅ, ἥ, οἵ, αἵ</span> when these words are used as demonstratives; but <span lang="grc">ὃ μέν . . . ὃ δέ</span> is rare.

**a.** The forms <span lang="grc">ὅς, ἥ</span>, here apparently relatives with an older demonstrative force, may be in reality demonstratives, <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> being the demonstrative (article) <span lang="grc">ὁ</span> to which the nominative sign <span lang="grc">-ς</span> has been added. From this <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> may be derived, by analogy, the demonstrative use of <span lang="grc">ὅ</span>, and of <span lang="grc">οἶς, οὕς</span> in fixed expressions ([1110](#s1110)).

##### <a id="s1115"></a> §1115

Also in <span lang="grc">τὸν καὶ τόν</span> _this one and that one_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 1.23">L. 1.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ καὶ τό</span> _this and that_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.68">D. 9.68</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ καὶ τά</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.141">D. 21.141</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὔτε τοῖς οὔτε τοῖς</span> _neither to these nor to those_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 701e">P. L. 701e</cite>. In the nom. <span lang="grc">ὃς καὶ ὅς</span> _such and such an one_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 4.68">Hdt. 4.68</cite>.

##### <a id="s1116"></a> §1116

In an oblique case before the relatives <span lang="grc">ὅς, ὅσος, οἶος</span>: <span lang="grc">τόν τε Εὐθύκριτον . . . καὶ τὸν ὃς ἔρη δεσπότης τούτου εἶναι, μάρτυρας παρέξομαι</span> _and as witness I will produce both Euthycritus and the man who said he was his master_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 23.8">L. 23.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὀρέγεται τοῦ ὃ ἔστιν ἴσον</span> _he aims at that which is equal_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 75b">P. Ph. 75b</cite>, and often in Plato in defining philosophical terms.

##### <a id="s1117"></a> §1117

Rarely with prepositions, except in <span lang="grc">πρὸ τοῦ</span> (or <span lang="grc">προτοῦ</span>) _before this time_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.118">T. 1.118</cite>. On <span lang="grc">ἐν τοῖς</span> with the superlative, see [1089](14-adjectives.md#s1089).

## <span lang="grc">**ό**, **ἡ**, **τό**</span> AS AN ARTICLE (_the_) IN ATTIC (ESPECIALLY IN PROSE)

##### <a id="s1118"></a> §1118

The article <span lang="grc">ὁ, ἣ, τό</span> marks objects as definite and known, whether individuals (the _particular_ article) or classes (the _generic_ <!-- page 287 --> article). The context must determine the presence of the generic article.

**a.** There is no indefinite article in Greek, but _a_, _an_ is often represented by <span lang="grc">τὶς</span> ([1267](17-pronouns.md#s1267)).

## THE PARTICULAR ARTICLE

##### <a id="s1119"></a> §1119

The particular article denotes individual persons or things as distinguished from others of the same kind. Thus, <span lang="grc">μαίνεται ἅ̄νθρωπος</span> _the man is mad_ (a definite person, distinguished from other men) <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 268c">P. Phae. 268c</cite>.

##### <a id="s1120"></a> §1120

Special uses of the particular article. The particular article defines

**a.** Objects well known: <span lang="grc">ὁ τῶν ἑπτὰ σοφώτατος Σόλων</span> _Solon the wisest of the Seven_ (_Sages_) <cite data-ref="Plat. Tim. 20d">P. Tim. 20d</cite>.

**b** Objects already mentioned or in the mind of the speaker or writer (the _anaphoric_ article): <span lang="grc">εἶπον ὅτι τάλαντον ἀργυρίου ἕτοιμος εἴην δοῦναι . . . ὁ δὲ λαβὼν τὸ τάλαντον κ.τ.λ.</span> _I said that I was ready to give him a talent of silver . . . and he taking the talent_, etc. <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.9">L. 12.9</cite>-10.

**c.** Objects specially present to the senses or mind (the _deictic_ article): <span lang="grc">λαβε τὸ βιβλίον</span> _take the book_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 143c">P. Th. 143c</cite>, <span lang="grc">βουλόμενος τὴν μάχην ποιῆσαι</span> _wishing to fight the battle_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.91">T. 4.91</cite>. Hence the article is regularly used with demonstrative pronouns ([1176](#s1176)).

N.—The foregoing (**a - c**) uses recall the old demonstrative force of the article. Words that ordinarily have no article may receive the article when this older force is present.

**d.** Objects particularized by an attributive or by a following description: <span lang="grc">ὁ δῆμος ὁ Ἀθηναίων</span> _the people of the Athenians_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.116">Aes. 3.116</cite>, <span lang="grc">λέγε τὴν ἐπιστολήν, ἣν ἔπεμψεν</span> _read the letter that he sent_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.39">D. 18.39</cite>. Cp. [1178 d](#s1178).

**e.** Objects marked as _usual_ or _proper_ under the circumstances: <span lang="grc">τὸ μέρος τῶν ψήφων ὁ διώκων οὐκ ἔλαβεν</span> _the prosecutor did not get the_ (requisite) _part of the votes_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.103">D. 18.103</cite>.

**f.** Objects representative of their class (the _distributive_ article, which resembles the generic use; often translated by _a_, _each_): <span lang="grc">ὑπισχνεῖται δώσειν τρία ἡμιδᾱρεικὰ τοῦ μηνὸς τῷ στρατιώτῃ</span> _he promises to give each soldier three half-darics a month_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.21">X. A. 1.3.21</cite>. But the article may be omitted: <span lang="grc">καὶ εἵλοντο δέκα, ἕνα ἀπὸ φῡλῆς</span> _and they chose ten, one from_ (_each_) _tribe_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.4.23">X. H. 2.4.23</cite>.

##### <a id="s1121"></a> §1121

The article often takes the place of an unemphatic possessive pronoun when there is no doubt as to the possessor. <span lang="grc">Κῦρος καταπηδήσᾱς ἀπὸ τοῦ ἅρματος τὸν θώρᾱκα ἐνέδῡ</span> _Cyrus leaped down from his chariot and put on his breastplate_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.3">X. A. 1.8.3</cite>.

## THE GENERIC ARTICLE

##### <a id="s1122"></a> §1122

The generic article denotes an entire class as distinguished from other classes. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁ ἄνθρωπος</span> _man_ (as distinguished from other beings), <span lang="grc">οἱ γέροντες</span> _the aged;_ <span lang="grc">δεῖ τὸν στρατιώτην φοβεῖσθαι μᾶλλον τὸν ἄρχοντα ἢ τοὺς πολεμίους</span> _the_ (_a_) _soldier should fear his commander_ <!-- page 288 --> _rather than the enemy_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.6.10">X. A. 2.6.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">πονηρὸν ὁ σῡκοφάντης</span> _the informer is a vile thing_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.242">D. 18.242</cite>.

##### <a id="s1123"></a> §1123

In the singular the generic article makes a single object the representative of the entire class; in the plural it denotes all the objects belonging to a class. The generic article is especially common, in the plural, with adjectives used substantively: <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἄν τις εἴποι ὡς τοὺς κακούργους καὶ ἀδίκους εἴᾱ καταγελᾶν</span> _no one could say that he permitted the malefactor and the wrongdoer to deride him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.9.13">X. A. 1.9.13</cite>.

##### <a id="s1124"></a> §1124

**The Article with Participles**.—A participle with the article may denote an entire class: <span lang="grc">ὁ βουλόμενος</span> _any one who wishes_. Cp. [2050](22-the-participle.md#s2050), [2052](22-the-participle.md#s2052).

<span lang="grc">ὁ τυχών</span> _any chance comer_, <span lang="grc">ὁ ἡγησόμενος</span> _a guide_, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀπορήσετε τῶν ἐθελησόντων ὑπὲρ ὑ̄μῶν κινδῡνεύειν</span> _you will not be in want of those who will be willing to encounter danger for you_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 20.166">D. 20.166</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ λογοποιοῦντες</span> _newsmongers_ 4. 49. The same sense is expressed by <span lang="grc">πᾶς ὁ</span> with a participle or adjective. On the article with a participle in the predicate, see [1152](#s1152).

**a.** When the reference is to a particular occasion, the article may be particular ([2052](22-the-participle.md#s2052)); as <span lang="grc">ὁ λέγων</span> the speaker on a definite occasion.

## THE ARTICLE WITH NUMERALS

##### <a id="s1125"></a> §1125

The article may be used with cardinal numerals

**a.** When the numeral states the definite part of a whole (expressed or understood): <span lang="grc">ἀπῆσαν τῶν λόχων δώδεκα ὄντων οἱ τρεῖς</span> _of the companies, numbering twelve_ (in all), _there were absent three_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 7.5.10">X. H. 7.5.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἷς παρὰ τοὺς δέκα</span> _one man in_ (comparison with) _ten_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 20.16">X. O. 20.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῶν πέντε τὰ̄ς δύο μοίπᾱς</span> _two fifths_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.10">T. 1.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">δύο μέρη</span> _two thirds_ 3. 15. (The genitive is omitted when the denominator exceeds the numerator by _one_.)

**b.** When the numeral is _approximate:_ <span lang="grc">ἔμειναν ἡμέρᾱς ἀμφὶ τὰ̄ς τριά̄κοντα</span> _they remained about thirty days_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.8.22">X. A. 4.8.22</cite>, <span lang="grc">γεγονότες τὰ πεντήκοντα ἔτη</span> _about fifty years of age_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.2.13">X. C. 1.2.13</cite>.

**c.** When the number is used abstractly (without reference to any definite object): <span lang="grc">ὅπως μὴ ἐρεῖς ὅτι ἔστιν τὰ δώδεκα δὶς ἕξ</span> _beware of saying 12 is twice 6_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 337b">P. R. 337b</cite>.

N. Ordinals usually omit the article and regularly do so in statements of time in the dative ([1540](18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1540)): <span lang="grc">δευτέρῳ μηνὶ τὴν πόλιν ἐτείχιζον</span> _in the second month they fortified the city_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.64">T. 8.64</cite>.

## FLUCTUATION IN THE USE OF THE ARTICLE: OMISSION OF THE ARTICLE

##### <a id="s1126"></a> §1126

The article is often omitted (1) in words and phrases which have survived from the period when <span lang="grc">ὁ, ἡ, τό</span> was a demonstrative pronoun; (2) when a word is sufficiently definite by itself; (3) when a word expresses a general conception without regard to its application to a definite person. The generic article is frequently omitted, especially with abstracts ([1132](#s1132)), without appreciable difference in meaning. Its presence or absence is often determined by the need of distinguishing subject from predicate ([1150](#s1150)), by the rhythm of the sentence, etc. <!-- page 289 -->

##### <a id="s1127"></a> §1127

The article is omitted in many adverbial designations of _time_, mostly with prepositions (except <span lang="grc">ἡμέρᾱς</span> _by day_, <span lang="grc">νυκτός</span> _by night_).

Thus, <span lang="grc">περὶ μέσᾱς νύκτας</span> _about midnight_, <span lang="grc">ἅμα ἕῳ</span> _just before daylight_, <span lang="grc">ὥρᾳ ἕτους</span> _at the season of the year_. So with <span lang="grc">ὄρθρος</span> _daybreak_, <span lang="grc">δείλη</span> _afternoon_, <span lang="grc">ἑσπέρᾱ</span> _evening_, <span lang="grc">ἔαρ</span> _spring_; and <span lang="grc">ἐκ παίδων</span> _from childhood_. Most of the above cases are survivals of the older period when the article had a demonstrative force.

##### <a id="s1128"></a> §1128

The article is very often omitted in phrases containing a preposition: <span lang="grc">ἐν ἀρχῇ τοῦ λόγου</span> _in the beginning of the speech_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 37.23">D. 37.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔξω βελῶν</span> _out of reach of the missiles_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.4.15">X. A. 3.4.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">Ἠιόνα τὴν ἐπὶ Στρῡμόνι</span> _Eion on the Strymon_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.98">T. 1.98</cite>.

##### <a id="s1129"></a> §1129

Words denoting persons, when they are used of a class, may omit the article. So <span lang="grc">ἄνθρωπος, στρατηγός, θεός</span> _divinity_, _god_ (<span lang="grc">ὁ θεός</span> the particular god). Thus, <span lang="grc">πάντων μέτρον ἄνθρωπός ἐστιν</span> _man is the measure of all things_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 178b">P. Th. 178b</cite>.

##### <a id="s1130"></a> §1130

Adjectives and participles used substantively have no article when the reference is general: <span lang="grc">μέσον ἡμέρᾱς</span> _midday_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.8">X. A. 1.8.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">ψῡχρόν</span> _cold_, <span lang="grc">θερμόν</span> _heat_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 186d">P. S. 186d</cite>, <span lang="grc">πέμψαι προκαταληψομένους τὰ ἄκρα</span> _to send_ men _to preoccupy the heights_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.14">X. A. 1.3.14</cite>. Rarely when an adverb is used adjectively: <span lang="grc">τῶν ἐχθρῶν ἄρδην ὄλεθρος</span> _the utter destruction of the enemy_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.141">D. 19.141</cite>.

## THE ARTICLE WITH ABSTRACT SUBSTANTIVES

##### <a id="s1131"></a> §1131

Abstract substantives generally have the article: <span lang="grc">ἡ ἀρετὴ μᾶλλον ἢ ἡ φυγὴ σῴζει τὰ̄ς ψῡχά̄ς</span> _valour rather than flight saves men's lives_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.1.5">X. C. 4.1.5</cite>.

##### <a id="s1132"></a> §1132

The names of the _virtues_, _vices_, _arts_, _sciences_, _occupations_ often omit the article: <span lang="grc">τί σωφροσύνη, τί μανίᾱ</span>; _what is temperance, what is madness?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.1.16">X. M. 1.1.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀρχὴ φιλίᾱς μὲν ἔπαινος, ἔχθρᾱς δὲ ψόγος</span> _praise is the beginning of friendship_, _blame of enmity_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.33">I. 1.33</cite>. Similarly <span lang="grc">μουσική</span> _music_, <span lang="grc">γεωργίᾱ</span> _agriculture_. So also with <span lang="grc">δόξα</span> _opinion_, <span lang="grc">νοῦς</span> _mind_, <span lang="grc">τέχνη</span> _art_, <span lang="grc">νόμος</span> _law_.

##### <a id="s1133"></a> §1133

The article must be used when reference is made to a definite person or thing or to an object well known: <span lang="grc">ἡ τῶν Ἑλλήνων εὔνοια</span> _the goodwill of the Greeks_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.70">Aes. 3.70</cite>, (<span lang="grc">ὑ̄μῖν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἡ σχολή</span> _your usual idleness_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.53">D. 8.53</cite>.

##### <a id="s1134"></a> §1134

The article may be omitted in designations of _space;_ as <span lang="grc">βάθος</span> _depth_, <span lang="grc">ὕψος</span> _height;_ also <span lang="grc">μέγεθος</span> _size_, <span lang="grc">πλῆθος</span> _size_, _amount_. <span lang="grc">γένος</span> and <span lang="grc">ὄνομα</span>, used as accusatives of respect ([1600](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1600)), may omit the article.

##### <a id="s1135"></a> §1135

The article may be omitted with some concrete words conveying a general idea, as <span lang="grc">ψῡχή</span> _soul_, <span lang="grc">σῶμα</span> _body_ (but the parts of the body regularly have the article).

## THE ARTICLE WITH PROPER NAMES

##### <a id="s1136"></a> §1136

Names of _persons_ and _places_ are individual and therefore omit the article unless previously mentioned ([1120 b](#s1120)) or specially marked as well known: <span lang="grc">Θουκῡδίδης Ἀθηναῖος</span> _Thucydides an Athenian_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.1">T. 1.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοὺς στρατιώτᾱς αὐτῶν, τοὺς παρὰ Κλέαρχον ἀπελθόντας, εἴᾱ Κῦρος τὸν Κλέαρχον ἔχειν</span> _their soldiers who seceded to Clearchus_, _Cyrus allowed Clearchus to retain_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.7">X. A. 1.4.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁ Σόλων</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 20.90">D. 20.90</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ Ἡρᾱ&lt;\*&gt;λέες</span> _the Heracleses_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 169b">P. Th. 169b</cite>. <!-- page 290 -->

##### <a id="s1137"></a> §1137

Names of **deities** omit the article, except when emphatic (<span lang="grc">νὴ τὸν Δία</span> _by Zeus_) or when definite cults are referred to: <span lang="grc">τὸ τῆς Ἀθηνᾶς ἕδος</span> _the sanctuary of Athena_ (at Athens) <cite data-ref="Isoc. 15.2">I. 15.2</cite>. Names of **festivals** vary in prose writers (no article in inscriptions): <span lang="grc">Παναθήναια</span> _the Panathenaea_ (but <span lang="grc">Παναθηναίοις τοῖς μῑκροῖς</span> _at the Lesser Panathenaea_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 21.4">L. 21.4</cite>). Names of **shrines** have the article.

##### <a id="s1138"></a> §1138

Names of **nations** may omit the article, but <span lang="grc">οἱ Ἕλληνες</span> is usual when opposed to <span lang="grc">οἱ βάρβαροι</span> _the barbarians_. When nations are opposed, the article is usually absent: <span lang="grc">ὁ πόλεμος Ἀθηναίων καὶ Πελοποννησίων</span> <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.1">T. 2.1</cite> (but <span lang="grc">ὁ πόλεμος τῶν Πελοποννησίων καὶ Ἀθηναίων</span> 1. 1). The name of a nation without the article denotes the entire people. Names of **families** may omit the article: <span lang="grc">Ἀσκληπιάδαι</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 406a">P. R. 406a</cite>.

##### <a id="s1139"></a> §1139

**Continents**: <span lang="grc">ἡ Εὐρώπη</span> _Europe_, <span lang="grc">ἡ Ἀσίᾱ</span> _Asia_. Other names of countries, except those originally adjectives (as <span lang="grc">ἡ Ἀττική</span> _Attica_), omit the article (<span lang="grc">Λιβύη</span> _Libya_). <span lang="grc">γῆ</span> and <span lang="grc">χώρᾱ</span> may be added only to such names as are treated as adjectives: <span lang="grc">ἡ Βοιωτίᾱ</span> (<span lang="grc">γῆ</span>) _Boeotia_. The names of countries standing in the genitive of the divided whole ([1311](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1311)) usually omit the article only when the genitive precedes the governing noun: <span lang="grc">Σικελίᾱς τὸ πλεῖστον</span> _the most of Sicily_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.12">T. 1.12</cite>. The article is generally used with names of **mountains** and **rivers**; but is often omitted with names of _islands_, _seas_ (but <span lang="grc">ὁ Πόντος</span> _the Pontus_), and _winds_. Names of **cities** usually omit the article. Names of cities, rivers, and mountains often add <span lang="grc">πόλις, ποταμός, ὄρος</span> ([1142 c](#s1142)). The article is omitted with proper names joined with <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> used predicatively ([1206 b](17-pronouns.md#s1206)): <span lang="grc">αὐτοὺς Ἀθηναίους</span> _the Athenians themselves_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.73">T. 4.73</cite>.

##### <a id="s1140"></a> §1140

Several appellatives, treated like proper names, may omit the article: <span lang="grc">βασιλεύς</span> the king of Persia (<span lang="grc">ὁ βασιλεύς</span> is anaphoric ([1120 b](#s1120)) or refers expressly to a definite person). **Titles of official persons**: <span lang="grc">πρυτάνεις</span> _the Prytans_, <span lang="grc">στρατηγοί</span> _the Generals_. **Names of relationship**, etc.: <span lang="grc">πατήρ</span> _father_, <span lang="grc">ἀνήρ</span> _husband_, <span lang="grc">γυνή</span> _wife_ (but the article is needed when a definite individual is spoken of). Thus: <span lang="grc">ἦκον δε` τῷ μὲν μήτηρ, τῷ δὲ γυνὴ καὶ παῖδες</span> _to one there came his mother, to another his wife and children_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.48">And. 1.48</cite>. So also <span lang="grc">πατρίς</span> _fatherland_.

##### <a id="s1141"></a> §1141

Similarly in the case of words forming a class by themselves, and some others used definitely: <span lang="grc">ἥλιος</span> _sun_, <span lang="grc">οὐρανός</span> _heaven_, <span lang="grc">ὧραι</span> _seasons_, <span lang="grc">κεραυνός</span> _thunder_, <span lang="grc">θάνατος</span> _death;_ <span lang="grc">ἄστυ, πόλις</span> _city_, <span lang="grc">ἀκρόπολις</span> _citadel_, <span lang="grc">ἀγορά̄</span> _market-place_, <span lang="grc">τεῖχος</span> _city-wall_, <span lang="grc">πρυτανεῖον</span> _prytaneum_, <span lang="grc">νῆσος</span> _island_ (all used of definite places), <span lang="grc">θάλαττα</span> _sea_ as opposed to the mainland, but <span lang="grc">ἡ θάλαττα</span> of a definite sea; similarly <span lang="grc">γῆ</span> _earth_, _land_.

##### <a id="s1142"></a> §1142

When the name of a person or place is defined by an appositive ([916](02-syntax-of-the-simple-sentence.md#s916)) or attributive, the following distinctions are to be noted:

**a. Persons**: <span lang="grc">Περδίκκᾱς Ἀλεξάνδρου</span> _Perdiccas_, _son of Alexander_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.99">T. 2.99</cite>: the _official_ designation merely stating the parentage. <span lang="grc">Δημοσθένης ὁ Ἀλκισθένους</span> (the _popular_ designation) distinguishes _Demosthenes_, _the son of Alcisthenes_ (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.91">T. 3.91</cite>) from other persons named Demosthenes. (Similarly with names of _nations_.)

**b. Deities**: the article is used with the name _and_ with the epithet or (less often) with neither: <span lang="grc">τῷ Διὶ τῷ Ὀλυμπίῳ</span> _to Olympian Zeus_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.31">T. 5.31</cite>, <span lang="grc">Διὶ ἐλευθερίῳ</span> _to Zeus guardian of freedom_ 2. 71. <!-- page 291 -->

**c. Geographical Names** are usually treated as attributives, as <span lang="grc">ὁ Εὐφρά̄της ποταμός</span> _the river Euphrates_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.11">X. A. 1.4.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ Βόλβη λίμνη</span> _lake Bolbe_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.103">T. 4.103</cite>. In a very few cases (six times in Thuc.) <span lang="grc">ὁ</span> is omitted with the name of a river when <span lang="grc">ποταμός</span> is inserted; but Hdt. often omits <span lang="grc">ὁ</span>. With the names of **mountains** the order is <span lang="grc">τὸ Πήλιον ὄρος</span> _Mt. Pelion_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 7.129">Hdt. 7.129</cite> when the gender agrees, but otherwise <span lang="grc">ἐς τὸ ὄρος τὴν Ἰστώνην</span> _to Mt. Istone_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.85">T. 3.85</cite> (rarely as <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ τῇ Αἴτνῃ τῷ ὄρει</span> _at the foot of Mt. Aetna_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.116">T. 3.116</cite>). With names of **islands**, **towns**, etc., the order varies: <span lang="grc">τὸ Παρθένιον πόλισμα</span> _the town of Parthenium_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.8.21">X. A. 7.8.21</cite>; <span lang="grc">ἡ Ψυττάλεια νῆσος</span> _the island of Psyttalea_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 8.95">Hdt. 8.95</cite>; <span lang="grc">Τραγίᾱ ἡ νῆσος</span> _the island of Tragia_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.116">T. 1.116</cite>; <span lang="grc">τοῦ Πειραιῶς τοῦ λιμένος</span> _of the harbour of Peiraeus_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.93">T. 2.93</cite>; <span lang="grc">τὸ φρούριον τὸ Λάβδαλον</span> _fort Labdalon_ 7. 3. _The city of Mende_ would be <span lang="grc">Μένδη πόλις, ἡ Μένδη ἡ πόλις, Μένδη ἡ πόλις</span>.

## OTHER USES OF THE ARTICLE

##### <a id="s1143"></a> §1143

A single article, used with the first of two or more nouns connected by _and_, produces the effect of a single notion: <span lang="grc">οἱ στρατηγοὶ καὶ λοχᾱγοί</span> _the generals and captains_ (the commanding officers) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.2.8">X. A. 2.2.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ̄ς μεγίστᾱς καὶ ἐλαχίστᾱς ναῦς</span> _the largest and the smallest ships_ (the whole fleet) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.10">T. 1.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ τῶν πολλῶν διαβολή τε καὶ φθόνος</span> _the calumniation and envy of the multitude_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 28a">P. A. 28a</cite>. Rarely when the substantives are of different genders: <span lang="grc">περὶ τὰ̄ς ἑαυτ ῶν ψῡχὰ̄ς καὶ σώματα</span> _concerning their own lives and persons_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.20">X. A. 3.2.20</cite>.

##### <a id="s1144"></a> §1144

A repeated article lays stress on each word: <span lang="grc">ὁ Θρᾷξ καὶ ὁ βάρβαρος</span> _the Thracian and the barbarian_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.132">D. 23.132</cite> (here the subject remains the same), <span lang="grc">οἱ στρατηγοὶ καὶ οἱ λοχᾱγοί</span> _the generals and the captains_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.1.13">X. A. 7.1.13</cite>.

##### <a id="s1145"></a> §1145

Instead of repeating a noun with the article it may suffice to repeat the article: <span lang="grc">ὁ βίος ὁ τῶν ἰδιωτευόντων η·̀ ὁ τῶν τυραννευόντων</span> _the life of persons in a private station or that of princes_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 2.4">I. 2.4</cite>.

##### <a id="s1146"></a> §1146

A substantive followed by an attributive genitive and forming with it a compound idea, usually omits the article: <span lang="grc">τελευτὴ τοῦ βίου</span> (the) _end of his life_ (‘life-end’ as _life-time_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.1">X. A. 1.1.1</cite>. (Less commonly <span lang="grc">ἡ τελευτὴ τοῦ βίου</span> <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.9.30">X. A. 1.9.30</cite>.) Cp. [1295 a](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1295).

##### <a id="s1147"></a> §1147

When the genitive dependent on a substantive is a proper name: <span lang="grc">μετὰ Εὐβοίᾱς ἅλωσιν</span> _after the capture of Euboea_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.2">T. 2.2</cite>, and <span lang="grc">μετὰ τὴν Λέσβου ἅλωσιν</span> _after the capture of Lesbos_ 3. 51. A preceding genitive thus often takes the place of the article: <span lang="grc">διὰ χρόνου πλῆθος</span> _by reason of the extent of time_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.1">T. 1.1</cite>.

##### <a id="s1148"></a> §1148

Concrete coördinated words forming a copulative expression may omit the article: <span lang="grc">πρὸς οὖν παίδων καὶ γυναικῶν ἱκετεύω ὑ̄μᾶς</span> _by your children and wives I beseech you_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 4.20">L. 4.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">πόλιν καὶ οἰκίᾱς ἡμῖν παράδοτε</span> _surrender to us your city and houses_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.72">T. 2.72</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἱέρειαι καὶ ἱερεῖς</span> _priestesses and priests_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 461a">P. R. 461a</cite>. Cp. _man and wife, horse and rider_.

##### <a id="s1149"></a> §1149

An appositive to the personal pronouns of the first and second persons has the article when the appositive would have it (as third person) with the pronoun omitted: <span lang="grc">ὑ̄μεῖς οἱ ἡγεμόνες πρὸς ἐμὲ πάντες συμβάλλετε</span> _do you, captains_, _all confer with me_ (<span lang="grc">οἱ ἡγεμόνες συμβάλλοι&lt;\*&gt;</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.2.41">X. C. 6.2.41</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ σφόδρα χρώμεθα οἱ Κρῆτες τοῖς ξενικοῖς ποιήμασιν</span> _we Cretans do not make very much use of foreign_ <!-- page 292 --> _poems_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 680c">P. L. 680c</cite>, <span lang="grc">χαίρω ἀκούων ὑ̄μῶν τῶν σοφῶν</span> _I delight in listening to you &lt;\*&gt;_ P. Ion 532 d.

## THE ARTICLE AND A PREDICATE NOUN

##### <a id="s1150"></a> §1150

A predicate noun has no article, and is thus distinguished from the subject: <span lang="grc">καλεῖται ἡ ἀκρόπολις ἔτι ὑπ᾽ Ἀθηναίων πόλις</span> _the acropolis is still called_ ‘_city_’ _by the Athenians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.15">T. 2.15</cite>.

##### <a id="s1151"></a> §1151

Predicate comparatives and superlatives, possessive pronouns, and ordinals have no article: <span lang="grc">ᾤμην τὴν ἐμαυτοῦ γυναῖκα πᾱσῶν σωφρονεστάτην &lt;\*&gt;</span> _I thought that my wife was_ (the) _most virtuous of all_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 1.10">L. 1.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">Χαιρεφῶν &lt;\*&gt; ἑταῖρος ἦν</span> _Chaerephon was a friend of mine_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 21a">P. A. 21a</cite>. Cp. [1125 d](#s1125).

##### <a id="s1152"></a> §1152

Even in the predicate the article is used with a noun referring to a definite object (an _individual_ or a _class_) that is well known, previously mentioned or hinted at, or identical with the subject: <span lang="grc">οἱ δ᾽ ἄλλοι ἐπιχειροῦσι βάλλειν &lt;\*&gt; Δέξιππον ἀνακαλοῦντες τὸν προδότην</span> _the rest try to strike Dexippus calling him_ ‘_the traitor_’ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.6.7">X. A. 6.6.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὗτοι ἦσαν οἱ φεύγοντες τὸν ἔλεγχον</span> _these men were those who_ (_as I have said_) _avoided the inquiry_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 6.27">Ant. 6.27</cite>. <span lang="grc">οἱ τιθέμενοι τοὺς νόμονς &lt;\*&gt; ἀσθενεῖς ἄνθρωποί εἰσι καὶ οἱ πολλοί</span> _the enactors of the laws are the weak men and the multitude_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 483b">P. G. 483b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπώπτευε δὲ εἶναι τὸν διαβάλλοντα Μένωνα</span> _he suspected that it was Menon who traduced him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.28">X. A. 2.5.28</cite> (here subject and predicate could change places). So also with <span lang="grc">ὁ αὐτός</span> _the same_ ([1209 a](17-pronouns.md#s1209)), <span lang="grc">θἄ̄τερον</span> _one of two_ ([69](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/02-euphony-of-vowels.md#s69)), <span lang="grc">τοὐναντίον</span> _the opposite_.

## SUBSTANTIVE-MAKING POWER OF THE ARTICLE

##### <a id="s1153"></a> §1153

The article has the power to make substantival any word or words to which it is prefixed.

**a.** Adjectives: <span lang="grc">ὁ σοφός</span> _the wise man_, <span lang="grc">τὸ δίκαιον</span> _justice_.

**b.** Participles (with indefinite force): <span lang="grc">ὁ βουλόμενος</span> _whoever wills, the first that offers_. Cp. [1124](#s1124).

N. 1.—Such participial nouns appear in active, middle, and passive forms, and admit the distinctions of tense: <span lang="grc">οἱ ἐθελήσοντες μένειν</span> _those who shall be willing to remain_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 7.5.24">X. H. 7.5.24</cite>.

N. 2.—Thucydides often substantivizes the neuter participle to form abstract expressions: <span lang="grc">τῆς πόλεως τὸ τῑμώμενον</span> _the dignity of the State_ 2. 63. Such participial nouns denote an action regulated by time and circumstance. Contrast <span lang="grc">τὸ δεδιός</span> _fear_ (in actual operation) 1. 36 with <span lang="grc">τὸ δέος</span> (simply _fear_ in the abstract).

**c.** Preposition and case: <span lang="grc">οἱ ἐπὶ τῶν πρᾱγμάτων</span> _those in power, the government_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.247">D. 18.247</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ ἐν τῇ ἡλικίᾳ</span> _those in the prime of life_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.24">T. 6.24</cite>.

**d.** With the genitive, forming a noun-phrase ([1299](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1299)): <span lang="grc">τὰ τῶν στρατιωτῶν</span> _the condition of the soldiers_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.20">X. A. 3.1.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ τῆς ὀργῆς</span> _the outbursts of wrath_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.60">T. 2.60</cite>.

**e.** Adverbs: <span lang="grc">οἵ τ᾽ ἔνδον συνελαμβάνοντο καὶ οἱ ἐκτὸς κατεκόπησαν</span> _those who were inside were arrested and those outside were cut down_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.32">X. A. 2.5.32</cite>. Similarly <span lang="grc">οἱ τότε</span> _the men of that time_, <span lang="grc">οἱ ἐκεῖ</span> _the dead_, <span lang="grc">οἱ πάλαι</span> _the ancients_.

N.—An adverb preceded by the article may be used like an adjective: <span lang="grc">ὁ ὀρθῶς κυβερνήτης</span> _the good pilot_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 341c">P. R. 341c</cite>. The article is rarely omitted. <!-- page 293 -->

**f.** Infinitives: <span lang="grc">καλοῦσί γε ἀκολασίᾱν τὸ ὑπὸ τῶν ἡδονῶν ἄρχεσθαι</span> _they call intemperance being ruled by one's pleasures_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 68e">P. Ph. 68e</cite>.

**g.** Any single word or clause: <span lang="grc">τὸ ὑ̄μεῖς ὅταν λέγω, τὴν πόλιν λέγω</span> _when I say You_, _I mean the State_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.88">D. 18.88</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπερβὰ̄ς τὸ δίκᾱς ὑπεχέτω τοῦ φόνου</span> _omitting_ (the words) ‘_let him submit to judgment for the murder_’ <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.220">D. 23.220</cite>.

## POSITION OF THE ARTICLE

### Attributive Position of the Article

##### <a id="s1154"></a> §1154

A word or group of words standing between the article and its noun, or immediately after the article if the noun, with or without the article, precedes, is an _attributive_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁ σοφὸς ἀνήρ, ὁ ἀνὴρ ὁ σοφός</span>, or <span lang="grc">ἀνὴρ ὁ σοφός</span> (cp. [1168](#s1168)).

##### <a id="s1155"></a> §1155

This holds true except in the case of such post-positive words as <span lang="grc">μέν, δέ, γέ, τέ, γάρ, δή, οἶμαι, οὖν, τοίνυν</span>; and <span lang="grc">τὶς</span> in Hdt.: <span lang="grc">τῶν τις Περσέων</span> _one of the Persians_ 1. 85. In Attic, <span lang="grc">τὶς</span> intervenes only when an attributive follows the article: <span lang="grc">τῶν βαρβάρων τινὲς ἱππέων</span> _some of the barbarian cavalry_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.32">X. A. 2.5.32</cite>.

##### <a id="s1156"></a> §1156

Adjectives, participles, adverbs, and (generally) prepositions with their cases, if preceded by the article, have _attributive position_.

##### <a id="s1157"></a> §1157

(1) Commonly, as in English, the article and the attributive precede the noun: <span lang="grc">ὁ σοφὸς ἀνήρ</span> _the wise man_. In this arrangement the emphasis is on the attributive. Thus, <span lang="grc">τῇ πρώτῃ ἡμέρᾳ</span> _on the first day_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.96">T. 3.96</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν τῷ πρὸ τοῦ χρόνῳ</span> _in former times_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 53.12">D. 53.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸν ἐκ τῶν Ἑλλήνων εἰς τοὺς βαρβάρους φόβον ἰδών</span> _seeing the terror inspired by the Greeks in the barbarians_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.18">X. A. 1.2.18</cite>.

##### <a id="s1158"></a> §1158

(2) Less often, the article and the attributive follow the noun preceded by the article: <span lang="grc">ὁ ἀνὴρ ὁ σοφός</span> _the wise man_. Thus, <span lang="grc">τὸ στράτευμα τὸ τῶν Ἀθηναίων</span> _the army of the Athenians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.50">T. 8.50</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν τῇ πορείᾳ τῇ μέχρι ἐπὶ θάλατταν</span> _on the journey as far as the sea_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.1.1">X. A. 5.1.1</cite>. In this arrangement the emphasis is on the noun, as something definite or previously mentioned, and the attributive is added by way of explanation. So <span lang="grc">τοὺς κύνας τοὺς χαλεποὺς διδέᾱσι</span> _they tie up the dogs_, _the savage ones_ (I mean) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.8.24">X. A. 5.8.24</cite>.

##### <a id="s1159"></a> §1159

(3) Least often, the noun takes no article before it, when it would have none if the attributive were dropped: <span lang="grc">ἀνὴρ ὁ σοφός</span> _the wise man_ (lit. _a man_, I mean _the wise_ one). Thus, <span lang="grc">μάχαις ταῖς πλείοσι</span> _in the greater number of battles_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.11">T. 7.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">σύνειμι μὲν θεοῖς, σύνειμι δὲ ἀνθρώποις τοῖς ἀγαθοῖς</span> _I associate with gods_, _I associate with good men_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.1.32">X. M. 2.1.32</cite>. In this arrangement the attributive is added by way of explanation; as in the last example: _with men_, _the good_ (I mean).

##### <a id="s1160"></a> §1160

A proper name, defining a preceding noun with the article, may itself have the article: <span lang="grc">ὁ ἀδελφὸς ὁ Ἀρεθούσιος</span> (his) _brother Arethusius_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 53.10">D. 53.10</cite>. Cp. [1142 c](#s1142). An appositive to a proper name has the article when it designates a characteristic or something well known: <span lang="grc">ὁ Σόλων ὁ παλαιὸς ἦν φιλόδημος</span> _Solon of ancient times was a lover of the people_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 1187">Ar. Nub. 1187</cite>, <span lang="grc">Πᾱσίων ὁ Μεγαρεύς</span> _Pasion_, _the Megarian_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.7">X. A. 1.4.7</cite>. <!-- page 294 -->

##### <a id="s1161"></a> §1161

The genitive of a substantive limiting the meaning of another substantive may take any one of four positions:—

**a.** <span lang="grc">τὸ τοῦ πατρὸς βιβλίον</span> _the father's book_ (very common). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἡ &lt;\*&gt; τεθνεώτων ἀρετή</span> _the valour of the dead_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.36">L. 12.36</cite>.

**b.** <span lang="grc">τὸ βιβλίον τὸ τοῦ πατρός</span> (less common). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἡ οἰκίᾱ ἡ Σίμωνος</span> _the house of Simon_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 3.32">L. 3.32</cite>.

**c.** <span lang="grc">τοῦ πατρὸς τὸ βιβλίον</span> (to emphasize the genitive or when a genitive has just preceded). Thus, <span lang="grc">τῆς ϝί̄κης τὸ μέγεθος</span> _the greatness of the victory_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.4.19">X. H. 6.4.19</cite>.

**d.** <span lang="grc">τὸ βιβλίον τοῦ πατρός</span> (very common). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἡ τόλμα τῶν λεγόντων</span> _the effrontery of the speakers_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.41">L. 12.41</cite>. The genitive of the divided whole ([1306](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1306)) is so placed or as in **c**.

N. 1.—A substantive with no article is sometimes followed by the article and the attributive genitive: <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ σκηνὴν ἰόντες τὴν Ξενοφῶντος</span> _going to the ten&lt;\*&gt;_ (namely, that) _of Xenophon_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.4.19">X. A. 6.4.19</cite>. Cp. [1159](#s1159).

##### <a id="s1162"></a> §1162

The order bringing together the _same_ forms of the article (<span lang="grc">περὶ τοῦ τ&lt;\*&gt; πατρὸς βιβλίου</span>) is avoided, but two or three articles of _different_ form may stand together: <span lang="grc">τὸ τῆς τοῦ ξαίνοντος τέχνης ἔργον</span> _the work of the art of the wool-carder_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Stat. 281a">P. Pol. 281a</cite>.

##### <a id="s1163"></a> §1163

The attributive position is employed with the possessive pronouns and the possessive genitives of the reflexive and demonstrative pronouns ([1184](#s1184)), <span lang="grc">αὐτο&lt;\*&gt;</span> meaning _same_ ([1173](#s1173)), and <span lang="grc">πᾶς</span> expressing the _sum total_ ([1174](#s1174)).

##### <a id="s1164"></a> §1164

Two or more attributives of a substantive are variously placed: (1) <span lang="grc">εἰς τὰ̄ς ἄλλᾱς Ἀρκαδικὰ̄ς πόλεις</span> _to the other Arcadian cities_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 7.4.38">X. H. 7.4.38</cite>. (2) <span lang="grc">τὸ ἐν Ἀρκαδίᾳ τὸ τοῦ Διὸς τοῦ Λυκαίου ἱερόν</span> _the sanctuary of Lycean Zeus in Arcadia_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 565d">P. R. 565d</cite>. (3) <span lang="grc">ἐς τὸν ἐπὶ τῷ στόματι τοῦ λιμένος στενοῦ ὄντος τὸν ἕτερον πύργον</span> _to the other tower at the mouth of the harbour which was narrow_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.90">T. 8.90</cite>. (4) <span lang="grc">ἐν τῇ οἰκίᾳ τῇ Χαρμίδου τῇ παρὰ τὸ Ὀλυμπιεῖον</span> _in the house of Charmides by the Olympieum_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.16">And. 1.16</cite>. (5) <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ τῶν ἐν τῇ Ἀσίᾳ πόλεων Ἑλληνίδων</span> _from the Greek cities in Asia_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.3.15">X. H. 4.3.15</cite>. (6) <span lang="grc">πρὸς τὴν ἐκ τῆς Σικελίᾱς τῶν Ἀθηναίων μεγάλην κακοπρᾱγίᾱν</span> _with regard to the great failure of the Athenians in Sicily_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.2">T. 8.2</cite>. (7) <span lang="grc">τὸ τεῖχος τὸ μακρὸν τὸ νότιον</span> _the long southern wall_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 3.7">And. 3.7</cite>.

##### <a id="s1165"></a> §1165

A relative or temporal clause may be treated as an attributive: <span lang="grc">Σόλων ἐμί̄σει τοὺς οἷος οὗτος ἀνθρώπους</span> _Solon detested men like this man here_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.254">D. 19.254</cite>.

##### <a id="s1166"></a> §1166

Position of an attributive participle with its modifiers (A = article, N = noun, P = participle, D = word or words dependent on P): (1) APND: <span lang="grc">τὸν ἐφεστηκότα κίνδῡνον τῇ πόλει</span> _the danger impending over the State_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.176">D. 18.176</cite>. (2) APDN: <span lang="grc">τοὺς περιεστηκότας τῇ πόλει κινδύ̄νους</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.179">D. 18.179</cite>. (3) ADPN: <span lang="grc">τὸν τότε τῇ πόλει περιστάντα κίνδῡνον</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.188">D. 18.188</cite>. (4) NADP: <span lang="grc">ἕτοιμον ἔχει δύναμιν τὴν . . . καταδουλωσουένην ἄπαντας</span> _he has in readiness a force to enslave all_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.46">D. 8.46</cite>.

##### <a id="s1167"></a> §1167

**a.** Especially after verbal substantives denoting an _action_ or a _state_ an attributive prepositional phrase is added without the article being repeated: <span lang="grc">τὴν μεγάλην στρατείᾱν Ἀθηναίων καὶ τῶν ξυμμάχων ἐς Αἴγυπτον</span> _the great expedition of the Athenians and their allies to Egypt_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.110">T. 1.110</cite>.

**b.** A word defining a substantivized participle, adjective, or infinitive may <!-- page 295 --> be placed before the article for emphasis: <span lang="grc">καὶ ταῦτα τοὺς εἰδότας καλοῦμεν</span> _and we will summon those who have knowledge of this_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 57.65">D. 57.65</cite>, <span lang="grc">τούτων τοῖς ἐναντίοις</span> _with the opposite of these_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.75">T. 7.75</cite>.

### Predicate Position of Adjectives

##### <a id="s1168"></a> §1168

A predicate adjective either precedes or follows the article and its noun: <span lang="grc">σοφὸς ὁ ἀνήρ</span> or <span lang="grc">ὁ ἀνὴρ σοφός</span> _the man is wise_.

Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀτελεῖ τῇ νί̄κῃ ἀνέστησαν</span> _they retired with their victory incomplete_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.27">T. 8.27</cite>, <span lang="grc">ψῑλὴν ἔχων τὴν κεφαλήν</span> _with his head bare_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.6">X. A. 1.8.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ̄ς τριήρεις ἀφείλκυσαν κενά̄ς</span> _they towed off the ships without their crews_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.93">T. 2.93</cite>.

**a.** This is called the predicate position, which often lends emphasis.

##### <a id="s1169"></a> §1169

A predicate adjective or substantive may thus be the equivalent of a clause of a complex sentence: <span lang="grc">ἀθάνατον τὴν περὶ αὑτῶν μνήμην καταλείψουσιν</span> _they will leave behind a remembrance of themselves that will never die_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 9.3">I. 9.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπήρετο πόσον τι ἄγοι τὸ στράτευμα</span> _he asked about how large the force was that he was leading_ ( = <span lang="grc">πόσον τι εἴη τὸ στράτευμα ὃ ἄγοι</span> [2647](32-interrogative-sentences-questions.md#s2647)) <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.1.2">X. C. 2.1.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">παρ᾽ ἑκόντων τῶν ξυμμάχων τὴν ἡγεμονίᾱν ἔλαβον</span> _they received the leadership from their allies_ (being willing) _who were willing to confer it_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.17">I. 1.17</cite>.

##### <a id="s1170"></a> §1170

A predicate expression may stand inside an attributive phrase: <span lang="grc">ὁ δεινὸς</span> (pred.) <span lang="grc">λεγόμενος γεωργός</span> _he who is called a skilful agriculturist_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 19.14">X. O. 19.14</cite>. This is common with participles of _naming_ with the article.

##### <a id="s1171"></a> §1171

The predicate position is employed with the demonstratives <span lang="grc">οὗτος, ὅδε, ἐκεῖνος</span>, and <span lang="grc">ἄμφω, ἀμφότερος, ἑκάτερος</span>, and <span lang="grc">ἕκαστος</span>; with the possessive genitives of personal and relative pronouns ([1185](#s1185), [1196](17-pronouns.md#s1196)) and of <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> ([1201](17-pronouns.md#s1201)); with <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> meaning _self_ ([1206 b](17-pronouns.md#s1206)); with the genitive of the divided whole ([1306](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1306)), as <span lang="grc">τούτων οἱ πλεῖστοι</span> _the most of these_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.13">X. A. 1.5.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ ἄριστοι τῶν περὶ αὐτόν</span> _the bravest of his companions_ 1. 8. 27; and with <span lang="grc">πᾶς</span> meaning _all_ ([1174 b](#s1174)).

**a.** _This wise man_ is <span lang="grc">οὗτος ὁ σοφὸς ἀνήρ, ὁ σοφὸς ἀνὴρ οὗτος</span> (and also <span lang="grc">ὁ σοφὸς οὗτος ἀνήρ</span>).

### PECULIARITIES OF POSITION WITH THE ARTICLE

##### <a id="s1172"></a> §1172

**Adjectives of Place**.—When used in the predicate position ([1168](#s1168)) <span lang="grc"> **ἄκρος** </span> (_high_) means _the top of_, <span lang="grc"> **μέσος** </span> (_middle_) means _the middle of_, <span lang="grc"> **ἔσχατος** </span> (_extreme_) means _the end of_. Cp. _summus_, _medius_, _extremus_.

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| _Attributive Position_ | _Predicate Position_ |  |
| <span lang="grc">τὸ ἄκρον ὅρος</span> _the lofty mountain_ | <span lang="grc">ἄκρον τὸ ὄρος</span> ) | _the top of_ |
|  | <span lang="grc">τὸ ὄρος ἄκρον</span> ) | _the mountain_ |
| <span lang="grc">ἡ μέση ἀγορά̄</span> _the central market_ | <span lang="grc">μέση ἡ ἀγορά̄</span> ) | _the centre of_ |
|  | <span lang="grc">ἡ ἀγορὰ̄ μέση</span> ) | _the market_ |
| <span lang="grc">ἡ ἐσχάτη νῆσος</span> _the farthest island_ | <span lang="grc">ἐσχάτη ἡ νῆσος</span> ) | _the verge of_ |
|  | <span lang="grc">ἡ νῆσος ἐσχάτη</span> ) | _the island_ |

Thus, <span lang="grc">περὶ ἄκραις ταῖς χερσὶ χειρῖδες</span> _gloves on the fingers_ (_points of the hands_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.8.17">X. C. 8.8.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">διὰ μέσου τοῦ παραδείσου ῥεῖ</span> _flows through the middle of the park_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.7">X. A. 1.2.7</cite>. The meaning of the predicate position is also expressed by (<span lang="grc">τὸ</span>) <span lang="grc">ἄκρον τοῦ ὄρους</span>, (<span lang="grc">τὸ</span>) <span lang="grc">μέσον τῆς ἀγορᾶς</span>, etc. <!-- page 296 -->

##### <a id="s1173"></a> §1173

<span lang="grc">**μόνος**, **ἥμισυς**</span>.—(1) Attributive: <span lang="grc">ὁ μόνος παῖς</span> _the only son_, <span lang="grc">αἱ ἡμίσειαι χάριτες</span> _half-favours_. (2) Predicate: <span lang="grc">μόνος ὁ παῖς</span> (or <span lang="grc">ὁ παῖς μόνος</span>) <span lang="grc">παίζει</span> _the boy plays alone_, <span lang="grc">ἥμισυς ὁ βίος</span> (or <span lang="grc">ὁ βίος ἥμισυς</span>) _half of life_, <span lang="grc">τὰ ἅρματα τὰ ἡμίσεα</span> _half of the chariots_.

<span lang="grc"> **αὐτός** </span>: (1) Attributive: <span lang="grc">ὁ αὐτὸς ἀνήρ</span> _the same man_. (2) Predicate: <span lang="grc">αὐτὸς ὁ ἀνήρ</span> or <span lang="grc">ὁ ἀνὴρ αὐτός</span> _the man himself_.

##### <a id="s1174"></a> §1174

<span lang="grc"> **πᾶς** </span> (and in the strengthened forms <span lang="grc">ἅπᾱς, σύμπᾱς</span> _all together_). **a.** In the attributive position <span lang="grc">πᾶς</span> denotes the whole regarded as the sum of all its parts (the _sum total_, the _collective body_): <span lang="grc">οἱ πάντες πολῖται</span> _the whole body of citizens_, <span lang="grc">ἡ πᾶσα Σικελίᾱ</span> _the whole of Sicily_, <span lang="grc">ἀποκτεῖναι τοὺς ἅπαντας Μυτιληναίους</span> _to put to death the entire Mitylenean population_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.36">T. 3.36</cite>.

N.—Hence, with numbers, <span lang="grc">οἱ πάντες, τὰ σύμπαντα</span> _in all:_ <span lang="grc">ἑξακόσιοι καὶ χί̄λιοι οἱ πάντες</span> _1600 in all_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.60">T. 1.60</cite>.

**b.** In the predicate (and usual) position <span lang="grc">πᾶς</span> means _all:_ <span lang="grc">πάντες οἱ πολῖται</span> or (often emphatic) <span lang="grc">οἱ πολῖται πάντες</span> _all the citizens_ (individually), <span lang="grc">περὶ πάντας τοὺς θεοὺς ἠσεβήκᾱσι καὶ εἰς ἅπᾱσαν τὴν πόλιν ἡμαρτήκᾱσιν</span> _they have committed impiety towards all the gods and have sinned against the whole State_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 14.42">L. 14.42</cite>.

**c.** Without the article: <span lang="grc">πάντες πολῖται</span> _all_ (conceivable) _citizens_, <span lang="grc">μισθωσάμενοι πάντας ἀνθρώπους</span> _hiring every conceivable person_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.60">L. 12.60</cite>.

N. 1.—In the meaning _pure_, _nothing but_, <span lang="grc">πᾶς</span> is strictly a predicate and has no article: <span lang="grc">κύκλῳ φρουρούμενος ὑπὸ πάντων πολεμίων</span> _hemmed in by a ring of guards all of whom are his enemies_ ( = <span lang="grc">πάντες ὑφ᾽ ὧν φρουρεῖται πολέμιοί εἰσι</span>) <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 579b">P. R. 579b</cite>. So <span lang="grc">πᾶσα κακίᾱ</span> _utter baseness_.

N. 2.—The article is not used with <span lang="grc">πᾶς</span> if the noun, standing alone, would have no article.

N. 3.—In the singular, <span lang="grc">πᾶς</span> often means _every:_ <span lang="grc">σὺν σοὶ πᾶσα ὁδὸς εὔπορος</span> _with you every road is easy to travel_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.9">X. A. 2.5.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">πᾶσα θάλασσα</span> _every sea_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.41">T. 2.41</cite>.

##### <a id="s1175"></a> §1175

<span lang="grc"> **ὅλος** </span>: (1) Attributive: <span lang="grc">τὸ ὅλον στράτευμα</span> _the whole army;_ (2) Predicate: <span lang="grc">ὅλον τὸ στράτευμα</span> (or <span lang="grc">τὸ στράτευμα ὅλον</span>) _the army as a whole_, <span lang="grc">τὴν νύκτα ὅλην</span> _the entire night_. With no article: <span lang="grc">ὅλον στράτευμα</span> _a whole army_, <span lang="grc">ὅλα στρατεύματα</span> _whole armies_.

##### <a id="s1176"></a> §1176

The demonstrative pronouns <span lang="grc">οὗτος, ὅδε, ἐκεῖνος</span>, and <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> _self_, in agreement with a noun, usually take the article, and stand in the predicate position ([1168](#s1168)): <span lang="grc">οὗτος ὁ ἀνήρ</span> or <span lang="grc">ὁ ἀνὴρ οὗτος</span> (never <span lang="grc">ὁ οὗτος ἀνήρ</span>) _this man_, <span lang="grc">αὐτὸς ὁ ἀνήρ</span> or <span lang="grc">ὁ ἀνὴρ αὐτός</span> _the man himself_ (<span lang="grc">ὁ αὐτὸς ἀνήρ</span> _the same man_ [1173](#s1173)).

##### <a id="s1177"></a> §1177

One or more words may separate the demonstrative from its noun: <span lang="grc">ὁ τούτου ἔρως τοῦ ἀνθρώπου</span> _the love of this man_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 213c">P. S. 213c</cite>. Note also <span lang="grc">τῶν οἰκείων τινὲς τῶν ἐκείνων</span> _some of their slaves_ (_some of the slaves of those men_) <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 33d">P. A. 33d</cite>.

##### <a id="s1178"></a> §1178

<span lang="grc">οὗτος, ὅδε, ἐκεῖνος</span> sometimes omit the article.

**a.** Regularly, when the noun is in the predicate: <span lang="grc">αὕτη ἔστω ἱκανὴ ἀπολογίᾱ</span> _let this be a sufficient defence_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 24b">P. A. 24b</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἶμαι ἐμὴν ταύτην πατρίδα εἶναι</span> _I think this is my native country_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.8.4">X. A. 4.8.4</cite>.

**b.** Usually, with proper names, except when anaphoric ([1120 b](#s1120)): <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος Θουκῡδίδης</span> _that_ (well-known) _Thucydides_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Ach. 708">Ar. Ach. 708</cite>. <!-- page 297 -->

**c.** Usually, with definite numbers: <span lang="grc">ταύτᾱς τριά̄κοντα μνᾶς</span> _these thirty minae_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 27.23">D. 27.23</cite>.

**d.** Optionally, when a relative clause follows: <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ γῆν τήνδε ἤλθομεν, ἐν ᾗ οἱ πατέρες ἡμῶν Μήδων ἐκράτησαν</span> _we have come against this land, in which our fathers conquered the Medes_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.74">T. 2.74</cite>.

**e.** In the phrase (often contemptuous) <span lang="grc">οὗτος ἀνήρ</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 505c">P. G. 505c</cite>; and in other expressions denoting some emotion: <span lang="grc">ἄνθρωπος ου῾τοσί̄</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.243">D. 18.243</cite>.

**f.** Sometimes, when the demonstrative follows its noun: <span lang="grc">ἐπίγραμμα τόδε</span> <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.59">T. 6.59</cite>. So often in Hdt.

**g.** Frequently, in poetry.

##### <a id="s1179"></a> §1179

<span lang="grc">ἄμφω, ἀμφότερος</span> _both_, <span lang="grc">ἑκάτερος</span> _each_ (of two), <span lang="grc">ἕκαστος</span> _each_ (of several) have the predicate position. But with <span lang="grc">ἕκαστος</span> the article is often omitted: <span lang="grc">κατὰ τὴν ἡμέρᾱν ἑκάστην</span> (_day by day and_) _every day_, <span lang="grc">καθ᾽ ἑκάστην ἡμέρᾱν</span> _every day_.

##### <a id="s1180"></a> §1180

The demonstratives of _quality_ and _quantity_, <span lang="grc">τοιοῦτος, τοιόσδε, τοσοῦτος, τοσόσδε, τηλικοῦτος</span>, when they take the article, usually follow it: <span lang="grc">τῶν τοσούτων καὶ τοιούτων ἀγαθῶν</span> _of so many and such blessings_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.305">D. 18.305</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦτο τὸ τοιοῦτον ἔθος</span> _such a practice as this_ 21. 123. <span lang="grc">ὁ δεῖνα</span> _such a one_ ([336](../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s336)) regularly takes the article.

**a.** But the predicate position occurs: <span lang="grc">τοσαύτη ἡ πρώτη παρασκευὴ πρὸς τὸν πόλεμον διέπλει</span> _so great was the first armament which crossed over for the war_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.44">T. 6.44</cite>.

##### <a id="s1181"></a> §1181

An attributive, following the article, may be separated from its noun by a pronoun: <span lang="grc">ἡ πάλαι ἡμῶν φύσις</span> _our old nature_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 189d">P. S. 189d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ στενὴ αὕτη ὁδός</span> (for <span lang="grc">αὕτη ἡ στενὴ ὁδός</span>) _this narrow road_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.2.6">X. A. 4.2.6</cite>.

##### <a id="s1182"></a> §1182

Possessive pronouns take the article only when a definite person or thing is meant, and stand between article and noun: <span lang="grc">τὸ ἐμὸν βιβλίον</span> _my book_, <span lang="grc">τὰ ἡμέτερα βιβλία</span> _our books_.

**a.** But names of relationship, <span lang="grc">πόλις, πατρίς</span>, etc., do not require the article ([1140](#s1140)).

##### <a id="s1183"></a> §1183

The article is not used with possessive pronouns or the genitive of personal and reflexive pronouns (cp. [1184](#s1184), [1185](#s1185)):

**a.** When no particular object is meant: <span lang="grc">ἐμὸν βιβλίον</span> or <span lang="grc">βιβλίον μου</span> _a book of mine_.

**b.** When these pronouns belong to the predicate: <span lang="grc">μαθητὴς γέγονα σός</span> _I have become a pupil of yours_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyph. 5a">P. Euth. 5a</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ λόγους ἐμαυτοῦ λέγων</span> _not speaking words of my own_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.41">D. 9.41</cite>.

### POSITION OF THE GENITIVE OF PRONOUNS AND THE ARTICLE

##### <a id="s1184"></a> §1184

In the attributive position ([1154](#s1154)) stands the genitive of the demonstrative, reflexive, and reciprocal pronouns. <span lang="grc">τὸ τούτου βιβλίον</span> or <span lang="grc">τὸ βιβλίον τὸ τούτου</span> _his book_, <span lang="grc">τὸ ἐμαυτοῦ βιβλίον</span> or <span lang="grc">τὸ βιβλίον τὸ ἐμαυτοῦ</span> _my own book;_ <span lang="grc">μετεπέμψατο τὴν ἑαυτοῦ θυγατέρα καὶ τὸν παῖδα αὐτῆς</span> _he sent for his daughter and her child_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.1">X. C. 1.3.1</cite>.

**a.** The type <span lang="grc">τὸ βιβλίον τούτου</span> is rare and suspected except when another attributive is added: <span lang="grc">τῇ νῦν ὕβρει τούτου</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.3">D. 4.3</cite>. The types <span lang="grc">τὸ βιβλίον ἐμαυτοῦ</span> (<cite data-ref="Hdt. 6.23">Hdt. 6.23</cite>) and <span lang="grc">τὸ αὐτοῦ βιβλίον</span> (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.102">T. 6.102</cite>) are rare. <!-- page 298 -->

##### <a id="s1185"></a> §1185

In the predicate position stands

**a.** The genitive of the personal pronouns (whether partitive or not): <span lang="grc">τὸ βιβλίον μου</span> (<span lang="grc">σου, αὐτοῦ</span>, etc.), or <span lang="grc">μου σου, αὐτοῦ</span>. etc.) <span lang="grc">τὸ βιβλίον</span> when other words precede, as <span lang="grc">ὃς ἔχει σου τὴν ὰδελοην</span> _who has your sister to wife_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.50">And. 1.50</cite>.

**b.** The genitive of the other pronouns used partitively.

N. 1.—Homer does not use the article in the above cases, and often employs the orthotone forms (<span lang="grc">σεῖο μέγα κλέος</span> _thy great fame_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 16.241"><span lang="grc">π</span> 241</cite>). Even in Attic <span lang="grc">ἐμοῦ</span> for <span lang="grc">μου</span> occurs (<span lang="grc">ἐμοῦ τὰ φορτία</span> _my wares_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Wasps 1398">Ar. Vesp. 1398</cite>).

N. 2.—The differences of position between 1184 and 1185 may be thus illustrated:

|  | <span lang="grc">καλόν ἐστί μου τὸ βιβλίον</span>. |
| _My pretty book:_ | <span lang="grc">τὸ καλόν μου βιβλίον</span>. |
| _They read their books:_ | <span lang="grc">τὰ έαυτῶν &lt;\*&gt;βιβλία ἀναγιγνώσκουσι</span>. |

### **INTERROGATIVES**, <span lang="grc">**ἄλλος**, **πολύς**, **ὀλίγος**</span> **WITH THE ARTICLE**

##### <a id="s1186"></a> §1186

The interrogatives <span lang="grc">τίς, ποῖος</span> may take the article when a question is asked about an object before mentioned: <span lang="grc">ΣΩ. νῦν δ&lt;\*&gt; ἐκε &lt;\*&gt;να, ὦ Φαῖδρε, δυνάμεθα κρί̄νειν. ΦΑΙ. τὰ ποῖα</span>; SOCR. _Now at last we can decide those questions_. PH. _The what questions?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 277a">P. Phae. 277a</cite>.

##### <a id="s1187"></a> §1187

So even with a personal pronoun: A. <span lang="grc">δεῦρο δὴ εὐθὺ ἡμῶν</span> . . . B. <span lang="grc">ποῖ λέγεις καὶ παρὰ τίνας τοὺς ὑ̄μᾶς</span>; A. _Come hither straight to us_. B. _Whither do you mean and who are you that I am to come to_ (you being who)? <cite data-ref="Plat. Lysis 203b">P. Lys. 203b</cite>.

##### <a id="s1188"></a> §1188

<span lang="grc"> **ἄλλος** </span> _other_.—<span lang="grc">ὁ ἄλλος</span> in the singular usually means _the rest_ (<span lang="grc">ἡ ἄλλη Ἑλλάς</span> _the rest of Greece_); in the plural. _the others_ (<span lang="grc">οἱ ἄλλοι Ἕλληνες</span> _the other_ (_ceteri_) _Greeks_, but <span lang="grc">ἄλλοι Ἕλληνες</span> _other &lt;\*&gt;lii_) _Greeks_). A substantivized adjective or participle usually has the article when it stands in apposition to <span lang="grc">οἱ ἄλλοι</span>: <span lang="grc">τἆλλα τὰ πολῑτικά</span> _the other civic affairs_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hiero 9.5">X. Hi. 9.5</cite>. On <span lang="grc">ἄλλος, ὁ ἄλλος</span> (sometimes <span lang="grc">ἕτερος</span>) _besides_, see [1272](17-pronouns.md#s1272).

##### <a id="s1189"></a> §1189

<span lang="grc">**πολύς**, **ὀλίγος**· τὸ πολύ</span> usually means _the great_(_er_) _part_, <span lang="grc">οἱ πολλοί</span> _the multitude_, _the vulgar crowd;_ <span lang="grc">πλείονες</span> _several_, <span lang="grc">οἱ πλείονες</span> _the majority, the mass;_ <span lang="grc">πλεῖστοι</span> _very many_, <span lang="grc">οἱ πλεῖστοι</span> _the most;_ <span lang="grc">ὁλίγοι</span> _few_, <span lang="grc">οἱ ὀλίγοι</span> _the oligarchs_ (as opposed to <span lang="grc">οἱ πολλοί</span>). Note <span lang="grc">πολύς</span> predicative: <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ ἑώρᾱ πολλὰ τὰ κρέᾱ</span> _when he saw that there was abundance of meat_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.6">X. C. 1.3.6</cite>.
