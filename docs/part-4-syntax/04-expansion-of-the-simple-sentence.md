# EXPANSION OF THE SIMPLE SENTENCE

##### <a id="s922"></a> §922

The subject and the predicate may be expanded by amplification or qualification:

##### <a id="s923"></a> §923

**Expansion of the Subject**.—The subject may be expanded: A. By _amplification:_ <span lang="grc">Ξενίᾱς καὶ Πᾱσίων ἀπέπλευσαν</span> _Xenias and Pasion sailed away_. B. By _qualification:_ 1. By an attributive adjective, <span lang="grc">ὁ ἀγαθὸς ἀνήρ</span> _the good man_, an attributive substantive denoting _occupation_, _condition_ , or _age_, <span lang="grc">ἀνὴρ στρατηγός</span> _a captain_ ([986](10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s986)), an adjective pronoun or numeral: <span lang="grc">ἡμέτερος φίλος</span> _a friend of ours_, <span lang="grc">δύο παῖδες</span> _two children_. 2. By the genitive of a noun or substantive pronoun (adnominal or attributive genitive): <span lang="grc">στέφανος χρῡσοῦ</span> _a crown of gold_, <span lang="grc">ὁ πατὴρ ἡμῶν</span> _our father_. 3. By a prepositional phrase: <span lang="grc">ὁδὸς κατὰ τοῦ γηλόφου</span> _a way down the hill_. 4. By an adverb: <span lang="grc">οἱ νῦν ἄνθρωποι</span> _the men of the present day_. 5. By an appositive ([916](02-syntax-of-the-simple-sentence.md#s916)). A substantive in any case may be qualified like the subject.

##### <a id="s924"></a> §924

**Expansion of the Predicate**.—The predicate may be expanded: A. By _amplification:_ <span lang="grc">οἱ λοχᾱγοὶ ἀπῆλθον καὶ ἐποίουν οὕτω</span> _the captains departed and did so_. B. By _qualification:_ 1. By the oblique case of a noun, a substantive pronoun, or a numeral. This is called the object ([919](02-syntax-of-the-simple-sentence.md#s919), [920](02-syntax-of-the-simple-sentence.md#s920)). Thus: <span lang="grc">ὁρῶ τὸν ἄνδρα</span> _I see the man_, <span lang="grc">φωνῆς ἀκούω</span> _I hear a voice_, <span lang="grc">εἵπετο τῷ ἡγεμόνι</span> _he followed the guide_, <span lang="grc">ἀγαπᾷ ἡμᾶς</span> _he loves us_, <span lang="grc">ἐνί̄κησε τὴν μάχην</span> _he won the battle_ (cognate accusative, [1567](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1567)), <span lang="grc">ἔδωκα δέκα</span> _I gave ten_. The oblique case may be followed by an adnominal genitive or a dative: <span lang="grc">ὁρῶ πολλοὺς τῶν πολῑτῶν</span> _I see many of the citizens_. 2. By a preposition with its appropriate case: <span lang="grc">ἦλθον ἐπὶ τὰ̄ς σκηνά̄ς</span> _they went to their tents_. 3. By an infinitive: <span lang="grc">ἐθέλει ἀπελθεῖν</span> _he wishes to depart_. 4. By a participle: <span lang="grc">ἄρξομαι λέγων</span> _I will begin my speech_. 5. By an adverb or adverbial expression: <span lang="grc">εὖ ἴστω</span> _let him know well_, <span lang="grc">τῆς νυκτὸς ἦλθε</span> _he came during the night_, <span lang="grc">ἀπῆλθε τριταῖος</span> _he departed on the third day_ ([1042](14-adjectives.md#s1042)). On complements to the predicate, see [909](02-syntax-of-the-simple-sentence.md#s909).
