# NEGATIVE SENTENCES

##### <a id="s2688"></a> §2688

The simple negative particles are <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> and <span lang="grc">μή. οὐ</span> is the negative of fact and statement, and _contradicts_ or _denies;_ <span lang="grc">μή</span> is the negative of the will and thought, and _rejects_ or _deprecates_. The difference between the simple negatives holds true also of their compounds <span lang="grc">οὔτε μήτε, οὐδέ μηδέ, οὐδείς μηδείς</span>, etc.

**a.** <span lang="grc">τὰ οὐκ ὄντα</span> is _that which does not exist_ independently of any opinion of the writer: <span lang="grc">τὰ οὐκ ὄντα λογοποιεῖν</span> _to fabricate what does not_ actually _exist_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 3.35">And. 3.35</cite>. <span lang="grc">τὰ μὴ ὄντα</span> is _that which is_ regarded as _not existing_, that which is dependent on the opinion of the writer, the whole sum of things that are outside of actual knowledge: <span lang="grc">τὰ μὴ ἐόντα οὔτε ὁρᾶται οὔτε γῑνώσκεται</span> _that which does not exist is neither seen nor known_ Hippocrates, de arte § 2; cp. <span lang="grc">τὸ μὴ ὄν</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 478b">P. R. 478b</cite>.

**b.** The rarer <span lang="grc"> **οὐχί** </span> (<span lang="grc">οὐ-χί</span>) denies with greater emphasis than <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>. The form <span lang="grc"> **μηκέτι** </span> _no longer_ is due to the analogy of <span lang="grc">οὐκ-έτι</span>.

##### <a id="s2689"></a> §2689

<span lang="grc">μή</span> as the negative of will and thought is used in various expressions involving emotion, as commands, prohibitions, wishes, hopes, prayers, petitions, promises, oaths, asseverations, and the like; in expressions marking condition, purpose, effort, apprehension, cautious assertion, surmise, and fear; in setting forth ideality, mere conceptions, abstractions as opposed to reality or to definite facts; in marking ideas as general and typical; when a person or thing is to be characterized as conceived of rather than real.—<span lang="grc">μή</span> is used not merely when the above notions are apparent but also when they are latent. Greek often conceives of a situation as marked by feeling where English regards it as one of fact; and hence uses <span lang="grc">μή</span> where we should expect <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>.

**a.** <span lang="grc">μή</span> corresponds to the Sanskrit prohibitive particle _mā´_, which in the Rig Veda is used with the independent indicative of an augmentless aorist or imper<!-- page 609 --> fect which has the force of the subjunctive; rarely with the optative. In later Sanskrit _mā´_ was used with the subjunctive, optative, and imperative.

**b.** <span lang="grc">μή</span> was originally used only in independent clauses; but later was employed in subordinate clauses, and with dependent infinitives and participles. On the origin of <span lang="grc">μή</span> as a conjunction, see [2222](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/03-object-clauses.md#s2222). In Homer <span lang="grc">μή</span> is used especially with the subjunctive, optative, and imperative (i.e. in commands and wishes); rarely with the indicative (in <span lang="grc">μὴ ὤφελλον</span>, in oaths, in questions, after _verbs of fearing_ referring to a past event); with the infinitive when used for the imperative after a _verb of saying_, etc. when the infinitive expresses a command or a wish, and when a dependent infinitive is used in an oath; with the participle only in connection with a command (<cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 14.48"><span lang="grc">Ξ</span> 48</cite>) or a wish (<cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 4.684"><span lang="grc">δ</span> 684</cite>).

**c.** In later Greek (Polybius, Lucian, Dio Chrysostomus, etc.) <span lang="grc">μή</span> has encroached on <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>, generally by extension of usages occurring rarely in the classical language. Thus Lucian has <span lang="grc">μή</span> after causal <span lang="grc">ὡς, ὅτι, διότι, ἐπεί</span>; in relative clauses (sometimes <span lang="grc">οὐδέν ἐστιν ὅτι μή</span>); with participles of cause (even <span lang="grc">ἅτε μή</span>) or of concession; with participles without the article following an adjective; with the infinitive after _verbs of saying_ and _thinking_. <span lang="grc">ὅτι μή</span> appears in indirect discourse (complete or partial) where the classical language would use the infinitive or <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> with the optative or <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> with the participle; so after _verbs of saying_ and _thinking_, after _verbs of emotion_, and even after _verbs of knowing_.

## POSITION OF <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> AND <span lang="grc">μή</span>

##### <a id="s2690"></a> §2690

<span lang="grc">οὐ</span> and <span lang="grc">μή</span> are generally placed before the word they negative; but may follow, when emphasis is laid on a particular word, as in contrasts.

<span lang="grc">ὑπολάβῃ δὲ μηδείς</span> _but let no one suppose_ T. C. 84, <span lang="grc">οἱ δὲ στρατηγοὶ ἐξῆγον μὲν οὔ, συνεκάλεσαν δέ</span> _and the generals did not lead them out, but called them together_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.4.20">X. A. 6.4.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">ξύμμαχοι ἐγενόμεθα οὐκ ἐπὶ καταδουλώσει τῶν Ἑλλήνων Ἀθηναίοις, ἀλλ᾽ ἐπ᾽ ἐλευθερώσει ἀπὸ τοῦ Μήδου τοῖς Ἕλλησιν</span> _we became allies, not to the Athenians_, _for the purpose of enslaving the Greeks_, _but to the Greeks for the purpose of freeing them from the Mede_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.10">T. 3.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπόλοιτο μὲν μή</span> _perish indeed —may he not_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 83">E. Med. 83</cite>.

**a.** A contrast must be supplied in thought when the negative precedes the article, a relative, a conjunction, or a preposition. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ περὶ ἡμῶν γνώσεσθε μὴ τὰ εἰκότα</span> _but if you pass upon us a sentence that is unjust_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.57">T. 3.57</cite>, <span lang="grc">πολεμεῖν δὲ μὴ πρὸς ὁμοίᾱν ἀντιπαρασκευὴν ἀδύνατοι</span> _unable to carry on a war against a power dissimilar in character to their own_ 1. 141, <span lang="grc">ἀμυνούμεθα τοὺς πολεμίους οὐκ εἰς μακρά̄ν</span> _we shall shortly_ (lit. _in no long time_) _punish the enemy_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.4.21">X. C. 5.4.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ κατὰ κόσμον</span> _disorderly_ B 214.

**b.** The order of the parts of a negative compound may be reversed for strong emphasis; as <span lang="grc">ἔτ᾽ οὐκ ὤν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐκέτι ὤν</span>) <cite data-ref="Soph. Trach. 161">S. Tr. 161</cite>, <span lang="grc">μίαν οὐκ</span> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐδεμίαν</span>) <cite data-ref="Hdt. 8.119">Hdt. 8.119</cite>.

**c.** The negative may be placed in front of an infinitive when English transfers it to another verb in the sentence; as <span lang="grc">εἰ βουλόμεθ᾽ ἡμεῖς μὴ προσποιεῖσθαι πολεμεῖν αὐτὸν ἡμῖν</span> _if we wish to assume that he is not waging war with us_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.58">D. 8.58</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡμᾶς οὐδ᾽ ἐναυλισθῆναι ἐπιτρέπεις</span> _you do not permit us even to take up our quarters_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.7.8">X. A. 7.7.8</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἐπιτρέπεις ῀ κωλύ̄εις</span>).

### <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> ADHERESCENT

<!-- page 610 -->

##### <a id="s2691"></a> §2691

<span lang="grc">οὐ</span> adherescent (or privative) placed before a verb (or other single word) not merely negatives the meaning of the simple verb but gives it an _opposite_ meaning, the two expressing a single negative idea; as <span lang="grc">οὔ φημι</span> _I deny_, _I refuse_ (not _I say not_). <span lang="grc">οὔ φημι</span> is preferred to <span lang="grc">φημὶ οὐ</span> as _nego_ is preferred to _aio non_.

##### <a id="s2692"></a> §2692

Adherescent <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is especially common with _verbs of saying_ or _thinking_, but occurs also with many _verbs of will_ or _desire_. In such cases <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> goes closely with the leading verb, forming a _quasi-_ compound; whereas it belongs in sense to a following infinitive if an infinitive depends on the leading verb. In Latin actual composition has taken place in _nego_, _nescio_, _nequeo_, _nolo_.

<span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔφη ἰέναι</span> _he refused to go_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.8">X. A. 1.3.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὔ φᾱσιν εἶναι ἄλλην ὁδόν</span> _they say that there is no other road_ 4. 1. 21 (cp. <span lang="grc">φῂς ἢ οὔ</span>; _yes or no?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 27d">P. A. 27d</cite>), <span lang="grc">τίνας δ᾽ οὐκ ᾤετο δεῖν λέγειν</span>; _who were those whom he thought ought not to speak?_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 1.28">Aes. 1.28</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἃ οὐκ ἐᾶτε ἡμᾶς . . . ποιεῖν</span> _what you forbid us to do_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.10">X. C. 1.3.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀξιοῖ . . . φεύγοντα τῑμωρεῖσθαι</span> _he said that it was not right to avenge himself on an exile_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.136">T. 1.136</cite>.

**a.** So with <span lang="grc">οὔ φημι</span> and <span lang="grc">οὐ φάσκω</span> _deny_, _refuse_ ( = <span lang="grc">ἀπαρνοῦμαι</span>), <span lang="grc">οὐκ οἴομαι, οὐ νομίζω, οὐ δοκῶ, οὐκ ἐῶ</span> and <span lang="grc">οὐ κελεύω</span> _forbid_ (_veto_), <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀξιῶ</span> _regard as unworthy_, _do not expect that, refuse_, <span lang="grc">οὐχ ὑπισχνοῦμαι</span> _refuse_, <span lang="grc">οὐ προσποιοῦμαι</span> _dissimulo_, <span lang="grc">οὐ συμβουλεύω</span> _dissuade_, _advise not to_, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἐθέλω</span> _am unwilling_, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἐπαινῶ</span> _disapprove_. This association often persists in participles, as <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἐῶν, οὐκ ἐθέλων</span>. Homer has <span lang="grc">οὔ φημι, φημὶ οὐ</span>, and <span lang="grc">οὔ φημι οὐ</span>.

##### <a id="s2693"></a> §2693

<span lang="grc">οὐ</span> with the principal verb may be equivalent in sense to <span lang="grc">μή</span> with a dependent infinitive; as <span lang="grc">οὐ συμβουλεύων Ξέρξῃ στρατεύεσθαι ἐπὶ τὴν Ἑλλάδα</span> _advising Xerxes not to march against Greece_ ( = <span lang="grc">συμβουλεύων μὴ στρατεύεσθαι</span>) <cite data-ref="Hdt. 7.46">Hdt. 7.46</cite>.

##### <a id="s2694"></a> §2694

Analogous to this use with verbs is the use of <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> with adjectives and adverbs.

<span lang="grc">οὐκ ὀλίγοι ῀ πολλοί, οὐκ ἐλάχιστος ῀ μέγιστος, οὐχ ἧττον ῀ μᾶλλον, οὐχ ἥκιστα ῀ μάλιστα, οὐ καλῶς</span> _basely_, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀφανής</span> _famous_, <span lang="grc">οὐκ εἰκότως</span> _unreasonably_, <span lang="grc">οὐ περὶ βραχέων</span> _on important matters_ (cp. [2690](#s2690) a), regularly <span lang="grc">οὐ πάνυ</span> _not at all_, as <span lang="grc">οὐ πάνυ χαλεπόν</span> _easy_.

##### <a id="s2695"></a> §2695

The origin of adherescent <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is to be found partly in the unwillingness of the early language to use the negative particle with the infinitive, partly in the preference for a negative rather than a positive assertion, and to the disinclination to make a strong positive statement (_litotes_, as in some of the cases of [2694](#s2694)), and partly in the absence of negative compounds, the development of which in adjectives and participles ([2071 a](22-the-participle.md#s2071)) was in turn restricted by the use of adherescent <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>.

##### <a id="s2696"></a> §2696

Adherescent <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is often found in a protasis with <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> and in other constructions where we expect <span lang="grc">μή</span>. <!-- page 611 -->

<span lang="grc">εἰ δ᾽ ἀποστῆναι Ἀθηναίων οὐκ ἠθελήσαμεν . . ., οὐκ ἠδικοῦμεν</span> _but if we refused to revolt from the Athenians_, _we were not doing wrong_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.55">T. 3.55</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ οὐκ ἐᾷς</span> _if thou forbiddest_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 1131">S. Aj. 1131</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">εἰ κωλύ̄εις</span>), <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ Πρόξενον οὐχ ὑπεδέξαντο, ἐσώθησαν ἄν</span> _if it had not been that they did not receive Proxenus_, _they would have been saved_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.74">D. 19.74</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν οὐ πολλοὶ ἦσαν</span> _if they were few_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 13.62">L. 13.62</cite> (emended by some to <span lang="grc">οὖν μή</span>). <span lang="grc"> **ἐὰ̄ν οὐ** </span> is rare, as <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν τε οὐ φῆτε ἐά̄ν τε φῆτε</span> _both if you deny it and if you admit it_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 25b">P. A. 25b</cite> (cp. <cite data-ref="Lys. 13.76">L. 13.76</cite>, <cite data-ref="Dem. 26.24">D. 26.24</cite>).

##### <a id="s2697"></a> §2697

But <span lang="grc">μή</span> often does not yield to <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>, as <span lang="grc">ἄ̄ντ᾽ ἐγὼ φῶ ἄ̄ν τε μὴ φῶ</span> _both if I assent and if I do not_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.205">D. 21.205</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ οἶδ᾽ ὅπως φῶ τοῦτο καὶ μὴ φῶ</span> _I know not how I shall say this and not say it_ <cite data-ref="Eur. IA 643">E. I. A. 643</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν μὴ . . . ἐᾶτε</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 16.12">D. 16.12</cite>, and in many cases where <span lang="grc">μή</span> goes closely with the following word, as <span lang="grc">εἰ ἐδίδου κρίσιν καὶ μὴ ἀφῃρεῖτο</span> _if he were granting a trial and not taking it away_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.91">D. 23.91</cite>.

### <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> AFTER <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span>)

##### <a id="s2698"></a> §2698

<span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is sometimes found in clauses introduced by <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span>).

**a.** When <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is adherescent ([2696](#s2696)).

**b.** When there is an emphatic assertion of fact or probability, as where a direct statement is quoted. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ οὐδὲν ἡμάρτηταί μοι</span> _if_ (as I have shown) _no error has been committed by me_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.33">And. 1.33</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ, ὡς νῦν φήσει, οὐ παρεσκεύαστο</span> _if_, _as he will presently assert, he had not made preparations_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 54.29">D. 54.29</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.18">X. A. 1.7.18</cite>, quoted in 2790.

**c.** When <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span>) is used instead of <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> _that_ (_because_) after _verbs of emotion_ ([2247](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/04-causal-clauses.md#s2247)). Thus, <span lang="grc">μὴ θαυμάσῃς εἰ πολλὰ τῶν εἰρημένων οὐ πρέπει σοι</span> _do not be surprised if much of what has been said does not apply to you_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.44">I. 1.44</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">μή</span> is possible.

**d.** When <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span>) approaches the idea of <span lang="grc">ἐπεί</span> _since_ (cp. [2246](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/04-causal-clauses.md#s2246), [2298](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2298) b). So <span lang="grc">εἰ τούσδε . . . οὐ στέργει πατήρ</span> _if_ (_since_) _their father has ceased to love these_ children <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 88">E. Med. 88</cite> (often explained as <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> adherescent). Here <span lang="grc">μή</span> is possible.

**e.** When a single <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> introduces a bimembered protasis _as a whole_, the <span lang="grc">μέν</span> clause and the <span lang="grc">δέ</span> clause of that protasis may have <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>. Such bimembered protases often depend on a preceding apodosis introduced by <span lang="grc">αἰσχρόν, ἄτοπον, δεινόν, θαυμαστόν ἐστι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἂν εἴη</span>) and like expressions of emotion (c). Thus, <span lang="grc">εἶτ᾽ οὐκ αἰσχρόν . . . εἰ τὸ μὲν Ἀργείων πλῆθος οὐκ ἐφοβήθη τὴν Λακεδαιμονίων ἀρχὴν . . ., ὑ̄μεῖς δὲ ὄντες Ἀθηναῖοι βάρβαρον ἄνθρωπον φοβήσεσθε</span>; _is it not then disgraceful, if_ it is true that _whereas the Argive commons did not fear the empire of the Lacedaemonians_, _you_, _who are Athenians_, _are going to be afraid of a barbarian?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 15.23">D. 15.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">αἰσχρὸν γάρ, εἰ πατὴρ μὲν ἐξεῖλεν Φρύγας, ὃ δ᾽ ἄνδρ᾽ ἕν᾽ ου᾽ δυνήσεται κτανεῖν</span> _for it is disgraceful that, whereas the father destroyed the Phrygians_, _the other_ (the son) _is not going to be able to destroy one_ foe <cite data-ref="Eur. El. 336">E. El. 336</cite>, <span lang="grc">δεινὸν ἂν εἴη, εἰ οἱ μὲν ἐκείνων ξύμμαχοι ἐπὶ δουλείᾳ τῇ αὑτῶν</span> (<span lang="grc">χρήματα</span>) <span lang="grc">φέροντες οὐκ ἀπεροῦσιν, ἡμεῖς δὲ ἐπὶ τῷ . . . αὐτοὶ σῴζεσθαι οὐκ ἄρα δαπανήσομεν</span> _it would be strange if, whereas their allies will not fail to pay tribute for their own enslavement, we on the other hand will not expend it for the purpose of saving ourselves_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.121">T. 1.121</cite>.

N. 1.—The second member of such protases has <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> if the verb stands in the indicative, but <span lang="grc">μή</span> (in classical Greek) i&lt;\*&gt; the verb is in the optative. In <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 2.157">Aes. 2.157</cite> <span lang="grc">οὐ κατάσχοιμι</span> is due to indirect discourse. <!-- page 612 -->

N. 2.—In such sentences <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> may (1) have a conditional force in both clauses, as <cite data-ref="Lys. 30.16">L. 30.16</cite>, 31. 24; (2) have a conditional force in the second member, but the force of <span lang="grc">ἐπεί</span> in the first member, as <cite data-ref="Lys. 20.36">L. 20.36</cite>, <cite data-ref="Isaeus 14.52">Is. 14.52</cite>; (3) have the force of <span lang="grc">ἐπεί</span> in the first member, and that of <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> in the second member, as <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.55">D. 8.55</cite>, <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.242">Aes. 3.242</cite>; (4) have the force of <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> in both members, as <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.35">T. 1.35</cite>, 1. 121, <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.5.84">X. C. 7.5.84</cite>.

**f.** A bimembered clause introduced by <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> may contain a negative clause with <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> directly opposed to a positive clause; as <span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ τῷ μέν, τοῖς δ᾽ οὔ</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.123">D. 23.123</cite>.

**g.** <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> _whether_ in simple and alternative indirect questions takes either <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> or <span lang="grc">μή</span> ([2676 c](33-indirect-dependent-questions.md#s2676), e).

##### <a id="s2699"></a> §2699

Homer has <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> and the indicative with <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> ([12](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s12) times) when the subordinate clause precedes the main clause; but usually <span lang="grc">εἰ μή</span>, when the subordinate clause follows. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ δέ μοι οὐ τείσουσι βοῶν ἐπιεικἔ ἀμοιβήν, δύ̄σομαι εἰς Ἀίδᾱο</span> _but if they will not pay a fitting compensation for the cattle_, _I will go down to Hades_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 12.382"><span lang="grc">μ</span> 382</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔνθα κεν Ἀργείοισιν ὑπέρμορα νόστος ἐτύχθη, εἰ μὴ Ἀθηναίην Ἥρη πρὸς μῦθον ἔειπεν</span> _then in that case the return of the Argives had been accomplished against fate, if Hera had not spoken a word to Athena_ B 155.

**a.** The Homeric <span lang="grc">εἰ οὐ</span> with the indicative has been explained either as a retention of the original use, <span lang="grc">μή</span> with that mood being an extension through the analogy of the subjunctive and optative; or because <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> went with the predicate, whereas <span lang="grc">μή</span> was closely attached to <span lang="grc">εἰ</span>.

##### <a id="s2700"></a> §2700

Homer has <span lang="grc">εἰ οὐ</span> (adherescent) with the subjunctive in <span lang="grc">εἰ δ᾽ ἂν . . . οὐκ ἐθέλωσιν Γ</span> 289, <span lang="grc">εἰ δέ κ᾽ . . . οὐκ εἰῶσιν Υ</span> 139.

##### <a id="s2701"></a> §2701

Herodotus has a few cases of <span lang="grc">εἰ οὐ</span> with the indicative, as 6. 9; <span lang="grc">ἢν οὐ</span> with the subjunctive is doubtful (6. [133](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/04-final-consonants.md#s133)).

## GENERAL RULE FOR <span lang="grc">μή</span>

##### <a id="s2702"></a> §2702

<span lang="grc">μή</span> stands

\1. With the imperative.

\2. In clauses with <span lang="grc">εἰ, ἐά̄ν</span> (exceptions, [2698](#s2698)).

\3. With the subjunctive, except after <span lang="grc">μή</span> _lest_, when <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is used.

\4. With the optative, except after <span lang="grc">μή</span> _lest_, or when the optative has <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or is in indirect discourse.

\5. With the infinitive, except in indirect discourse.

\6. With participles when they have a conditional or general force.

## <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> AND <span lang="grc">μή</span> WITH THE INDICATIVE AND OPTATIVE

### SIMPLE SENTENCES AND INDEPENDENT CLAUSES

##### <a id="s2703"></a> §2703

Statements ([2153](24-summary-of-the-forms-of-simple-sentences.md#s2153)) expressed by simple sentences and independent clauses take <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>. Direct questions take either <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> or <span lang="grc">μή</span> ([2651](32-interrogative-sentences-questions.md#s2651)). The independent future indicative has <span lang="grc">μή</span> only in questions.

##### <a id="s2704"></a> §2704

In _wishes_ <span lang="grc">μή</span> is used with the indicative ([1780](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1780)-[1781](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1781)) or the optative ([1814](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1814), cp. [2156](24-summary-of-the-forms-of-simple-sentences.md#s2156)). <!-- page 613 -->

<span lang="grc">εἴθε σε μήποτ᾽ εἰδόμᾱν</span> _would that I had never seen thee_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 1218">S. O. T. 1218</cite>, <span lang="grc">μήποτ᾽ ὤφελον λιπεῖν τὴν Σκῦρον</span> _would that I had never left Scyrus_ S. Ph 969.

<span lang="grc">μὴ ζῴην</span> _may I not live_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Kn. 833">Ar. Eq. 833</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀναιδὴς οὔτ᾽ εἰμὶ μήτε γενοίμην</span> _I neither am nor may I become shameless_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.68">D. 8.68</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὔτ᾽ ἂν δυναίμην μήτ᾽ ἐπισταίμην λέγειν</span> _neither could I tell nor may I be capable of telling_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 686">S. Ant. 686</cite>.

**a.** That <span lang="grc">ὤφελον</span> takes <span lang="grc">μή</span>, not <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>, shows that it has lost to a certain extent its verbal nature. In late Greek it even became a particle like <span lang="grc">εἴθε</span>.

**b.** Indirect expressions of wishing with <span lang="grc">πῶς ἄν</span> and the optative ([1832](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1832)), <span lang="grc">βουλοίμην ἄν</span> ([1827](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1827)), <span lang="grc">ἐβουλόμην</span> (<span lang="grc">ἄν</span>) with the infinitive, take <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> ([1782](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1782), [1789](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1789)).

**c.** The use is the same in dependent clauses; as <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὴ δ᾽ ἃ μήποτ᾽ ὤφελε</span> (<span lang="grc">συμβῆναι</span>) <span lang="grc">συνέβη</span> _but when that happened which I would had never happened_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.320">D. 18.320</cite>.

### SUBORDINATE CLAUSES IN THE INDICATIVE OR OPTATIVE

##### <a id="s2705"></a> §2705

In subordinate clauses <span lang="grc">μή</span> or <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is used.

**a. Final clauses** have <span lang="grc">μή</span>, as <span lang="grc">φίλος ἐβούλετο εἶναι τοῖς μέγιστα δυναμένοις, ἵνα ἀδικῶν μὴ διδοίη δίκην</span> _he wished to be on friendly terms with men in power in order that he might not pay the penalty for his wrong-doing_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.6.21">X. A. 2.6.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔδει τὰ ἐνέχυρα τότε λαβεῖν, ὡς μηδ᾽ ει᾽ ἐβούλετο ἐδύνατο ἐξαπατᾶν</span> quoted in 2185 c.

**b. Object clauses** with <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span> after _verbs of effort_ have <span lang="grc">μή</span>, as <span lang="grc">φρόντιζ᾽ ὅπως μηδὲν ἀνάξιον τῆς τῑμῆς ταύτης πρά̄ξεις</span> _see to it that you do nothing unworthy of this honour_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 2.37">I. 2.37</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπεμέλετο ὅπως μήτε ἄσῑτοι μήτε ἄποτοί ποτε ἔσοιντο</span> _he took care that they should never be without food or drink_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.1.43">X. C. 8.1.43</cite>.

**c. Conditional clauses** regularly have <span lang="grc">μή</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ ὑ̄μεῖς ἤλθετε, ἐπορευόμεθα ἂν ἐπὶ βασιλέᾱ</span> _if you had not come, we should be marching against the king_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.4">X. A. 2.1.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀπελείπετο αὐτοῦ, εἰ μή τι ἀναγκαῖον εἴη</span> _he never left him unless there was some necessity for it_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.2.40">X. M. 4.2.40</cite>. So in concessive clauses ([2369](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/11-concessive-clauses.md#s2369)). On <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> adherescent in conditional clauses see [2696](#s2696).

**d. Relative Clauses**, if conditional, have <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> with a definite antecedent, <span lang="grc">μή</span> with an indefinite antecedent ([2505](30-adjective-clauses-relative-clauses-2488-2573.md#s2505)). <span lang="grc">μή</span> is thus used when the case in question is typical of a class (<span lang="grc">μή</span> ‘generic’). Thus, <span lang="grc">προσημαίνουσιν ἅ τε χρὴ ποιεῖν καὶ ἃ οὐ χρή</span> _they signify beforehand what one must do and what not_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.6.46">X. C. 1.6.46</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἃ μὴ οἶδα οὐδὲ οἴομαι εἰδέναι</span> _what I do not know_, _I do not even think I know_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 21d">P. A. 21d</cite>.

N. 1.—Homer has <span lang="grc">ὃς</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅσος</span>) <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> with the indicative (<span lang="grc">μή</span> B [301](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/08-declension-of-participles.md#s301)).

N. 2.—<span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is regular in relative clauses when an opposition is expressed (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.11">T. 1.11</cite>. 2), and when a negative clause precedes; as <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔστιν ὅστις</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅπως</span>) <span lang="grc">οὐ οὐδεὶς ὅστις οὐ</span>, etc. (<cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.25">X. C. 1.4.25</cite>, <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.4.3">X. A. 2.4.3</cite>).

**e.** The expression <span lang="grc">**τοιοῦτος**, **ὅς**</span> (<span lang="grc"> **ὅστις** </span>, etc.), when preceded by a negative, takes <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>; as <span lang="grc">ταμιεῖον μηδενὶ εἶναι μηδὲν τοιοῦτον, εἰς ὃ οὐ πᾶς ὁ βουλόμενος εἴσεισι</span> it is necessary that _no one shall have_ (_such_) _a storehouse that anybody who pleases may not enter it_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 416d">P. R. 416d</cite>. But even when no negative precedes, we have <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>, when the relative clause makes an assertion or defines attributively; as <span lang="grc">συγγραφεὺς τῶν λόγων . . . τοιοῦτος, οἷος οὐδεὶς ἄλλος γέγονε</span> _such a writer of speeches as no one had been_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 15.35">I. 15.35</cite>. When the antecedent is general or is thought of in respect of its _character_ we have <span lang="grc">μή</span>; as <span lang="grc">βουληθεὶς τοιοῦτον μνημεῖον καταλιπεῖν, ὃ μὴ τῆς ἀνθρωπίνης φύσεώς ἐστιν</span> _wishing to leave behind him such a memorial as would surpass human nature_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.89">I. 4.89</cite>; cp. [2705 g](#s2705). <!-- page 614 -->

**f. Relative clauses of purpose** take <span lang="grc">μή</span>, as <span lang="grc">θαλάσσιον ἐκρί̄ψατ᾽, ἔνθα μήποτ᾽ εἰσόψεσθ᾽ ἔτι</span> _cast me out into the sea where ye may never see me more_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 1411">S. O. T. 1411</cite>, <span lang="grc">κρύψᾱσ᾽ ἑαυτήν, ἔνθα μή τις εἰσίδοι</span> _hiding herself where no one might see her_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Trach. 903">S. Tr. 903</cite>.

**g.** Clauses with a relative pronoun referring to an antecedent thought of in respect of its _character_ (_of such a sort_) take <span lang="grc">μή</span>. The use of <span lang="grc">μή</span> characteristic comes from the generic meaning of <span lang="grc">μή</span>, i.e. the antecedent is not regarded simply as a person _who_ does something but as a person _of such a nature as, one who typifies a class_. In such cases <span lang="grc">ὃς μή</span> may refer to a definite person or thing. So especially in relative clauses of cause and result, which ordinarily take <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ταλαίπωρος ἄρα τις σύ γε ἄνθρωπος εἶ . . ., ᾧ μήτε θεοὶ πατρῷοί εἰσι μήτε ἱερά</span> _a wretched being art thou then, who hast neither ancestral gods nor shrines_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyd. 302b">P. Eu. 302b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ψηφίσασθε τοιαῦτα ἐξ ὧν μηδέποτε ὑ̄μῖν μεταμελήσει</span> _pass such a vote that you will never repent of it_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 3.41">And. 3.41</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοιαῦτα λέγειν . . ., οἷς μηδεὶς ἂν νεμεσήσαι</span> _to use language at which no one could feel just resentment_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.161">D. 21.161</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁ . . . μηδὲν ἂν ὀμόσᾱς</span> _the man who would not take an oath_ 54. 40. Sophocles is especially fond of the generic <span lang="grc">μή</span>.

**h. Consecutive clauses** (and consecutive relative clauses) with <span lang="grc">ὤστε</span> take <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> with the indicative and optative. Thus, (<span lang="grc">Λακεδαιμόνιοι</span>) <span lang="grc">εἰς τοῦτ᾽ ἀπληστίᾱς ἦλθον ὥστ᾽ οὐκ ἐξήρκεσεν αὐτοῖς ἔχειν τὴν κατὰ γῆν ἀρχήν</span> the Lacedaemonians _became so insatiate in their desires that they were not satisfied with their empire on the land_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 12.103">I. 12.103</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὥστ᾽ οὐκ ἂν αὐτὸν γνωρίσαιμ᾽ ἂν εἰσιδών</span> _so that I should not recognize him, if I were to see him_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Orest. 379">E. Or. 379</cite>. On <span lang="grc">τοιοῦτος ὃς οὐ</span> see [2705 e](#s2705).

**i.** _Oaths_ and _protestations_ in the indicative with <span lang="grc">μή</span> express a solemn denial or refusal, or repudiate a charge. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἴστω νῦν Ζεὺς . . . μὴ μὲν τοῖς ἵπποισιν ἀνὴρ ἐποιχήσεται ἄλλος</span> _let Zeus now know_ (i.e. I swear by Zeus) _that no other man shall mount these horses_ K 329, <span lang="grc">μὰ τὴν Ἀφροδί̄την . . . μὴ ᾿γώ σ᾽ ἀφήσω</span> _by Aphrodite_, far be it from me _that I should release you_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Eccl. 999">Ar. Eccl. 999</cite>. Cp. [2716](#s2716).

## <span lang="grc">μή</span> WITH THE SUBJUNCTIVE AND IMPERATIVE

##### <a id="s2706"></a> §2706

The subjunctive is a mood of _will_, and therefore takes <span lang="grc">μή</span>.

##### <a id="s2707"></a> §2707

**Independent clauses** take <span lang="grc">μή</span>: the hortatory subjunctive ([1797](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1797)), the prohibitive subjunctive ([1800](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1800)), the deliberative subjunctive ([1805](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1805)), the subjunctive of doubtful assertion ([1801](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1801)).

**a.** The anticipatory subjunctive in Homer takes <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> ([1810](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1810), cp. [1813](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1813)).

##### <a id="s2708"></a> §2708

**Dependent clauses** take <span lang="grc">μή</span>: final clauses, as <span lang="grc">δοκεῖ μοι κατακαῦσαι τὰ̄ς ἁμάξᾱς . . . ἵνα μὴ τὰ ζεύγη ἡμῶν στρατηγῇ</span> _it seems to me advisable to burn the wagons that our baggage-train may not be our general_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.27">X. A. 3.2.27</cite>. Object clauses after _verbs of effort_, as <span lang="grc">οὐ φυλάξεσθ᾽ ὅπως μὴ . . . δεσπότην εὕρητε</span>; _will you not be on your guard lest you find a master?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 6.25">D. 6.25</cite>. So in conditional clauses with <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span>, in conditional relative clauses and in relative clauses referring to indefinite time, place, and manner.

**a.** After <span lang="grc">μή</span> _lest_, <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is used ([2221](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/03-object-clauses.md#s2221)).

##### <a id="s2709"></a> §2709

The imperative is a mood of _will_ and therefore takes <span lang="grc">μή</span> in prohibitions ([1840](20-the-verb-voices/08-imperative.md#s1840)).

**a.** The future indicative after interrogative <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> has an imperative sense ([1918](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1918)).

## NEGATIVES OF INDIRECT DISCOURSE

<!-- page 615 -->

##### <a id="s2710"></a> §2710

The negatives of direct discourse are retained in indirect discourse introduced by <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> or <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>.

<span lang="grc">ἐνθῡμηθῆναι χρὴ ὅτι οὐδείς ἐστιν ἀνθρώπων φύσει οὔτε ὀλιγαρχικὸς οὔτε δημοκρατικός</span> _it must be borne in mind that no man by nature is disposed either to oligarchy or to democracy_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 25.8">L. 25.8</cite>.

<span lang="grc">εἶπε . . . ὅτι οὐ περὶ πολῑτείᾱς ὑ̄μῖν ἔσται ἀλλὰ περὶ σωτηρίᾱς, εἰ μὴ ποιήσαιθ᾽ ἃ Θηρᾱμένης κελεύοι</span> _he said that the question would not be about your constitution but about your safety, if you did not accept the propositions of Theramenes_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.74">L. 12.74</cite>.

**a.** In <span lang="grc">προεῖπεν ὡς μηδεὶς κῑνήσοιτο ἐκ τῆς τάξεως</span> _he gave orders that no one should move from his position_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.1.22">X. H. 2.1.22</cite> <span lang="grc">μηδείς</span> is due to the fact that the main verb denotes a command.

On the negative in indirect discourse with the infinitive see [2722](#s2722), [2737](#s2737), 2738; with the participle, 2729, 2737, 2738; and in indirect questions, 2676.

## <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> AND <span lang="grc">μή</span> WITH THE INFINITIVE

##### <a id="s2711"></a> §2711

The infinitive not in indirect discourse has <span lang="grc">μή</span>; the infinitive in indirect discourse has <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>, but sometimes <span lang="grc">μή</span>. The articular infinitive has <span lang="grc">μή</span>. On the use with <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> see [2742](#s2742) ff.

**a.** The ordinary negative of the infinitive is <span lang="grc">μή</span>, which could be so used since the infinitive was employed as early as Homer in an imperative sense. <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> with the infinitive in indirect discourse is probably due to the analogy of <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> with the indicative and optative in clauses of indirect discourse introduced by <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> (<span lang="grc">ὡς</span>). <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> became the natural negative of indirect discourse as soon as the infinitive came to represent the indicative or optative.

##### <a id="s2712"></a> §2712

<span lang="grc">μή</span> is used with the articular infinitive.

<span lang="grc">παράδειγμα τοῦ μὴ ὑ̄μᾶς ἀδικεῖν</span> _a warning not to injure you_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 27.5">L. 27.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπὲρ τοῦ μὴ τὸ κελευόμενον ποιῆσαι</span> _in order to avoid doing what was commanded_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.204">D. 18.204</cite>. On <span lang="grc">τὸ</span> (<span lang="grc">τοῦ</span>) <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span>, see [2744](#s2744). 9. 10, 2749 b, d.

### <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> AND <span lang="grc">μή</span> WITH THE INFINITIVE NOT IN INDIRECT DISCOURSE

##### <a id="s2713"></a> §2713

<span lang="grc">μή</span> is the regular negative after all verbs, adjectives, adverbs, and substantives, which take an infinitive not in indirect discourse. Thus, after verbs and other words denoting _ability_, _fitness_, _necessity_ (and their opposites). Cp. [2000](21-verbal-nouns.md#s2000)-2007.

<span lang="grc">εἰκὸς σοφὸν ἄνδρα μὴ ληρεῖν</span> _it is proper for a wise man not to talk idly_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 152b">P. Th. 152b</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ̄ς ὁμοίᾱς χάριτας μὴ ἀντιδιδόναι αἰσχρόν</span> _it is disgraceful not to repay like services_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.63">T. 3.63</cite>.

##### <a id="s2714"></a> §2714

<span lang="grc"> **χρή** </span> (<span lang="grc">χρῆν, ἐχρῆν</span>) takes either <span lang="grc">μή</span> or <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>.

<span lang="grc">χρὴ μὴ καταφρονεῖν τοῦ πλήθους</span> _one must not despise the multitude_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 5.79">I. 5.79</cite>, <span lang="grc">χρῆν οὔ σ᾽ ἁμαρτάνειν</span> _thou oughtst not to do wrong_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hipp. 507">E. Hipp. 507</cite>, <span lang="grc">χρῆ δ᾽ οὔποτ᾽</span> <!-- page 616 --> <span lang="grc">εἰπεῖν οὐδέν᾽ ὄλβιον βροτῶν</span> _it is not right ever to call any son of man happy_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Andr. 100">E. And. 100</cite>.

**a.** For original <span lang="grc">οὐ χρή</span> was substituted (for emphasis) <span lang="grc">χρὴ οὐ</span>, where the <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> was still taken with <span lang="grc">χρή</span>; ultimately <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> was felt to belong with the infinitive and hence came to be separated from <span lang="grc">χρή</span>.

**b.** <span lang="grc">δεῖ</span> takes <span lang="grc">μή</span>, as <span lang="grc">μὴ ὀκνεῖν δεῖ αὐτούς</span> _they must not fear_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.120">T. 1.120</cite>. <span lang="grc">οὐ δεῖ</span> may be used for <span lang="grc">δεῖ μή</span> ([2693](#s2693)). In <span lang="grc">δεῖ οὐχ ἁπλῶς εἰπεῖν</span> _one must not speak in a general way_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 15.117">I. 15.117</cite> <span lang="grc">οὐχ</span> is adherescent. Note <span lang="grc">οἶμαι δεῖν οὐ, φημὶ χρῆναι οὐ, οἶμαι χρῆναι μή</span>.

##### <a id="s2715"></a> §2715

<span lang="grc">μή</span> is used with the infinitive in wishes and prohibitions. Thus, <span lang="grc">θεοὶ πολῖται, μή με δουλείᾱς τυχεῖν</span> _ye gods of my country, may bondage not be my lot_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Seven 253">A. Sept. 253</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἷς μὴ πελάζειν</span> _do not approach these_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 712">A. Pr. 712</cite>.

##### <a id="s2716"></a> §2716

<span lang="grc">μή</span> is used with the infinitive in oaths and protestations. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἴστω νῦν τόδε γαῖα . . . μή τί τοι αὐτῷ πῆμα κακὸν βουλευσέμεν ἄλλο</span> _let earth now know this_ (i.e. I swear by earth) _that I will not devise any harmful mischief to thine own hurt_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 5.187"><span lang="grc">ε</span> 187</cite>. Cp. [2705](#s2705) i.

##### <a id="s2717"></a> §2717

<span lang="grc">μή</span> is used with the infinitive of purpose (cp. [2719](#s2719)) or result ([2260](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/06-omegasigmatauepsilon-rarely-omegasigma-with-the-infinitive.md#s2260)). Cp. [2759](#s2759). On <span lang="grc">ἐφ᾽ ᾧ μή</span> see [2279](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/08-clauses-with-epsilonphi-omega-and-epsilonphi-omegatauepsilon.md#s2279); on <span lang="grc">ὥστε οὐ</span> see [2269](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/06-omegasigmatauepsilon-rarely-omegasigma-with-the-infinitive.md#s2269).

##### <a id="s2718"></a> §2718

<span lang="grc">μή</span> is used when the infinitive stands in _apposition_ ([1987](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1987)), and hence is like <span lang="grc">τὸ μή</span> with the infinitive. Thus, <span lang="grc">τοῦτο ἕν ἐστιν ὧν φημι, μηδένα ἂν ἐν βραχυτέροις ἐμοῦ τὰ αὐτὰ εἰπεῖν</span> _this is one of the things I maintain—that no one can say the same things in fewer words than I can_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 449c">P. G. 449c</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 173">A. Pr. 173</cite>, 431, 435, <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 497b">P. R. 497b</cite>. Such cases are not to be confused with <span lang="grc">μή</span> after _verbs of asseveration_ or _belief_ ([2725](#s2725)).

##### <a id="s2719"></a> §2719

<span lang="grc">μή</span> is used with the infinitive introduced by _verbs of will_ or _desire_ ([1991](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1991)) or by verbs expressing activity to the end that something _shall_ or _shall not be done;_ as <span lang="grc">τὴν Κέρκῡραν ἐβούλοντο μὴ προέσθαι</span> _they wished not to give up Corcyra_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.44">T. 1.44</cite>, <span lang="grc">φυλακὴν εἶχε μήτ᾽ ἐκπλεῖν . . . μηδένα μήτ᾽ ἐσπλεῖν</span> _he kept guard against any one either sailing out or in_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.69">T. 2.69</cite>.

##### <a id="s2720"></a> §2720

_Verbs of commanding_ and _exhorting_ (<span lang="grc">κελεύω, λέγω, βοῶ</span>), _asking_ (<span lang="grc">αἰτῶ, ἀξιῶ</span>), _advising_ (<span lang="grc">συμβουλεύω</span>), and other _verbs of will_ or _desire_ of like meaning, take <span lang="grc">μή</span>.

<span lang="grc">ἐκέλευε . . . μὴ ἐρεθίζειν</span> _he ordered_ him _not to provoke his wrath_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 393e">P. R. 393e</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔλεγον αὐτοῖς μὴ ἀδικεῖν</span> _they told them not to commit injustice_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.5">T. 2.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐβόων ἀλλήλοις μὴ θεῖν</span> _they shouted to each other not to run_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.19">X. A. 1.8.19</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἱ̄κέτευε μὴ κτεῖναι</span> _he besought_ them _not to kill_ him <cite data-ref="Lys. 1.25">L. 1.25</cite>, <span lang="grc">συμβουλεύω σοι . . . μὴ ἀφαιρεῖσθαι ἃ ἂν δῷς</span> _I advise you not to take away what you may have given_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.5.32">X. C. 4.5.32</cite>.

##### <a id="s2721"></a> §2721

<span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is used after _verbs of will_ or _desire_ only when it is attached to the leading verb or to some particular word; when it marks a contrast inserted parenthetically; where a compound negative takes up <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> used with the leading verb; and when <span lang="grc">οὐδείς</span> may be resolved into <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> and <span lang="grc">τὶς, οὐ</span> going with the leading verb. Examples in 2738.

### <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> AND <span lang="grc">μή</span> WITH THE INFINITIVE IN INDIRECT DISCOURSE

<!-- page 617 -->

##### <a id="s2722"></a> §2722

_Verbs of saying_ and _thinking_ take <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> with the infinitive in indirect discourse. Here <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is retained from the direct discourse.

<span lang="grc">ᾗ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀνάγκῃ</span>) <span lang="grc">φαμεν οὐδένα θεῶν οὔτε μάχεσθαι τὰ νῦν οὔτε μαχεῖσθαί ποτε</span> _we declare that no one of the gods either now contends with necessity, or ever will_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 818e">P. L. 818e</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς . . . μάχεται . . . μαχεῖται</span>), <span lang="grc">λέγοντες οὐκ εἶναι αὐτόνομοι</span> _saying that they were not independent_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.67">T. 1.67</cite>, ( = <span lang="grc">οὔκ ἐσμεν</span>), <span lang="grc">οἶμαι γὰρ ἂν οὐκ ἀχαρίστως μοι ἔχειν</span> _for I think it would not be unattended with gratitude to me_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.18">X. A. 2.3.18</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν ἔχοι</span>), <span lang="grc">ἡγήσαντο ἡμᾶς οὐ περιόψεσθαι</span> _they thought that we should not view it with indifference_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.39">T. 1.39</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐ περιόψονται</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐμοὶ δὲ δοκοῦσιν οὗτοι οὐ τὸ αἴτιον αἰτιᾶσθαι</span> _but these persons seem to me not to blame the real cause_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 329b">P. R. 329b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐνόμισεν οὐκ ἂν δύνασθαι μένειν τοὺς πολιορκοῦντας</span> _he thought the besiegers would not be able to hold their position_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.4.22">X. A. 7.4.22</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν δύναιντο</span>).

##### <a id="s2723"></a> §2723

_Verbs of saying_ and _thinking_ take <span lang="grc">μή</span> in emphatic declarations and expressions of thought which involve a wish that the utterance may hold good. So with <span lang="grc">φημί, λέγω, ἡγοῦμαι, νομίζω, οἶμαι</span>. Cp. [2725](#s2725).

<span lang="grc">φαίην δ᾽ ἂν ἔγωγε μηδενὶ μηδεμίαν εἶναι παίδευσιν παρὰ τοῦ μὴ ἀρέσκοντος</span> _but for my part I would maintain that no one gets any education from a teacher who is not pleasing_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.2.39">X. M. 1.2.39</cite>, <span lang="grc">πάντες ἐροῦσι . . . μηδὲν εἶναι κερδαλεώτερον ἀρετῆς</span> _all will say that nothing is more profitable than bravery_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.1.18">X. C. 7.1.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">τίς δ᾽ ἂν ἀνθρώπων θεῶν μὲν παῖδας ἡγοῖτο εἶναι, θεοὺς δὲ μή</span>; _who in the world would think that they were the sons of gods and not gods?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 27d">P. A. 27d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπῇσαν . . . νομίσαντες μὴ ἂν ἔτι . . . ἱκανοὶ γενέσθαι κωλῦσαι τὸν ἐπὶ τὴν θάλασσαν τειχισμόν</span> _they departed in the belief that they would no longer prove able to prevent the building of the wall to the sea_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.102">T. 6.102</cite>.

**a.** Cp. <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 155a">P. Th. 155a</cite> (<span lang="grc">φημί</span>), <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.139">T. 1.139</cite>, 6. 49, <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 346e">P. R. 346e</cite> (<span lang="grc">λέγω</span>), <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.2.41">X. M. 1.2.41</cite>, <cite data-ref="Dem. 54.44">D. 54.44</cite> (<span lang="grc">οἶμαι</span>), <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.5.59">X. C. 7.5.59</cite> (<span lang="grc">νομίζω</span>), <cite data-ref="Plat. Soph. 230c">P. Soph. 230c</cite> (<span lang="grc">διανοοῦμαι</span>).

**b.** Cases where the infinitive is in apposition, or depends on an imperative, or occurs after a condition, do not belong here.

##### <a id="s2724"></a> §2724

<span lang="grc">μή</span> with the infinitive is often found after verbs denoting an oracular response or a judicial decision actual or implied. Cp. [2725](#s2725). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀνεῖλεν ἡ Πῡθίᾱ μηδένα σοφώτερον εἶναι</span> _the Pythian prophetess made answer that no one was wiser_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 21a">P. A. 21a</cite> (in direct discourse <span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς σοφώτερός ἐστι</span>). So after <span lang="grc">κρί̄νω</span>, as <span lang="grc">ἔκρῑνε μὴ Ἀρίστωνος εἶναι Δημάρητον παῖδα</span> the Pythian prophetess _gave decisior that Demaretus was not the son of Ariston_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 6.66">Hdt. 6.66</cite>, <span lang="grc">κέκρισθε . . . μόνοι τῶν πάντων μηδενὸς ἂν κέρδους τὰ κοινὰ δίκαια τῶν Ἑλλήνων προέσθαι</span> _you are adjudged to be the only people who would not betray for lucre the common rights of the Greeks_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 6.10">D. 6.10</cite>. So <span lang="grc">καταγιγνώσκω μή</span> <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.51">T. 7.51</cite>, <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.1.36">X. C. 6.1.36</cite>.

##### <a id="s2725"></a> §2725

<span lang="grc">μή</span> is often used with verbs and other expressions of _asseveration_ and _belief_, after which we might expect <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> with the infinitive in indirect discourse. Such verbs are those signifying _to hope_, _expect_, _promise_, _put trust in, be persuaded, agree_, _testify_, _swear_, etc. <!-- page 618 --> The use of <span lang="grc">μή</span> indicates strong assurance, confidence, and resolve; and generally in regard to the _future_. Cp. [2723](#s2723).

<span lang="grc">ἐλπὶς ὑ̄μᾶς μὴ ὀφθῆναι</span> there is _hope that you will not be seen_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.4.23">X. C. 2.4.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπῑσχνοῦντο μηδὲν χαλεπὸν αὐτοὺς πείσεσθαι</span> _they promised that they should suffer no harm_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.4.5">X. H. 4.4.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">πιστεύω . . . μὴ ψεύσειν με ταύτᾱς τὰ̄ς ἀγαθὰ̄ς ἐλπίδας</span> _I trust that these good hopes will not deceive me_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.5.13">X. C. 1.5.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">θαυμάζω ὅπως ἐπείσθησαν Ἀθηναῖοι Σωκράτην περὶ θεοὺς μὴ σωφρονεῖν</span> _I wonder how the Athenians were persuaded that Socrates did not hold temperate opinions regarding the gods_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.1.20">X. M. 1.1.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁμολογεῖ μὴ μετεῖναί οἱ μακρολογίᾱς</span> _he acknowledges that he cannot make a long speech_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 336b">P. Pr. 336b</cite>, <span lang="grc">αὐτὸς ἑαυτοῦ καταμαρτυρεῖ μὴ ἐξ ἐκείνου γεγενῆσθαι</span> _he proves by his own testimony that he is not his son_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 40.47">D. 40.47</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὤμοσεν ἦ μὴν μὴ εἰ῀ναί οἱ υἱὸν ἄλλον μηδὲ γενέσθαι πώποτε</span> _he swore that he had no other son and that none other had ever been born to him_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.126">And. 1.126</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὤμνυε . . . μηδὲν εἰρηκέναι</span> _he swore that he had said nothing_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.119">D. 21.119</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὀμοῦμαι μήποτ᾽ . . . ἀλεξήσειν κακὸν ἦμαρ</span> _I will swear that I will never ward off the evil day_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 21.373"><span lang="grc">Φ</span> 373</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Wasps 1047">Ar. Vesp. 1047</cite>, 1281, <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.90">And. 1.90</cite>, Lyc. 76. With <span lang="grc">ὄμνῡμι</span> the infinitive may refer to the present, past, or future.

##### <a id="s2726"></a> §2726

Such verbs are _hope_ <span lang="grc"> **ἐλπίζω** </span>; _expect_ <span lang="grc">**ἐλπίζω**, **προσδοκῶ**, **δοκῶ**, **οἴομαι**, **εἰκός ἐστι**</span>; _promise_ <span lang="grc">**ὑπισχνοῦμαι**, **ἐπαγγέλλομαι**</span>; _swear_ <span lang="grc"> **ὄμνῡμι** </span>; _agree_ <span lang="grc">**ὁμολογῶ**, **συγχωρῶ**</span>; _pledge_ <span lang="grc"> **ἐγγυῶμαι** </span>; _put trust in_ <span lang="grc"> **πιστεύω** </span>; _am persuaded_ <span lang="grc"> **πέπεισμαι** </span>; _testify_ <span lang="grc"> **μαρτυρῶ** </span>; _repudiate_ <span lang="grc"> **ἀναίνομαι** </span>; _threaten_ <span lang="grc"> **ἀπειλῶ** </span>, etc.

**a.** <span lang="grc">μή</span> is regular after _verbs of promising;_ common after _verbs of hoping_ and _swearing_. With <span lang="grc">ὄμνῡμι, πιστεύω, πείθομαι, μαρτυρῶ</span>, etc. there is an idea of _deprecation_.

##### <a id="s2727"></a> §2727

<span lang="grc"> **ἐπίσταμαι** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **οἶδα** </span> usually take <span lang="grc">μή</span> when they denote confident belief ( = _I warrant_ from what I know; cp. <span lang="grc">πιστεύω μή, ὄμνῡμι μή</span>). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐξίσταμαι μή του τόδ᾽ ἀγλάϊσμα πλὴν κείνου μολεῖν</span> _I assure you this fair offering has not come from any one save from him_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 908">S. El. 908</cite> (cp. Ant. [1092](14-adjectives.md#s1092)). In <span lang="grc">τοσοῦτόν γ᾽ οἶδα μήτε μ᾽ ἂν νόσον μήτ᾽ ἄλλο πέρσαι μηδέν</span> _so much at least I know—that neither sickness nor aught else can undo me_ (<cite data-ref="Soph. OT 1455">S. O. T. 1455</cite>) the infinitive may be appositional ([2718](#s2718)). Cases of <span lang="grc">ἴσθι μή</span> (_be assured_ = _I assure you_) may have <span lang="grc">μή</span> by reason of the imperative ([2737 a](#s2737)). So <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 1329">S. Ph. 1329</cite>.

## <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> AND <span lang="grc">μή</span> WITH THE PARTICIPLE

##### <a id="s2728"></a> §2728

The participle has <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> when it states a fact, <span lang="grc">μή</span> when it states a condition. On <span lang="grc">μή</span> due to the force of the leading verb, see [2737](#s2737).

<span lang="grc">οὐ πιστεύων</span> _since_ (_as_, _when_, etc.) _he does not believe_, <span lang="grc">μὴ πιστεύων</span> _if he does not believe_, <span lang="grc">ἀνέβη ἐπὶ τὰ ὄρη οὐδενὸς κωλύ̄οντος</span> _he went up on the mountains since no one hindered him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.22">X. A. 1.2.22</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν δύναιο μὴ καμὼν εὐδαιμονεῖν</span> _thou canst not be happy if thou hast not toiled_ E. fr. 461, <span lang="grc">ὡς ἡδὺ τὸ ζῆν μὴ φθονούσης τῆς τύχης</span> _how sweet_ is _life if fortune is not envious_ Men. Sent. 563.

**a.** <span lang="grc">μή</span> with the articular participle is the abridged equivalent of a conditional relative sentence. Thus, in <span lang="grc">ὁ μὴ ταῦτα ποιῶν ἄδικός ἐστι, ὁ μὴ ποιῶν</span> is virtually the generic <span lang="grc">ὃς ἂν μὴ ποιῇ</span> or <span lang="grc">ὅστις μὴ ποιεῖ</span> compressed into a noun.

##### <a id="s2729"></a> §2729

<span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is used with a supplementary participle (in indirect discourse) in <!-- page 619 --> agreement with a noun (or pronoun, expressed or unexpressed) depending on a _verb of knowing_, _showing_, _seeing_, _perceiving_, etc. ([2106](22-the-participle.md#s2106)-[2115](22-the-participle.md#s2115)); and also with such supplementary participles (not in indirect discourse) after _verbs of emotion_ ([2100](22-the-participle.md#s2100)), etc. In most such cases <span lang="grc">ὅτι οὐ</span> might have been used.

<span lang="grc">οὐδένα γὰρ οἶδα μῑσοῦντα τοὺς ἐπαινοῦντας</span> _for I know of no one who dislikes his admirers_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.6.33">X. M. 2.6.33</cite>, <span lang="grc">φανερὸν πᾶσιν ἐποίησαν οὐκ ἰδίᾳ πολεμοῦντες</span> _they made it clear to all that they were not waging war for their own interests_ Lyc. 50, <span lang="grc">ὁρῶσι τοὺς πρεσβυτέρους οὐ . . . ἀπιόντας</span> _they see that their elders do not depart_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.2.8">X. C. 1.2.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς μήποθ᾽ εὕρῃ . . . οὐδὲν ἐλλειφθέν</span> _no one will ever find that anything has been left undone_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.246">D. 18.246</cite>; <span lang="grc">Κύ̄ρῳ ἥδετο οὐ δυναμένῳ σῑγᾶν</span> _he rejoiced that Cyrus was unable to remain silent_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.15">X. C. 1.4.15</cite>.

##### <a id="s2730"></a> §2730

<span lang="grc"> **ἐπίσταμαι** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **οἶδα** </span> denoting confident belief may take <span lang="grc">μή</span> for <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔξοιδα φύσει σε μὴ πεφῡκότα τοιαῦτα φωνεῖν κακά</span> _well do I know that by nature thou art not adapted to utter such guile_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 79">S. Ph. 79</cite>; cp. <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 656">S. O. C. 656</cite>, <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.76">T. 1.76</cite>, 2. 17. This use of <span lang="grc">μή</span> is analogous to that with the infinitive ([2727](#s2727)).

##### <a id="s2731"></a> §2731

<span lang="grc">μή</span> is used when the reason for an action is regarded as the condition under which it takes place; as <span lang="grc">οὐ τοῦ πλέονος μὴ στερισκόμενοι χάριν ἔχουσιν</span> _they are not grateful at not being deprived of the greater part_ of their rights <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.77">T. 1.77</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ στερίσκοντο</span>).

##### <a id="s2732"></a> §2732

The participle with <span lang="grc">**ὡς**, **ὥσπερ**, **ἅτε**, **οἷον**, **οἷα**</span> ([2085](22-the-participle.md#s2085)-[2087](22-the-participle.md#s2087)) has <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>; as <span lang="grc">ἐθορυβεῖτε ὡς οὐ ποιήσοντες ταῦτα</span> _you made a disturbance by way of declaring that you did not intend to do this_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.73">L. 12.73</cite>. The use of <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> shows that there is nothing conditional in the use of <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> though it is often translated by _as if_. <span lang="grc">μή</span> occurs only after an imperative or a conditional word ([2737](#s2737)).

##### <a id="s2733"></a> §2733

Participles of opposition or concession ([2083](22-the-participle.md#s2083)) take <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>; as <span lang="grc">πείθου γυναιξὶ καίπερ οὐ στέργων ὅμως</span> _hearken to women albeit thou likest it not_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Seven 712">A. Sept. 712</cite>.

##### <a id="s2734"></a> §2734

The participle with the article has <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> when a definite person or thing is meant, but <span lang="grc">μή</span> when the idea is indefinite and virtually conditional (_whoever_, _whatever_); and when a person or thing is to be characterized (_of such a sort, one who;_ 2705 g). Cp. [2052](22-the-participle.md#s2052).

<span lang="grc">οἱ οὐκ ὄντες</span> _the dead_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.44">T. 2.44</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ οὐκ ἐθέλοντες</span> the particular _persons_ (or _party_) _who are unwilling_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 6.26">Ant. 6.26</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ οὐ βουλόμενοι ταῦτα οὕτως ἔχειν</span> _the party of opposition_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.9">And. 1.9</cite>; <span lang="grc">οἱ μὴ δυνάμενοι</span> _any who are unable_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.5.11">X. A. 4.5.11</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">οἵτινες μὴ δύνανται</span> or <span lang="grc">ὅσοι ἂν μὴ δύνωνται</span>), <span lang="grc">ὁ μὴ δαρεὶς ἄνθρωπος οὐ παιδεύεται</span> _he who gets no flogging gets no training_ Men. Sent. 422, <span lang="grc">ὁ μὴ λέγων ἃ φρονεῖ</span> _the man who does not say what he thinks_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.282">D. 18.282</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁ μηδὲν ἀδικῶν οὐδενὸς δεῖται νόμου</span> _he who does no wrong needs no law_ Antiph. 288.

## <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> AND <span lang="grc">μή</span> WITH SUBSTANTIVES AND ADJECTIVES USED SUBSTANTIVELY

##### <a id="s2735"></a> §2735

<span lang="grc">οὐ</span> and <span lang="grc">μή</span> are used with substantives and substantivized adjectives with the same difference as with participles. Here the generic <span lang="grc">μή</span> is much more common than <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>.

<span lang="grc">ἡ τῶν γεφῡρῶν . . . οὐ διάλυσις</span> _the non-destruction of the bridges_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.137">T. 1.137</cite>, <!-- page 620 --> <span lang="grc">κατὰ τὴν τῶν χωρίων ἀλλήλοις οὐκ ἀπόδοσιν</span> _because of their non-surrender of the places to each other_ 5. 35 ( = <span lang="grc">ὅτι οὐκ ἀπέδοσαν</span>), <span lang="grc">διὰ τὴν τῶν Κορινθίων οὐκέτι ἐπαναγωγήν</span> _because the Corinthians no longer sailed out against them_ 7. 36. Cp. _non-regardance_ (Shakesp.), _nonresidences_ (Milton). So even with concrete nouns: <span lang="grc">οἱ οὐχὶ δοῦλοι</span> E. fr. 831.

<span lang="grc">ἡ μὴ ἐμπειρίᾱ</span> _lack of experience_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Eccl. 115">Ar. Eccl. 115</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁ μὴ ἰ̄ᾱτρός</span> _he who is not a physician_ (_the non-physician_) <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 459b">P. G. 459b</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ μὴ πλούσιοι</span> _whoever are not rich_ (_the non-rich_) <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 330a">P. R. 330a</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔστιν ἐν τοῖς μὴ καλοῖς βουλεύμασιν οὐδ᾽ ἐλπίς</span> _in schemes that are unwise there is no place even for hope_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Trach. 725">S. Tr. 725</cite>.

**a.** The use of the negative here compensates for the absence of negative compounds. Cp. <span lang="grc">αἱ οὐκ ἀναγκαῖαι πόσεις</span> _unnecessary potations_ X. R. <cite data-ref="Lys. 5.4">L. 5.4</cite>.

## <span lang="grc">οὐδείς, μηδείς</span>

##### <a id="s2736"></a> §2736

<span lang="grc">οὐδείς, οὐδέν</span> denote that which is actually non-existent or of no account; <span lang="grc">μηδείς, μηδέν</span> denote that which is merely thought of as nonexistent or of no account. Both are used as the opposite of <span lang="grc">τὶς</span> or <span lang="grc">τὶ</span> (<span lang="grc">εἶναι</span>) _to be somebody_ (_something_, cp. [1269](17-pronouns.md#s1269)). The neuter forms are often used of persons; <span lang="grc">τὸ μηδέν</span> (indeclinable) is used of persons and things.

<span lang="grc">ὦ νῦν μὲν οὐδείς, αὔριον δ᾽ ὑπέρμεγας</span> _oh thou who art now a nobody_ (an actual fact), _but to-morrow exceeding great_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Kn. 158">Ar. Eq. 158</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὄντες οὐδένες</span> _being nobodies_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Andr. 700">E. And. 700</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ ἠξίου τοὺς μηδένας</span> _for he was not wont to esteem_ (those whom he regarded as) _nobodies_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 1114">S. Aj. 1114</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ μηδὲν εἰς οὐδὲν ῥέπει</span> _what was thought to be nothing_ now _inclines_ (shows itself) to be _actually nothing_ E. fr. 532, <span lang="grc">ὅτ᾽ οὐδὲν ὢν τοῦ μηδὲν ἀνέστης ὕπερ</span> _when though naught thyself_ (a fact) _thou hast stood up for him who is as naught_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 1231">S. Aj. 1231</cite>. So <span lang="grc">τὸ οὐδέν</span> _zero_, actually nothing, <span lang="grc">τὸ μηδέν</span> abstract nonentity.

**a.** The construction may influence the choice between <span lang="grc">οὐδείς</span> and <span lang="grc">μηδείς</span>; as <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν δοκῶσί τι εἶναι μηδὲν ὄντες, ὀνειδίζετε αὐτοῖς</span> _rebuke them if they think they are something when_ in reality _they are nothing_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 41e">P. A. 41e</cite>. Cp. [2737 b](#s2737).

## APPARENT EXCHANGE OF <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> AND <span lang="grc">μή</span>

##### <a id="s2737"></a> §2737

Where <span lang="grc">μή</span> is used when we expect <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> the negative expression usually depends on a verb that either has <span lang="grc">μή</span> or would have it, if negatived.

**a.** After imperatives. Thus, <span lang="grc">σάφ᾽ ἴσθι μή με θωπεύσοντά σε</span> _know well that I shall not fawn upon thee_ E. Heracl. 983, <span lang="grc">νόμιζε μηδὲν εἶναι τῶν ἀνθρωπίνων βέβαιον εἶναι</span> _consider nothing in human life to be secure_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.42">I. 1.42</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">μὴ νόμιζέ τι κτλ</span>.), <span lang="grc">ὡς οὖν μὴ μόνον κρί̄νοντες, ἀλλὰ καὶ θεωρούμενοι, οὕτω τὴν ψῆφον φέρετε</span> _cast your ballots then in the belief not only that you are passing judgment but also that the eyes of the world are upon you_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.247">Aes. 3.247</cite> (cp. [2732](#s2732)). See also [2086 b](22-the-participle.md#s2086).

**b.** After conditional expressions. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ δέ τις . . . νομίζει τι μὴ ἱκανῶς εἰρῆσθαι</span> _but if any one thinks some point has not been sufficiently mentioned_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.70">And. 1.70</cite>, <span lang="grc">λύ̄σετε δὲ οὐδὲ τὰ̄ς Λακεδαιμονίων σπονδὰ̄ς δεχόμενοι</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν δέχησθε</span>) <span lang="grc">ἡμᾶς μηδετ&lt;\*&gt;´οων ὄντας ξυμμάχους</span> _and by receiving us, who are allies of neither_, <!-- page 621 --> _you will not be violating the treaty with the Lacedaemonians either_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.35">T. 1.35</cite>. Cp. [2736 a](#s2736).

**c.** Other cases: <span lang="grc">κελεύει μεῖναι ἐπὶ τοῦ ποταμοῦ μὴ διαβάντας</span> _he ordered them to remain by the river without crossing_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.3.28">X. A. 4.3.28</cite> (here <span lang="grc">μεῖναι</span>, if negatived, would take <span lang="grc">μή</span>, [2720](#s2720)), <span lang="grc">ὑπέσχετο εἰρήνην ποιήσειν μήτε ὅμηρα δοὺς μήτε τὰ τείχη καθελών</span> _he promised that he would bring peace about without giving hostages or destroying the walls_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.68">L. 12.68</cite> (here <span lang="grc">ποιήσειν</span>, if negatived, would take <span lang="grc">μή</span>, [2725](#s2725)).

N.—But <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> may assert itself even under the above circumstances; as <span lang="grc">μὴ ὅ γε οὐ χρὴ ποίει</span> _don't do what is_ really _wrong_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyd. 307b">P. Eu. 307b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἢ ἀφί̄ετέ με ἢ μὴ ἀφί̄ετε ὡς ἐμοῦ οὐκ ἂν ποιήσαντος ἄλλα</span> _either acquit me or do not acquit me in the knowledge that I should not act otherwise_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 30b">P. A. 30b</cite> (cp. [2732](#s2732)), <span lang="grc">εἰ νομίζεις οὐχ ὑφέξειν τὴν δίκην</span> _if thou thinkest not to suffer the penalty_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 551">S. O. T. 551</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐχ ὑφέξω</span>), <span lang="grc">εἰ γνωσθησόμεθα ξυνελθόντες μέν, ἀμύ̄νεσθαι δὲ οὐ</span> (some Mss.) <span lang="grc">τολμῶντες</span> _if we shall be known to have come together, and yet not to have the courage to avenge ourselves_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.124">T. 1.124</cite> (it would be said of them: <span lang="grc">ξυνῆλθον μέν, ἀμύ̄νεσθαι δὲ οὐκ ἐτόλμων</span>, a contrast, cp. [2690](#s2690)).

**d.** On <span lang="grc">μή</span> in questions where we might expect <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>, see [2676 b](33-indirect-dependent-questions.md#s2676).

##### <a id="s2738"></a> §2738

<span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is sometimes used where we expect <span lang="grc">μή</span>.

**a.** Where <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> stands in a clause introduced by <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> or other words after which <span lang="grc">μή</span> might be expected ([2698](#s2698)). Thus, <span lang="grc">ὄφρα καὶ οὐκ ἐθέλων τις ἀναγκαι´ῃ πολεμίζοι</span> _that every one must of necessity fight even though he would not_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 4.300"><span lang="grc">Δ</span> 300</cite> (cp. [2692](#s2692) a).

**b.** Where <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> goes strictly with the leading verb though it stands with the infinitive. Thus, <span lang="grc">βουλοίμην δ᾽ ἃν οὐκ εἶναι τόδε</span> _I would fain it were not so_ (_I should not wish that this were so_) <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 73">E. Med. 73</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὀμώμοκεν οὐ χαριεῖσθαι . . . ἀλλὰ δικάσειν κατὰ τοὺς νόμους</span> _he has sworn, not that he will show favour, but that he will judge according to the laws_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 35c">P. A. 35c</cite> (some explain this as the <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> of direct discourse).

**c.** Where <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> in a contrast goes closely with a following word or words, or stands in a partial parenthesis. Thus, <span lang="grc">κελεύων οὐκ ἐν τῇ ἐκκλησίᾳ ἀλλ᾽ ἐν τῷ θεά̄τρῳ τὴν ἀνάρρησιν γίγνεσθαι</span> (he has violated the law) _in demanding that the proclamation be made not in the Assembly but in the theatre_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.204">Aes. 3.204</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁμολογοίην ἂν ἔγωγε οὐ κατὰ τούτους εἶναι ῥήτωρ</span> _I should acknowledge that I am an orator, but not after their style_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 17b">P. A. 17b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑ̄μᾶς νῦν ἀξιοῦντες οὐ ξυμμαχεῖν, ἀλλὰ ξυναδικεῖν</span> _demanding that you should be, not their allies, but their partners in wrong-doing_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.39">T. 1.39</cite>.

**d.** When a compound negative with the infinitive repeats <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> used with the leading verb. Thus, (<span lang="grc">ὁ νόμος</span>) <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἐᾷ εἰσιέναι, οὗ ἂν ᾖ ὁ τετελευτηκώς, οὐδεμίαν γυναῖκα</span> _the law does not permit any women to enter where the dead may be_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 43.63">D. 43.63</cite>.

**e.** When <span lang="grc">οὐδείς</span> may be resolved into <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> and <span lang="grc">τὶς, οὐ</span> going with the leading verb. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐδενὸς</span> ( = <span lang="grc">οὔ τινος</span>) <span lang="grc">ἁμαρτεῖν . . . δίκαιός ἐστιν</span> _there is nothing he deserves to miss_ Ant. 4. a. 6 ( = he does not deserve to miss anything), <span lang="grc">ἀξιῶ ἐγὼ ὧν ὀμωμόκατε παραβῆναι οὐδέν</span> _I ask that you do not break any of the conditions to which you have sworn_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.4.42">X. H. 2.4.42</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀξιῶ . . . παραβῆναι τι</span>). Cp. <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 88">S. Ph. 88</cite>.

## <span lang="grc">μή</span> AND <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> WITH THE INFINITIVE

<!-- page 622 -->

### REDUNDANT OR SYMPATHETIC NEGATIVE

#### I. With the Infinitive depending on Verbs of Negative Meaning

##### <a id="s2739"></a> §2739

Verbs and expressions of negative meaning, such as _deny_, _refuse_, _hinder_, _forbid_, _avoid_, often take the infinitive with a redundant <span lang="grc">μή</span> to confirm the negative idea of the leading verb.

With this compare: “First he denied you had in him no right” (Shakesp., Com. of Er. 4. 2. 7); and “La pluie . . . empêche qu'on ne se promène” (Racine); “Verbot ihnen Jesus, dass sie Niemand sagen sollten” (St. Mark 9. 9).

<span lang="grc">καταρνῇ μὴ δεδρᾱκέναι τάδε</span>; _dost thou deny that thou hast done this?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 442">S. Ant. 442</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀποκωλῦσαι τοὺς Ἕλληνας μὴ ἐλθεῖν</span> _to hinder the Greeks from coming_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.4.24">X. A. 6.4.24</cite>, <span lang="grc">κήρῦκα προέπεμψεν αὐτοῖς . . . ἀπεροῦντα μὴ πλεῖν</span> _they sent a herald to forbid them to sail_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.29">T. 1.29</cite>, <span lang="grc">εὐλαβήσεσθε μὴ πολλῶν ἐναντίον λέγειν</span> _you will beware of speaking in public_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyd. 304a">P. Eu. 304a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπέσχοντο μὴ ἐπὶ τὴν ἑκατέρων γῆν στρατεῦσαι</span> _they abstained from marching upon each other's territory_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.25">T. 5.25</cite>.

##### <a id="s2740"></a> §2740

The redundant <span lang="grc">μή</span> is used after <span lang="grc"> **ἀμφιλέγω** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **ἀμφισβητῶ** </span> _dispute_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀνατίθεμαι** </span> _retract an opinion_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀντιλέγω** </span> _speak against_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀπαγορεύω** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **ἀπειπεῖν** </span> _forbid_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀπιστῶ** </span> _doubt_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀπογιγνώσκω** </span> _abandon an intention_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀποκρύπτομαι** </span> _conceal_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀπολύ̄ω** </span> _acquit_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀποστερῶ** </span> _deprive_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀποστρέφω** </span> _divert_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀποχειροτονῶ** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **ἀποψηφίζομαι** </span> _vote against_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀρνοῦμαι** </span> (and compounds, and <span lang="grc">**ἄπαρνός εἰμι**, **ἔξαρνός εἰμι**</span>) _deny_, <span lang="grc"> **διαμάχομαι** </span> _refuse_, <span lang="grc"> **εἴργω** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **ἐμποδών εἰμι** </span> _prevent_, <span lang="grc"> **ἐναντιοῦμαι** </span> _oppose_, <span lang="grc"> **εὐλαβοῦμαι** </span> _beware of_, <span lang="grc"> **ἔχω** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **ἀπέχω** </span> _prevent_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀντέχω, ἀπέχομαι, ἐπέχω, κατέχω** </span> _abstain from_, <span lang="grc"> **κωλύ̄ω** </span> (and compounds) _hinder_, <span lang="grc"> **μεταβουλεύομαι** </span> _alter one's plans_, <span lang="grc"> **μεταγιγνώκω** </span> _change one's mind_, <span lang="grc"> **ὄκνον παρέχω** </span> _make hesilate_, <span lang="grc"> **φεύγω** </span> (and compounds) _escape_, _avoid_, _disclaim_, <span lang="grc"> **φυλάττομαι** </span> _guard against_, etc.

##### <a id="s2741"></a> §2741

Also after the following verbs: <span lang="grc"> **ἀπαυδῶ** </span> _forbid_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀπεύχομαι** </span> _deprecor_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀποδοκεῖ** </span> _resolve not_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀπροσδόκητός εἰμι** </span> _do not expect_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀφαιροῦμαι** </span> _prevent_, <span lang="grc"> **ἀφί̄ημι** </span> _acquit_, <span lang="grc"> **δέδοικα** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **φοβοῦμαι** </span> _fear_, <span lang="grc"> **ἐρύ̄κω** </span> _hinder_, <span lang="grc"> **καταδεῖ** </span> _lack_, <span lang="grc"> **μεταδοκεῖ μοι** </span> _change one's mind_, <span lang="grc"> **παύω** </span> _put an end to_, <span lang="grc"> **ῥύομαι** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **σῴζω** </span> _save from_, <span lang="grc"> **ὑπεκτρέχω** </span> _escape from_, <span lang="grc"> **ὑφί̄εμαι** </span> _give up_, etc.

##### <a id="s2742"></a> §2742

When a _verb of denying_, _refusing_, _hindering_, _forbidding_, etc., is itself negatived, either directly or by appearing in a question expecting a negative answer, the infinitive has <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span>. Here both the introductory clause and the dependent clause have virtually an affirmative sense.

<span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς πώποτ᾽ ἀντεῖπεν μὴ οὐ καλῶς ἔχειν αὐτούς</span> (<span lang="grc">τοὺς νόμους</span>) _no one ever denied that they_ (the laws) _were excellent_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 24.24">D. 24.24</cite>, <span lang="grc">τίνα οἴει ἀπαρνήσεσθαι μὴ οὐχὶ καὶ αὐτὸν ἐπίστασθαι τὰ δίκαια</span>; _who_, _think you, will deny that he too understands what is just?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 461c">P. G. 461c</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς ἀπαρνήσεται</span>). But <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> is not used after <span lang="grc">οὔ φημι, οὐκ ἐῶ, οὐκ ἐθέλω</span> ([2692 a](#s2692)).

**a.** <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> with the infinitive here, and elsewhere, is used only when the introductory word or words has an actual or a virtual negative. Since, in <span lang="grc">ἀρνοῦμαι μὴ ταῦτα δοᾶσαι</span> _I deny that I did this_, <span lang="grc">μή</span> confirms the negative idea in <span lang="grc">ἀρνοῦμαι</span>, so <!-- page 623 --> in <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀρνοῦμαι μὴ οὐ ταῦτα δρᾶσαι</span> _I do not deny that I did this_, <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> after the strengthening <span lang="grc">μή</span> confirms the <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> prefixed to the leading verb. Cp. “Je ne nie pas que je ne sois infiniment flatté” (Voltaire). In the first sentence <span lang="grc">μή</span> repeats the ‘negative result’ of <span lang="grc">ἀρνοῦμαι</span> (single sympathetic negative, untranslatable); in the second sentence <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is repeated with the infinitive to sum up the effect of <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀρνοῦμαι</span> (double sympathetic negative; both untranslatable). After verbs negative in meaning (_deny_, etc.) <span lang="grc">μή</span> and <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> cannot be translated in modern English (see [2739](#s2739)). After verbs not negative in character but preceded by a negative, and after virtually negative expressions, <span lang="grc">μή</span> or <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> has a negative force ([2745](#s2745), [2746](#s2746)).

**b.** <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> with the infinitive regularly indicates a certain pressure of interest on the part of the person involved.

##### <a id="s2743"></a> §2743

After _deny_, _speak against, doubt_, etc., followed by <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> or <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span>, a redundant <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is often inserted. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὡς μὲν οὐκ ἀληθῆ ταῦτ᾽ ἐστίν, οὐχ ἕξετ᾽ ἀντιλέγειν</span> _that this is true you will not be able to deny_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.31">D. 8.31</cite>.

**a.** Here the <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> clause is an internal accusative (accusative of content) after <span lang="grc">ἀντιλέγειν</span>. Originally the meaning seems to have been ‘you will not be able to deny in this way—this is not true’ where <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is not redundant.

##### <a id="s2744"></a> §2744

_Summary of Constructions after Verbs of Hindering, etc_.

After verbs signifying (or suggesting) _to hinder_ and the like, the infinitive admits the article <span lang="grc">τό</span> or <span lang="grc">τοῦ</span> (the ablatival genitive, [1392](18-the-cases/05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1392)). Hence we have a variety of constructions, which are here classed under formal types. The simple infinitive is more closely connected with the leading verb than the infinitive with <span lang="grc">τὸ μή</span> or <span lang="grc">τὸ μὴ οὐ</span>, which often denotes the result (cp. <span lang="grc">ὥστε μή</span>) of the action of the leading verb and is either an accusative of respect or a simple object infinitive. The genitive of the infinitive is very rare with <span lang="grc">κωλύ̄ω</span> and its compounds.

**a.** Some scholars regard the infinitive with the negative as an internal accusative, not as a simple object infinitive; and the infinitive without the negative as an external accusative.

\1. <span lang="grc"> **εἴργει με μὴ γράφειν** </span> (the usual construction: examples [2739](#s2739)).

\2. <span lang="grc"> **εἴργει με γράφειν** </span> (less common). Since the redundant <span lang="grc">μή</span> is not obligatory, we have the simple infinitive as object ([1989](21-verbal-nouns.md#s1989)), as <span lang="grc">εἰ τοῦτό τις εἴργει δρᾶν ὄκνος</span> _if some scruple prevents_ us _from doing this_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Soph. 242a">P. Soph. 242a</cite>, <span lang="grc">δ̀ν θανε&lt;\*&gt;ῖν ἐρρῡσάμην</span> _whom I saved from death_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Alc. 11">E. Alc. 11</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ θεῶν ἡμᾶς ὅρκοι κωλύ̄ουσι πολεμίους εἶναι ἀλλήλοις</span> _the oaths sworn in the name of the gods prevent our being enemies to each other_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.7">X. A. 2.5.7</cite>, and so usually with <span lang="grc">κωλύ̄ω</span> (cp. [2744](#s2744). 7).

\3. <span lang="grc"> **εἴργει με τὸ μὴ γράφειν** </span> (rather common; cp. [1](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s1)): <span lang="grc">εἶργον τὸ μὴ . . . κακουργεῖν</span> _they prevented_ them _from doing damage_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.1">T. 3.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἷοί τε ἦσαν κατέχειν τὸ μὴ δακρύ̄ειν</span> _they were able to restrain their weeping_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 117c">P. Ph. 117c</cite>.

\4. <span lang="grc"> **εἴργει με τὸ γράφειν** </span> (not uncommon; cp. [2](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s2)): <span lang="grc">ἐπέσχον τὸ εὐθέως τοῖς Ἀθηναίοις ἐπιχειρεῖν</span> _they refrained from immediately attacking the Athenians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.33">T. 7.33</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔστιν τις, ὅς σε κωλύ̄σει τὸ δρᾶν</span> _there is some one who will prevent thee from the deed_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 1241">S. Ph. 1241</cite>.

\5. <span lang="grc"> **εἴργει με τοῦ μὴ γράφειν** </span>, with the ablatival genitive, 1392 (not so common as 3): <span lang="grc">πᾶς γὰρ ἀσκὸς δύο ἄνδρας ἕξει τοῦ μὴ καταδῦναι</span> _for each skin-bag will pre-_ <!-- page 624 --> _vent two men from sinking_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.5.11">X. A. 3.5.11</cite>. Other cases are: <cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.86">Hdt. 1.86</cite>, <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.76">T. 1.76</cite>, <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.4.13">X. C. 2.4.13</cite>, 2. 4. 23, 3. 3. 31, <cite data-ref="Isoc. 7.17">I. 7.17</cite>, 12. 80, 15. 122, <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 637c">P. L. 637c</cite>, 832 b, <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.149">D. 23.149</cite>, 33. 25. Observe that this idiom does not have the logical meaning ‘from _not_,’ which we should expect. Some write <span lang="grc">τὸ μή</span> or <span lang="grc">μή</span> alone.

\6. <span lang="grc"> **εἴργει με τοῦ γράφειν** </span> (not common, and very rare with <span lang="grc">κωλύ̄ω</span>, as <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.6.2">X. A. 1.6.2</cite>): <span lang="grc">τοῦ δὲ δρᾱπετεύειν δεσμοῖς ἀπείργουσι</span>; _do they prevent_ their slaves _from running away by fetters?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.1.16">X. M. 2.1.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπέσχομεν τοῦ δακρύ̄ειν</span> _we desisted from weeping_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 117e">P. Ph. 117e</cite> (cp. [3](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s3)).

\7. <span lang="grc"> **οὐκ εἴργει με γράφειν** </span> (not very common, but more often with <span lang="grc">οὐ κωλύ̄ω</span>; cp. [2](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s2)): <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ διακωλύ̄ουσι ποιεῖν ὧν ἂν ἐπιθῡμῇς</span>; _nor will they prevent you from doing what you desire?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lysis 207e">P. Lys. 207e</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί κωλύ̄ει</span> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν κ.</span>) <span lang="grc">καὶ τὰ ἄκρα ἡμῖν κελεύειν Κῦρον προκαταλαβεῖν</span>; _what hinders our ordering Cyrus to take also the heights in advance for us?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.16">X. A. 1.3.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">ταῦτά τινες οὐκ ἐξαρνοῦνται πρά̄ττειν</span> _certain people do not deny that they are doing these things_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.250">Aes. 3.250</cite>.

\8. <span lang="grc"> **οὐκ εἴργει με μὴ οὐ γράφειν** </span> (the regular construction): <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀμφισβητῶ μὴ οὐχὶ σὲ σοφώτερον ἢ ἐμέ</span> _I do not dispute that you are wiser than I_ P. Hipp. Minor 369 d, <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν ἐδύνατο ἀντέχειν μὴ οὐ χαρίζεσθαι</span> _he was not able to resist granting the favour_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.2">X. C. 1.4.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί ἐμποδὼν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν ἐμποδών</span>) <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐχὶ . . . ὑβριζομένους ἀποθανεῖν</span>; _what hinders our being put to death ignominiously?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.13">X. A. 3.1.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί δῆτα μέλλεις μὴ οὐ γεγωνίσκειν τὸ πᾶν</span>; _why pray dost thou hesitate to declare the whole?_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 627">A. Pr. 627</cite>.

\9. <span lang="grc"> **οὐκ εἴργει με τὸ μὴ γράφειν** </span> (since occasionally the sympathetic <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is not added; cp. [3](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s3)): <span lang="grc">καὶ φημὶ δρᾶσαι κοὐκ ἀπαρνοῦμαι τὸ μή</span> (<span lang="grc">δρᾶσαι</span>) _I both assent that I did the deed and do not deny that I did it_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 443">S. Ant. 443</cite>, <span lang="grc">τίς . . . σοῦ ἀπελείφθη τὸ μή σοι ἀκολουθεῖν</span>; _who failed to follow you?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.1.25">X. C. 5.1.25</cite>.

\10. <span lang="grc"> **οὐκ εἴργει με τὸ μὴ οὐ γράφειν** </span> (very common; cp. [8](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s8)): <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἐναντιώσομαι τὸ μὴ οὐ γεγωνεῖν πᾶν</span> _I will not refuse to declare all_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 786">A. Pr. 786</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ μὲν οὖν μὴ οὐχὶ ἡδέα εἶναι τὰ ἡδέα λὁγος οὐδεὶς ἀμφισβητεῖ</span> _no argument disputes that sweet things are sweet_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phileb. 13a">P. Phil. 13a</cite>.

Very unusual constructions are

\11. <span lang="grc"> **οὐκ εἴργει τὸ γράφειν** </span> (<span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν ἀρνοίμην τὸ δρᾶν</span> _I will not refuse the deed_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 118">S. Ph. 118</cite>).

\12. <span lang="grc"> **οὐκ εἴργει μὴ γράφειν** </span> (<span lang="grc">οὔτ᾽ ἠμφεσβήτησε μὴ σχεῖν</span> _neither did he deny that he had_ the money <cite data-ref="Dem. 27.15">D. 27.15</cite>).

\13. <span lang="grc"> **οὐκ εἴργει τοῦ μὴ οὐ γράφειν** </span> (once only: <cite data-ref="Eur. Hipp. 48">E. Hipp. 48</cite>, where <span lang="grc">τὸ μὴ οὐ</span> is read by some).

On the negative after <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span>, see [2759](#s2759).

#### II._ <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> _with the Infinitive depending on Negatived Verbs

##### <a id="s2745"></a> §2745

Any infinitive that would take <span lang="grc">μή</span>, takes <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> (with a negative force), if dependent on a negatived verb. Here <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is the sympathetic negative and is untranslatable.

<span lang="grc">οὐκ ἂν πιθοίμην μὴ οὐ τάδ᾽ ἐκμαθεῖν σαφῶς</span> _I cannot consent not to learn this exactly as it is_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 1065">S. O. T. 1065</cite>.

##### <a id="s2746"></a> §2746

<span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> with the infinitive thus often follows verbs and other <!-- page 625 --> expressions formed by <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> (or <span lang="grc">α</span>-_privative_) with a positive word and denoting what is _impossible_, _improbable_, _wrong_, _senseless_, and the like.

<span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς οἷός τ᾽ ἐστὶν ἄλλως λέγων μὴ οὐ καταγέλαστος εἶναι</span> _no one by speaking otherwise can avoid being ridiculous_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 509a">P. G. 509a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπέσχου ζητήσειν ὡς οὐχ ὅσιόν σοι ὂν μὴ οὐ βοηθεῖν δικαιοσύνῃ</span> _you promised to make the inquiry on the ground that it would not be right for you not to assist justice_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 427e">P. R. 427e</cite>, <span lang="grc">πάνυ ἀνόητον ἡγοῦμαι εἶναί σοι μὴ οὐ καὶ τοῦτο χαρίζεσθαι</span> _I think it is utterly senseless for me not to grant you this favour also_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 218c">P. S. 218c</cite>.

##### <a id="s2747"></a> §2747

Such expressions are, e.g. <span lang="grc">οὐχ ὅσιός τ᾽ εἰμί, οὐχ ὁ̂όν τ᾽ ἐστί, οὐχ ἱκανός εἰμι, οὐκ ἔστι, ἀδύνατός εἰμι, οὐ δίκαιόν ἐστι, οὐχ ὅσιόν ἐστι, οὐ προσδοκίᾱ ἐστί, ἄλογόν ἐστι, οὐκ ἀνεκτόν ἐστι, ἄνοιά ἐστι</span>, and many others.

##### <a id="s2748"></a> §2748

Some expressions denoting repugnance to the moral sense involve a negative idea, and may have the same construction. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὥστε πᾶσιν αἰσχύ̄νην εἶναι μὴ οὐ συσπουδάζειν</span> _so that all were ashamed not_ (i.e. felt it was _not_ right) _to coöperate zealously_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.11">X. A. 2.3.11</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">αἰσχρόν ἐστι</span> ( = <span lang="grc">οὐ καλόν ἐστι</span>), <span lang="grc">δεινόν ἐστι</span>.

##### <a id="s2749"></a> §2749

Instead of <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> we find also <span lang="grc">μή, τὸ μή, τοῦ μή, τὸ μὴ οὐ</span> (but not <span lang="grc">τοῦ μὴ οὐ</span>).

**a.** <span lang="grc"> **μή** </span> (rarely; cp. [2744](#s2744). 1): <span lang="grc">ἔλεγον ὅτι . . . οὐ δυνήσοιντο μὴ πείθεσθαι τοῖς Οηβαίοις</span> _they said that they could not help submitting to the Thebans_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.1.1">X. H. 6.1.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">αἰσχρὸν . . . γίγνεται ἐμέ γε μὴ ἐθέλειν</span> _it is disgraceful for me at least not to be willing_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 458d">P. G. 458d</cite>.

**b.** <span lang="grc"> **τὸ μή** </span> (cp. [2744](#s2744). 3): <span lang="grc">ἔφη . . . οὐχ οι&lt;\*&gt;῀όν τ᾽ εἶναι τὸ μὴ ἀποκτεῖναί με</span> _he said it was not possible not to condemn me to death_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 29c">P. A. 29c</cite>.

**c.** <span lang="grc"> **τοῦ μή** </span> (cp. [2744](#s2744). 5): <span lang="grc">ἡ ἀπορίᾱ τοῦ μὴ ἡσυχάζειν</span> _the inability to rest_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.49">T. 2.49</cite>.

**d.** <span lang="grc"> **τὸ μὴ οὐ** </span> (cp. [2744](#s2744). [10](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s10)): <span lang="grc">οὐ μέντοι ἔπειθέ γε τὸ μὴ οὐ μεγαλοπά̄γμων . . . εἶναι</span> _he could not, however_, _persuade them that he was not a man who entertained grand designs_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 5.2.36">X. H. 5.2.36</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄλογον τὸ μὴ οὐ τέμνειν διχῇ</span> _it is irrational not to make a two-fold division_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Soph. 219e">P. Soph. 219e</cite>.

## <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> WITH THE PARTICIPLE DEPENDING ON NEGATIVED VERBS

##### <a id="s2750"></a> §2750

<span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span>, instead of <span lang="grc">μή</span>, is sometimes found with the participle after expressions preceded by <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> or involving a negative, and usually when such expressions denote impossibility or moral repugnance. <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> here denotes an exception, and has the force of _except_, _unless_ (cp. <span lang="grc">εἰ μή</span>, 2346 a).

<span lang="grc">οὐκ ἄρα ἐστὶν φίλον τῷ φιλοῦντι οὐδὲν μὴ οὐκ ἀντιφιλοῦν</span> _nothing then is beloved by a lover except it love in return_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lysis 212d">P. Lys. 212d</cite>, <span lang="grc">δυσάλγητος γὰρ ἂν εἴην τοίᾱνδε μὴ οὐ κατοικτί̄ρων ἕδρᾱν</span> _for I should prove hard of heart, did I not pity such a supplication as this_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 11">S. O. T. 11</cite> (<span lang="grc">δυσάλγητος ῀ οὐκ οἰκτίρμων, μὴ οὐ κατοικτί̄ρων ῀ εἰ μὴ κατοικτί̄ροιμι</span>). <!-- page 626 -->

## <span lang="grc">μή</span> AND <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> WITH THE SUBJUNCTIVE AND INDICATIVE

##### <a id="s2751"></a> §2751

The use of <span lang="grc">μή</span> and <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> with the subjunctive is different from that with the infinitive.

**a.** In doubtful assertions ([1801](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1801)-[1802](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1802)) expressing _anxiety_, _suspicion_, _surmise_, <span lang="grc">μή</span> is used of that which may be true, <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> of that which may not be true.

**b.** After _verbs of fear_ and _caution_, where <span lang="grc">μή</span> means _lest_, <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> means _lest not_, _that not_ ([2221](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/03-object-clauses.md#s2221), [2225](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/03-object-clauses.md#s2225)).

##### <a id="s2752"></a> §2752

<span lang="grc">μή</span> and <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> are used with the indicative in doubtful assertions ([1772](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1772)). In questions with <span lang="grc">μὴ οὐ</span> the <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> belongs to a single word ([2651 d](32-interrogative-sentences-questions.md#s2651)).

On <span lang="grc">ὅπως μή, ὅπως μὴ οὐ</span> with the future, see [1920](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1920), [1921](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1921), 2203.

## REDUNDANT <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> WITH <span lang="grc">πλήν</span>, ETC.

##### <a id="s2753"></a> §2753

Redundant <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> appears after the negative words <span lang="grc">πλήν, χωρίς, ἐκτός, ἄνευ</span> _except_, _without_, and after <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> (and <span lang="grc">μᾶλλον ἤ</span> usually) preceded by a negative, which may be involved in a question.

<span lang="grc">νῦν δὲ φαίνεται</span> (<span lang="grc">ἡ ναῦς</span>) . . . <span lang="grc">πλέουσα πανταχόσε πλὴν οὐκ εἰς Ἀθήνᾱς</span> _but now it seems that the ship is sailing everywhere except to Athens_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 56.23">D. 56.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρὶν δ᾽ οὐδὲν ὀρθῶς εἰδέναι, τί σοι πλέον λῡπουμένῃ γένοιτ᾽ ἄν</span>; _before thou knowest the facts, what can sorrow avail thee?_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hel. 322">E. Hel. 322</cite>, <span lang="grc">εὖ δ᾽ ἴστε ὅτι οὐ περὶ τῶν ἐμῶν ἰδίων μᾶλλον τῑμωρήσεσθε Πολυκλέᾱ ἢ οὐχ ὑπέρ ὑ̄μῶν αὐτῶν</span> _but be assured that you will punish Polycles rather for your own good than for my private interests_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 50.66">D. 50.66</cite>. Cp. “j'irai vous voir avant que vous _ne_ preniez aucune résolution,” “le bon Dieu est cent fois meilleur qu'on _ne_ le dit.”

## <span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ μή** </span>

##### <a id="s2754"></a> §2754

<span lang="grc">οὐ μή</span>, and the compounds of each, are used in emphatic negative predictions and prohibitions.

**a.** <span lang="grc">οὐ μή</span> marks strong personal interest on the part of the speaker. In its original use it may have belonged to colloquial speech and as such we find it in comedy; but in tragedy it is often used in stately language. <span lang="grc">οὐ μή</span> is rare in the orators.

##### <a id="s2755"></a> §2755

(I) In negative predictions to denote a strong denial.

**a.** With the (first or second) aorist subjunctive, less often with the present subjunctive ([1804](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1804)). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἢν νῑκήσωμεν, οὐ μή ποτε ὑ̄μῖν Πελοποννήσιοι ἐσβάλωσιν ἐς τὴν χώρᾱν</span> _if we are victorious, the Peloponnesians will never invade your territory_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.95">T. 4.95</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς μηκέτι μείνῃ τῶν πολεμίων</span> _not one of the enemy will stand his ground any longer_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.8.13">X. A. 4.8.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὔτι μὴ φύγητε</span> _you shall not escape_ (a threat) <cite data-ref="Eur. Hec. 1039">E. Hec. 1039</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ μή σοι δύνωνται ἀντέχειν οἱ πολέμιοι</span> _your enemies will not be able to withstand you_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hiero 11.15">X. Hi. 11.15</cite>.

**b.** With the future indicative (first and third person). Thus, <span lang="grc">οὔ σοι μὴ μεθέψομαί ποτε</span> _never will I follow thee_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 1052">S. El. 1052</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ μὴ δυνήσεται Κῦρος εὑρεῖν</span> _Cyrus_ <!-- page 627 --> _will not be able to find_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.1.5">X. C. 8.1.5</cite>. In indirect discourse, the future optative or infinitive; as <span lang="grc">ἐθέσπισεν . . . ὡς οὐ μή ποτε πέρσοιεν</span> _he prophesied that they never would destroy_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 611">S. Ph. 611</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἶπεν . . . οὐ μή ποτε εὖ πρά̄ξειν πόλιν</span> _he declared that the city would never prosper_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Phoen. 1590">E. Phoen. 1590</cite>.

##### <a id="s2756"></a> §2756

(II) In strong prohibitions (cp. [1919](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1919)).

**a.** With the future indicative (second person singular). Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐ μὴ καταβήσει</span> _don't come down_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Wasps 397">Ar. Vesp. 397</cite>.

**b.** With the aorist subjunctive rarely ([1800](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1800) N.). Thus <span lang="grc">οὐ μὴ ληρήσῃς</span> _don't talk twaddle_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 367">Ar. Nub. 367</cite>. Many editors change the aorist subjunctive to the future indicative.

##### <a id="s2757"></a> §2757

There are two cases in which <span lang="grc">οὐ μή</span> is not used in conjunction, but where each negative has its own verb.

**a.** A positive command in the future indicative (second person) may be joined by <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> or <span lang="grc">δέ</span> to a prohibition introduced by <span lang="grc">οὐ μή</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐ μὴ λαλήσεις ἀλλ᾽ ἀκολουθήσεις ἐμοί</span> _don't prattle but follow me_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 505">Ar. Nub. 505</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ μὴ δυσμενὴς ἔσει τοῖς φίλοις, παύσει δὲ θῡμοῦ</span> _do not be angry with thy friends, but cease thy wrath_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 1151">E. Med. 1151</cite>. (In <cite data-ref="Eur. Ba. 343">E. Bacch. 343</cite> <span lang="grc">δέ</span> with the future is followed by <span lang="grc">μηδέ</span> with the future.) In such sentences the force of <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> continues into the <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> or <span lang="grc">δέ</span> clause. Such sentences are generally printed as questions.

**b.** A positive command with <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> and the future indicative (second person) may be followed by the future in a prohibition introduced by <span lang="grc">μηδέ</span> or <span lang="grc">καὶ μή</span>. Here the clause with <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> has the form of a question expecting the answer _yes_, while the whole sentence has the form of a question expecting the answer _no_. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐ σῖγ᾽ ἀνέξει μηδὲ δειλίᾱν ἀρεῖ</span>; _wilt thou not keep silence and not win for thyself the reputation of cowardice?_ ( = _keep silence and do not get the reputation of being a coward_) <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 75">S. Aj. 75</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν καλεῖς αὐτὸν καὶ μὴ ἀφήσεις</span>; _will you not call him and_ (_will you not_) _send him away?_ ( = _call him and don't send him away_) <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 175a">P. S. 175a</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is to be taken also with the following clause. Some scholars make the question in the second clause independent of <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>.

##### <a id="s2758"></a> §2758

The origin of the use of <span lang="grc">οὐ μή</span> is obscure and disputed. See Kvičala _Zeitschrift für österreichische Gymnasien_ 1856, p. 755; Goodwin _Moods and Tenses_ 389; Gildersleeve _American Journal of Philology_ 3. 202, 23. 137; Jebb on Sophocles _Ajax_ 75 (appendix); Chambers _Classical Review_ 10. 150, 11. 109; Wharton _o.c._ 10. 239; Whitelaw _o.c._ 10. 239, 16. 277; Sonnenschein _o.c._ 16. 165; Kühner-Gerth _Grammatik der griechischen Sprache_ 2. § 514. 8.

## NEGATIVES WITH <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> AND THE INFINITIVE

##### <a id="s2759"></a> §2759

<span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> with the infinitive shows the following uses of the negatives.

**a.** <span lang="grc"> **μή** </span> in ordinary result clauses including such as express an intended result; as <span lang="grc">πᾶν ποιοῦσιν ὥστε δίκην μὴ διδόναι μηδ᾽ ἀπαλλάττεσθαι τοῦ μεγίστου κακοῦ</span> _they use every effort_ (_so as_) _to avoid being punished and released from the greatest of evils_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 479c">P. G. 479c</cite>.

**b.** <span lang="grc">μή</span> _sympathetic_, after _verbs of hindering;_ as <span lang="grc">ἀπεχόμενοι ὥστε μὴ ἐμβάλλειν</span> _refraining from attacking_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.49">T. 1.49</cite> (cp. [2744](#s2744). 1). <!-- page 628 -->

N.—After _verbs of hindering_ <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> is rarely used for <span lang="grc">ὥστε μή</span> (cp. [2744](#s2744). 2); as <span lang="grc">ὥστε γὰρ τὴν σύντομον πρὸς τοὺς Πελληνέᾱς ἀφικέσθαι ἡ πρὸ τοῦ τείχους φάραγξ εἶργε</span> _the ravine in front of the walls prevented them from reaching the short cut to the Pellenians_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 7.2.13">X. H. 7.2.13</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyd. 305d">P. Eu. 305d</cite>.

**c.** <span lang="grc"> **ου᾽** </span>, when the <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> clause depends on a clause itself subordinate to a _verb of saying_ or _thinking_ ([2269](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/06-omegasigmatauepsilon-rarely-omegasigma-with-the-infinitive.md#s2269)).

**d.** <span lang="grc"> **μὴ οὐ** </span> after a negatived _verb of hindering_ (cp. [2744](#s2744). 8); as <span lang="grc">οὔτε σφέας Εὐρυβιάδης κατέχειν δυνήσεται . . . ὥστε μὴ οὐ διασκεδασθῆναι τὴν στρατιήν</span> _neither will Eurybiades be able to prevent the fleet from being scattered_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 8.57">Hdt. 8.57</cite>. Also when the <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> clause depends on a negatived verb ([2745](#s2745)); as <span lang="grc">πείσομαι γὰρ οὐ τοσοῦτον οὐδὲν ὥστε μὴ οὐ καλῶς θανεῖν</span> _for I will suffer nothing so much as not to die nobly_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 97">S. Ant. 97</cite>.

**e.** <span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ μή** </span> (cp. [2754](#s2754) a); as <span lang="grc">οὕτως ἐπετεθύ̄μηκα ἀκοῦσαι ὥστε . . . οὐ μή σου ἀπολειφθῶ</span> _I have conceived such a desire to hear that I shall not fall behind you_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 227d">P. Phae. 227d</cite>.

## ACCUMULATION OF NEGATIVES

##### <a id="s2760"></a> §2760

If in the same clause a _simple_ negative (<span lang="grc">οὐ</span> or <span lang="grc">μή</span>) with a verb follows a negative, each of the two negatives keeps its own force if they belong to different words or expressions. If they belong to the same word or expression, they make an affirmative.

<span lang="grc">οὐ διὰ τὸ μὴ ἀκοντίζειν οὐκ ἔβαλον αὐτόν</span> _it was not because they did not throw that they did not hit him_ Ant. 3. <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 4.6"><span lang="grc">δ</span>. 6</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὔ τοι μὰ τὴν Δήμητρα δύναμαι μὴ γελᾶν</span> _by Demeter I am not able to help laughing_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Frogs 42">Ar. Ran. 42</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς οὐκ ἔπασχέ τι</span> _no one was not suffering something_ (i.e. everybody suffered) <cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 1">X. S. 1</cite>. 9 (<span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς ὅστις οὐ</span> = _everybody_ is commonly used for <span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς οὐ</span>), <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ τὸν Φορμίων᾽ ἐκεῖνος οὐχ ὁρᾷ</span> _nor does he not see Phormio_ (i.e. he sees him very well) <cite data-ref="Dem. 36.46">D. 36.46</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽ εἴ τις ἄλλος σοφός</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐστιν</span>) <span lang="grc">οὐ φιλοσοφεῖ</span> _nor if there is any other man who is wise, does he love wisdom_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 204a">P. S. 204a</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδέ γε ὁ ἰδίᾳ πονηρὸς οὐκ ἂν γένοιτο δημοσίᾳ χρηστός</span> _nor can the man who is base in private prove himself noble in a public capacity_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.78">Aes. 3.78</cite>.

##### <a id="s2761"></a> §2761

If in the same clause one or more _compound_ negatives follow a negative with the same verb, the compound negative simply confirms the first negative.

<span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς οὐδὲν πενίᾳ δρά̄σει</span> _no one will do anything because of want_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Eccl. 605">Ar. Eccl. 605</cite>, <span lang="grc">μὴ θορυβήσῃ μηδείς</span> _let no one raise an uproar_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 5.15">D. 5.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ οὔτε ἐπέθετο οὐδεὶς οὐδαμόθεν οὔτε πρὸς τὴν γέφῡραν οὐδεὶς ἦλθε</span> _and neither did any one make an attack from any quarter nor did any one come to the bridge_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.4.23">X. A. 2.4.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">τούτους φοβούμενοι μήποτε ἀσεβὲς μηδὲν μηδὲ ἀνόσιον μήτε ποιήσητε μήτε βουλεύσητε</span> _holding them_ (the gods) _in fear never do or intend anything either impious or unholy_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.7.22">X. C. 8.7.22</cite>. So <span lang="grc">οὐ . . . οὐδέ</span> _non . . . ne . . quidem_, <span lang="grc">οὐ μὴν οὐδέ</span> ([2768](#s2768)). <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ πολλοῦ δεῖ</span>, after a negative, means _far from it_. Cp. “no sonne, were he never so old of years, might not marry” (Ascham's Scholemaster), “We may not, nor will we not suffer this” (Marlowe).

**a.** In <span lang="grc"> **οὐδὲ γὰρ οὐδέ** </span> the first negative belongs to the whole sentence, while the <!-- page 629 --> second limits a particular part. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ γὰρ οὐδὲ τοῦτο ἐψεύσατο</span> _for he did not deceive me even in this_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.2.20">X. C. 7.2.20</cite> (cp. _neque enim . . . ne . . quidem_). Cp. E 22, <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 8.32"><span lang="grc">θ</span> 32</cite>. So <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ μὲν οὐδέ</span> B 703, <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 10.551"><span lang="grc">κ</span> 551</cite>.

##### <a id="s2762"></a> §2762

The negative of one clause is often repeated in the same or in another clause either for emphasis or because of lax structure.

<span lang="grc">δ̀ς οὐκ, ἐπειδὴ τῷδε ἐβούλευσας μόρον, δρᾶσαι τόδ᾽ ἔργον οὐκ ἔτλης</span> _who did not_, _after you had planned his death, dare to do this deed_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 1634">A. Ag. 1634</cite>. The repetition is rhetorical when the negative is repeated directly, as <span lang="grc">οὐ σμῑκρός, οὔχ, ἁ̄γὼν ὅδε</span> _not trifling, is this struggle, no in truth_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 587">S. O. C. 587</cite>.

## SOME NEGATIVE PHRASES

##### <a id="s2763"></a> §2763

<span lang="grc"> **μὴ ὅτι, οὐχ ὅπως** </span>, rarely <span lang="grc"> **οὐχ ὅτι** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **μὴ ὅπως** </span>, _not to speak of, to say nothing of, not only, not only not, so far from_ (Lat. _tantum aberat ut_) are idiomatic phrases probably due to an (early, and later often unconscious) ellipsis of a _verb of saying_. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐ λέγω</span> (or <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἐρῶ</span>) <span lang="grc">ὅπως, μὴ εἴπω</span> (<span lang="grc">λέγε</span> or <span lang="grc">εἴπῃς</span>) <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> _I do_ (_will_) _not say that, let me not say that, do not say that_. <span lang="grc">μὴ ὅτι</span>, etc. are often used where these verbal forms cannot be supplied by reason of the form of the sentence.

**a.** <span lang="grc"> **οὐχ ὅτι** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **οὐχ ὅπως, μὴ ὅτι** </span>) . . . <span lang="grc"> **ἀλλὰ** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **καί** </span> _not only . . . but_ (_also_). Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐχ ὅτι μόνος ὁ Κρίτων ἐν ἡσυχίᾳ ἦν, ἀλλὰ καὶ οἱ φίλοι αὐτοῦ</span> _not only was Crito in peace, but his friends also_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.9.8">X. M. 2.9.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἶμαι ἂν μὴ ὅτι ἰδιώτην τινά, ἀλλὰ τὸν μέγαν βασιλέᾱ εὑρεῖν κτλ</span>. _I think that not merely any private person but the Great King would find_, etc. <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 40d">P. A. 40d</cite>.

**b.** <span lang="grc"> **οὐχ ὅπως** </span> (rarely <span lang="grc"> **οὐχ ὅτι** </span>) or <span lang="grc"> **μὴ ὅτι . . . ἀλλὰ** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **καί** </span>) is shown by the context to mean _not only not_ (_so far from_) . . . _but_ (_also_). Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐχ ὅπως χάριν αὐτοῖς ἔχεις, ἀλλὰ μισθώσᾱς σαυτὸν κατὰ τουτωνὶ̄ πολῑτεύει</span> _not only are you not grateful to them, but you let yourself out for hire as a public man to their prejudice_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.131">D. 18.131</cite>; <span lang="grc">μὴ ὅτι</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 581e">P. R. 581e</cite>.

**c.** <span lang="grc"> **οὐχ ὅπως** </span> (rarely <span lang="grc"> **οὐχ ὅτι** </span>) or <span lang="grc"> **μὴ ὅτι** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **μὴ ὅπως** </span>) . . . <span lang="grc"> **ἀλλ᾽ οὐδέ** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **μηδέ** </span>) or <span lang="grc"> **ἀλλ̓ου᾽** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **μή** </span>) is shown by the context to mean _not only not_ (_so far from_) . . . _but not even_. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐχ ὅπως τῆς κοινῆς ἐλευθερίᾱς μετέχομεν, ἀλλ᾽ οὐδὲ δουλείᾱς μετρίᾱς τυχεῖν ἠξιώθημεν</span> _not only do we not share in the general freedom, but we were not thought worthy of obtaining even a moderate servitude_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 14.5">I. 14.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">νομίζει ἑαυτὸν μὴ ὅτι Πλαταιέᾱ εἶναι, ἀλλ᾽ οὐδ᾽ ἐλεύθερον</span> _he considers himself not only not a Plataean but not even a free man_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 23.12">L. 23.12</cite>.

N. When a negative precedes, the meaning may be _not only . . . but not even;_ as <span lang="grc">τὴν οἰκίᾱν . . . οὐδενὶ ἂν μὴ ὅτι προῖκα δοίης, ἀλλ᾽ οὐδ᾽ ἔλᾱττον τῆς ἀξίᾱς λαβών</span> _you would offer your house to no one not only gratis, but not even for a lower price than it is worth_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.6.11">X. M. 1.6.11</cite>.

**d.** <span lang="grc"> **μὴ ὅτι** </span> (less often <span lang="grc"> **οὐχ ὅπως** </span>) in the second of two balanced clauses, after an expressed or implied negative in the first clause, means _much less_ (Lat. _nedum_)_;_ as <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ πλεῖν, μὴ ὅτι ἀναιρεῖσθαι τοὺς ἄνδρας δυνατὸν ἦν</span> _it was not possible even to sail, much less to rescue the man_ (i.e. _to say nothing of rescuing_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.3.35">X. H. 2.3.35</cite>. The preceding negative may be contained in a question or be otherwise implicit. Thus, <span lang="grc">δοκεῖ σοι ῥᾴδιον εἶναι οὕτω ταχὺ μαθεῖν . . . ὁτιοῦν πρᾶγμα, μὴ ὅτι τοσοῦτον κτλ</span>.; _does it appear to you to be easy to learn so quickly any subject whatever_, _much less a subject of so great importance?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crat. 427e">P. Crat. 427e</cite>; cp. <cite data-ref="Dem. 54.17">D. 54.17</cite>. <!-- page 630 -->

The rare <span lang="grc"> **οὐχ ὅτι** </span> in the second member means _though_ (<cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 336d">P. Pr. 336d</cite>).

**e.** <span lang="grc"> **μή τί γε** </span>, in the orators instead of <span lang="grc">μὴ ὅτι</span>, after a negative means _much less_, after a positive _much more_. Cp. <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.137">D. 19.137</cite>, 8. 27.

##### <a id="s2764"></a> §2764

<span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ μόνον . . . ἀλλὰ καί** </span> (negative <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ οὐδέ</span>) _not only . . . but also_ (Lat. _non solum . . . sed etiam_). <span lang="grc">καί</span> may be omitted: usually when the <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> clause either includes the first clause or is strongly contrasted with it. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἱ̄μάτιον ἠμφίεσαι οὐ μόνον φαῦλον, ἀλλά τὸ αὐτὸ θέρους τε καὶ χειμῶνος</span> _you put on a cloak that is not merely wretched but is the same both summer and winter alike_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.6.2">X. M. 1.6.2</cite>; cp. <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.26">D. 18.26</cite>.

##### <a id="s2765"></a> §2765

<span lang="grc"> **ὅ τι μή, ὅσον μή** </span> _except_, _unless_. <span lang="grc">ὅ τι</span> (sometimes written <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span>) <span lang="grc">μή</span>, and <span lang="grc">ὅσον μή, ὅσα μή</span> are used, without any verb, to limit a preceding assertion (cp. <span lang="grc">εἰ μή</span> 2346 a).

<span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ ἦν κρήνη, ὅ τι μὴ μία ἐν αὐτῇ τῇ ἀκροπόλει</span> _for there was no spring, except one on the acropolis itself_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.26">T. 4.26</cite>, <span lang="grc">πείθουσα δὲ ἐκ τούτων μὲν ἀναχωρεῖν, ὅσον μὴ ἀνάγκη αὐτοῖς χρῆσθαι</span> philosophy _persuading_ the soul _to withdraw from them_, _except so far as she has to make use of them_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 83a">P. Ph. 83a</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῆς γῆς ἐκράτουν ὅσα μὴ προϊόντες πολὺ ἐκ τῶν ὅπλων</span> _they were masters of the country, so far as they could be without advancing far from their camp_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.111">T. 1.111</cite> (<span lang="grc">ὅσα κρατεῖν ἐδύναντο</span>).

##### <a id="s2766"></a> §2766

<span lang="grc"> **μόνον οὐ** </span> (lit. _only not_), <span lang="grc"> **ὅσον οὐ** </span> (of time) _almost_, _all but_ (Lat. _tantum non_). Thus, <span lang="grc">μόνον οὐ διεσπάσθην</span> _I was almost torn in pieces_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 5.5">D. 5.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐνόμιζε . . . ὅσον οὐκ ἤδη ἔχειν τὴν πόλιν</span> _he thought that he already was all but in possession of the city_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.2.16">X. H. 6.2.16</cite>.

##### <a id="s2767"></a> §2767

<span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ μὴν ἀλλά, οὐ μέντοι ἀλλά** </span> _nevertheless_, _notwithstanding_, cp. Lat. _uerum tamen;_ the colloquial <span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ γὰρ ἀλλά** </span> has about the force of _nay_, _for indeed_, cp. Lat. _non enim . . . sed_. These elliptical phrases require a verb or some other word to be supplied from the context or general run of the thought; but they often resist strict analysis since the contrasted idea is too vague to be supplied. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁ ἵππος . . . μῑκροῦ κἀ̄κεῖνον ἐξετραχήλισεν· οὐ μὴν</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐξετραχήλισεν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ ἐπέμεινεν ὁ Κῦρος</span> _the horse was within a little of throwing him also over its head;_ (_not_ that it did throw him _however_, _but_ = ) _nevertheless Cyrus kept his seat_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.8">X. C. 1.4.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀεὶ μὲν οὖν οἵ θ᾽ ἡμέτεροι πρόγονοι καὶ Λακεδαιμόνιοι φιλοτί̄μως πρὸς ἀλλήλους εἶχον, οὐ μὴν</span> (_scil_. <span lang="grc">περὶ κακῶν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ περὶ καλλίστων . . . ἐφιλονί̄κησαν</span> _while our ancestors and the Lacedaemonians were continually jealous of each other_ (_not indeed_ about base objects _but_ = ) _nevertheless they were rivals about the noblest objects_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.85">I. 4.85</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ γὰρ ἂν δόξειεν οὕτω γ᾽ εἶναι ἄλογον· οὐ μέντοι</span> (_scil_. <span lang="grc">ἄλογόν ἐστιν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἴσως ἔχει τινὰ λόγον</span> _and in fact put thus it would seem to be unreasonable;_ (it is _not however_ unreasonable _but_ = ) _nevertheless perhaps it has some sense_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 62b">P. Ph. 62b</cite>, <span lang="grc">μὴ σκῶπτέ μ᾽, ὦδέλφ᾽, οὐ γὰρ ἀλλ᾽ ἔχω κακῶς</span> _don't mock me, brother; nay_, _for really I am in a bad way_ Ar. Ran 58 (lit. _for_ it is _not_ so _but_, i.e. it is _not_ a case for mocking, _but_). In these phrases <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> seems to show traces of its original force of _otherwise_ ([2775](36-particles/02-alphalambdalambdaalpha.md#s2775)).

##### <a id="s2768"></a> §2768

<span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ μὴν οὐδέ** </span> _nor_ (_yet_) _again_, _not however that_ corresponds to the positive <span lang="grc">οὐ μὴν</span> (<span lang="grc">μέντοι</span>) <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐ μὴν οὐδὲ βαρβάρους εἴρηκε</span> _nor again has he spoken of barbarians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.3">T. 1.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ μὰ̄ν οὐδ᾽ Ἀχιλεύς</span> _no_, _nor even Achilles_ B 703, <span lang="grc">οὐ μὴν οὐδὲ ἀναισθήτως αὐτοὺς κελεύω τοὺς . . . ξυμμάχους ἡμῶν ἐᾶν βλάπτειν</span> _not however that I bid you tamely permit them to injure our allies_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.82">T. 1.82</cite>.
