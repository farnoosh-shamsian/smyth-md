# ADJECTIVE CLAUSES (RELATIVE CLAUSES: 2488-2573)

##### <a id="s2488"></a> §2488

Relative clauses correspond to attributive adjectives (or participles), since like adjectives they serve to define substantives. Like adjectives, too, they often have the value of substantives and stand in any case.

<span lang="grc">δ̀ν γὰρ θεοὶ φιλοῦσιν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ὁ θεοφιλής</span>), <span lang="grc">ἀποθνῄσκει νέος</span> _for whom the gods love_, _dies young_ Men. Sent. 425, <span lang="grc">ἦ θίγω δῆθ᾽ οἵ μ᾽ ἔφῡσαν</span>; ( = <span lang="grc">τῶν με φῡσάντων</span>) _am I to embrace him who begat me?_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Ion 560">E. Ion 560</cite>, <span lang="grc">σὺν τοῖς θησαυροῖς οἷς ὁ πατὴρ κατέλιπεν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">τοῖς ὑπὸ τοῦ πατρὸς καταλειφθεῖσι</span>) _with the treasures which my father left_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.33">X. C. 3.1.33</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν αὐτοῖς οἷς ἐτῑμᾶσθε</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἐν αὐταῖς ταῖς τῑμαῖς</span>) _in the very honours which you received_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.238">D. 19.238</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὧν ἔλαβεν ἅπᾱσι μετέδωκεν</span> _it shared with all what it received_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.29">I. 4.29</cite>.

##### <a id="s2489"></a> §2489

Relative clauses are introduced by relative pronouns or by relative ad_verbs of time_, _place_ , or _manner_ (cp. [340](../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s340), [346](../part-2-inflection/07-adverbs.md#s346)), and refer to an antecedent expressed or implied in the main clause.

**a.** Temporal clauses, which are like relative clauses in many respects, have been treated in 2389 ff. On relatives used as indirect interrogatives and as exclamations, see [2668](33-indirect-dependent-questions.md#s2668) ff., 2685 ff.

##### <a id="s2490"></a> §2490

Many relative clauses are equivalent to coördinate clauses (e.g. [2553](#s2553)). In such cases the relative has the force of a demonstrative or personal pronoun with a connective (<span lang="grc">καί, ἀλλά, δέ, γάρ, οὖν, ἄρα</span>, etc.). Thus, <span lang="grc">πῶς οὖν ἂν ἔνοχος εἴη τῇ γραφῇ; ὃς</span> ( = <span lang="grc">οὗτος γὰρ</span>) . . . <span lang="grc">φανερὸς ἦν θεραπεύων τοὺς θεούς</span> _how then could he be subject to the indictment? For he manifestly worshipped the gods_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.2.64">X. M. 1.2.64</cite>. Greek often uses here the demonstrative (contrast <span lang="grc">ταῦτα δὲ εἰπών</span> with _quae cum dixisset_). <!-- page 561 -->

##### <a id="s2491"></a> §2491

A relative must often be resolved into a conjunction and a pronoun ([2555](#s2555)).

##### <a id="s2492"></a> §2492

A truly subordinate relative clause may precede the main clause or be incorporated into it ([2536](#s2536)). The relative clause is often made emphatic by placing after it the main clause with the demonstrative antecedent. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὅ τι βούλεται, τοῦτο ποιείτω</span> _whatever he wants_, _that let him do_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyd. 285e">P. Eu. 285e</cite>.

##### <a id="s2493"></a> §2493

<span lang="grc"> **ὅς** </span> _who_ and the other simple relatives (e.g. <span lang="grc">οἷος, ὅσος</span>) refer to a particular and individual person or thing.

<span lang="grc">ἦν τις ἐν τῇ στρατιᾷ Ξενοφῶν Ἀθηναῖος, ὃς οὔτε στρατηγὸς οὔτε στρατιώτης ὢν συνηκολούθει</span> _there was in the army one Xenophon_, _an Athenian_, _who accompanied it though he was neither general nor soldier_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.4">X. A. 3.1.4</cite>.

**a.** On the relation of the relative <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> to the demonstrative <span lang="grc">ὅς</span>, see [1113](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1113), [1114](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1114).

**b.** <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> is often used instead of <span lang="grc">ὅστις</span> (or <span lang="grc">οἷος</span>) especially with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or <span lang="grc">μή</span>. Cp. [2508](#s2508). <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> _whoever_ with the indicative generally adds (in prose) <span lang="grc">δή ποτε, δήποτ᾽ οὖν</span> ([339 e](../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s339)).

##### <a id="s2494"></a> §2494

<span lang="grc"> **ὅ** </span> (sometimes <span lang="grc">ἅ</span>) at the beginning of a sentence may have the force of _as to what_ (cp. _quod_), suggesting the matter to which it pertains.

<span lang="grc">δ̀ δ᾽ ἐζήλωσας ἡμᾶς, ὡς τοὺς μὲν φίλους . . . εὖ ποιεῖν δυνάμεθα . . ., οὐδὲ ταῦθ᾽ οὕτως ἔχει</span> _as to what excited your envy of us—that we are able to benefit our friends—not even is this as you suppose_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hiero 6.12">X. Hi. 6.12</cite>. The postponed antecedent may be omitted (<cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.1.29">X. A. 6.1.29</cite>).

**a.** An introductory relative clause with <span lang="grc">ὅ</span> may stand in apposition to an entire clause that follows. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὅ πάντων θαυμαστότατον, Σωκράτη μεθύοντα οὐδεὶς πώποτε ἑώρᾱκεν ἀνθρώπων</span> _what is most wonderful, no one whatsoever ever saw Socrates drunk_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 220a">P. S. 220a</cite>. (So with an infinitive, <cite data-ref="Isoc. 14.18">I. 14.18</cite>.) The main clause, following such a relative clause, may be introduced by <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> or <span lang="grc">γάρ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">δ̀ μὲν πάντων θαυμαστότατον ἀκοῦσαι, ὅτι ἓν ἕκαστον ὧν ἐπῃνέσαμεν ἀπόλλῡσι τὴν ψῡχήν</span> _what is most wonderful of all to hear_, (that) _each one of the things we approved ruins the soul_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 491b">P. R. 491b</cite>. Cp. [994](10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s994), [995](10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s995).

##### <a id="s2495"></a> §2495

<span lang="grc"> **ὅσπερ** </span> _the same as_ (_qui quidem_) is especially definite and denotes identity ([338 c](../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s338)). <span lang="grc"> **ὅς γε** </span> (_quippe qui_) is causal ([2555 a](#s2555)).

##### <a id="s2496"></a> §2496

<span lang="grc"> **ὅστις** </span> _whoever_ and the other compound relatives (e.g. <span lang="grc">ὁποῖος, ὁπόσος</span>) denote a person or thing in general, or mark the _class_, _character_, _quality_, or _capacity_ of a person (less often of a thing).

<span lang="grc">μακάριος ὅστις οὐσίᾱν καὶ νοῦν ἔχει</span> _happy is the man who possesses property and sense_ Men. Sent. 340.

**a.** After a negative expressed or implied, <span lang="grc">ὅστις</span> (not <span lang="grc">ὅς</span>) is used because of its general meaning. So <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔστιν ὅστις, τίς ἐστιν ὅστις; οὐδείς ἐστιν ὅστις</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">οὔτις ἐστὶν ὅς</span>), <span lang="grc">πᾶς ὅστις</span> (plural usually <span lang="grc">πάντες ὅσοι</span>). Cp. [2557](#s2557).

**b.** <span lang="grc">ἐξ ὅτου</span> is common for <span lang="grc">ἐξ οὗ</span> _since_. In Ionic (and Thuc. 6. 3) <span lang="grc">ὅστις</span> is used of a definite object. Cp. <cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.7">Hdt. 1.7</cite>, 2. 99.

##### <a id="s2497"></a> §2497

<span lang="grc"> **οἷος** </span> _of such sort as to, proper for_, and <span lang="grc"> **ὅσος** </span> _of such amount as_ <!-- page 562 --> _to_, _enough for_, denote result and commonly take the infinitive (negative <span lang="grc">μή</span>).

<span lang="grc">καλόν τε</span> (<span lang="grc">δοκεῖ</span>) <span lang="grc">εἶναι ἡ ἐπιστήμη καὶ οἷον ἄρχειν τοῦ ἀνθρώπου</span> _knowledge seems to be both a noble thing and able to command man_ P. Prot. 352 c, <span lang="grc">ὅσον μόνον γεύσασθαι ἑαυτῷ καταλιπών</span> _leaving himself only enough to taste_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.3.22">X. A. 7.3.22</cite>. So <span lang="grc">οἷός τε</span> _able to_ (for <span lang="grc">τοιοῦτος οἶός τε</span>); thus, <span lang="grc">συμβουλεύειν οἷοί τ᾽ ἐσόμεθα</span> _we shall be able to give counsel_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 455d">P. G. 455d</cite>.

**a.** On clauses with <span lang="grc">οἷος</span> or <span lang="grc">ὅσος</span> following a main clause after which we supply a _verb of reflection_, see [2687](34-exclamatory-sentences.md#s2687).

**b.** <span lang="grc">ὅσος</span> is used elliptically in <span lang="grc">ὅσαι ἡμέραι</span> (<span lang="grc">ὁσημέραι</span>) _daily_, <span lang="grc">ὅσα ἔτη</span> _yearly_.

##### <a id="s2498"></a> §2498

Local clauses are introduced by the relative adverbs <span lang="grc">οὗ, ὅπου, ἔνθα, ἵνα</span> (usually poetic, but sometimes in Plato) _where_, <span lang="grc">οἷ, ὅποι, ἔνθα</span> _whither_, <span lang="grc">ὅθεν, ὁπόθεν, ἔνθεν</span> _whence_, <span lang="grc">ᾗ, ὅπῃ</span> _which way, where_, _whither_. <span lang="grc">ὅθι</span> and <span lang="grc">ὁπόθι</span> _where_ are Epic and Lyric, <span lang="grc">ἧχι</span> _where_ is Epic. <span lang="grc">ἔνθα</span> and <span lang="grc">ἔνθεν</span> are also demonstratives (_there_, _thence_).

##### <a id="s2499"></a> §2499

With names of things the relative adverbs <span lang="grc"> **ἔνθα, ᾗ, ὅθεν, οἷ, οὗ** </span> are often used instead of the relative pronouns preceded by <span lang="grc">ἐν, εἰς, ἐξ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">πλησίον ἦν ὁ σταθμὸς ἔνθα</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἰς ὃν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἔμελλε καταλύ̄ειν</span> _the stopping-place was near where he intended to make a halt_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.1">X. A. 1.8.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν τῷ σταθμῷ . . . ὅθεν</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἐξ οὗ</span>) <span lang="grc">ὡρμῶντο</span> _at the stopping-place whence they set out_ 2. 1. 3. A relative adverb may also refer to a personal antecedent, as <span lang="grc">καταβαίνειν πρὸς τοὺς ἄλλους ἔνθα τὰ ὅπλα ἔκειντο</span> _to descend to the others where the armed force was stationed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.2.20">X. A. 4.2.20</cite>.

##### <a id="s2500"></a> §2500

On comparative clauses of manner introduced by <span lang="grc">ὡς, ὥσπερ</span> etc., see [2463](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/13-clauses-of-comparison.md#s2463) ff.

## CONCORD OF RELATIVE PRONOUNS

##### <a id="s2501"></a> §2501

A relative pronoun agrees with its antecedent in gender, number, and person; its case is determined by the construction of the clause in which it stands.

<span lang="grc">οὗτός ἐστιν ὁ ἀνὴρ δ̀ς ἦλθε</span> _this is the man who came_, <span lang="grc">αὕτη ἐστὶν ἡ γυνὴ ἢν ἐζητοῦμεν</span> _this is the woman whom we were looking for_, <span lang="grc">λαβὼν τοὺς ἱππέᾱς οἳ ἦσαν αὐτῷ</span> _taking the cavalry which he had_, <span lang="grc">ἔχων τοὺς ὁπλἱ̄τᾱς ὧν ἐστρατήγει</span> _having the hoplites which he commanded_, <span lang="grc">τριῶν θυρῶν οὐσῶν, ἃ̄ς ἔδει με διελθεῖν</span> _there being three doors through which I had to go_.

**a.** If the main clause as a whole is regarded as the antecedent, the relative stands in the neuter singular with or without a demonstrative. Thus, <span lang="grc">πλεῖν ἐπὶ Σελῑνοῦντα πά̄σῃ τῇ στρατιᾷ, ἐφ᾽ ὅπερ μάλιστα ἐπέμφθησαν</span> _to sail for Selinus with all their force, for which purpose especially they had been sent_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.47">T. 6.47</cite>.

**b.** The person of the verb in a relative clause, in which the relative pronoun is the subject, is regularly determined by the person of the antecedent pronoun expressed or implied. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐκ οἶδ᾽ ὅστις ἄνθρωπος γεγένημαι</span> _I do not know what sort of a person I have become_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.12">X. C. 1.4.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ οἰκίᾱ γε πολὺ μείζων ἡ ὑ̄μετέρᾱ τῆς ἐμῆς, οἵ γε οἰκίᾳ χρῆσθε γῇ τε καὶ οὐρανῷ</span> _and your habitation is much larger than mine since you occupy both heaven and earth as a habitation_ 5. 2. 15. The third person rarely follows a vocative (P [248](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/05-third-declension.md#s248)). <!-- page 563 -->

##### <a id="s2502"></a> §2502

Variations from the law of agreement are, in general, the same as in the case of other pronouns ([926](05-agreement-the-concords.md#s926)).

**a.** The construction according to sense ([950](10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s950), [1013](12-peculiarities-in-the-use-of-gender.md#s1013)) often occurs, as <span lang="grc">φίλον θάλος, δ̀ν τέκον αὐτή</span> _my dear child, whom I myself bore_ X 87; so with collective nouns, as <span lang="grc">τὰ δόξαντα πλήθει, οἵπερ δικάσουσι</span> _what is approved by the multitude_, _who will judge_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 260a">P. Phae. 260a</cite>.

**b.** A relative in the plural may follow a singular antecedent denoting a whole class: <span lang="grc">θησαυροποιὸς ἀνήρ, οὓς . . . ἐπαινεῖ τὸ πλῆθος</span> _a man who lays up a store_, the class of men _which the multitude approves_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 554a">P. R. 554a</cite>. This construction is less common in prose than in poetry; as <span lang="grc">ἦ μάλα τις θεὸς ἔνδον, οἳ οὐρανὸν εὐρὺν ἔχουσιν</span> _in truth there is within some one of the gods who occupy the wide heaven_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 19.40"><span lang="grc">τ</span> 40</cite>.

**c.** A relative in the singular having a collective force may have its antecedent in the plural; as <span lang="grc">τούτους ἐπαινεῖν, δ̀ς ἂν ἑκὼν μηδὲν κακὸν ποιῇ</span> _to commend those who voluntarily do nothing evil_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 345d">P. Pr. 345d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ᾥτινι ἐντυγχάνοιεν . . . πάντας ἔκτεινον</span> _they slew all whom they met_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.32">X. A. 2.5.32</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> with the indicative is rare.

**d.** The relative may stand in the neuter, in agreement with the notion implied in the antecedent rather than with the antecedent itself; as <span lang="grc">διὰ τὴν πλεονεξίᾱν, δ̀ πᾶσα φύσις διώκειν πέφῡκεν ὡς ἀγαθόν</span> _for the sake of profit_, a thing _which every nature is inclined to pursue as a good_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 359c">P. R. 359c</cite>.

**e.** The relative may agree in gender and number, not with the antecedent but with a following predicate noun. This is common with _verbs of naming;_ as <span lang="grc">λόγοι μήν εἰσιν ἐν ἑκάστοις ἡμῶν, ἃ̄ς ἐλπίδας ὀνομάζομεν</span>; _assuredly there are propositions in each of us which we call hopes?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phileb. 40a">P. Phil. 40a</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ̄πεν ὅτι . . . διαγεγένηται πρά̄ττων τὰ δίκαια καὶ τῶν ἀδίκων ἀπεχόμενος, ἥνπερ νομίζοι καλλίστην μελέτην ἀπολογίᾱς εἶναι</span> _he said that he had continued to do what was just and to refrain from what was unjust, which he thought was the best practice for his defence_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.8.4">X. M. 4.8.4</cite>.

**f.** A relative may agree with a predicate noun when it follows that noun immediately and not its own substantive: <span lang="grc">καὶ δίκη ἐν ἀνθρώποις πῶς οὐ καλόν, ὃ πάντα ἡμέρωκε τὰ ἀνθρώπινα</span>; _and justice among men, how is not that something beautiful_, _which civilizes all human things?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 937d">P. L. 937d</cite>.

### THE ANTECEDENT OF RELATIVE CLAUSES

##### <a id="s2503"></a> §2503

The demonstrative antecedents of the relative pronouns are commonly: <span lang="grc">οὗτος . . . ὅς, τοιοῦτος . . . οἷος, τοσοῦτος . . . ὅσος, τηλικοῦτος . . . ἡλίκος</span>, etc.

**a.** The antecedent of <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> is often <span lang="grc">τοιοῦτος</span> ([1249](17-pronouns.md#s1249)). The antecedent of <span lang="grc">ὅς, ὅσπερ, οἷος</span>, may be <span lang="grc">ὅμοιος, παραπλήσιος, ἴσος</span>.

##### <a id="s2504"></a> §2504

On comparative clauses of degree with <span lang="grc">τοσούτῳ . . . ὅσῳ</span>, etc., see [2468](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/13-clauses-of-comparison.md#s2468) ff.

##### <a id="s2505"></a> §2505

**Definite and Indefinite Antecedent**.—The antecedent of a relative pronoun or adverb may be definite or indefinite. <!-- page 564 -->

**a.** A _definite_ antecedent refers to a definite or particular person, thing, time, place, or manner. When the antecedent is definite, the relative clause takes any form that occurs in an independent sentence ([921](03-kinds-of-simple-sentences.md#s921)); with <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> as the negative, unless the particular construction requires <span lang="grc">μή</span>.

**b.** An _indefinite_ antecedent refers to an indefinite person, thing, time, place, or manner. When the antecedent is indefinite, the relative clause commonly has a conditional force, and, if negative, takes <span lang="grc">μή</span> like the protasis of a conditional sentence.

##### <a id="s2506"></a> §2506

In general when the relative clause has the indicative, the antecedent is either definite (negative <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>) or indefinite (negative <span lang="grc">μή</span>). When the relative clause has the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or the optative (not in a wish), the antecedent is indefinite (negative <span lang="grc">μή</span>).

DEFINITE: <span lang="grc">ταῦτα ἃ βούλεται πρά̄ττει</span> _he does what he wants_ (i.e. the particular thing he wants to do). Negative <span lang="grc">ταῦτα ἃ οὐ βούλεται πρά̄ττει</span>.

INDEFINITE: <span lang="grc">ταῦτα ἅτινα βούλεται πρά̄ττει</span> _he does whatever he wants_ (i.e. if he wants to do anything, he does it); negative <span lang="grc">ταῦτα ἅτινα μὴ βούλεται πρά̄ττει</span>. So <span lang="grc">ταῦτα ἅτινα ἂν βούληται πρά̄ττει</span> _whatever he wants to do, that he_ always _does_, <span lang="grc">ταῦτα ἅτινα βούλοιτο ἔπρᾱττε</span> _whatever he wanted to do, that he_ always _did_, <span lang="grc">ταῦτα ἅτινα ἂν βούληται πρά̄ξει</span> _whatever he wants to do, that he will do_, <span lang="grc">ταῦτα ἅτινα βούλοιτο, πρά̄ττοι ἄν</span> _whatever he might want to do, that he would_ (_will_) _do_. In the last four sentences the negative of the relative clause is <span lang="grc">μή</span>.

##### <a id="s2507"></a> §2507

When the verb of the relative clause stands in the indicative, the distinction between a definite and indefinite antecedent is commonly clear only in negative sentences.

<span lang="grc">ἃ μὴ οἶδα οὐδὲ οἴομαι εἰδέναι</span> _whatever I do not know_ (= <span lang="grc">εἴ τινα μὴ οἶδα</span>) _I do not even think I know_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 21d">P. A. 21d</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">ἃ οὐκ οἶδα</span> would mean _the particular things I am ignorant of_, and would have no conditional force whatever. So in <span lang="grc">οὐκ οἶδ᾽· ἐφ᾽ οἷς γὰρ μὴ φρονῶ σῑγᾶν φιλῶ</span> _I do not know; for I am wont to be silent in matters which I do not understand_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 569">S. O. T. 569</cite>.

##### <a id="s2508"></a> §2508

When the antecedent is definite, the simple relatives (<span lang="grc">ὅς, οἷος, ὅσος</span>, etc.) are used; when indefinite, the compound relatives (<span lang="grc">ὅστις, ὁποῖος, ὁπόσος</span>, etc.) are used, but the simple relatives are often employed instead. When the antecedent is indefinite, <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> usually has the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or the optative; while <span lang="grc">ὅστις</span> is preferred to <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> if the verb is indicative ([2569](#s2569)).

##### <a id="s2509"></a> §2509

**Omission of the Antecedent to a Relative**.—The demonstrative pronoun antecedent to a relative is often omitted: either when it is in the same case as the relative, or in a different case from the relative. The omission occurs when the antecedent expresses the general idea of person or thing, and often when the relative clause precedes.

<span lang="grc">ἐγὼ δὲ καὶ</span> (<span lang="grc">οὗτοι</span>) <span lang="grc">ὧν κρατῶ μενοῦμεν</span> _but I and_ those _whom I command will remain_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.1.26">X. C. 5.1.26</cite>, <span lang="grc">καλὸν τὸ θνῄσκειν οἷς</span> (for <span lang="grc">τούτοις οἷς</span>) <span lang="grc">ὕβριν τὸ ζῆν φέρει</span> _death is sweet_ to those _to whom life brings contumely_ Men. Sent. 291, <span lang="grc">λέγω πάντας</span> <!-- page 565 --> <span lang="grc">εἰσφέρειν ἀφ᾽ ὅσων</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ τοσούτων ὅσα</span>) <span lang="grc">ἕκαστος ἔχει</span> _I say that all must contribute according to the ability of each_ (_from such means as each man has_) <cite data-ref="Dem. 2.31">D. 2.31</cite>.

##### <a id="s2510"></a> §2510

In general statements in the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or the indicative, the relative, referring to a person, is often without an antecedent and has the force of <span lang="grc">εἴ τις</span>. In such cases the main clause contains a substantive or a neuter adjective with <span lang="grc">ἐστί</span> (which is commonly omitted), and the relative is the subject of the sentence or in apposition to it.

<span lang="grc">συμφορὰ̄ δ᾽, δ̀ς ἂν τύχῃ κακῆς γυναικός</span> _it is a calamity if a man gets a bad wife_ E. fr. 1056, <span lang="grc">καὶ τοῦτο μεῖζον τῆς ἀληθείᾱς κακόν, ὅστις τὰ μὴ προσόντα κέκτηται κακά</span> _and this_ is _a misfortune exceeding the reality, if a man incurs_ the blame for _evils that are not his doing_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hel. 271">E. Hel. 271</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅστις . . . πρὸς θεῶν κακοῦται, βαρύ</span> _if a man suffers ill-usage from the gods, it_ is _grievous_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hel. 267">E. Hel. 267</cite>.

**a.** The antecedent may be a genitive of quality ([1320](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1320)). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀπόρων ἐστὶ . . ., οἵτινες ἐθέλουσι δι᾽ ἐπιορκίᾱς . . . πρά̄ττειν τι</span> _it is the characteristic of men without resources to wish_ (lit. _who wish_) _to accomplish their purposes by perjury_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.21">X. A. 2.5.21</cite> (here <span lang="grc">ἐθέλειν</span> alone might be expected, but <span lang="grc">οἵτινες ἐθέλουσι</span> follows as if <span lang="grc">ἄποροί εἰσιν</span> had preceded), <span lang="grc">τοῦτο ἡγοῦμαι μέγα τεκμήριον ἄρχοντος ἀρετῆς εἶναι ᾧ ἂν</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν τινι</span> or <span lang="grc">αὐτῷ</span>) <span lang="grc">ἑκόντες ἕπωνται</span> _I regard this as striking testimony to the merit of a ruler if men follow one_ (_him_) _of their own free will_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 4.19">X. O. 4.19</cite>.

##### <a id="s2511"></a> §2511

The antecedent of a neuter relative is often omitted, leaving the relative with the force of a conjunction. So <span lang="grc">ἐξ οὗ</span> and <span lang="grc">ἀφ᾽ οὗ</span> _since_, <span lang="grc">ἐν ᾧ</span> _while_, <span lang="grc">εἰς &lt;\*&gt;῞</span> _till_, <span lang="grc">μέχρι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἄχρι</span>) <span lang="grc">οὗ</span> _until_. <span lang="grc">ἀνθ᾽ ὧν</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐξ ὧν</span> _because_ (cp. <span lang="grc">οὕνεκα, ὁθούνεκα</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐφ᾽ ᾧτε</span> _on condition that_ ([2279](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/08-clauses-with-epsilonphi-omega-and-epsilonphi-omegatauepsilon.md#s2279)).

##### <a id="s2512"></a> §2512

A demonstrative adverb may be suppressed: <span lang="grc">ἄξω ὑ̄μᾶς ἔνθα</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖσε ἔνθα</span>) <span lang="grc">τὸ πρᾶγμα ἐγένετο</span> _I will bring you_ to the spot _where the affair took place_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.4.21">X. C. 5.4.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀποκλείοντες ὅθεν</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖθεν ὅθεν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἄν τι λαβεῖν ᾖ</span> _shutting them out_ from places _whence it may be possible to take anything_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.1.16">X. M. 2.1.16</cite>.

##### <a id="s2513"></a> §2513

<span lang="grc">**ἔστιν ὅστις, εἰσὶν οἵ**.</span>—The antecedent is omitted in the phrases <span lang="grc">ἔστιν ὅστις</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">ὅς</span>) _there is some one who, somebody_, plural <span lang="grc">εἰσὶν οἵ</span> _some_ (less often <span lang="grc">ἔστιν οἵ</span>), <span lang="grc">ἦσαν οἵ</span> (of the past).

<span lang="grc">ἔστιν οὖν ὅστις βούλεται ὑπὸ τῶν συνόντων βλάπτεσθαι</span>; _is there then any one who wishes to be harmed by his companions?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 25d">P. A. 25d</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὔτε..ἔστιν οὔτ᾽ ἔσται ὅτῳ ἐγὼ καταλείψω τὸν ἐμὸν οἶκον</span> _there neither is nor will there be any one to whom I may leave my property_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.4.30">X. C. 5.4.30</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰσὶ δὲ καὶ οἳ . . . φεύγουσιν</span> _some_ horses _too run away_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Horse. 3.4">X. Eq. 3.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰσὶ δ᾽ αὐτῶν οὓς οὐδ᾽ ἂν παντάπᾱσι διαβαίητε</span> _and some of them you would not be able even to cross at all_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.18">X. A. 2.5.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἦσαν δὲ οἳ καὶ πῦρ προσέφερον</span> _and some brought firebrands too_ 5. 2. 14, <span lang="grc">ἔστιν ὅτῳ . . . πλείω ἐπιτρέπεις ἢ τῇ γυναικί</span>; _is there any one to whom you entrust more than to your wife?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 3.12">X. O. 3.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔστιν οἵ καὶ ἐτύγχανον καὶ θωρά̄κων καὶ γέρρων</span> _some hit both the cuirasses and wicker-shields_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.3.18">X. C. 2.3.18</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἔστιν οἵ</span> is not an example of 961, but due to the analogy of <span lang="grc">ἔστιν ὅτε</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐνίοτε</span>), <span lang="grc">ἔστιν οὗ</span>, etc.

##### <a id="s2514"></a> §2514

The oblique cases of <span lang="grc">εἰσὶν οἵ</span> _there are those who_ = _some_ (<span lang="grc">ἔνιοι</span>) are regularly formed by <span lang="grc">ἔστιν ὧν, ἔστιν οἷς, ἔστιν οὕς</span> (or <span lang="grc">οὕστινας</span>), which are used also of the past and future. <!-- page 566 -->

<span lang="grc">πλὴν Ἰώνων . . . καὶ ἔστιν ὧν ἄλλων ἐθνῶν</span> _except the Ionians and some other nations_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.92">T. 3.92</cite>, <span lang="grc">αὐχμοὶ ἔστι παρ᾽ οἷς μεγάλοι</span> _great droughts among some_ 1. 23, <span lang="grc">ἔστι μὲν οὓς αὐτῶν κατέβαλον</span> _some of them they struck down_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.4.6">X. H. 2.4.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔστιν ἃ καὶ πολίσματα εἷλεν</span> _he captured also some towns_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.65">T. 1.65</cite>.

**a.** Xenophon also uses <span lang="grc">ἦν οἵ</span>; thus, <span lang="grc">τῶν δὲ πολεμίων ἦν οὓς ὑποσπόνδους ἀπέδοσαν</span> _there were some of the enemy whom they restored under a truce_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 7.5.17">X. H. 7.5.17</cite>.

##### <a id="s2515"></a> §2515

Here belong certain idiomatic phrases due to the omission of the antecedent: <span lang="grc"> **ἔστιν οὗ** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **ὅπου** </span>) _somewhere_, _sometimes_, <span lang="grc"> **ἔστιν ᾗ** </span> _in some way_, <span lang="grc"> **ἔστιν ὅτε** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **ἐνίοτε** </span> (= <span lang="grc"> **ἔνι ὅτε** </span>, cp. [175](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s175) b) _sometimes_, <span lang="grc"> **ἔστιν ὅπως** </span> _somehow_ (in questions = _is it possible that?_), <span lang="grc"> **οὐκ ἔστιν ὅπως** </span> _in no way_, _it is not possible that_ (lit. _there is not how_).

<span lang="grc">ἔστι δ᾽ οὗ σῑγὴ λόγου κρείσσων γένοιτ᾽ ἄν</span> _but sometimes silence may prove better than speech_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Orest. 638">E. Or. 638</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔστιν ὅτε καὶ οἷς</span> ([2514](#s2514)) <span lang="grc">βέλτῑον τεθνάναι ἢ ζῆν</span> _sometimes and for some people it is better to die than to live_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 62a">P. Ph. 62a</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔσθ᾽ ὅπως . . . ἂν ἡμᾶς ἔτι λάθοι</span> _it is not possible that he should elude us again_ A. Vesp. 212, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔστιν ὅπως οὐκ ἐπιθήσεται ἡμῖν</span> _it is not possible that he will not attack us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.4.3">X. A. 2.4.3</cite>.

##### <a id="s2516"></a> §2516

<span lang="grc"> **οὐδὲν οἷον** </span> (with the inf.) _there is nothing like_ stands for <span lang="grc">οὐδέν ἐστι τοιοῦτον, οἷόν ἐστι</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν οἷον τὸ αὐτὸν ἐρωτᾶν</span> _there is nothing like questioning him_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 447c">P. G. 447c</cite>.

##### <a id="s2517"></a> §2517

**Relative not repeated**.—If two or more relative clauses referring to the same antecedent are connected by a copulative conjunction and the second relative would have to stand in a different case from the first, it is either omitted or its place is taken by <span lang="grc">αὐτός</span> (less frequently by <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> or <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος</span>) or a personal pronoun. Here, instead of a repeated relative, we have an independent sentence coördinated with the relative clause.

<span lang="grc">Ἀριαῖος, ὃν ἡμεῖς ἠθέλομεν βασιλέᾱ καθιστάναι, καὶ</span> (<span lang="grc">ᾧ</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐδώκαμεν καὶ</span> (<span lang="grc">παρ᾽ οὗ</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐλάβομεν πιστὰ . . . ἡμᾶς κακῶς ποιεῖν πειρᾶται</span> _Ariaeus_, _whom we wished to set up as king, and to whom we gave, and from whom we received pledges, is attempting to injure us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.5">X. A. 3.2.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ποῦ δὴ ἐκεῖνός ἐστιν ὁ ἀνὴρ δ̀ς συνεθήρᾱ ἡμῖν καὶ σύ μοι μάλα ἐδόκεις θαυμάζειν αὐτόν</span>; _where_, _pray_, _is that man who used to hunt with us and whom you seemed to me to admire greatly?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.38">X. C. 3.1.38</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ νῦν τί χρὴ δρᾶν; ὅστις ἐμφανῶς θεοῖς ἐχθαίρομαι, μῑσεῖ δέ μ᾽ Ἑλλήνων στρατός</span> _and now what must I do? Since I_ (lit. _I who_) _am manifestly hateful to the gods, and the army of the Greeks hates me_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 457">S. Aj. 457</cite>. Cp. “Whose fan is in His hand, and He shall thoroughly purge His floor.”

**a.** The relative is sometimes repeated as in English (<cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.3">X. A. 1.7.3</cite>, <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.43">T. 2.43</cite>. 2, 44. 1).

##### <a id="s2518"></a> §2518

If the demonstrative would have to stand in the nominative, it is commonly omitted unless the demands of emphasis require its presence: (<span lang="grc">τέχναις</span>) <span lang="grc">ᾱς ἐπιστήμᾱς μὲν πολλάκις προσείπομεν διὰ τὸ ἔθος, δέονται δὲ ὀνόματος ἄλλου</span> arts _which we have often called sciences because it is usual to do so, but_ they _require another name_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 533d">P. R. 533d</cite> (here <span lang="grc">αὗται</span>, not <span lang="grc">αἵ</span>, is the subject). <!-- page 567 -->

##### <a id="s2519"></a> §2519

**Preposition not repeated**.—A preposition governing a relative pronoun is usually omitted if it stands in the same case as the preceding noun or pronoun before which the preposition has already been used. See [1671](19-prepositions.md#s1671).

##### <a id="s2520"></a> §2520

**Verb omitted**.—The verb of a relative clause is often omitted when it belongs also to the main clause.

<span lang="grc">φίλους νομίζουσ᾽ οὕσπερ ἂν πόσις σέθεν</span> (<span lang="grc">νομίζῃ φίλους</span>) _regarding as friends those whom thy husband_ so regards <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 1153">E. Med. 1153</cite>. Or the verb of the main clause may be omitted: <span lang="grc">τὰ γὰρ ἄλλα</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐποίει</span>) <span lang="grc">ὅσαπερ καὶ ὑ̄μεῖς ἐποιεῖτε</span> _for the rest_ he did _just what you too were doing_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.1.3">X. C. 4.1.3</cite>.

##### <a id="s2521"></a> §2521

Transition from a relative to an independent clause sometimes occurs.

(<span lang="grc">ἰχθύων</span>) <span lang="grc">οὓς οἱ Σύροι θεοὺς ἐνόμιζον καὶ ἀδικεῖν οὐκ εἴων, οὐδὲ τὰ̄ς περιστερά̄ς</span> fish _which the Syrians regard as gods and_ which _they will not permit to be injured_, _nor_ do they permit _the doves_ to be injured <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.9">X. A. 1.4.9</cite>.

##### <a id="s2522"></a> §2522

**Attraction**.—A relative pronoun is often attracted from its proper case into the case of its antecedent, especially from the accusative into the genitive or dative. A demonstrative pronoun to whose case the relative is attracted, is usually omitted if unemphatic. Cp. “Vengeance is his, or whose he sole appoints:” Milton.

**a. Genitive**.—<span lang="grc">ἄξιοι τῆς ἐλευθερίᾱς ἧς</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἣν</span>) <span lang="grc">κέκτησθε</span> _worthy of the freedom which you possess_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.3">X. A. 1.7.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρὸ τῶν κακῶν ὧν</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἃ</span>) <span lang="grc">οἶδα</span> _instead of the evils which I know_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 29b">P. A. 29b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀφ᾽ ὧν</span> (for <span lang="grc">τούτων ἃ</span>) <span lang="grc">ἴστε</span> _from what you know_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.216">D. 19.216</cite>, <span lang="grc">Μήδων ὅσων</span> (for <span lang="grc">ὅσους</span>) <span lang="grc">ἑώρᾱκα . . . ὁ ἐμὸς πάππος κάλλιστος</span> _my grandfather is the handsomest of_ all _the Medes I have seen_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.2">X. C. 1.3.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">μὴ ὑποκειμένων οἵων δεῖ θεμελίων</span> (for <span lang="grc">τοιούτων οἷα δεῖ ὑποκεῖσθαι</span>) _if the foundations were not as they ought to be_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Horse. 1.2">X. Eq. 1.2</cite>.

**b. Dative**.—<span lang="grc">φοβοίμην ἂν τῷ ἡγεμόνι ᾧ</span> (for <span lang="grc">δ̀ν</span>) <span lang="grc">δοίη ἕπεσθαι</span> _I should fear to follow the leader whom he might give_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.17">X. A. 1.3.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπαινῶ σε ἐφ᾽ οἷς</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ τούτοις ἃ</span>) <span lang="grc">λέγεις</span> _I commend you for what you say_ 3. 1. 45, <span lang="grc">οἷς</span> (for <span lang="grc">τούτοις ἃ</span>) <span lang="grc">ηὐτυχήκεσαν ἐν Λεύκτροις οὐ μετρίως ἐκέχρηντο</span> _they had not used with moderation the success they gained at Leuctra_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.18">D. 18.18</cite>.

##### <a id="s2523"></a> §2523

A relative in the nominative or dative is very rarely attracted. Thus, <span lang="grc">βλάπτεσθαι ἀφ᾽ ὧν</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ ἐκείνων ἃ</span>) <span lang="grc">ἡμῖν παρεσκεύασται</span> _to be harmed by what has been prepared by us_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.67">T. 7.67</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὀλίγοι ὧν</span> (for <span lang="grc">τούτων οἷς</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐγὼ ἐντετύχηκα</span> _a few of those whom I have met with_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 531e">P. R. 531e</cite>.

##### <a id="s2524"></a> §2524

The pronouns subject to attraction are <span lang="grc">ὅς, οἷος, ὅσος</span>, but not <span lang="grc">ὅστις</span> (except in [2534](#s2534)). Attraction is not _necessary_, and takes place only (but not always) when the relative clause is essential to complete the meaning of the antecedent. When the relative clause is added merely as a remark, attraction does not take place. An attracted relative clause virtually has the force of an attributive adjective.

##### <a id="s2525"></a> §2525

Predicate nouns follow the case of the relative attracted to an antecedent expressed or omitted ([2531 b](#s2531)). <!-- page 568 -->

##### <a id="s2526"></a> §2526

An omitted antecedent to which the relative has been attracted may afterward be supplied in the main clause. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀφ᾽ ὧν</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ τούτων ἃ</span>) . . . <span lang="grc">προσαιτεῖ καὶ δανείζεται, ἀπὸ τούτων διάγει</span> _from what he begs and borrows, from that he lives_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.26">D. 8.26</cite>.

##### <a id="s2527"></a> §2527

Before <span lang="grc"> **βούλει** </span>, which with the relative is treated almost like one word (cp. _quivis_), attraction to various cases from the accusative is rare. Thus, <span lang="grc">οἷα τούτων δ̀ς</span> (for <span lang="grc">δ̀ν</span>) <span lang="grc">βούλει εἴργασται</span> _such deeds as any one you please of these has done_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 517a">P. G. 517a</cite>; cp. <cite data-ref="Plat. Crat. 432a">P. Crat. 432a</cite>, Phil. 43 d.

##### <a id="s2528"></a> §2528

Attraction takes place also in the case of relative adverbs; as <span lang="grc">διεκομίζοντο ὅθεν</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖθεν οἷ</span>) <span lang="grc">ὑπεξέθεντο παῖδας</span> _they conveyed their children from the places where_ (_whither_) _they had deposited them_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.89">T. 1.89</cite>.

##### <a id="s2529"></a> §2529

**Case of the Relative with Omitted Antecedent**.—When the antecedent is omitted the relative either retains its own case or is attracted.

##### <a id="s2530"></a> §2530

When the omitted antecedent is nominative or accusative, the relative retains its own case. Thus, <span lang="grc">οἷς μάλιστα τὰ παρόντα ἀρκεῖ</span> (<span lang="grc">οὗτοι</span>) <span lang="grc">ἥκιστα τῶν ἀλλοτρίων ὀρέγονται</span> _those who are best satisfied with what they have, covet least what is their neighbour's_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 4">X. S. 4</cite>. 42, <span lang="grc">στυγῶν μὲν ἥ</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἐκείνην ἥ</span>) <span lang="grc">μ᾽ ἔτικτεν</span> _hating_ her _who bore me_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Alc. 338">E. Alc. 338</cite>.

##### <a id="s2531"></a> §2531

When the omitted antecedent is genitive or dative, the relative (if standing in a different case) is usually attracted into the genitive or dative. But a relative in the nominative masculine or feminine (sometimes in the neuter), or a relative depending on a preposition, retains its own case.

**a. Genitive**: <span lang="grc">ὧν</span> (for <span lang="grc">τούτων οἷς</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐντυγχάνω πολὺ μάλιστα ἄγαμαι σέ</span> _of those whom I meet with_, _I admire you by far the most_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 361e">P. Pr. 361e</cite>, <span lang="grc">δηλοῖς δὲ καὶ ἐξ ὧν</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἐκ τούτων ἃ</span>) <span lang="grc">ζῇς</span> _you show it also by the life you lead_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.198">D. 18.198</cite>. But <span lang="grc">εἰδέναι τὴν δύναμιν</span> (<span lang="grc">τούτων</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐφ᾽ οὓς ἂν ἴωσιν</span> _to discover the strength_ of those _against whom they are to proceed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.1.8">X. A. 5.1.8</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Eur. Ion 560">E. Ion 560</cite> (in [2488](#s2488)) where <span lang="grc">οἵ ῀ τούτων οἵ</span>).

**b. Dative**: <span lang="grc">τοῦτο δ᾽ ὅμοιόν ἐστιν ᾧ</span> (for <span lang="grc">τούτῳ δ̀</span>) <span lang="grc">νῦν δὴ ἐλέγετο</span> _this is like that which was said just now_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 69a">P. Ph. 69a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐμμένομεν οἷς</span> (for <span lang="grc">τούτοις ἃ</span>) <span lang="grc">ὡμολογήσαμεν δικαίοις οὖσιν ἢ οὔ</span>; _do we abide by what we agreed was just, or not?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 50a">P. Cr. 50a</cite>. But <span lang="grc">διὰ τὸ ἀναγκαῖον αὐτοῖς εἶναι διαλέγεσθαι</span> (<span lang="grc">τούτοις</span>) <span lang="grc">παρ᾽ ὧν λάβοιεν τὸν μισθόν</span> _because it is necessary for them to give lessons_ to those _from whom they expect to receive their fee_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.2.6">X. M. 1.2.6</cite>.

##### <a id="s2532"></a> §2532

The relatives <span lang="grc">οἷος, ὅσος, ἡλίκος, ὅστις δή, ὁστισοῦν</span> (and some others) and a following nominative with the copula may be attracted to the case of the antecedent. Thus, <span lang="grc">χαριζόμενος τοιούτῳ ἀνδρὶ οἷος δὺ εἶ</span> _showing favour to such a man as you are_ is commonly condensed to <span lang="grc">χαριζόμενος οἵῳ σοι ἀνδρί</span> (<cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.9.3">X. M. 2.9.3</cite>). Here the whole relative clause (with copula omitted) is attracted. The antecedent, if expressed, is often incorporated ([2536](#s2536)) in the relative clause.

<span lang="grc">πρὸς ἄνδρας τολμηροὺς οἵους καὶ Ἀθηναίους</span> (for <span lang="grc">οι῟οι καὶ Ἀθηναῖοί εἰσι</span>) _to bold men such as the Athenians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.21">T. 7.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀνίστη Ἀγριᾶνας . . . καὶ ἄλλα ὅσα ἔθνη Παιονικά</span> _he called out the Agrianes and all the other Paeonian tribes_ 2. 96, <span lang="grc">χειμῶνος ὄντος</span> <!-- page 569 --> <span lang="grc">οἵου λέγεις</span> _when the weather is such as you describe_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.8.3">X. A. 5.8.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀνέλαμψεν οἰκίᾱ . . . ὅτου δὴ ἐνάψαντος</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἐνάψαντός τινος ὅστις δὴ ἦν</span>) _the house burst into flames_, _some one or other having set it on fire_ 5. 2. 24.

**a.** <span lang="grc">οἷος</span> is often attracted with superlatives: <span lang="grc">ὄντος πάγου οἵου δεινοτάτου</span> (for <span lang="grc">τοιούτου οἷός ἐστι δεινότατος</span>) _when the frost was tremendous_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 220b">P. S. 220b</cite>. Cp. [1087](14-adjectives.md#s1087).

**b.** The article may appear in this construction with <span lang="grc">οἷος</span> and <span lang="grc">ἡλίκος</span>, the relative clause being treated like a substantive: <span lang="grc">τοῖς οἵοις ἡμῖν</span> _to such as we are_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.3.25">X. H. 2.3.25</cite>.

**c.** The subject of the relative clause rarely stands in the nominative, not being attracted along with <span lang="grc">οἷος</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">κιναίδους οἵουσπερ σύ</span> _rascals just like you_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 2.151">Aes. 2.151</cite>. This occurs only when the number of the subject is different from that of the attracted relative. When the article precedes, as in <span lang="grc">Σόλων ἐμί̄σει τοὺς οἷος οὗτος ἀνθρώπους</span> _Solon detested men like him_ (<cite data-ref="Dem. 19.254">D. 19.254</cite>), editors generally read <span lang="grc">τοὺς οἵους οὗτος</span>.

##### <a id="s2533"></a> §2533

**Inverse Attraction**.—An antecedent nominative or (oftener) accusative may be attracted to the case of the relative. The attracted antecedent is often prefixed for emphasis to the relative clause, which thus separates it from the verb it governs or by which it is governed. Cp. _urbem quam statuo vestra est_, and “Him (= he whom) I accuse, By this, the city ports hath enter'd” (Shakespeare), where the antecedent is attracted into the case of the (omitted) relative.

<span lang="grc">τά̄σδε</span> (for <span lang="grc">αἵδε</span>) <span lang="grc">δ᾽ ἅ̄σπερ εἰσορᾷς . . . χωροῦσι</span> _but the women whom thou seest are coming_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Trach. 283">S. Tr. 283</cite>, <span lang="grc">πολῑτείᾱν</span> (for <span lang="grc">πολῑτείᾱ</span>) <span lang="grc">οἵᾱν εἶναι χρή παρὰ μόνοις ἡμῖν ἐστιν</span> _we alone have an ideal constitution_ (lit. _such as ought to be_) <cite data-ref="Isoc. 6.48">I. 6.48</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔλεγον ὅτι Λακεδαιμόνιοι ὧν δέονται πάντων</span> (for <span lang="grc">πάντα</span>) <span lang="grc">πεπρᾱγότες εἶεν</span> _they said that the Lacedaemonians had gained all they asked for_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 1.4.2">X. H. 1.4.2</cite>.

**a.** The main clause may contain a resumptive demonstrative pronoun; as <span lang="grc">τὸν ἄνδρα τοῦτον, δ̀ν πάλαι ζητεῖς . . ., οὗτός ἐστιν ἐνθάδε</span> _this man whom you have long been searching for, this man is here_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 449">S. O. T. 449</cite>.

**b.** The rare cases of the inverse attraction of the dative are suspected or admit another explanation (<cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 12">E. Med. 12</cite>, <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 653">S. El. 653</cite>, <cite data-ref="Xen. Hiero 7.2">X. Hi. 7.2</cite>).

**c.** So with adverbs: <span lang="grc">καὶ ἄλλοσε</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἄλλοθι</span>) <span lang="grc">ὅποι ἂν ἀφίκῃ ἀγαπήσουσί δε</span> _and elsewhere_, _wherever you go, they will love you_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 45c">P. Cr. 45c</cite>.

##### <a id="s2534"></a> §2534

<span lang="grc"> **οὐδεὶς ὅστις οὐ** </span> _every one_ (lit. _nobody who not_) for <span lang="grc">οὐδείς ἐστιν ὅστις οὐ</span>, commonly shows inverse attraction, is treated like a single pronoun, and inflected <span lang="grc">οὐδενὸς ὅτου οὐ, οὐδενὶ ὅτῳ οὐ, οὐδένα ὅντινα οὐ</span>.

<span lang="grc">οὐδενὸς ὅτου οὐχὶ ἀλογώτερον</span> _than which there is nothing more irrational_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Charm. 175c">P. Charm. 175c</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδενὶ ὅτῳ οὐκ ἀποκρῑνόμενος</span> _replying to every one_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Meno 70c">P. Men. 70c</cite>, <span lang="grc">περὶ ὧν οὐδένα κίνδῡνον ὅντιν᾽ οὐχ ὑπέμειναν οἱ πρόγονοι</span> _for which our ancestors underwent every danger_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.200">D. 18.200</cite>.

**a.** Cp. <span lang="grc">οὐδαμῶν Ἑλληνικῶν τῶν οὐ πολλὸν μέζω</span> his power _was much greater than any Hellenic power_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 7.145">Hdt. 7.145</cite> (= <span lang="grc">οὐδαμά ἐστι τῶν</span>), <span lang="grc">οὐδαμῶς ὡς οὐ φήσομεν</span> _it can in no wise be that we should say no_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Stat. 308b">P. Pol. 308b</cite>.

##### <a id="s2535"></a> §2535

**<span lang="grc">ὅσος</span> preceded by an Adjective**.—Here the subject of the relative clause is identical with that of the main clause, and is omitted together with the <!-- page 570 --> copula: <span lang="grc">χρήματα ἔλαβε θαυμαστὰ ὅσα</span> (for <span lang="grc">θαυμαστόν ἐστιν ὅσα</span>) _he received_ a _wonderful amount of money_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Hipp. Maj. 282c">P. Hipp. M. 282c</cite>, <span lang="grc">μετὰ ἱδρῶτος θαυμαστοῦ ὅσου</span> (for <span lang="grc">θαυμαστόν ἐστιν μεθ᾽ ὅσου</span>) _with an astonishing amount of sweat_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 350d">P. R. 350d</cite>. So <span lang="grc">θαυμασίως ὡς</span> (for <span lang="grc">θαυμαστόν ἐστιν ὡς</span>) <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 92a">P. Ph. 92a</cite>.

##### <a id="s2536"></a> §2536

**Incorporation**.—The antecedent taken up into the relative clause is said to be incorporated. The relative and antecedent then stand in the same case, the relative agreeing adjectively with its antecedent. If the antecedent is a substantive, it often stands at the end of the relative clause, and commonly has no article. An antecedent in the nominative or accusative is more frequently incorporated than one in the genitive or dative.

##### <a id="s2537"></a> §2537

A nominative, accusative, or vocative antecedent, when incorporated, usually conforms to the case of the relative.

<span lang="grc">εἰ ἔστιν, ἣν σὺ πρότερον ἔλεγες ἀρετήν, ἀληθής</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἔστιν ἡ ἀρετὴ ἀληθής, ἣν</span>) _if the virtue which you were speaking of before, is real_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 503c">P. G. 503c</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰς δὲ ἣν ἀφί̄κοντο κώμην μεγάλη ἦν</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἡ κώμη εἰς ἣν</span>) _the village at which they arrived was large_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.4.2">X. A. 4.4.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">κλῦθί μευ, δ̀ χθιζὸς θεὸς ἤλυθες</span> (for <span lang="grc">θεὸς δ̀</span> or <span lang="grc">ὦ θεός</span>) _hear me thou that camest yesterday in thy godhead_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 2.262"><span lang="grc">β</span> 262</cite>.

**a.** An accusative antecedent is incorporated in the accusative when the verb of the relative clause takes the accusative. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀπεκρύπτετο ἣν εἶχε γνώμην</span> (for <span lang="grc">τὴν γνώμην ἣν</span>) _he did not conceal the opinion he had_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.4.1">X. M. 4.4.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">μηδ᾽ . . . ἀφέλησθε ὑ̄μῶν αὐτῶν ἣν διὰ παντὸς ἀεὶ τοῦ χρόνου δόξαν κέκτησθε καλήν</span> (for <span lang="grc">τὴν καλὴν δόξαν ἣν</span>) _do not deprive yourselves of the fair fame which you have enjoyed throughout all time_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 20.142">D. 20.142</cite>.

**b.** An accusative antecedent may be incorporated as nominative, genitive, or dative, e.g. <span lang="grc">εἴ τινα ὁρῴη . . . κατασκευάζοντα ἧς ἄρχοι χώρᾱς</span> (for <span lang="grc">τὴν χώρᾱν ἧς ἄρχοι</span>) _if ever he saw any one improving the district which he governed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.9.19">X. A. 1.9.19</cite>.

##### <a id="s2538"></a> §2538

A genitive or dative antecedent, when incorporated, usually attracts the relative to its own case.

<span lang="grc">περὶ δ᾽ οὗ πρότερον . . . ἔθηκε νόμου διελθών</span> (for <span lang="grc">τοῦ νόμου δ̀ν</span>) _dealing in detail with the law which he formerly passed_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 24.61">D. 24.61</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπορεύετο σὺν ᾗ εἶχε δυνάμει</span> (for <span lang="grc">σὺν τῇ δυνάμει ἣν</span>) _he advanced with what force he had_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.1.23">X. H. 4.1.23</cite>. Even when the antecedent is omitted, the attraction takes place: <span lang="grc">πρὸς ᾧ εἶχε συνέλεγε . . . στράτευμα</span> (for <span lang="grc">πρὸς τούτῳ τῷ στρατεύματι δ̀</span>) _he was collecting an army in addition to that which he had_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.1.41">X. H. 4.1.41</cite>.

**a.** But a genitive or dative antecedent, when incorporated, is attracted into the case of a _nominative_ relative. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐν δικαστηρίοις καὶ ὅσοι ἄλλοι δημόσιοι σύλλογοι</span> (sc. <span lang="grc">εἰσί</span>) _in courts and all the other public assemblies_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 261a">P. Phae. 261a</cite> (for <span lang="grc">τοσούτοις ἄλλοις συλλόγοις, ὅσοι δημόσιοί εἰσι</span>).

**b.** When an antecedent in the genitive or dative is incorporated, the place of the antecedent is usually taken by a demonstrative pronoun in the genitive or dative. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐδέ νυ τῶν περ μέμνηαι, ὅσα δὴ πάθομεν κακά</span> _nor do you remember all the evils we suffered_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 21.441"><span lang="grc">Φ</span> 441</cite>.

### OTHER PECULIARITIES OF RELATIVE CLAUSES

<!-- page 571 -->

##### <a id="s2539"></a> §2539

Appositives to the antecedent may be drawn into the relative clause as the nearest construction or for the sake of emphasis. Thus, <span lang="grc">εὑρήσει τοὺς . . . δικαστά̄ς, οἵπερ καὶ λέγονται ἐκεῖ δικάζειν, Μί̄νως τε καὶ Ῥαδάμανθυς κτλ</span>. _he will find the judges, who are said to pronounce judgment there_, _Minos and Rhadamanthys_, etc. <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 41a">P. A. 41a</cite>.

##### <a id="s2540"></a> §2540

A substantive, usually with the article, is often taken over into the relative clause, to explain, by a necessary addition, the idea conveyed by that clause; and stands in the same case as the relative. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ μανθάνεις ὃ βούλομαι λέγειν τὸ εἶδος</span> _if you understand the class I wish to describe_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 477c">P. R. 477c</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὔτε αὐτοὶ οὔτε οὕς φαμεν ἡμῖν παιδευτέον εἶναι τοὺς φύλακας</span> _neither ourselves nor the guardians whom we say we must instruct_ 402 c.

##### <a id="s2541"></a> §2541

The antecedent may be reserved for the main clause, which follows the relative clause. Thus, <span lang="grc">καθ᾽ οὓς μὲν ἀπήχθην, οὐκ ἔνοχός εἰμι τοῖς νόμοις</span> _I am not subject to the laws in virtue of which I was arrested_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.85">Ant. 5.85</cite>.

##### <a id="s2542"></a> §2542

An attributive adjective, or an attributive genitive belonging to a substantive standing in the main clause, may be placed either in the relative clause (if either is emphatic) or in the main clause. Two adjectives may be divided between the two clauses. The substantives may remain in the main clause or be transferred to the relative clause. Thus, <span lang="grc">τὸ τείχισμα δ̀ ἦν αὐτόθι τῶν Συρᾱκοσίων αἱροῦσι</span> _they captured the fort of the Syracusans which was there_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.43">T. 7.43</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὧν ἐγὼ ἤθελον τούτῳ ταύτην ἥτις εἴη μεγίστη πίστις δοῦναι</span> _of which I was willing to offer to the plaintiff the assurance that was most solemn_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 52.12">D. 52.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπιδεῖξαι . . . τὴν δικαίᾱν ἥτις ἐστὶν ἀπολογίᾱ</span> _to show what the fair line of defence is_ 19. 203, <span lang="grc">ἔφρῑξεν δὲ μάχη . . . ἐγχείῃσιν μακρῇς, ἃ̄ς εἶχον ταμεσίχροας</span> _and the battle bristled with the long spears, the flesh-piercing spears, which they grasped_ N 339.

**a.** From the transference of superlatives to the relative clause arise such expressions as <span lang="grc">ἤγαγον συμμάχους ὁπόσους πλείστους ἐδυνάμην</span> ([1087](14-adjectives.md#s1087)). Similarly <span lang="grc">ὡς τάχιστα</span> (scil. <span lang="grc">δύνασαι</span> or the like) _as soon as, as soon as possible_, <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅτε</span>) <span lang="grc">τάχιστα</span> _as soon as_.

##### <a id="s2543"></a> §2543

A participial or subordinate clause depending on a following main clause may be joined to a preceding clause containing the antecedent of the relative. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔφη εἶναι ἄκρον δ̀ εἰ μή τις προκαταλήψοιτο, ἀδύνατον ἔσεσθαι παρελθεῖν</span> _he said that there was a height which would be impossible to pass_, _unless it was seized in advance_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.1.25">X. A. 4.1.25</cite>. The case of the relative may be not that required by its own verb, but that of an omitted pronoun dependent on a participle or a subordinate verb inserted in the relative clause. Thus, <span lang="grc">καταλαμβάνουσι τεῖχος . . . ὅ ποτε Ἀκαρνᾶνες τειχισάμενοι κοινῷ δικαστηρίῳ ἐχρῶντο</span> (for <span lang="grc">ᾧ ἐχρῶντο τειχισάμενοι αὐτό</span>) _they seized a fortress which the Acarnanians once built and used as a common place of judgment_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.105">T. 3.105</cite>.

##### <a id="s2544"></a> §2544

When the relative clause contains a _verb of naming_, the main clause is fused with the relative clause. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔνθα καλεῖται Ἀρτέμιδος τέμενος</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἔνθα τέμενός ἐστι, δ̀ καλεῖται Ἀρτέμιδος</span>) _where there is a precinct of Artemis_ Simonides 107.

## USE OF THE MOODS IN RELATIVE CLAUSES

<!-- page 572 -->

##### <a id="s2545"></a> §2545

The ordinary uses of the moods in relative clauses are as follows:

**a.** The present and past tenses of the indicative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> express a fact or the assumption of a fact. The future indicative is used to denote purpose, present intention, or an intended result.

**b.** The indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> denotes unreality.

**c.** The subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> expresses a possible or supposed fact in future time or a generality in present time. The subjunctive without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is used in indirect questions ([1805 b](20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1805)).

**d.** The optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> expresses a wish, a possibility less distinctly conceived, or a generality in past time.

**e.** The optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is potential, and is used either in conditional relative clauses with an optative in the main clause, or alone, as <span lang="grc">μἴ ἔστιν ἐλπίς, ᾗ μόνῃ σωθεῖμεν ἄν</span> _there is one hope by which alone we may be saved_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hel. 815">E. Hel. 815</cite>.

**f.** The imperative occurs in relative clauses ([1842](20-the-verb-voices/08-imperative.md#s1842), [2553](#s2553)).

**g.** The infinitive occurs in relative clauses in indirect discourse ([2631](31-dependent-substantive-clauses-2574-2635.md#s2631)).

### THE USE OF THE MOODS IN CERTAIN RELATIVE CLAUSES

##### <a id="s2546"></a> §2546

An extension of the deliberative subjunctive not infrequently occurs in relative clauses after such expressions as <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔχω, οὐκ ἔστι</span>, etc., which usually denote baffled will, the existence of an obstacle to carrying out an act desired by the speaker or some one else. The subjunctive is much less common after the positive <span lang="grc">ἔχω</span> _I have the means_. The pronoun or adverb introducing such clause is an interrogative that has taken on the function of a relative.

##### <a id="s2547"></a> §2547

The subjunctive here follows primary tenses; the optative follows secondary tenses.

**a.** <span lang="grc">οὐ τοῦτο δέδοικα μὴ οὐκ ἔχω ὅ τι δῶ ἑκάστῳ τῶν φίλων . . ., ἀλλὰ μὴ οὐκ ἔχω ἱκανοὺς οἷς δῶ</span> _I do not fear that I shall not have something to give to each of my friends_, _but that I shall not have enough friends to give to_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.7">X. A. 1.7.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐχ ἕξουσιν ἐκεῖνοι ὅποι φύγωσιν</span> _they will not have_ any place _whither to escape_ 2. 4. 20, <span lang="grc">οὐκέτ᾽ εἰσὶν ἐλπίδες ὅποι τραπόμενος θάνατον . . . φύγω</span> _I have no longer any hopes to which I may turn and escape death_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Orest. 722">E. Or. 722</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἕξει ὅ τι λέγῃ</span> _he will be able to say something_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 6.42">L. 6.42</cite>.

**b.** <span lang="grc">οὐδένα γὰρ εἶχον ὅστις . . . τὰ̄ς ἐμὰ̄ς ἐπιστολὰ̄ς πέμψειε</span> _for I had no one to bring my letter_ <cite data-ref="Eur. IT 588">E. I. T. 588</cite>.

**c.** Attic never, or rarely, has the _positive_ forms <span lang="grc">ἔχω ὅ τι ἄν, ἔστιν δ̀ς ἄν</span> (K [170](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s170)), <span lang="grc">πέμπω ὅστις ἄν</span>, with the potential optative.

##### <a id="s2548"></a> §2548

The subjunctive _with_ <span lang="grc">κέ</span> in Homer does not involve _will_ in <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔσθ᾽ οὗτος ἀνὴρ . . . οὐδὲ γένηται, ὅς κεν Φαιήκων . . . ἐς γαῖαν ἵκηται</span> _that man lives not nor will ever be born who shall come to the land of the Phaeacians_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 6.202"><span lang="grc">ζ</span> 202</cite>; cp. <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 4.756"><span lang="grc">δ</span> 756</cite>, <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 23.345"><span lang="grc">Ψ</span> 345</cite>. <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 21.103"><span lang="grc">Φ</span> 103</cite> involves a different aspect of _will_ from that in 2547 a.

##### <a id="s2549"></a> §2549

The deliberative future ([1916](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1916)) occurs in relative clauses; as <span lang="grc">ὅπως μολούμεθ᾽ ἐς δόμους οὐκ ἔχω</span> _I do not know how we are to go home_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 1742">S. O. C. 1742</cite>. <!-- page 573 -->

The deliberative subjunctive is more common; as <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔχω ὅ τι χρήσωμαι τοῖς λόγοις</span> _I am not able to deal with your argument_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyd. 287c">P. Eu. 287c</cite>.

##### <a id="s2550"></a> §2550

In a few cases the future is used like the subjunctives of 2547 a; and may be explained as a dependent deliberative future. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐ γάρ τις ὅρμος ἔστιν, οὐδ᾽ ὅποι πλέων ἐξεμπολήσει κέρδος</span> _for there is no harbour, nor is there_ any place _to which a man may voyage and sell his wares at a profit_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 303">S. Ph. 303</cite>, <span lang="grc">αὐτὸν γάρ σε δεῖ προμηθέως ὅτῳ τρόπῳ τῆσδ᾽ ἐκκυκλισθήσει τύχης</span> _for thou thyself hast need of forethought whereby thou shalt extricate thyself from this trouble_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 86">A. Pr. 86</cite>.

##### <a id="s2551"></a> §2551

<span lang="grc"> **οὐκ ἔστιν ὅς** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **ὅπως, ὅπου, ὡς** </span>) are used with the future indicative to introduce _statements_ as regards the future. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐ γάρ τις ἔστιν δ̀ς πάροιθ᾽ αἱρήσεται τὴν σὴν ἀχρεῖον δύναμιν ἀντ᾽ Εὐρυσθέως</span> _there is no one who will prefer thy feeble power rather than Eurystheus_ E. Heracl. 57, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔσθ᾽ ὅπως ὄψει σὺ δεῦρ᾽ ἐλθόντα με</span> _thou wilt in no wise_ (lit. _it is not possible how thou shalt_) _see me coming here_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 329">S. Ant. 329</cite>. The indicative present or aorist is also used in statements as regards the present or past. All these indicatives may be dependent deliberatives. Cp. [2557](#s2557).

##### <a id="s2552"></a> §2552

The optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (probably potential) occurs in Attic poetry after <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔστιν ὅστις</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅπως, ὅποι</span>) and the interrogative <span lang="grc">τίς ἐστίν ὅς</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅστις</span>) and <span lang="grc">ἔσθ᾽ ὅπως</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔστιν ὅστις πλὴν ἐμοῦ κείραιτό νιν</span> _there is no one except myself who could cut it_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Lib. 172">A. Ch. 172</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔσθ᾽ ὅπως λέξαιμι τὰ ψευδῆ καλά</span> _I could not_ (lit. _there is no way how I could_) _call false tidings fair_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 620">A. Ag. 620</cite>, <span lang="grc">τίς τῶνδ᾽ . . . δωμάτων ἔχει κράτος, ὅστις ξένους δέξαιτο</span>; _who has authority in this house that might receive guests?_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Thes. 871">Ar. Thesm. 871</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔστ᾽ οὖν ὅπως ῎ Αλκηστις ἐς γῆρας μόλοι</span>; _is there a way by which Alcestis might reach old age?_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Alc. 52">E. Alc. 52</cite>. The potential optative _with_ <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> occurs after these expressions (<cite data-ref="Eur. Alc. 80">E. Alc. 80</cite>, <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 1168">S. O. C. 1168</cite>, <cite data-ref="Plat. Lach. 184c">P. Lach. 184c</cite>). Attic does not use the optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> after the positive form <span lang="grc">ἔστιν ὅπως</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅστις</span>).

## CLASSES OF RELATIVE CLAUSES

##### <a id="s2553"></a> §2553

**Ordinary Relative Clauses** define more exactly a definite antecedent, and show the mood and the negative of simple sentences.

**Indicative:** <span lang="grc">ταῦτ᾽ ἐστὶν ἃ ἐγὼ δέομαι</span> _this is what I want_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.2.34">X. A. 7.2.34</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὦ δύστᾱνα γένη βροτ ῶν, οἷς μὴ μέτριος αἰών</span> _alas_, _ill-starred races of men, whose destiny is beyond due measure_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 179">S. Ph. 179</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅθεν οὖν ῥᾷστα μαθήσεσθε περὶ αὐτῶν, ἐντεῦθεν ὑ̄μᾶς καὶ ἐγὼ πρῶτον πειρά̄σομαι διδάσκειν</span> _I will first try to inform you_ (lit.) _from the source from which you will most easily learn about them_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 27.3">D. 27.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">παρ᾽ ἐμὲ ἀφικόμενος οὐ πείσεται ἅπερ ἂν ἔπαθεν ἄλλῳ τῳ συγγενόμενος τῶν σοφιστῶν</span> _in coming to me he will not meet with the treatment he would have suffered had he consorted with any other of the sophists_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 318d">P. Pr. 318d</cite>.

**Subjunctive:** <span lang="grc">Ἄνυτος ὅδε παρεκαθέζετο, ᾧ μεταδῶμεν τῆς ζητήσεως</span> _Anytus has taken his seat here_ (lit.) _to whom let us give a share in the investigation_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Meno 80e">P. Men. 80e</cite>, <span lang="grc">κλύων ὁθούνεκα . . . μήτηρ δ᾽ ἐν οἴκοις· ἣν σὺ μὴ δείσῃς</span> _hearing that our mother is in the house_, (lit.) _of whom have thou no fear_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 1309">S. El. 1309</cite>. <!-- page 574 -->

**Optative:** <span lang="grc">οἴομαι ἂν ἡμᾶς τοιαῦτα παθεῖν, οἷα τοὺς ἐχθροὺς οἱ θεοὶ ποιήσειαν</span> _I think we should endure such things as_ I pray _the gods may inflict upon our enemies_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.3">X. A. 3.2.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">δόρατα ἔχοντες . . . ὅσα ἀνὴρ ἂν φέροι μόλις</span> _having spears, such as a man could carry with difficulty_ 5. 4. 25, <span lang="grc">ἄρξομαι δ᾽ ἐντεῦθεν ὅθεν . . . ἐγὼ τάχιστ᾽ ἂν διδάξαιμι</span> _I will begin at_ (_from_) _that point where I can most quickly inform_ you <cite data-ref="Dem. 29.5">D. 29.5</cite>. The potential optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is very rare ([2552](#s2552)).

**Imperative:** <span lang="grc">πλάνην φράσω, ἣν ἐγγράφου σὺ μνήμοσιν δέλτοις φρενῶν</span> _I will tell thy wandering, which do thou inscribe in the tablets of thy memory_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 788">A. Pr. 788</cite>. On <span lang="grc">οἶσθ᾽ δ̀ δρᾶσον</span>, see [1842 a](20-the-verb-voices/08-imperative.md#s1842).

**a.** Ordinary relative clauses are explanatory, and (in sense) are equivalent to independent coördinated clauses. See [2490](#s2490).

**b.** Homer has <span lang="grc">κέ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the future: <span lang="grc">παρ᾽ ἐμοί γε καὶ ἄλλοι, οἵ κέ με τῑμήσουσι</span> _I have others by my side who will honour me_ A 175.

##### <a id="s2554"></a> §2554

**Relative Clauses of Purpose** (**Final Relative Clauses**) regularly take the future indicative, even after past tenses (negative <span lang="grc">μή</span>). The antecedent of final relative clauses is usually indefinite. <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> is commoner than <span lang="grc">ὅστις</span>. (The construction with the future participle is more frequent: [2065](22-the-participle.md#s2065)).

<span lang="grc">φημὶ δὴ δεῖν ἡμᾶς . . . πρεσβείᾱν πέμπειν, ἣ τοὺς μὲν διδάξει ταῦτα, τοὺς δὲ παροξυνεῖ</span> _I say that we must send an embassy, which will inform some of this and incite others_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 2.11">D. 2.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">πέμψον τιν᾽ ὅστις σημανεῖ</span> _send some one who will announce_ <cite data-ref="Eur. IT 1209">E. I. T. 1209</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔδοξε τῷ δήμῳ τριά̄κοντα ἄνδρας ἑλέσθαι, οἳ τοὺς πατρίους νόμους συγγράψουσι, καθ᾽ οὓς πολῑτεύσουσι</span> _the people voted to choose thirty men who should codify the ancestral laws by which they were to conduct the government_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.3.2">X. H. 2.3.2</cite>. So in local clauses: <span lang="grc">κρύψω τόδ᾽ ἔγχος . . . ἔνθα μή τις ὄψεται</span> _I will hide this sword where no one shall see it_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 659">S. Aj. 659</cite>.

**a.** After a secondary tense the future optative occurs rarely: <span lang="grc">οἱ δὲ τριά̄κοντα ᾑρέθησαν μὲν ἐπεὶ τάχιστα τὰ μακρὰ τείχη . . . καθῃρέθη· αἱρεθέντες δ᾽ ἐφ᾽ ᾧτε ξυγγράψαι νόμους, καθ᾽ οὕστινας πολῑτεύσοιντο κτλ</span>. _the thirty were chosen as soon as the long walls were destroyed; and having been chosen for the purpose of codifying the laws, according to which they were to conduct the government_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.3.11">X. H. 2.3.11</cite>. In local clauses: <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 796">S. O. T. 796</cite>.

**b.** A past purpose may be expressed by <span lang="grc">ἔμελλον</span> and the infinitive. Thus, <span lang="grc">ναύαρχον προσέταξαν᾽ Αλκίδᾱν, δ̀ς ἔμελλεν ἐπιπλεύσεσθαι</span> _they appointed Alcidas as admiral who was to sail in command_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.16">T. 3.16</cite>.

**c.** Homer uses the subjunctive (with <span lang="grc">κέ</span>, except <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 3.287"><span lang="grc">Γ</span> 287</cite>) after primary tenses, the optative after secondary tenses. Thus, <span lang="grc">μάντις ἐλεύσεται, ὅς κέν τοι ἔπῃσιν ὁδόν</span> _a seer will come to tell thee the way_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 10.538"><span lang="grc">κ</span> 538</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄγγελον ἧκαν δ̀ς ἀγγείλειε γυναικί</span> _they sent a messenger to tell the woman_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 15.458"><span lang="grc">ο</span> 458</cite>. The future also occurs (<cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 14.332"><span lang="grc">ξ</span> 332</cite>). The present or aorist optative is rare in Attic (<cite data-ref="Soph. Trach. 903">S. Tr. 903</cite>, Ph. [281](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/06-irregular-declension.md#s281)).

##### <a id="s2555"></a> §2555

**Relative Clauses of Cause** take the indicative (negative <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>). <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> is more common than <span lang="grc">ὅστις</span>.

<span lang="grc">θαυμαστὸν ποιεῖς, δ̀ς</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ὅτι σὺ</span>) <span lang="grc">ἡμῖν . . . οὐδὲν δίδως</span> _you do a strange thing in giving us nothing_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.7.13">X. M. 2.7.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">Λοξίᾳ δὲ μέμφομαι, ὅστις μ᾽ ἐπά̄ρᾱς ἔργον ἀνοσιώτατον τοῖς μὲν λόγοις ηὔφρᾱνε κτλ</span>. _I blame Loxias_, _who after inciting me to_ <!-- page 575 --> _a deed most unhallowed, cheered me with words_, etc. <cite data-ref="Eur. Orest. 285">E. Or. 285</cite>. So when the relative is a dependent exclamation (<span lang="grc">οἷος ῀ ὅτι τοιοῦτος</span>, etc., [2687](34-exclamatory-sentences.md#s2687)).

**a.** <span lang="grc">γε</span> is often added to <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> or <span lang="grc">ὅστις</span>.

**b.** <span lang="grc">μή</span> is used when there is also an idea of characteristic (_of such a sort_) or condition (perhaps to avoid a harsher form of statement). Cp. [2705 g](35-negative-sentences.md#s2705).

##### <a id="s2556"></a> §2556

**Relative Clauses of Result** (**Consecutive Relative Clauses**) usually take the indicative (for <span lang="grc">οἷος, ὅσος</span> with the infinitive see [2497](#s2497)). The negative is <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> when the relative clause approximates <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span> (<span lang="grc">οὐ</span>) with the indicative, as is generally the case when the main clause is negative, expressed or implied. Here <span lang="grc">ὅστις</span> is commoner than <span lang="grc">ὅς</span>. The negative is <span lang="grc">μή</span> when the relative clause expresses an intended ([2557](#s2557)) or anticipated ([2558](#s2558)) result, where <span lang="grc">ὥστε μή</span> with the infinitive would be less precise.

<span lang="grc">τίς οὕτω μαίνεται ὅστις οὐ βούλεται σοὶ φίλος εἶναι</span>; _who is so mad that he does not wish to be a friend to you?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.12">X. A. 2.5.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν γὰρ οὕτω βραχὺ ὅπλον ἑκάτεροι εἶχον ᾧ οὐκ ἐξῑκνοῦντο ἀλλήλων</span> _for each side did not have weapons so short that they could not reach each other_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 7.5.17">X. H. 7.5.17</cite>.

**a.** The indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> and the optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> are rare. Thus, <span lang="grc">τίς δ᾽ ἦν οὕτως . . . μῑσαθήναιος, ὅστις ἐδυνήθη ἂν ἄτακτον αὑτὸν ὑπομεῖναι ἰδεῖν</span>; _who was such a hater of Athens that he could endure to see himself not at his post?_ Lyc. 39, <span lang="grc">τίς οὕτως ἰσχῡρός, δ̀ς . . . ῥἱ̄γει δύναιτ᾽ ἂν μαχόμενος στρατεύεσθαι</span> _who is so vigorous that he could carry on war while battling with cold?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.1.15">X. C. 6.1.15</cite>. A potential optative with <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> follows a potential optative in <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 360b">P. R. 360b</cite>.

##### <a id="s2557"></a> §2557

The indicative is normal in consecutive relative clauses introduced by <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔστιν ὅστις</span> (<span lang="grc">οὐ</span>), <span lang="grc">οὐδείς ἐστιν ὅστις</span> (<span lang="grc">οὐ</span>), <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔστιν ὅπως</span> (<span lang="grc">οὐ</span>), <span lang="grc">εἰσὶν οἵ, ἔστιν οἷς</span>, etc.

<span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔστιν οὐδεὶς ὅστις οὐχ αὑτὸν φιλεῖ</span> _there is no one who does not love himself_ Men. Sent. 407, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔστιν ὅπως ἥβην κτήσῃ πάλιν αὖθις</span> _in no way canst thou regain thy youth_ E. Heracl. 707. See [2551](#s2551).

**a.** The indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> and the optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> also occur. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ ἦν ὅ τι ἂν ἐποιεῖτε</span> _for there was nothing that you could have done_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.43">D. 18.43</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὧν οὐκ ἔστιν ὅστις οὐκ ἂν καταφρονήσειεν</span> _whom every one would despise_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 8.52">I. 8.52</cite>.

**b.** On the subjunctive and optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>, see [2546](#s2546), [2547](#s2547), 2552.

##### <a id="s2558"></a> §2558

The future indicative is often used to express an _intended_ result (negative <span lang="grc">μή</span>).

<span lang="grc">ἀνόητον ἐπὶ τοιούτους ἰέναι ὧν κρατήσᾱς μὴ κατασχήσει τις</span> _it is senseless to attack men of such a kind that we shall not hold them in subjection if we conquer them_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.11">T. 6.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὗτοι δὲ τοιαῦτ᾽ . . . ὑποσχήσονται, ἐξ ὧν μηδ᾽ ἂν ὁτιοῦν ᾖ κῑνηθήσονται</span> _these men shall make promises in consequence of which_ the Athenians _will not better themselves under any circumstances_ (lit. _even if anything occurs_) <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.324">D. 19.324</cite>.

##### <a id="s2559"></a> §2559

The future indicative is especially common when the main clause contains an idea of _ability_, _capacity_, or _characteristic_, and the relative clause denotes what is to be expected of the subject. <!-- page 576 -->

<span lang="grc">ἱκανοί ἐσμεν . . . ὑ̄μῖν πέμψαι ναῦς τε καὶ ἄνδρας οἵτινες συμμαχοῦνταί τε καὶ τὴν ὁδὸν ἡγήσονται</span> (cp. <span lang="grc">ὥστε συμμάχεσθαι</span>) _we are able to send you ships and men who will fight with you and direct your journey_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.4.10">X. A. 5.4.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὔτε πλοῖα ἔστι τὰ ἀπάξοντα οὔτε σῖτος ᾧ θρεψόμεθα μένοντες</span> _we have neither ships to convey us away nor provisions to feed us while we remain_ 6. 5. 20, <span lang="grc">δεῖταί τινος ὅστις αὐτὸν ὀνήσει</span> _he needs some one to improve him_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyd. 306d">P. Eu. 306d</cite>, (<span lang="grc">ἔδει</span>) <span lang="grc">ψήφισμα νῑκῆσαι τοιοῦτο δι᾽ οὗ Φωκεῖς ἀπολοῦνται</span> _a bill had to be passed of such a character as to destroy the Phocians_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.43">D. 19.43</cite>.

##### <a id="s2560"></a> §2560

**Conditional Relative Clauses** may be resolved into _if_ clauses, <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅστις</span>) corresponding to <span lang="grc">εἴ τις</span> and <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅστις</span>) <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> to <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν τις</span>. The negative is <span lang="grc">μή</span>.

**a.** The antecedent of conditional relative clauses is indefinite ([2505 b](#s2505)).

**b.** Such relative clauses, like temporal clauses, correspond in form to the protases of ordinary conditional sentences. Conditional relative sentences show, in general, the same substitutions permitted in the corresponding conditional sentences. <span lang="grc">δ̀ς ἄν</span> is always generic, <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span> may be particular in prose.

##### <a id="s2561"></a> §2561

The correspondence in construction between the common forms of conditional, temporal, and conditional relative, sentences is shown by the following table:

| Unreal: | <span lang="grc">εἴ</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅτε, ὅ</span>) <span lang="grc">τι</span> | <span lang="grc">εἶχεν</span> | <span lang="grc">ἐδίδου ἄν</span> |

## PRESENT AND PAST CONDITIONAL RELATIVE CLAUSES

### First Form: SIMPLE PRESENT AND PAST CONDITIONAL RELATIVES

##### <a id="s2562"></a> §2562

Simple present and past conditional relative clauses have the present or past indicative. The main clause has the indicative or any other form of the simple sentence (cp. [2298](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2298), [2300](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2300)).

<span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ ἃ πρά̄ττουσιν οἱ δίκαιοι, ἀλλ᾽ ἃ</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἴ τινα</span>) <span lang="grc">μὴ πρά̄ττουσι, ταῦτα λέγεις</span> _for it is not what the just do, but what they do not do, that you keep telling_ us <!-- page 577 --> <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.4.11">X. M. 4.4.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῶν Ἑλλήνων οἳ</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἴ τινες</span>) <span lang="grc">μὴ ἔτυχον ἐν ταῖς τάξεσιν ὄντες εἰς τὰ̄ς τάξεις ἔθεον</span> _those of the Greeks who happened not to be in rank ran into their ranks_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.2.14">X. A. 2.2.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">διέβαλλεν αὐτὸν ὅ τι ἐδύνατο</span> _he slandered him all he could_ 6. 1. 32, <span lang="grc">ἄνδρας τῶν Ἀθηναίων ἀπέκτειναν ὅσοι μὴ ἐξένευσαν</span> _they killed_ all _of the Athenians who had not escaped by swimming_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.90">T. 2.90</cite>, <span lang="grc">δ̀ δέ γε μηδὲν κακὸν ποιεῖ οὐδ᾽ ἄν τινος εἴη κακοῦ αἴτιον</span>; _and that which produces no evil cannot be the cause of any evil either?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 379b">P. R. 379b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἃ μὴ προσήκει μήτ᾽ ἄκουε μήθ᾽ ὅρᾱ</span> _neither hear nor behold that which beseems thee not_ Men. Sent. 39, <span lang="grc">ὅστις ζῆν ἐπιθῡμεῖ, πειρά̄σθω νῑκᾶν</span> _whoever longs to live, let him strive to conquer_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.39">X. A. 3.2.39</cite>.

**a.** Since the antecedent of these clauses is indefinite, simple present conditional relative clauses with the present indicative in the main clause often have the value of _general_ conditions. But general clauses with <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> (<span lang="grc">μή</span>) usually take the subjunctive or optative ([2567](#s2567), [2568](#s2568)), and those with <span lang="grc">ὅστις</span> (<span lang="grc">μή</span>) the indicative ([2569](#s2569)).

##### <a id="s2563"></a> §2563

If the relative clause expresses a _present intention_ or _necessity_, the future indicative may be used.

<span lang="grc">ἐν τούτῳ κεκωλῦσθαι</span> ([1950](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1950)) <span lang="grc">ἐδόκει ἑκάστῳ τὰ πρά̄γματα ᾧ μή τις αὐτὸς παρέσται</span> _each thought that progress was surely impeded in any undertaking in which he was not going to take part in person_ (= <span lang="grc">ἐν τούτῳ κεκώλῡται ᾧ μὴ παρέσομαι</span>) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.8">T. 2.8</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 186c">P. Th. 186c</cite>. More common is <span lang="grc">μέλλω</span> with the present or future infinitive: <span lang="grc">ἕλοισθ᾽ ὅ τι . . . ἅπᾱσι συνοίσειν ὑ̄μῖν μέλλει</span> _may you adopt whatever course is likely to be of advantage to you all_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 3.36">D. 3.36</cite>.

**a.** Elsewhere the future indicative is not regular in conditional relative sentences.

### Second Form: PRESENT AND PAST UNREAL CONDITIONAL RELATIVES

##### <a id="s2564"></a> §2564

Present and past unreal conditional relative clauses have a secondary tense of the indicative. The main clause has a secondary tense with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (cp. [2303](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2303)).

<span lang="grc">οὔτε γὰρ ἂν αὐτοὶ ἐπεχειροῦμεν πρά̄ττειν ἃ</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἴ τινα</span>) <span lang="grc">μὴ ἠπιστάμεθα κτλ</span>. _for_ (if that were so) _neither should we ourselves be undertaking_ (as we are) _to do what we did not understand_, etc. <cite data-ref="Plat. Charm. 171e">P. Charm. 171e</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ παῖδες ὑ̄μῶν, ὅσοι</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἴ τινες</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐνθάδε ἦσαν, ὑπὸ τούτων ἂν ὑ̄βρίζοντο</span> (if that were so) _your children, as many of them as were present_ (but none were present), _would be insulted by these men_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.98">L. 12.98</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁπότερα τούτων ἐποίησεν, οὐδενὸς ἂν ἧττον πλούσιοι ἦσαν</span> _whichever of these things he had done, they would be no less rich than any one_ 32. 23.

## FUTURE CONDITIONAL RELATIVE CLAUSES

### Third Form: MORE VIVID FUTURE CONDITIONAL RELATIVES

##### <a id="s2565"></a> §2565

Conditional relative clauses that vividly anticipate the realization of a future event take the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>. The main clause has the future indicative or any other form referring to the future. <!-- page 578 -->

<span lang="grc">τῷ ἀνδρὶ δ̀ν ἂν</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν τινα</span>) <span lang="grc">ἕλησθε πείσομαι</span> _I will obey whatever man you may choose_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.15">X. A. 1.3.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἷς</span> (for <span lang="grc">ἃ</span>) <span lang="grc">ἂν οἱ ἄλλοι ἐργάζωνται, τούτοις σὺ χρήσῃ</span> _whatever others acquire by labour, that you shall enjoy_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.1.25">X. M. 2.1.25</cite>, <span lang="grc">πειρά̄σομαι ὅ τι ἂν δύνωμαι ὑ̄μᾶς ἀγαθὸν ποιεῖν</span> _I will try to do you all the good I can_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.1.33">X. A. 6.1.33</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅποι ἂν ἔλθω, λέγοντος ἐμοῦ ἀκροά̄σονται οἱ νέοι</span> _wherever I go the young men will listen to my speaking_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 37d">P. A. 37d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπόκρῑναι ὅ τι ἄν δε ἐρωτῶ</span> _answer whatever I ask you_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.24">L. 12.24</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἕπεσθε ὅπῃ ἄν τις ἡγῆται</span> _follow where any one may lead you_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.11">T. 2.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡς ἂν</span> (= <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν πως</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐγὼ εἴπω, πειθώμεθα</span> _let us all obey as I shall bid_ B 139. Potential optative: <span lang="grc">ὥστ᾽ ἀποφύγοις ἂν ἥντιν᾽ ἂν βούλῃ δίκην</span> _so that you can get off in any suit you please_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 1151">Ar. Nub. 1151</cite>.

**a.** The future indicative is scarcely ever used in a conditional relative clause of this sort (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.22">T. 1.22</cite> <span lang="grc">ὅσοι βουλήσονται</span>; cp. [1913](20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1913)).

**b.** Homer has some cases of the subjunctive without <span lang="grc">κέ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (e.g. N [234](../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/02-second-declension-stems-in-omicron.md#s234)). Homer sometimes uses the future with <span lang="grc">κέ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> in the main clause: <span lang="grc">ὁ δέ κεν κεχολώσεται, ὅν κεν ἵκωμαι</span> _and he will be wroth to whom I shall come_ A 139.

### Fourth Form: LESS VIVID FUTURE CONDITIONAL RELATIVES

##### <a id="s2566"></a> §2566

Conditional relative clauses that set forth less vividly the realization of a future event take the optative. The main clause has the optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span>.

<span lang="grc">ὀκνοίην ἂν εἰς τὰ πλοῖα ἐμβαίνειν ἃ</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἴ τινα</span>) <span lang="grc">ἡμῖν Κῦρος δοίη</span> _I should hesitate to embark in the vessels that Cyrus might give us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.17">X. A. 1.3.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">δ̀ δὲ μὴ ἀγαπῴη, οὐδ᾽ ἂν φιλοῖ</span> _nor could he love what he does not desire_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lysis 215b">P. Lys. 215b</cite>.

**a.** The main clause has the optative without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> in _wishes:_ <span lang="grc">δῶρα θεῶν ἔχοι, ὅττι διδοῖεν</span> _may he keep the gifts of the gods whatever they may give_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 18.142"><span lang="grc">ς</span> 142</cite>.

**b.** Homer sometimes uses <span lang="grc">κέ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> in the relative clause (<cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 21.161"><span lang="grc">φ</span> 161</cite>).

## GENERAL CONDITIONAL RELATIVE CLAUSES

### Fifth Form PRESENT GENERAL CONDITIONAL RELATIVES

##### <a id="s2567"></a> §2567

Present general conditional relative clauses have <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> with the subjunctive. The main clause has the present indicative or an equivalent.

<span lang="grc">νέος δ᾽ ἀπόλλυθ᾽ ὅντιν᾽</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἴ τινα</span>) <span lang="grc">ἂν φιλῇ θεός</span> ‘_he dieth young, whome'er a god doth love_’ Stob. Flor. 120.13, <span lang="grc">οὓς</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἴ τινας</span>) <span lang="grc">ἂν ὁοᾷ φιλοκινδύ̄νως ἔχοντας πρὸς τοὺς πολεμίους, τῑμᾷ</span> _whomever he sees zealous of danger in the face of the enemy_, these _he honours_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.1.6">X. H. 6.1.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">γαμοῦσί τε ὁπόθεν ἂν βούλωνται, ἐκδιδόωσί τε εἰς οὓς ἂν ἐθέλωσι</span> _they both get a wife from whatever family they please and give their daughters in marriage to whomsoever they choose_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 613d">P. R. 613d</cite>, <span lang="grc">πατρὶς γάρ ἐστι πᾶσ᾽ ἵν᾽ ἂν πρά̄ττῃ τις εὖ</span> _for every land is a man's own country wheresoever he fares well_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Pl. 1151">Ar. Plut. 1151</cite>.

**a.** Gnomic aorist in the main clause: <span lang="grc">ὅς κε θεοῖς ἐπιπείθηται, μάλα τ᾽ ἔκλυον αὐτοῦ</span> _whoever obeys the gods, him they most do hear_ A 218. <!-- page 579 -->

**b.** The subjunctive without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (<span lang="grc">κέ</span>) is usual in Homer and occurs occasionally in Attic and lyric poetry. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀνθρώπους ἐφορᾷ καὶ τί̄νυται ὅς τις ἁμάρτῃ</span> _he watches over men and punishes whoever transgresses_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 13.214"><span lang="grc">ν</span> 214</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῶν δὲ πημονῶν μάλιστα λῡποῦσ᾽ αἳ φανῶσ᾽ αὐθαίρετοι</span> _but those griefs pain the most which are seen to be self-sought_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 1231">S. O. T. 1231</cite>. Cases of the sort appear in Hdt., but are very rare in Attic prose, e.g. <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.18">T. 4.18</cite>. The subjunctive without <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> (<span lang="grc">κέ</span>) is much commoner in Homer than in the corresponding clauses with <span lang="grc">εἰ</span> ([2339](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2339)).

**c.** The apodosis here usually expresses a general truth, less often iterative action. In 2568 the apodosis refers to iterative action, usually on the part of designated individuals.

### Sixth Form PAST GENERAL CONDITIONAL RELATIVES

##### <a id="s2568"></a> §2568

Past general conditional relative clauses have the optative. The main clause has the imperfect or an equivalent.

<span lang="grc">ἀεὶ πρὸς ᾧ</span> (= <span lang="grc">εἰ πρός τινι</span>) <span lang="grc">εἴη ἔργῳ, τοῦτο ἔπρᾱττεν</span> _whatever work he was engaged in, that he always performed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.8.22">X. H. 4.8.22</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔπρᾱττεν ἃ δόξειεν αὐτῷ</span> _he_ always _did whatever he pleased_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.235">D. 18.235</cite>, <span lang="grc">πάντας . . . ὅσους λάβοιεν διέφθειρον</span> _they used to destroy as many as they captured_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.67">T. 2.67</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐθήρᾱ ὅπου περ ἐπιτυγχάνοιεν θηρίοις</span> _he used to hunt wherever they fell in with large game_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.5">X. C. 3.3.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀνέκραγον . . . ἱκετεύουσαι πάντας ὅτῳ ἐντυγχάνοιεν μὴ φεύγειν</span> _they screamed out_, _entreating all they met not to flee_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.67">X. C. 3.3.67</cite>.

**a.** An iterative tense with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> in the main clause: <span lang="grc">ὅπῃ μέλλοι ἀ̄ριστοποιεῖσθαι τὸ στράτευμα . . ., ἐπανήγαγεν ἄν τὸ κέρας</span>, _when the squadron was about to take breakfast_, _he would draw back the wing_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.2.28">X. H. 6.2.28</cite>.

#### INDICATIVE FORM OF GENERAL CONDITIONAL RELATIVE CLAUSES

##### <a id="s2569"></a> §2569

The present indicative instead of the subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> occurs in general conditional relative clauses (cp. [2342](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2342)). This occurs chiefly after <span lang="grc">ὅστις</span>, which is itself sufficiently general in meaning.

<span lang="grc">οἵτινες πρὸς τὰ̄ς ξυμφορὰ̄ς γνώμῃ ἥκιστα λῡποῦνται, ἔργῳ δὲ μάλιστα ἀντε´χουσιν</span> _those who in feeling are least depressed at misfortunes, in action resist them most_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.64">T. 2.64</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅστις δ᾽ ἐπὶ μεγίστοις τὸ ἐπίφθονον λαμβάνει, ὀρθῶς βουλεύεται</span> _he counsels wisely who incurs envy in a great cause_ 2. 64, <span lang="grc">ὅστις δὲ πλοῦτον ἢ εὐγένειαν εἰσιδὼν γαμεῖ πονηρά̄ν, μῶρός ἐστιν</span> _whoever fixes his gaze on wealth or noble lineage and weds a wicked woman, is a fool_ <cite data-ref="Eur. El. 1097">E. El. 1097</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅ τι καλὸν φίλον ἀ̄εί</span> _whatsoever is fair is dear forever_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Ba. 881">E. Bacch. 881</cite>.

**a.** Cases of the imperfect instead of the optative are rare and generally ill supported: <span lang="grc">ὅπου ᾤετο τὴν πατρίδα τι ὠφελήσειν, οὐ πόνων ὑφί̄ετο</span> _whenever he thought that he could benefit his country in any respect, he did not shrink from toil_ X. Ag. 7. 1. Cp. <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.5">X. A. 1.1.5</cite>, 1. 9. 27.

##### <a id="s2570"></a> §2570

The indicative is generally used in parenthetical or appended relative clauses with <span lang="grc">ὅστις</span> (<span lang="grc">ὅστις ποτέ</span>). Thus, <span lang="grc">δουλεύομεν θεοῖς, ὅ τι ποτ᾽ εἰσὶν οἱ θεοί</span> _we serve the gods, whatever those gods are_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Orest. 418">E. Or. 418</cite>. <!-- page 580 -->

**a.** The subjunctive with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> is also used when the reference is to future time or to general present time. Cp. <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 1.127">Aes. 1.127</cite>, <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.27">D. 4.27</cite>.

## LESS USUAL FORMS OF CONDITIONAL RELATIVE SENTENCES

##### <a id="s2571"></a> §2571

The potential optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> in the main clause with the indicative ([2562](#s2562)) or subjunctive ([2565](#s2565)) in the relative clause.

##### <a id="s2572"></a> §2572

Indicative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> or potential optative with <span lang="grc">ἄν</span> in the relative clause.

<span lang="grc">ὅντιν᾽ ἂν ὑ̄μεῖς εἰς ταύτην τὴν τάξιν κατεστήσατε . . ., οὗτος . . . τῶν ἴσων ἂν αἴτιος ἦν κακῶν ὅσωνπερ καὶ οὗτος</span> _whomsoever you might have appointed to this post_, _such a one would have been the cause of as many evils as this man has been_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.29">D. 19.29</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ̄ς δ᾽ ἐπ᾽ Ἰλλυρίους . . . καὶ ὅποι τις ἂν εἴποι παραλείπω στρατείᾱς</span> _I omit his expeditions against the Illyrians and many others_ (lit. _whithersoever_) _one might speak of_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 1.13">D. 1.13</cite>. Cp. X. Ag. 2. 24.

##### <a id="s2573"></a> §2573

The optative in the relative clause with the present or future indicative or the imperative in the main clause (cp. [2359](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2359)). With the present this occurs especially in general statements and maxims. The main clause is often introduced by a verb requiring the infinitive.

<span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ δ̀ν πόλις στήσειε, τοῦδε χρὴ κλύειν</span> _but whomever the State might appoint_, _him we must obey_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 666">S. Ant. 666</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦ μὲν αὐτὸν λέγειν, ἁ` μὴ σαφῶς εἰδείη, εἴργεσθαι δεῖ</span> _one should refrain from saying oneself what one does not know for certain_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.6.19">X. C. 1.6.19</cite>.

**a.** The present indicative sometimes may have the force of an emphatic future (<cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 6.286"><span lang="grc">ζ</span> 286</cite>). Sometimes the optative indicates a case/that is not likely to occur; as <span lang="grc">ἄλλῳ νεμεσᾶτον, ὅτις τοιαῦτά γε ῥέζοι</span> _you are ready to be wroth with another_, _supposing any one do such things_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 23.494"><span lang="grc">Ψ</span> 494</cite>.

**b.** Other examples of the present: Homer P 631 (doubtful); Theognis 689; Aes. Pr. 638; Soph. O. T. 315, 979; Lys. 12. 84; Xen. C. 2. 4. 10, 7. 5. 56, H. 3. 4. 18, 7. 3. 7; Plato Charm. 164 a, Eu. 292 e (doubtful), L. 927 c. Temporal: <cite data-ref="Soph. Trach. 92">S. Tr. 92</cite>, <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 332a">P. R. 332a</cite>.

**c.** The future indicative occurs in <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 19.510"><span lang="grc">τ</span> 510</cite> (temporal N [317](../part-2-inflection/05-comparison-of-adjectives.md#s317)); the perfect indicative in <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 4.262"><span lang="grc">Δ</span> 262</cite> and <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 24.254"><span lang="grc">ω</span> 254</cite> (temporal); the aorist imperative in <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.14">X. C. 1.4.14</cite>.
