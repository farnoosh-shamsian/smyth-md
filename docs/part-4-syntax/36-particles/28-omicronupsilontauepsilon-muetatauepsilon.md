# PARTICLES: οὔτε (μήτε)

##### <a id="s2942"></a> §2942

**<span lang="grc">οὔτε</span> (<span lang="grc">μήτε</span>)** is usually repeated: <span lang="grc"> **οὔτε . . . οὔτε** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **μήτε . . . μήτε** </span>) _neither . . . nor_ (_nec . . . nec_). <span lang="grc">οὔτε . . . οὔτε</span> is the negative of <span lang="grc">τὲ . . . τέ</span>, and unites single words or clauses.

<span lang="grc">οὔτε ἔστιν οὔτε ποτὲ ἔσται</span> _neither is nor ever shall be_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 241c">P. Phae. 241c</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὔτε Χειρίσοφος ἧκεν οὔτε πλοῖα ἱκανὰ ἦν οὔτε τὰ ἐπιτήδεια ἦν λαμβάνειν ἔτι</span> _neither had Chirisophus come nor were there enough boats nor was it possible any longer to secure provisions_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.3.1">X. A. 5.3.1</cite>.

After a negative clause: <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔπειθεν οὔτε τοὺς στρατηγοὺς οὔτε τοὺς στρατιώτᾱς</span> _he could not persuade either the generals or the soldiers_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.4">T. 4.4</cite>.

**a. <span lang="grc">οὔτε . . . μήτε</span>** is found when each negative is determined by a different construction, as <span lang="grc">ἀναιδὴς οὔτ᾽ εἰμὶ μήτε γενοίμην</span> _neither am I nor may I become shameless_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.68">D. 8.68</cite>.

**b.** When <span lang="grc">οὔτε . . . οὔτε</span> stands between <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ . . . οὐδέ</span> the members thus correlated are subordinate to those expressed by <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ . . . οὐδέ</span>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 1.19">Aes. 1.19</cite>.

##### <a id="s2943"></a> §2943

Sometimes the first <span lang="grc">οὔτε</span> is omitted in poetry: <span lang="grc">νόσοι δ᾽ οὔτε γῆρας</span> _disease nor old age_ Pindar, Pyth. 10. 41, <span lang="grc">ἑκόντα μήτ᾽ ἄ̄κοντα</span> _willingly nor unwillingly_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 771">S. Ph. 771</cite>. Cp. “my five wits nor my five senses” (Shakesp.).

##### <a id="s2944"></a> §2944

For the first <span lang="grc">οὔτε</span> the poets sometimes have <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>, as <span lang="grc">οὐ νιφετὸς οὔτ᾽ ἂρ χειμών</span> _not snow nor storm_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 4.566"><span lang="grc">δ</span> 566</cite>.

##### <a id="s2945"></a> §2945

<span lang="grc"> **οὔτε . . . τε´** </span> _on the one hand not . . . but_, _not only not . . . but_ (cp. _neque . . . et_). The <span lang="grc">τέ</span> clause often denotes the contrary of that set forth in the <span lang="grc">οὔτε</span> clause (_so far from_). Thus, <span lang="grc">οὔτε διενοήθην πώποτε ἀποστερῆσαι ἀποδώσω τε</span> _so far from ever thinking to deprive_ them of their pay _I will give_ it to them <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.7.48">X. A. 7.7.48</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὤμοσαν . . . μήτε προδώσειν ἀλλήλους σύμμαχοί τε ἔσεσθαι</span> _they swore that they would not betray one another and that they would be allies_ 2. 2. 8. So <span lang="grc"> **οὔτε . . . οὔτε . . . τέ. τὲ . . . οὔτε** </span> is not used.

**a.** Sometimes the negative may be added in the <span lang="grc">τέ</span> clause: <span lang="grc">οὔτε ἐκεῖνος ἔτι κατενόησε τό τε μαντεῖον οὐκ ἐδήλου</span> _neither did he stop to consider and the oracle would not make it plain_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.126">T. 1.126</cite>.

##### <a id="s2946"></a> §2946

<span lang="grc"> **οὔτε . . . τε οὐ** </span> <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 763">S. Ant. 763</cite>. <span lang="grc"> **οὔτε . . . τε . . . οὔτε** </span> <cite data-ref="Eur. Her. 1341">E. H. F. 1341</cite>.

##### <a id="s2947"></a> §2947

<span lang="grc"> **οὔτε . . . δέ** </span> is used when the second clause is opposed to the first; as <span lang="grc">οὔτε πλοῖά ἐστιν οἷς ἀποπλευσόμεθα, μένουσι δὲ αὐτοῦ οὐδὲ μιᾶς ἡμέρᾱς ἔστι τὰ ἐπιτήδεια</span> _we have no vessels by which we can sail away; on the other hand, if we stay here, we haven't provisions even for a single day_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.3.16">X. A. 6.3.16</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Eur. Supp. 223">E. Supp. 223</cite>, <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 388e">P. R. 388e</cite>, 389 a.

##### <a id="s2948"></a> §2948

<span lang="grc"> **οὔτε . . . οὐ** </span> is rare in prose; as <span lang="grc">οὔτε νιφετός, οὐκ ὄμβρος</span> _neither rain nor snow_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 8.98">Hdt. 8.98</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 249">S. Ant. 249</cite>. <span lang="grc"> **οὔτε . . . οὐ . . . οὔτε** </span> <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 479">A. Pr. 479</cite>. <span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ . . . οὔτε** </span> is generally changed to <span lang="grc">οὐ . . . οὐδέ</span> in Attic prose.

##### <a id="s2949"></a> §2949

<span lang="grc"> **οὔτε . . . οὐδέ** </span> corresponds to the sequence of <span lang="grc">τὲ . . . δέ</span> in affirmative clauses. The emphatic <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> here adds a new negative idea as after any other preceding negative; and is most common after <span lang="grc">οὔτε . . . οὔτε</span>: _neither . . . nor . . . no_, _nor yet_ (_nor . . . either_). <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> is often followed by an <!-- page 663 --> emphasizing particle, as <span lang="grc">αὖ, γέ, μήν</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὔτε πόλις οὔτε πολῑτείᾱ οὐδέ γ᾽ ἀνήρ</span> _neither a State nor a constitution nor yet an individual_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 499b">P. R. 499b</cite>, <span lang="grc">μήτε παιδείᾱ . . . μήτε δικαστήρια μήτε νόμοι μηδὲ ἀνάγκη μηδεμία</span> _neither education nor courts of justice nor laws, no nor yet restraint_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 327d">P. Pr. 327d</cite>.

##### <a id="s2950"></a> §2950

A subordinate clause with <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> may come between <span lang="grc">οὔτε . . . οὔτε</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὔτε γὰρ ὡς ὀφείλοντά με κατέλειπεν ὁ πατὴρ . . . ἀπέφηνεν οὐδὲ . . . παρέσχηται μάρτυρας οὔτ᾽ αὖ τὸν ἀριθμὸν . . . ἐπανέφερεν</span> _for neither did he show that my father left me in debt, nor yet has he adduced witnesses, nor did he put into the account the sum_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 27.49">D. 27.49</cite>.
