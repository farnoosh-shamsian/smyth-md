# PARTICLES: ἄρα

##### <a id="s2787"></a> §2787

<span lang="grc"> **ἄρα** </span> (Epic <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> and enclitic <span lang="grc">ἄρ</span> before a consonant, <span lang="grc">ῥά</span> usually after monosyllables; all postpositive), a connective, confirmatory, and inferential particle marking the immediate connection and succession of events and thoughts; the natural, direct, and expected consequence of a previous statement of the existing situation, or of the realization of experience of some sort; and agreement of various kinds, as between assertion and reality, cause and result, premise and conclusion, explanation and what was to be explained.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> marks a consequence drawn from the connection of thought, and expresses impression or feeling; the stronger <span lang="grc">οὖν</span> marks a consequence drawn from facts (a positive conclusion).

##### <a id="s2788"></a> §2788

The etymology of <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span>, and hence its original meaning, is obscure. Some derive it from the root <span lang="grc">ἀρ</span>, seen in <span lang="grc">ἀρ-αρ-ίσκω</span> _fit_, _join_, <span lang="grc">ἄρτι</span> _just;_ and thus regard the proper sense as _fittingly_, _accordingly_. Others think the earliest meaning was _truly_, _forsooth_ and connect <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> with a lost adj. <span lang="grc">ἀρίς</span>, surviving in <span lang="grc">ἄρι-στος, ἀρί-γνωτος</span>. On this interpretation <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> would originally assert the truth of its _own_ clause. <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> is found also in <span lang="grc">ἆρα</span> and <span lang="grc">γάρ</span>.

##### <a id="s2789"></a> §2789

<span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> is used in Homer much more freely than in Attic, and often so as to defy exact translation. In general <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> in Epic marks immediate connection and succession, a natural consequence of something already said or done; gives an explanation of an antecedent statement; or is used in recapitulations and transitions. Thus, <span lang="grc">αὐτὰρ ἐπεί ῤ̔ ἤγερθεν . . ., βῆ ῤ̔ ἴμεν εἰς ἀγορήν</span> _but when they were collected, then he started to go to the assembly_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 2.9"><span lang="grc">β</span> 9</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὣς ἔφαθ᾽, οἱ δ᾽ ἄρα πάντες ἀκὴν ἐγένοντο σιωπῇ</span> _thus he spake, and all accordingly became hushed in silence_ H 92, <span lang="grc">σῖτον δέ σφιν ἔνειμε Μεσαύλιος, ὅν ῥα συβώτης αὐτὸς ἐκτήσατο</span> _and Mesaulius distributed food to them_, a slave _whom_ (and this was the reason for his so doing) _the swineherd had acquired_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 14.449"><span lang="grc">ξ</span> 449</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὣς ἄρ᾽ ἐφώνησεν καὶ ἀπὸ ἕο τόξον ἔθηκεν</span> _thus then he spake and put the bow from him_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 21.163"><span lang="grc">φ</span> 163</cite>. So also in the later language; as <span lang="grc">ἐρωτήσης δὲ αὐτὸν τῆς μητρὸς . . . ἀπεκρί̄νατο ἄρα ὁ Κῦρος</span> _on his mother's questioning him Cyrus naturally replied_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.2">X. C. 1.3.2</cite>.

##### <a id="s2790"></a> §2790

In Attic, and in part also in Homer, <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> marks an inference (_conse-_ <!-- page 636 --> _quently_, _so then, therefore_, _it seems, after all, of course_, etc.). Thus, <span lang="grc">εἶπεν αὐτῷ ὅτι βασιλεὺς οὐ μαχεῖται δέκα ἡμερῶν· Κῦρος δ᾽ εἶπεν· οὐκ ἄρα ἔτι μαχεῖται, εἰ ἐν ταύταις οὐ μαχεῖται ταῖς ἡμέραις</span> the seer _said to him that the king would not fight within ten days. And Cyrus answered:_ “_Well then if he does not fight within that time he will not fight at all_” <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.18">X. A. 1.7.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδεὶ&lt;\*&gt; ποτοῦ ἐπιθῡμεῖ, ἀλλὰ χρηστοῦ ποτοῦ . . ., πάντες γὰρ ἄρα τῶν ἀγαθῶν ἐπιθῡμοῦσιν</span> _no one desires drink_ merely, _but good drink, since of course everybody desires good things_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 438a">P. R. 438a</cite>.

##### <a id="s2791"></a> §2791

<span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> is often used of direct logical conclusions in conducting an argument (especially in Plato); as <span lang="grc">τί οὖν περὶ ψῡχῆς λέγομεν; ὁρᾱτὸν ἢ ἀόρᾱτον εἶναι; οὐχ ὁρᾱτόν. ἀιδὲς ἄρα; ναί. ὁμοιότερον ἄρα ψῡχὴ σώματός ἐστιν τῷ ἀιδεῖ, τὸ δὲ τῷ ὁρᾱτῷ</span> _what then do we say about the soul? That it is visible or invisible? Not visible. Then it is invisible? Yes. Consequently soul has a closer resemblance to the invisible than the body, and the latter to the visible_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 79b">P. Ph. 79b</cite>.

##### <a id="s2792"></a> §2792

In the argument _ex contrario_ set forth in clauses with <span lang="grc">μέν</span> and <span lang="grc">δέ, ἄρα</span>, usually meaning _in sooth_, is commonly placed with the second clause (<cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 80d">P. Ph. 80d</cite>, R. 445 b), occasionally with the first (<cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 46d">P. Cr. 46d</cite>, L. 840 b), or with both (<cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 97a">P. Ph. 97a</cite>, R. 600 c).

##### <a id="s2793"></a> §2793

In direct questions <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> adds liveliness, while at the same time it marks connection or consequence. So <span lang="grc">τίς ἄρα</span> _who then?_ <span lang="grc">πῶς ἄρα</span> _how then?_ In questions of anxiety <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> marks increase of feeling. Thus, <span lang="grc">τί μ᾽ ἄρα τί μ᾽ ὀλέκεις</span>; _why then_, _why dost thou destroy me?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 1285">S. Ant. 1285</cite>.

##### <a id="s2794"></a> §2794

<span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> occurs in questions in which the admissibility of one opinion is inferred from the rejection of another. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰπέ μοι, ἔφη, ὦ Οεοδότη, ἔστι σοι ἀγρός; οὐκ ἔμοιγ᾽, ἔφη. ἀλλ᾽ ἄρα οἰκίᾱ προσόδους ἔχουσα</span>; ‘_tell me_,’ _said he_, ‘_Theodote_, _have you an estate?_’ ‘_Not I indeed_,’ _said she_. ‘_But perhaps then you have a house that brings in an income?_’ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.11.4">X. M. 3.11.4</cite>. Such questions are often ironical (<cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 25a">P. A. 25a</cite>).

##### <a id="s2795"></a> §2795

<span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> is often used to indicate new perception, or surprise genuine or affected; as when the truth is just realized after a previous erroneous opinion and one finds oneself undeceived either agreeably or disagreeably. So, especially with the imperfect of <span lang="grc">εἶναι, ἄρα</span> means _after all, it seems, why then, so then_, _sure enough_. See [1902](../20-the-verb-voices/10-the-tenses.md#s1902).

##### <a id="s2796"></a> §2796

<span lang="grc"> **ει᾽ ἄρα, ἐὰ̄ν ἄρα** </span> _if really, if after all, if indeed_, are commonly used of that which is improbable or undesirable; <span lang="grc"> **ει᾽** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **ἐὰ̄ν** </span>) <span lang="grc"> **μὴ ἄρα** </span> _unless perhaps_ (_nisi forte_, _nisi vero_) is often ironical. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ ἄρα γέγονεν ὡς οὗτοι ἔλεγον</span> _if indeed it did take place as they said_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 56.28">D. 56.28</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ μὴν εἰ καὶ τοῦτ᾽ ἄρα δεῖ μ᾽ εἰπεῖν</span> _and yet if I must after all say this too_ 18. 317, <span lang="grc">πολλάκις τοῖς Ἀθηναίοις παρῄνει, ἢν ἄρα ποτὲ κατὰ γῆν βιασθῶσι . . . ται῀ς ναυσὶ πρὸς ἅπαντας ἀνθίστασθαι</span> _he often counselled the Athenians_, _if after all they should ever be hard pressed on the land side, to fight the world with their fleet_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.93">T. 1.93</cite>, <span lang="grc">πῶς ἂν οὖν ὁ τοιοῦτος ἀνὴρ διαφθείροι τοὺς νέους; εἰ μὴ ἄρα ἡ τῆς ἀρετῆς ἐπιμέλεια διαφθορά̄ ἐστιν</span> _how then could such a man corrupt the young? unless perchance the study of virtue is corruption_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.2.8">X. M. 1.2.8</cite>.

##### <a id="s2797"></a> §2797

<span lang="grc">εἰ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> is common after <span lang="grc">σκοπῶ</span>, etc. See [2672](../33-indirect-dependent-questions.md#s2672).

##### <a id="s2798"></a> §2798

<span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> is often used, especially with <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>, to introduce the statement of others which, in the view of the speaker, is (usually) to be rejected. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀκούω</span> <!-- page 637 --> <span lang="grc">αὐτὸν ἐρεῖν ὡς ἄρ᾽ ἐγὼ πάντων ὧν κατηγορῶ κοινωνὸς γέγονα</span> _I hear that he is going to say that I forsooth_ (or _if you please_) _have been a partner in all that I denounced_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.202">D. 19.202</cite>.

##### <a id="s2799"></a> §2799

Attic has, in bimembral clauses, <span lang="grc"> **εἴτε ἄρα . . . εἴτε** </span> or <span lang="grc"> **εἴτε . . . εἴτε ἄρα** </span>, as <span lang="grc">εἴτ᾽ ἀληθὲς εἴτ᾽ ἄρ᾽ οὖν μάτην</span> _whether truly or after all, it may be, falsely_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 345">S. Ph. 345</cite>. Hom. has also a similar use with <span lang="grc">οὔτε . . . οὔτε</span>, and <span lang="grc">ἢ . . . ἤ</span>. Hom. has <span lang="grc">ἄρα . . . ἄρα</span> (<cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 23.887"><span lang="grc">ψ</span> 887</cite>).
