# PARTICLES: μήν

##### <a id="s2920"></a> §2920

<span lang="grc"> **μήν** </span> (postpositive): (1) asseverative, _in truth, surely;_ (2) adversative, especially after a negative, _yet_, _however_. The forms <span lang="grc">μήν</span> (Hom., Att.), <span lang="grc">μά̄ν</span> (Hom., Lesb., Dor., lyric parts of tragedy), <span lang="grc">μέν</span> _truly_ (Hom., Att.) and <span lang="grc">μά</span> in oaths are all connected. <span lang="grc">μήν</span> emphasizes either a whole statement or a single word.

<span lang="grc">ὧδε γὰρ ἐξερέω, καὶ μὴν τετελεσμένον ἔσται</span> _for thus I will declare, and verily it shall be accomplished_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 23.410"><span lang="grc">Ψ</span> 410</cite>; <span lang="grc">καλὸν μὲν ἡ ἀλήθεια . . ., ἔοικε μὴν οὐ ῥᾴδιον πείθειν</span> _truth is a fine thing, yet it does not seem an easy thing to persuade_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 663e">P. L. 663e</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ δ᾽ ἄγε μήν</span> _come now, on then_ A 302, <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν μὴν κωλύ̄ει</span> _but nothing hinders_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 268e">P. Phae. 268e</cite>.

##### <a id="s2921"></a> §2921

**Combinations of <span lang="grc">μήν</span>**:

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλὰ μήν** </span> ( . . . <span lang="grc"> **γε** </span>) _but surely; but yet; nay_, _indeed; well_, _in truth_. Often used to add something of greater importance, or in transitions when a new idea is opposed to the foregoing. <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ μήν</span> is often separated by a negative.

<span lang="grc"> **ἦ μήν** </span> _verily_, _verily_. Often to introduce an oath or a threat. <!-- page 659 -->

<span lang="grc"> **καὶ μήν** </span> _and verily_ or _and yet_ according to the context. <span lang="grc">καὶ μήν</span> frequently introduces a new fact or thought and hence often denotes transition, sometimes opposition (_further_, _however_, _and yet_). In tragedy this formula is used to mark the beginning of a new scene, as when the arrival of a newcomer is thus signalized (_but here comes_); as <span lang="grc">καὶ μὴν ἄναξ ὅδε</span> _and lo! here is the king_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 549">S. O. C. 549</cite>. In _replies_, <span lang="grc">καὶ μήν</span> usually confirms the last remark, accedes to a request, or denotes hearty assent; sometimes there is an adversative sense (_and yet; and_ (_yet_) _surely; oh_, _but_). In _enumerations_, <span lang="grc">καὶ μήν</span> adds a new fact (_and besides_).

<span lang="grc"> **και μὴν . . . γε** </span> in transitions or enumerations marks something of still greater importance; but it is not so strong as <span lang="grc">καὶ μὲν δή</span>. Here <span lang="grc">γέ</span> emphasizes the word or words with which it is immediately connected. In replies, _and indeed_, _and yet_ or _oh_, _but;_ as <span lang="grc">καὶ μὴν ποιήσω γε</span> _and yet I will do it_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 1045">S. El. 1045</cite>.

<span lang="grc"> **καὶ μὴν καί** </span> (neg. <span lang="grc">καὶ μὴν οὐδέ</span>) _and in truth also_.

<span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ μήν** </span> _surely not_, <span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ μὴν ἀλλά** </span> _nevertheless_ ([2767](../35-negative-sentences.md#s2767)), <span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ μὴν οὐδέ** </span> _nor again_ ([2768](../35-negative-sentences.md#s2768)), <span lang="grc"> **οὐδὲ μήν** </span> _and certainly not_.

<span lang="grc"> **τί μήν** </span>; lit. _what indeed_ (_quid uero_), as <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ τί μὴν δοκεῖς</span>; _but what in truth is your opinion?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 162b">P. Th. 162b</cite>. <span lang="grc">τί μήν</span>; standing alone, has the force of _naturally_, _of course_. Thus, <span lang="grc">λέγουσιν ἡμᾶς ὡς ὀλωλότας, τί μήν</span>; _they speak of us as dead_, _and why should they not?_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 672">A. Ag. 672</cite>. Often in Plato to indicate assent. <span lang="grc"> **τί μὴν οὔ** </span>; (_why indeed not_=) _of course I do_.
