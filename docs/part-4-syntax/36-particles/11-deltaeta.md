# PARTICLES: δή

##### <a id="s2840"></a> §2840

<span lang="grc"> **δή** </span> (postpositive except in Hom. <span lang="grc">δὴ γάρ</span> and poetic <span lang="grc">δὴ τότε</span>) marks something as immediately present and clear to the mind, and gives greater precision, positiveness, and exactness. It sets forth what is obvious, acknowledged, and natural, and often corresponds to _voilà_. <span lang="grc">δή</span> is used with single words (especially adjectives, adverbs, pronouns, and conjunctions) or, as a sentence adverb, with whole clauses. <span lang="grc">δή</span> usually stands after the word it emphasizes, though it may be separated from it by one or more other words.

##### <a id="s2841"></a> §2841

**<span lang="grc">δή</span> of what is Obvious and Natural**.—Thus, <span lang="grc">ἴστε δή</span> _you know of course_, <span lang="grc">δεῖ δή</span> _it is manifestly necessary_. So <span lang="grc">οὐχ οἵτως ἔχει; ἔχει δή</span> _is not this so? Of course it is_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 27c">P. A. 27c</cite>, <span lang="grc">νῦν δ᾽ ὁρᾶτε δή</span> _but now you certainly see_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.2.12">X. C. 3.2.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">Παρύσατις μὲν δὴ ἡ μήτηρ ὑπῆρχε τῷ Κύ̄ρῳ</span> _Parysatis_, _his mother, naturally supported Cyrus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.4">X. A. 1.1.4</cite>.

##### <a id="s2842"></a> §2842

**Ironical <span lang="grc">δή</span>**.—Thus, <span lang="grc">Σωκράτης ὁ σοφὸς δή</span> _Socrates the wise forsooth_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 27a">P. A. 27a</cite>; often <span lang="grc"> **ὡς δή** </span>, as <span lang="grc">ὡς δὴ σύ μοι τύραννος Ἁργείων ἔσῃ</span> _that you forsooth should be the lord and master of the Argives!_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 1633">A. Ag. 1633</cite>.

##### <a id="s2843"></a> §2843

**Intensive <span lang="grc">δή</span>** emphasizes, and makes definite, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns, and other words. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἅπαντες δή</span> _absolutely all_, <span lang="grc">κράτιστοι δή</span> _the very best_, <span lang="grc">μόνος δή</span> _quite alone_, <span lang="grc">ὀλίγοι δή</span> _very few;_ <span lang="grc">οὕτω δή</span> _just so_, <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ δή</span> _exactly as_, <span lang="grc">πολλάκις δή</span> _very often_, <span lang="grc">δῆλα δή</span> _quite plain_, <span lang="grc">νῦν δή</span> _just now, now at once;_ <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖνος δή</span> _this_ (_and no other_), <span lang="grc">δ̀ς δή</span> _who indeed_. With indefinite pronouns <span lang="grc">δή</span> increases the indefiniteness ([339 e](../../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s339)); as <span lang="grc">ὅστις δή</span> _whoever at all_. With other words: <span lang="grc">εἰ δή</span> _if indeed_, <span lang="grc">οὐ δή</span> _no indeed_, <span lang="grc">ἵνα δή</span> _that in truth_.

**a.** With imperatives and in questions <span lang="grc">δή</span> adds urgency; as <span lang="grc">ἄκουε δή</span> _pray listen!_ <span lang="grc">τί δή</span>; _why_, _pray?_

##### <a id="s2844"></a> §2844

<span lang="grc">δή</span> may introduce emphatically the conclusion of a temporal sentence or of a narrative on passing to a new topic; as <span lang="grc">ἐνταῦθα δή, τότε δή</span> _then indeed_, _then and not till then, then it was that_. Cp. <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.10.1">X. A. 1.10.1</cite>.

##### <a id="s2845"></a> §2845

**Temporal <span lang="grc">δή</span>** often, especially with <span lang="grc">καί</span>, approximates in meaning to <span lang="grc">ἤδη</span> _already_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁ δὲ θανὼν κεύθει κάτω δὴ γῆς</span> _but he is dead and already is hidden beneath the earth_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 967">S. O. T. 967</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁπότε . . . θηρῴης καὶ δὴ δύο ἡμέρᾱς</span> _when you have hunted_ (_already_) _for two days_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.4.17">X. C. 2.4.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ δὴ λέγω σοι</span> _well I will tell thee_ (without further ado) <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 245">S. Ant. 245</cite>. So also in <span lang="grc">τέλος δή, νῦν δή</span>.—Of succession, <span lang="grc">δή</span> means _next_.—Poetic <span lang="grc"> **δαὖτε** </span> (<span lang="grc">δὴ αὖτε</span>) means _now again_.

##### <a id="s2846"></a> §2846

**Consecutive and Resumptive <span lang="grc">δή</span>** is used to set forth an inference, draw a conclusion, denote a consequence, and mark a transition (<span lang="grc">μὲν δὴ . . . δέ</span>). Here <span lang="grc">δή</span> is a sentence adverb: _accordingly_, _then_, _of course, clearly_, _you_ <!-- page 647 --> _see_, _I say_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔλεγον ὅτι κατίδοιεν νύκτωρ πολλὰ πυρὰ φαίνοντα. ἐδόκει δὴ τοῖς στρατηγοῖς οὐκ ἀσφαλὲς εἶναι διασκηνοῦν</span> _they said that they had seen many fires visible in the night; accordingly it seemed to the generals to be unsafe to encamp apart_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.4.10">X. A. 4.4.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">φεραύλᾱς μὲν δὴ οὕτως εἶπεν· ἀνί̄σταντο δὲ καὶ ἄλλοι πολλοί</span> _Pherauias then spake thus; and many others also rose to speak_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.3.16">X. C. 2.3.16</cite>.

##### <a id="s2847"></a> §2847

<span lang="grc"> **καὶ δή** </span>: (**a**) Introduces a climax, as <span lang="grc">καὶ δὴ τὸ μέγιστον</span> _and above all_, _what is the main thing_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 41b">P. A. 41b</cite>. (**b**) In replies = _well;_ as <span lang="grc">βλέψον κάτω· καὶ δὴ βλέπω</span> _look down! Well_, _I_ am _looking_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Birds 175">Ar. Av. 175</cite>. This is akin to the temporal use. (**c**) In assumptions = _suppose_ ([1771](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1771)). On <span lang="grc">καὶ δὴ καί</span> see [2890](17-kappaalphaiota.md#s2890).
