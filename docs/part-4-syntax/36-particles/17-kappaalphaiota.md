# PARTICLES: καί

##### <a id="s2868"></a> §2868

<span lang="grc"> **καί** </span> is both a copulative conjunction (_and_) connecting words, clauses, or sentences; and an adverb meaning _also_, _even_.

## _Conjunctional_ <span lang="grc">καί</span>

##### <a id="s2869"></a> §2869

Copulative <span lang="grc">καί</span> often has an intensive or heightening force; as where it joins a part and the whole, the universal and the particular. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐν Ἀθηναίοις καὶ τοῖς Ἕλλησι</span> <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 413">Ar. Nub. 413</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὦ Ζεῦ καὶ θεοί</span> Ar. Pl. 1 (<span lang="grc">θεοὶ καὶ Ζεύς</span> _the gods and_ above all _Zeus_), <span lang="grc">ἐνταῦθα ἔμειναν ἡμέρᾱς τρεῖς καὶ ἧκε Μένων</span> <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.6">X. A. 1.2.6</cite>. On <span lang="grc"> **καὶ ταῦτα** </span>, see [947](../09-the-predicate.md#s947), [2083](../22-the-participle.md#s2083).

**a.** Here <span lang="grc">καί</span> often = _namely_, _for example, and so_ where an antecedent statement is explained either by another word or by an example. Cp. <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.9.14">X. A. 1.9.14</cite>, 4. 1. 19, 5. 2. 9, 5. 6. 8.

##### <a id="s2870"></a> §2870

The heightening force is also seen where <span lang="grc">καί</span> with corrective force may be rendered by _or;_ often to set forth a climax and not an alternative. Thus, <span lang="grc">σοφίᾱ ὀλίγου τινὸς ἀξίᾱ καὶ οὐδενός</span> _wisdom worth little or nothing_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 23a">P. A. 23a</cite>, <span lang="grc">μαχαιροποιοὶ . . . ἀνὰ πέντε μνᾶς καὶ ἕξ</span> _sword-cutlers worth five or six minas each_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 27.9">D. 27.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">προιοῦσι δὲ καὶ ἀπιοῦσι πόλεμος</span> _but war if we advance or retire_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.21">X. A. 2.1.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ δίκαια κἄ̄δικα</span> _right or wrong_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 99">Ar. Nub. 99</cite>, <span lang="grc">σὸς</span> (<span lang="grc">γόνος</span>), <span lang="grc">κει᾽ μὴ σός</span> _thy son, or if not thine_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 1323">S. O. C. 1323</cite>.

##### <a id="s2871"></a> §2871

<span lang="grc">καί</span> often has an adversative force; as where it joins a negative to an affirmative clause. Here <span lang="grc">καὶ οὐ</span> (<span lang="grc">μή</span>) is almost = _but not_, as in <span lang="grc">ἐμ᾽ ἐχειροτόνησαν καὶ οὐχ ὑ̄μᾶς</span> _they elected me and_ (= _but_) _not you_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.288">D. 18.288</cite>. So also where <span lang="grc">καί</span> <!-- page 651 --> is like <span lang="grc">καίτοι</span> _and yet;_ as <span lang="grc">χαίρων ἄπιθι· καί σ᾽ ἄ̄κων ἐγὼ λείπω</span> _fare thee well; and yet I leave thee unwillingly_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Kn. 1250">Ar. Eq. 1250</cite>. To connect _negative_ clauses <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> is used.

##### <a id="s2872"></a> §2872

In questions, <span lang="grc">καί</span> _before_ an interrogative expression marks an objection occasioned by surprise or indignation; as <span lang="grc">καὶ τίς θανόντων ἦλθεν ἐξ Ἅιδου πάλιν</span>; _and_, _pray_, _who of the dead has come back from Hades?_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Her. 297">E. H. F. 297</cite>. So <span lang="grc">καὶ πῶς</span>; _pray_, _how comes it that?_ Cp. Eng. _and_ when a speaker is stopped by an abrupt question.

**a.** _After_ an interrogative expression adverbial <span lang="grc">καί</span> asks for further information concerning a statement assumed to be true. Thus, <span lang="grc">ποίου χρόνου δὲ καὶ πεπόρθηται πόλις</span>; _but when_ **was** _the city captured?_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 278">A. Ag. 278</cite>. Cp. [2884](#s2884).

##### <a id="s2873"></a> §2873

In imperative sentences <span lang="grc">καί</span> often means _and now, just_. Thus, <span lang="grc">καί μοι ἀνάγνωθι τὸ ψήφισμα</span> _and now read me the bill_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 13.35">L. 13.35</cite>, <span lang="grc">καί μοι ἀπόκρῑναι</span> _just answer me_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 25a">P. A. 25a</cite>.

##### <a id="s2874"></a> §2874

<span lang="grc">καί</span> may mark a result (<cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 154c">P. Th. 154c</cite>, quoted in [2288](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/09-conditional-clauses.md#s2288)).

##### <a id="s2875"></a> §2875

After expressions of _sameness_ and _likeness_ <span lang="grc">καί</span> has the force of _as_ (Lat. _ac_). Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁ αὐτὸς ὑ̄μῖν στόλος ἐστὶ καὶ ἡμῖν</span> _your expedition is the same as ours_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.2.10">X. A. 2.2.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐχ ὁμοίως καὶ πρίν</span> _not the same as before_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.28">T. 7.28</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἴσα καὶ ἱκέται</span> _the same as suppliants_ 3. 14, <span lang="grc">ταὐτὰ καί</span> _the same as_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.18">X. C. 1.3.18</cite>. This use is commoner in prose than poetry.

##### <a id="s2876"></a> §2876

In expressions denoting coincidence of time <span lang="grc">καί</span> often has the force of _when_. So <span lang="grc"> **ἅμα . . . καί** </span> ([2169](../26-syntax-of-the-compound-sentence.md#s2169)), <span lang="grc"> **ἤδη . . . καί** </span> <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.7">X. A. 2.1.7</cite>, <span lang="grc"> **οὔπω . . . καί** </span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyd. 277b">P. Eu. 277b</cite>, <span lang="grc"> **οὐκ ἔφθην . . . καί** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **εὐθύς** </span>) _I had not got the start . . . when_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 19.22">I. 19.22</cite>, <cite data-ref="Dem. 43.69">D. 43.69</cite>. Cp. <span lang="grc"> **καὶ . . . καί** </span> in <span lang="grc">καὶ ἥκομεν καὶ ἡμῖν ἐξελθὼν ὁ θυρωρὸς . . . εἶπεν περιμένειν</span> _as soon as we arrived the doorkeeper came out and told us to wait_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 59e">P. Ph. 59e</cite>.

##### <a id="s2877"></a> §2877

<span lang="grc"> **καὶ . . . καί** </span> _both . . . and_, _not only . . . but also, as . . . so_, _as well as . . . as also_, sometimes _whether . . . or_, emphasizes each member separately, and forms a less close combination than <span lang="grc">τὲ καί</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">καὶ τότε καὶ νῦν</span> _not only then but also now_. So <span lang="grc">τῑμὰ̄ς δοτέον καὶ ζῶντι καὶ τελευτήσαντι</span> _honours must be paid him both when living and after death_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 414a">P. R. 414a</cite>, <span lang="grc">σὺ καὶ δέδορκας κου᾽ βλέπεις</span> _thou both hast sight and_ (yet) _dost not see_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 413">S. O. T. 413</cite>, <span lang="grc">κἀ̄πεμπόμην πρὸς ταῦτα καὶ τὸ πᾶν φράσω</span> _as I was sent for this purpose so I will tell thee all_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 680">S. El. 680</cite>, <span lang="grc">τολμᾶν ἀνάγκη, κἂ̄ν τύχω κἂ̄ν μὴ τύχω</span> _I must dare whether I succeed or fail_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hec. 751">E. Hec. 751</cite>.

##### <a id="s2878"></a> §2878

In a series of more than two ideas <span lang="grc">καί</span> is used before each, where English would use _and_ only before the last. Thus, <span lang="grc">συντυγχάνουσιν αὐτῷ καὶ λαμβάνουσιν αὐτὸν καὶ γυναῖκα καὶ παῖδας καὶ τοὺς ἵππους καὶ πάντα τὰ ὄντα</span> _they fell upon him and seized him, his wife, his children, his horses, and all his possessions_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.8.22">X. A. 7.8.22</cite>.

##### <a id="s2879"></a> §2879

Adjectives of quantity, as <span lang="grc"> **πολύς** </span> and <span lang="grc"> **ὀλίγος** </span> in the plural, are usually joined to an adjective in the same construction by <span lang="grc">καί</span> or <span lang="grc">τὲ καί</span> (also by <span lang="grc">τέ</span> or <span lang="grc">τὲ . . . τέ</span> in poetry). Thus, <span lang="grc">πολλὰ καὶ δεινά</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 37.57">D. 37.57</cite> (<span lang="grc">δεινὰ καὶ πολλά</span> 37. [57](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/02-euphony-of-vowels.md#s57)), <span lang="grc">πολλά τε καὶ δεινά</span> <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.5.8">X. A. 5.5.8</cite>. In <span lang="grc">πολλὰ καὶ μεγάλα ἀγαθά</span> (<cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.5.9">X. C. 1.5.9</cite>), the substantive is qualified by two adjectives; whereas in English the second adjec<!-- page 652 --> tive is taken with the substantive and treated as a _unit_ modified by the first adjective (_many good-things_).

**a.** <span lang="grc"> **πολλοὶ καὶ ἄλλοι** </span> means _many others also_ (with <span lang="grc">καί</span> adverbial). For _many others_ we find <span lang="grc">ἄλλοι πολλοί</span> (very common) or <span lang="grc">πολλοὶ ἄλλοι</span>.

##### <a id="s2880"></a> §2880

Some combinations of conjunctional <span lang="grc">καί</span> are:

<span lang="grc"> **καὶ . . . μέντοι** </span> _and however, and of course_ (in <span lang="grc">καὶ μέντοι καί</span> the first <span lang="grc">καί</span> may be adverbial: _yes indeed and_).

<span lang="grc"> **καὶ . . . τοίνυν** </span> _and . . . further_, in connecting a thought with the preceding.

## _Adverbial_ <span lang="grc">καί</span>

##### <a id="s2881"></a> §2881

Adverbial <span lang="grc">καί</span> _also_, _even_ (Lat. _etiam_) influences single words or whole clauses. Adverbial <span lang="grc">καί</span> stresses an important idea; usually the idea set forth in the word that follows, but sometimes also a preceding word when that word stands first in its clause. <span lang="grc">καί</span> often serves to increase or diminish the force of particular words; sometimes it gives a tone of modesty.

##### <a id="s2882"></a> §2882

With single words: **a.** <span lang="grc">κᾆτα</span> _then too_, <span lang="grc">καὶ ἐγώ</span> _I on my part_, <span lang="grc">σὸν ἢ κἀ̄μὸν γένος</span> _offspring from thee or me either_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 965">S. El. 965</cite>, <span lang="grc">βουλόμενος δὲ καὶ αὐτὸς λαμπρόν τι ποιῆσαι</span> _desirous of himself too doing something illustrious_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.4.15">X. C. 5.4.15</cite>.

**b.** <span lang="grc">καὶ πρίν</span> _even before_, <span lang="grc">καὶ ὀψέ</span> _late though it be_, <span lang="grc">καὶ οὕτως</span> _even so_, <span lang="grc">καὶ ἔτι καὶ νῦν</span> _and now too, and still even now_, <span lang="grc">ὀκνῶ καὶ λέγειν</span> _I fear even to say it_, <span lang="grc">πολλὴ μωρίᾱ καὶ τοῦ ἐπιχειρήματος</span> _the very attempt is utter folly_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 317a">P. Pr. 317a</cite>. On <span lang="grc">καί</span> _though_ with a participle, see [2083](../22-the-participle.md#s2083).

**c.** Often with adverbs of intensity, as <span lang="grc">καὶ μάλα</span> _exceedingly_, _certainly_, <span lang="grc">καὶ κάρτα</span> _very greatly_, <span lang="grc">καὶ πάνυ</span> _absolutely_. With comparatives and superlatives: <span lang="grc">καὶ μᾶλλον</span> _yet more_, <span lang="grc">καὶ μωρότατον</span> _altogether the most foolish thing_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.22">X. A. 3.2.22</cite>.

##### <a id="s2883"></a> §2883

With a whole phrase or clause; as <span lang="grc">ἄμφω γὰρ αὐτὼ καὶ κατακτανεῖν νοεῖς</span>; _what_, _dost thou indeed intend to put them both to death?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 770">S. Ant. 770</cite>. Other examples in 2885-2887.

##### <a id="s2884"></a> §2884

When <span lang="grc">καί</span> stresses a verb in interrogative and conditional sentences it is often to be rendered by an emphatic auxiliary, often by _at all_. Thus, <span lang="grc">πολλάκις ἐσκεψάμην τί καὶ βούλεσθε</span> _I have often asked myself the question what you_ **can** _want_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.38">T. 6.38</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί καὶ χρὴ προσδοκᾶν</span>; _what on earth_ **is** _one to expect?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.46">D. 4.46</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί γὰρ ἄν τις καὶ ποιοῖ ἄλλο</span>; _for what else_ **could** _one do?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 61e">P. Ph. 61e</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ δεῖ καὶ μῦθον λέγειν καλόν</span> _if it is well to tell a fable at all_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 110b">P. Ph. 110b</cite>. Cp. [2872 a](#s2872).

**a.** In affirmative independent clauses or sentences <span lang="grc">καί</span> often has an emphasis which is difficult to render; as <span lang="grc">ὁ κίνδῡνος νῦν δὴ καὶ δόξειεν ἂν δεινὸς εἶναι</span> _the danger must now indeed seem to be dreadful_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 107c">P. Ph. 107c</cite>.

##### <a id="s2885"></a> §2885

**<span lang="grc">Καί</span> of Balanced Contrast**.—In order to mark the connection of thought between antecedent and consequent, <span lang="grc">καί</span> _also_, _too_, is often placed in the subordinate clause or in the main clause or in both.

**a.** Greek has thus the following modes of expression where a comparison is instituted between the parts of such bimembral sentences: “What _I_ do, that you _also_ do” (as in English) or “What I _also_ ( = I on my part) do, that you do” or “What I _also_ do, that you _also_ do.” In the subordinate clause <span lang="grc">καί</span> seems superfluous to English idiom. <!-- page 653 -->

##### <a id="s2886"></a> §2886

<span lang="grc">Καί</span> of balanced contrast occurs frequently when the subordinate clause sets forth something corresponding to, or deducible from, the main clause; and when an antithesis is to be emphasized. It is found especially in relative, causal, and final clauses, and has the effect of putting such subordinate clauses on a plane with the main clause. A relative word often adds <span lang="grc">-περ</span> or is followed by <span lang="grc">δή</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">τὰ δὲ τῆς πόλεως ἔπρᾱττον, ὧνπερ ἕνεκεν καὶ Σωκράτει προσῆλθον</span> _they devoted themselves to those affairs of state on account of which they had in fact associated with Socrates_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.2.47">X. M. 1.2.47</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ ἡμῖν ταὐτὰ δοκεῖ ἅπερ καὶ βασιλεῖ</span> _we hold exactly the same views as the king_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.22">X. A. 2.1.22</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὴ καὶ ἡ πόλις ἐσώθη . . . ἀξιῶ κἀ̄μοὶ σωτηρίᾱν γενέσθαι</span> _since the city has been saved I beg that safety be granted to me as well_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.143">And. 1.143</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔμαθον καὶ ἐγὼ ὥσπερ καὶ οἱ ἄλλοι</span> _I_ (on my part) _learned just as the rest did too_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Alc. 1 110d">P. Alc. 110d</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῑμωρίᾱ γὰρ οὐκ εὐτυχεῖ δικαίως ὅτι καὶ ἀδικεῖται</span> _for vengeance is not successful in accordance with justice, because it is taken upon a wrong_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.62">T. 4.62</cite>.

##### <a id="s2887"></a> §2887

In final clauses <span lang="grc">ἵνα καί</span> is common, and sometimes, like Eng. _just_, serves to show that the fact answers to the expectation, or the effect to the cause (or _vice versa_). Thus, <span lang="grc">βούλει οὖν ἕπεσθαι ἵνα καὶ ἴδῃς τοὺς ὄντας αὐτόθι</span>; _do you wish to go along then just to see those who are there?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lysis 204a">P. Lys. 204a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄρξομαι δὲ ἀπὸ τῆς ἰ̄ᾱτρικῆς λέγων ἵνα καὶ πρεσβεύωμεν τὴν τέχνην</span> _I will begin my speech with medicine in order that we may do honour to our art_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 186b">P. S. 186b</cite>.

##### <a id="s2888"></a> §2888

<span lang="grc">Καί</span> of balanced contrast appears also in coördinate clauses; as <span lang="grc">ἤδη γὰρ ἔγωγε καὶ Φιλολά̄ου ἤκουσα . . . ἤδη δὲ καὶ ἄλλων τινῶν</span> _for I have ere now heard Philolaus . . . and ere now certain others besides him_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 61e">P. Ph. 61e</cite>, <span lang="grc">κατὰ πολλὰ μὲν καὶ ἄλλα, οὐχ ἥκιστα δὲ καὶ κατὰ ταῦτα</span> _as in many other respects also and not least_ (_too_) _in this_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 1.108">Aes. 1.108</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ τῶν τἀ̄νταῦθα διοικήσειν . . . καὶ πρὶν ὑπεσχημένων καὶ νῦν δὲ πρᾱττόντων</span> _by those who had promised to manage things there before and are now also doing them_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 7.5">D. 7.5</cite>. The negative of <span lang="grc">καὶ . . . καὶ . . . δέ</span> is <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ . . . οὐδὲ . . . δέ</span>.

**a.** So in disjunctive phrases or clauses. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἴτε διὰ τὸ ἐπιβόημα εἴτε καὶ αὐτῷ ἄλλο τι . . . δόξαν</span> _either because of the exclamation or also because some other thought occurred to him_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.65">T. 5.65</cite>; and so <span lang="grc">ἢ καί</span> 2862. Cp. <span lang="grc">ἐζητεῖτο οὐδέν τι μᾶλλον ὑπὸ τῶν ἄλλων ἢ καὶ ὑπ᾽ ἐμοῦ</span> _he was not searched for by the others more than he was by me_ (_on my part_) <cite data-ref="Antiph. 5.23">Ant. 5.23</cite>.

##### <a id="s2889"></a> §2889

Similarly the <span lang="grc">καί</span> of <span lang="grc"> **εἴ τις καὶ ἄλλος** </span> is superfluous; as <span lang="grc">εἴπερ τι καὶ ἄλλο καὶ τοῦτο μαθητόν</span> _if any other thing is learnable, this is too_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 2">X. S. 2</cite>. 6. But <span lang="grc">καί</span> is usually omitted in the main clause; as <span lang="grc">ἐπίσταται δ᾽ εἴ τις καὶ ἄλλος</span> _he knows as well as anybody else_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.15">X. A. 1.4.15</cite>. So <span lang="grc">ὥς τις καὶ ἄλλος</span> _as also any other_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.6.8">X. A. 2.6.8</cite>.

##### <a id="s2890"></a> §2890

<span lang="grc"> **καὶ δὴ καί** </span> _and especially, and in particular, and what is more_, lays stress on a particular instance or application of a general statement. Here the second <span lang="grc">καί</span> emphasizes the following word. <span lang="grc">καὶ δὴ καί</span> is usually attached to a preceding <span lang="grc">τέ</span> or <span lang="grc">καί</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">καὶ δὴ καὶ τότε πρῳαίτερον συνελέγημεν</span> _and on that especial occasion we came together somewhat earlier_ than usual <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 59d">P. Ph. 59d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν ἄλλοις τε πολλοῖς καὶ δὴ καὶ ἐν τοῖς κάμνουσιν</span> _in the case of many others and particularly in that of the sick_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.6.21">X. C. 1.6.21</cite>.

##### <a id="s2891"></a> §2891

<span lang="grc"> **καὶ . . . δέ** </span> _and . . . also_, _and . . . moreover_. Here <span lang="grc">καί</span> empha<!-- page 654 --> sizes the important intervening word or words, while <span lang="grc">δέ</span> connects. Thus, <span lang="grc">καὶ σὲ δ᾽ ἐν τούτοις λέγω</span> _and I count thee also among these_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 973">A. Pr. 973</cite>. _And also not_ is <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ . . . δέ</span>. Hom. has <span lang="grc">καὶ δέ</span> _and further, and even_ (H [113](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s113)), not <span lang="grc">καὶ . . . δέ. καὶ . . . δέ</span> (for <span lang="grc">τέ</span>) is different (<cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 432">S. Ant. 432</cite>).
