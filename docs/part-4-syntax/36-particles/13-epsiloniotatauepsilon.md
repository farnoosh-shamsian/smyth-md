# PARTICLES: εἴτε

##### <a id="s2852"></a> §2852

<span lang="grc"> **εἴτε** </span> (from <span lang="grc">εἰ ¨ τέ</span>), a disjunctive particle, generally doubled: <span lang="grc">εἴτε . . . εἴτε</span> _whether . . . or_ ([2675](../33-indirect-dependent-questions.md#s2675)), _if . . . or_ (_siue . . . siue_), giving equal value to each supposition.

**a.** With the subjunctive we find <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν τε</span> (<span lang="grc">ἤν τε, ἄ̄ν τε</span>). Hom. has <span lang="grc">εἴτε . . . εἴτε</span>, but not <span lang="grc">ἤν τε . . . ἤν τε</span>, with the subjunctive. In the same sense Hom. has <span lang="grc">ἢ . . . ἤ</span> and <span lang="grc">ἤτε . . . ἤτε</span> with the subjunctive.

##### <a id="s2853"></a> §2853

There are various forms of <span lang="grc">εἴτε</span> clauses:

**a.** Both <span lang="grc">εἴτε</span> clauses may have the same finite verb in common, which verb is used only once; as <span lang="grc">εἴτε βούλεσθε πολεμεῖν ἡμῖν εἴτε φίλοι εἶναι</span> _whether you wish to wage war upon us or to be our friends_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.2.13">X. C. 3.2.13</cite>. <!-- page 648 -->

**b.** Each <span lang="grc">εἴτε</span> clause has its own verb and its own main clause; as <span lang="grc">ἐκέλευσέ σε, εἴτε πάντας αἰτιᾷ, κρί̄ναντα σὲ αὐτὸν χρῆσθαι ὅ τι ἂν βούλῃ, εἴτε ἕνα τινὰ ἢ δύο . . . αἰτιᾷ, τούτους ἀξιοῦσι παρασχεῖν σοι ἑαυτοὺς εἰς κρίσιν</span> the army _requests that, if you accuse all, you pass sentence on them and treat them as you may think best; or_, _if you accuse one or two, they think it right that these men should surrender themselves to you for judgment_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.6.20">X. A. 6.6.20</cite>.

**c.** One main clause refers to both <span lang="grc">εἴτε</span> clauses; as <span lang="grc">ὁ ἀγαθὸς ἀνὴρ . . . εὐδαίμων ἐστὶ . . . ἐά̄ν τε μέγας καὶ ἰσχῡρός, ἐά̄ν τε σμῑκρὸς καὶ ἀσθενὴς ᾖ</span> _the good man is happy whether he is large and strong or small and weak_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 660e">P. L. 660e</cite>.

**d.** Neither <span lang="grc">εἴτε</span> clause has a verb, which is to be supplied from the main clause; as <span lang="grc">λέγοντες, εἴτ᾽ ἀληθὲς εἴτ᾽ ἄρ᾽ οὖν μάτην</span> (<span lang="grc">ἔλεγον</span>) _saying_, _whether truly or after all, it may be, falsely_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 345">S. Ph. 345</cite>.

**e.** One <span lang="grc">εἴτε</span> clause has its own verb, while the other gets its verb from the main clause (rare); as <span lang="grc">ἐμοὶ σὺ . . . φαίνῃ . . . χρησμῳδεῖν, εἴτε παρ᾽ Εὐθύφρονος ἐπίπνους γενόμενος</span> (<span lang="grc">χρησμῳδεῖς</span>), <span lang="grc">εἴτε καὶ ἄλλη τις μοῦσα πάλαι σε ἐνοῦσα ἐλελήθει</span> _you seem to me to utter prophecies, whether you were inspired by Euthyphron or whether some other muse has long been present in you without your knowing it_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crat. 428c">P. Crat. 428c</cite>.

##### <a id="s2854"></a> §2854

**Variations: <span lang="grc">εἴτε . . . ἤ</span>** (common): <span lang="grc">εἴτε Λῡσίᾱς ἤ τις ἄλλος πώποτε ἔγραψεν ἢ γράψει κτλ</span>. _whether Lysias or anybody else whoever wrote or will write_, etc. <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 277d">P. Phae. 277d</cite>. <span lang="grc"> **ἢ . . . εἴτε** </span>: only in poetry (<cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 175">S. Aj. 175</cite>). <span lang="grc"> **εἴτε . . . εἰ δέ** </span>: when the second member is more important (<cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 952c">P. L. 952c</cite>). On <span lang="grc"> **ει᾽ . . . εἴτε** </span> see [2675 d](../33-indirect-dependent-questions.md#s2675). On <span lang="grc"> **εἴτε** </span> for <span lang="grc">εἴτε . . . εἴτε</span> see [2675 b](../33-indirect-dependent-questions.md#s2675), N. 2.

##### <a id="s2855"></a> §2855

<span lang="grc">εἴτε</span> may be strengthened by <span lang="grc">ἄρα, δή, καί</span>, or <span lang="grc">οὖν. οὖν</span> is usually placed after the first <span lang="grc">εἴτε</span>; like <span lang="grc">καί</span>, it may stand after the second also. When <span lang="grc">καί</span> stands only after the second <span lang="grc">εἴτε</span>, its clause is weaker than the first (<cite data-ref="Dem. 18.57">D. 18.57</cite>).
