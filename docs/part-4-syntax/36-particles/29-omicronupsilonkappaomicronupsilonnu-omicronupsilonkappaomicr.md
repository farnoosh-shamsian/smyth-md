# PARTICLES: οὐκοῦν, οὔκουν

##### <a id="s2951"></a> §2951

**<span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν</span> interrogative**: _not therefore? not then?_ (_nonne_, _igitur? nonne ergo?_). Here the stress lies on the inferential <span lang="grc">οὖν</span> and an affirmative answer is expected as a matter of course. <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν</span> stands at the beginning of its clause.

<span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν . . . εὖ σοι δοκοῦσι βουλεύεσθαι; πρός γε ἃ ὁρῶσι</span> _do you not then think that they lay their plans well? Yes_, _with regard to what they see_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.1.8">X. C. 7.1.8</cite>.

**a.** When a negative answer is expected we have <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν οὐ</span> (<cite data-ref="Plat. Phileb. 43d">P. Phil. 43d</cite>).

**b.** <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν</span> and <span lang="grc">οὖν</span> stand in parallel questions in <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.6.7">X. A. 1.6.7</cite>-8.

**c.** Some scholars write <span lang="grc">οὔκουν</span> or <span lang="grc">οὐκ οὖν</span> for <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν</span> interrogative (and inferential).

##### <a id="s2952"></a> §2952

**<span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν</span> inferential**: _then_, _well then, therefore_, _accordingly_ (_ergo_, _igitur_). Inferential <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν</span> was developed, probably in colloquial speech, from the interrogative use, the speaker anticipating the affirmative answer to his question and emphasizing only the inference. From the negative question all that was left was an expression of his own opinion on the part of the speaker. <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν</span> has become so completely equivalent to <span lang="grc">οὖν</span> that a negative has to be added if one is required.

<span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν, ὅταν δὴ μὴ σθένω, πεπαύσομαι</span> _well then, when my strength fails_, _I shall cease_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 91">S. Ant. 91</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἢ . . . τοὺς ἀμύ̄νεσθαι κελεύοντας πόλεμον ποιεῖν φήσομεν; οὐκοῦν ὑπόλοιπον δουλεύειν</span> _or shall we say that those who bid us defend ourselves make war? Then it is left for us to be slaves_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.59">D. 8.59</cite>. <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν</span> is used even with imperatives; as <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν . . . ἱκανῶς ἐχέτω</span> _accordingly let it suffice_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 274b">P. Phae. 274b</cite>.

**a.** Editors often differ whether, in certain cases, <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν</span> is interrogative or inferential.

##### <a id="s2953"></a> §2953

<span lang="grc"> **οὔκουν** </span> _not then, therefore not, so not, at any rate . . . not_, _surely not_ (_non igitur, non ergo_). Here <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> is strongly emphasized, and <span lang="grc">οὖν</span> is either confirmative or inferential. <span lang="grc">οὔκουν</span> is usually placed at the beginning of its clause.

**a.** In emphatic negative answers; as <span lang="grc">οὔκουν ἔμοιγε δοκεῖ</span> _certainly not, in my opinion at least_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 1.9">X. O. 1.9</cite>.

**b.** In continuous discourse (<cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 807a">P. L. 807a</cite>).

**c.** <span lang="grc">οὔκουν . . . γε</span> returns a negative answer with qualified acquiescence in a preceding statement. Thus, <span lang="grc">τούτων ἄρα Ζεύς ἐστιν ἀσθενέστερος; οὔκουν ἂν ἐκφύγοι</span> <!-- page 664 --> <span lang="grc">γε τὴν πεπρωμένην</span> _is Zeus then weaker than these? Fate at least he surely cannot escape_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 517">A. Pr. 517</cite>.

**d.** In impatient or excited questions (_non? non igitur?_). Thus, <span lang="grc">οὔκουν ἐρεῖς ποτ᾽, εἶτ᾽ ἀπαλλαχθεὶς ἄπει</span>; _wilt thou not speak and so depart and be gone?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 244">S. Ant. 244</cite>.

##### <a id="s2954"></a> §2954

<span lang="grc"> **οὐκ** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **μὴ** </span>) <span lang="grc"> **οὖν** </span> is to be distinguished from <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν</span> or <span lang="grc">οὔκουν</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὁπότε καὶ πείρᾳ του σφαλεῖεν, οὐκ οὖν καὶ τὴν πόλιν γε τῆς σφετέρᾱς ἀρετῆς ἀξιοῦντες στερίσκειν</span> _whenever they were foiled in any attempt they did not for this reason think it right to deprive their city of their valour_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.43">T. 2.43</cite> (<span lang="grc">μὴ οὖν</span> 8. [91](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s91)).

**a.** Hdt. has <span lang="grc"> **οὐκ ὦν** </span> (sometimes written <span lang="grc">οὔκων</span>) to emphasize an idea opposed to what goes before (_non tamen_). Thus, <span lang="grc">ταῦτα λέγοντες τοὺς Κροτωνιήτᾱς οὐκ ὦν ἔπειθον</span> _by these words they did not however persuade the men of Croton_ 3. 137.
