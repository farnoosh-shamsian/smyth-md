# PARTICLES: ὥς, ὡς

##### <a id="s2988"></a> §2988

**Demonstrative <span lang="grc">ὥς</span>** (also accented <span lang="grc">ὡς, ὧς</span>) _thus_, _so_ is originally an ablative from the demonstrative stem <span lang="grc">ὁ-</span> (from <span lang="grc">σο-</span>), from which come the article and <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> _he_ in <span lang="grc">καὶ ὅς, ἦ δ᾽ ὅς</span> ([1113](../16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1113)). For the <span lang="grc">-ς</span>, see [341](../../part-2-inflection/07-adverbs.md#s341). Cp. also <span lang="grc">ὧ-δε</span> _thus_.

So <span lang="grc">καὶ ὥς</span> _even so, nevertheless_, <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽</span> (<span lang="grc">μηδ᾽</span>) <span lang="grc">ὥς</span> _not even thus, in no wise_, <span lang="grc">ὡς αὔτως</span> (<span lang="grc">ὡσαύτως</span>) _in the same way, just so_ (ablative of <span lang="grc">ὁ αὐτός</span>). <span lang="grc">ὡς ἑτέρως</span> (lit. _thus otherwise_, _in that other way_) _quite otherwise_ and <span lang="grc">ὡς ἀληθῶς</span> (lit. _thus truly_) _in very truth_ also probably belong here.

**a.** In some cases it is uncertain whether <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> is demonstrative or relative; e.g. <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> in exclamatory clauses. Cp. [2998](#s2998), [3001](#s3001).

##### <a id="s2989"></a> §2989

**Relative <span lang="grc">ὡς</span>** _as_, _how_ is originally an ablative (_in which way_) from the relative stem <span lang="grc">\[ιγλιδε\]ο-</span>, whence come also <span lang="grc">ὅς, ἥ, ὅ</span>. For the <span lang="grc">-ς</span>, see [341](../../part-2-inflection/07-adverbs.md#s341). Relative <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> has various uses as an adverb or **a** conjunction, all of which represent the primitive meaning.

## Relative_ <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> _as an Adverb

##### <a id="s2990"></a> §2990

In comparative clauses, often correlated with <span lang="grc">οὕτως</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">πιστὸς ἦν, ὡς ὑ̄μεῖς ἐπίστασθε</span> _I was faithful, as you know_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.3.2">X. A. 3.3.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐκέλευσε τοὺς Ἕλληνας, ὡς νόμος αὐτοῖς εἰς μάχην, οὕτω ταχθῆναι</span> _he ordered the Greeks_ (_thus_) _to be stationed as was their custom for battle_ 1. 2. 15. Cp. [2462](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/13-clauses-of-comparison.md#s2462) ff. In similes and comparisons, 2481 ff.

##### <a id="s2991"></a> §2991

<span lang="grc">ὡς</span> is rarely used for <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> after comparatives; as <span lang="grc">μή μου προκήδου μᾶσσον ὡς ἐμοὶ γλυκύ</span> _care not for me further than I wish_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 629">A. Pr. 629</cite>. Cp. [1071](../14-adjectives.md#s1071).

##### <a id="s2992"></a> §2992

In adverbial clauses <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> is often used parenthetically; as <span lang="grc">ὡς ἐμοὶ δοκεῖ</span> _as it seems to me_. Instead of <span lang="grc">ὡς δοκεῖ, ὡς ἔοικε</span> the personal construction is often preferred; as <span lang="grc">ἀπέπλευσαν, ὡς μὲν τοῖς πλείστοις ἐδόκουν, φιλοτῑμηθέντες</span> _they sailed away out of jealousy, as it seemed to most people_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.7">X. A. 1.4.7</cite>.

##### <a id="s2993"></a> §2993

**<span lang="grc">ὡς</span> restrictive** _for_ (cp. _ut_), involving the judgment of the observer, occurs often in elliptical phrases; as (<span lang="grc">Βρᾱσίδᾱς</span>) <span lang="grc">ἦν οὐδὲ ἀδύνατος, ὡς Λακεδαιμόνιος, εἰπεῖν</span> _Brasidas was, for a Lacedaemonian_, _not a bad speaker either_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.84">T. 4.84</cite>, <span lang="grc">ταῦτα ἀκούσᾱς Ξέρξης ὡς ἐκ κακῶν ἐχάρη</span> _on hearing this Xerxes rejoiced_ as much &lt;\*&gt; could be expected _considering his misfortunes_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 8.101">Hdt. 8.101</cite>. On <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> restrictive with the dative, cp. [1495 a](../18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1495), [1497](../18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1497); with the absolute infinitive, 2012.

##### <a id="s2994"></a> §2994

<span lang="grc">ὡς</span> is often used to heighten a superlative ([1086](../14-adjectives.md#s1086)).

##### <a id="s2995"></a> §2995

With numerals and words indicating degree <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> means _about_, _nearly_, _not far from;_ as <span lang="grc">ὁπλί̄τᾱς ἔχων ὡς πεντακοσίους</span> _having about five hundred hoplites_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.3">X. A. 1.2.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡς ἐπὶ πολύ</span> _for the most part_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 377b">P. R. 377b</cite> (lit. _about over the great_(_er_) _part_). <!-- page 671 -->

##### <a id="s2996"></a> §2996

<span lang="grc">ὡς</span> often indicates the thought or the assertion of the subject of the principal verb or of some other person prominent in the sentence. Here <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> expresses a real intention or an avowed plea. So often with participles ([2086](../22-the-participle.md#s2086)); and also with the prepositions <span lang="grc">εἰς, ἐπί, πρός</span>; as <span lang="grc">ἀπέπλεον . . . ἐκ τῆς Σικελίᾱς ὡς ἐς τὰ̄ς Ἀθήνᾱς</span> _they sailed away from Sicily as though bound for Athens_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.61">T. 6.61</cite>.

##### <a id="s2997"></a> §2997

<span lang="grc"> **ὡς ἕκαστος** </span> means _each for himself;_ as <span lang="grc">ἀπέπλευσαν ἐξ Ἑλλησπόντου ὡς ἕκαστοι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀπέπλευσαν</span>) <span lang="grc">κατὰ πόλεις</span> _they sailed away from the Hellespont each to his own State_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.89">T. 1.89</cite>.

##### <a id="s2998"></a> §2998

<span lang="grc">ὡς</span> _exclamatory_ ([2682](../34-exclamatory-sentences.md#s2682)) may be the relative adverb <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> _how_, the relative clause originally being used in explanation of an exclamation. Exclamatory <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> has also been explained as <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> demonstrative (_so_).

##### <a id="s2999"></a> §2999

On <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> in wishes, see [1815](../20-the-verb-voices/07-the-moods-in-simple-sentences.md#s1815).

## Relative_ <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> _as a Conjunction

##### <a id="s3000"></a> §3000

<span lang="grc">ὡς</span> conjunctive is found in dependent clauses.

Declarative: _that_, like <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span>. Cp. [2577](../31-dependent-substantive-clauses-2574-2635.md#s2577) ff., 2614 ff.

Final: _that_, _in order that;_ like <span lang="grc">ἵνα</span>, but not used in standard Attic prose. Cp. [2193](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/02-purpose-clauses-final-clauses.md#s2193).

Object clauses after _verbs of effort: that_, like <span lang="grc">ὅπως</span>; cp. [2209](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/03-object-clauses.md#s2209). Rarely after _verbs of fearing: that_. Cp. [2235](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/03-object-clauses.md#s2235).

Causal: _as_, _inasmuch as, since_, _seeing that_, like <span lang="grc">ὅτι, ἐπεί</span>, etc. Cp. [2240](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/04-causal-clauses.md#s2240).

Consecutive: _so that_, like <span lang="grc">ὥστε</span>. Usually with the infinitive, sometimes with the indicative. Cp. [2260](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/06-omegasigmatauepsilon-rarely-omegasigma-with-the-infinitive.md#s2260).

Temporal: _after_, like <span lang="grc">ἐπεί</span>; sometimes _when_, _whenever_. Cp. [2383](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/12-temporal-clauses.md#s2383).

##### <a id="s3001"></a> §3001

<span lang="grc">ὡς</span> is often found before sentences apparently independent, where it is sometimes explained as a conjunction with the verb suppressed. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὡς τῆσδ᾽ ἑκοῦσα παιδὸς οὐ μεθήσομαι</span> (_know_) _that of my own accord I will not relinquish my child_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hec. 400">E. Hec. 400</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡς δὴ σύ μοι τύραννος Ἀργείων ἔσει</span> (_do you mean_) _that you forsooth shall be lord and master of Argives_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 1633">A. Ag. 1633</cite>. Some scholars regard this <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> as causal, others regard it as demonstrative, others as comparative.

## <span lang="grc">ὥς</span> _as_, _like_

##### <a id="s3002"></a> §3002

<span lang="grc">ὥς</span> _as_, _like_ (postpositive) in Hom., as <span lang="grc">ὄρνῑθες ὥς Γ</span> 2, stands for <span lang="grc">ϝως</span>, which is of uncertain origin.

## <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> _to_

##### <a id="s3003"></a> §3003

<span lang="grc">ὡς</span> _to_, a preposition with persons (once in Hom., <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 17.218"><span lang="grc">ρ</span> 218</cite>) is obscure in origin.
