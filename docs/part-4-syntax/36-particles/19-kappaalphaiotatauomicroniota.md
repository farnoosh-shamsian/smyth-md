# PARTICLES: καίτοι&lt;*&gt;

##### <a id="s2893"></a> §2893

<span lang="grc"> **καίτοι** </span> (<span lang="grc">καὶ ¨ τοι&lt;\*&gt;</span>), not in Homer, means _and yet, although_, rarely _and so then_. Here <span lang="grc">τοί</span> marks something worthy of note, which is commonly opposed to what precedes. <span lang="grc">καίτοι</span> is used in making a correction (sometimes in the form of a question), in passing to a new idea, and in the statement of a conclusion. The common <span lang="grc">καίτοι . . . γε</span> is stronger than <span lang="grc">καίτοι</span>.

<span lang="grc">καίτοι οὐδὲν ὅτι οὐκ ἀληθὲς εἴρηκα ὧν προεῖπον</span> _and yet there is nothing untrue in what I said before_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyph. 3c">P. Euth. 3c</cite>.

**a.** A sentence preceding <span lang="grc">καίτοι</span> is often _restated_ by a clause introduced by <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ὅμως</span>), <span lang="grc">δέ</span>, or <span lang="grc">νῦν δέ</span>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 77a">P. Ph. 77a</cite>, Charm. 175 c, A. 40 b, G. 499 c.

**b.** <span lang="grc">καίτοι</span> is rarely, if ever, used with the participle in classical Greek. It is best attested in <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 511d">P. R. 511d</cite>; emendation is resorted to in <cite data-ref="Lys. 31.34">L. 31.34</cite>, <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Eccl. 159">Ar. Eccl. 159</cite>.
