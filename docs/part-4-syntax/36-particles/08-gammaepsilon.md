# PARTICLES: γέ

##### <a id="s2821"></a> §2821

<span lang="grc"> **γέ** </span> (postpositive and enclitic) is an intensive and restrictive particle with the force of _at least, at any rate, even_, _certainly_, _indeed;_ but often to be rendered by intonation. <span lang="grc">γέ</span> may indicate assent, concession, banter, scorn, deprecation, irony, etc. <span lang="grc">γέ</span> emphasizes single words or whole phrases or clauses.

**a.** Single words. So often with pronouns, as <span lang="grc">ἔγωγε</span> _I at least_ (excluding others), <span lang="grc">ἐμέ γε</span> cp. _mi-ch_, <span lang="grc">ὅ γε</span> _even he_ (Hom.), <span lang="grc">οὗτός γε</span>, and with a repeated pronoun (<cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 117">S. Ph. 117</cite>). Other words, as <span lang="grc">ὅ τι βούλει γε</span> _whatever you_ like <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Frogs 3">Ar. Ran. 3</cite>, <span lang="grc">πλήθει γε οὐχ ὑπερβαλοίμεθ᾽ ἂν τοὺς πολεμίους</span> _in numbers at least we should not surpass the enemy_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.1.18">X. C. 2.1.18</cite>.

**b.** With phrases or clauses. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὡς μή μ᾽ ἄτῑμον, τοῦ θεοῦ γε προστάτην, οὕτως ἀφῇ με</span> _that he may not thus send me away in dishonour—who am the suppliant of the god_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 1278">S. O. C. 1278</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀνθρώπους τί̄νυσθον, ὅτις γ᾽ ἐπίορκον ὀμόσσῃ</span> ye who _punish men who swear falsely_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 3.279"><span lang="grc">Γ</span> 279</cite>.

##### <a id="s2822"></a> §2822

<span lang="grc">γέ</span> may be used twice in the same sentence. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐπεί γ᾽ ἀρκοῦνθ᾽ ἱκανὰ τοῖς γε σώφροσιν</span> _since indeed that which suffices their wants is enough for the wise_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Phoen. 545">E. Phoen. 545</cite>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.187">Hdt. 1.187</cite>, <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Wasps 1507">Ar. Vesp. 1507</cite>.

##### <a id="s2823"></a> §2823

<span lang="grc">γέ</span> stands between article and noun, as <span lang="grc">οἵ γ᾽ ἄνθρωποι</span> (after a preposition, as <span lang="grc">ἔν γε τῷ φανερῷ</span>); between noun and adjective, or after the adjective, as <span lang="grc">ἀνήρ γε σοφός</span>, or <span lang="grc">ἀνὴρ σοφός γε</span>; after a possessive pronoun, as <span lang="grc">ἐμός γε θῡμός</span>; after <span lang="grc">μέν, δέ, τέ</span>, as <span lang="grc">ὅτι δέ γε ἀληθῆ λέγω</span>. When <span lang="grc">γέ</span> influences a whole clause it stands as near as possible to the introductory conjunction; as <span lang="grc">εἴ γε, ἆρά γε</span>.

##### <a id="s2824"></a> §2824

<span lang="grc">γέ</span> in contrasts and alternatives; as <span lang="grc">σὺ δ᾽ ου᾽ λέγεις γε</span> (<span lang="grc">αἰσχρά</span>), <span lang="grc">δρᾷς δέ με</span> _thou dost not indeed say, but do shameful things to me_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Andr. 239">E. And. 239</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἤτοι κρύφα γε ἢ φανερῶς</span> _either secretly or openly_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.34">T. 6.34</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἢ σοφοὶ ἢ τί̄μιοι ἢ γέροντές γε</span> _or wise or held in honour aye or old_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Hipp. Maj. 301a">P. Hipp. M. 301a</cite> (here <span lang="grc">γέ</span> indicates a change in an alternative series; cp. <span lang="grc">οὔτε . . . οὔτε . . . οὐδέ γε</span> and <span lang="grc">καὶ . . . γε</span> [2829](#s2829)). <!-- page 643 -->

##### <a id="s2825"></a> §2825

<span lang="grc">γέ</span> in replies and comments (_yes_, _well_). Thus, <span lang="grc">δοκεῖ παρεικαθεῖν; ὅσον γ᾽, ἄναξ, τάχιστα</span> _does it seem best_ to you _that I should give way? Aye_, _my lord_, _and with all speed_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 1102">S. Ant. 1102</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">καὶ . . . γε</span> is common, as <span lang="grc">καὶ οὐδέν γε ἀτόπως</span> _yes_, _and no wonder_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 142b">P. Th. 142b</cite>.

##### <a id="s2826"></a> §2826

<span lang="grc"> **ὅς γε** </span> (rarely <span lang="grc"> **ὅστις γε** </span>) has a causal force, much like _qui quidem_, _quippe qui_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἄτοπα λέγεις . . . ὅς γε κελεύεις ἐμὲ νεώτερον ὄντα καθηγεῖσθαι</span> _you are talking absurdly in bidding me who am the younger take precedence_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.3.15">X. M. 2.3.15</cite>. So with other relatives, as <span lang="grc">οἷος, ὅσος, ὥσπερ</span>.

##### <a id="s2827"></a> §2827

<span lang="grc">γέ</span> sometimes marks an ellipse (<cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 1409">S. Ph. 1409</cite>). When the verb of the apodosis is omitted, the protasis often has <span lang="grc">γέ</span> (so usually in Aristophanes, e.g. Nub. [267](../../part-2-inflection/04-substantives/05-third-declension.md#s267)).

##### <a id="s2828"></a> §2828

When <span lang="grc">γέ</span> is followed by other particles, it belongs with the emphasized word, and the other particles retain their original force; as <span lang="grc">τούς γε μέντοι ἀγαθούς</span> _yet the brave at least_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.9.14">X. A. 1.9.14</cite>. So <span lang="grc">γε δή, γε μὲν δή, γέ τοι</span> (often used like <span lang="grc">γοῦν</span> in giving a reason for a belief), <span lang="grc">γέ τοι δή</span>. With the imperative, <span lang="grc">γέ</span> is rare except when it is followed by another particle, as <span lang="grc">ὅρᾱ γε μήν</span> <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 587">S. O. C. 587</cite>.

##### <a id="s2829"></a> §2829

**After other Particles**.—For example:

<span lang="grc"> **δέ γε** </span>: here <span lang="grc">γέ</span> usually does not emphasize <span lang="grc">δέ</span> but either a single word or the whole clause; as <span lang="grc">ἡμῖν δέ γε οἶμαι πάντα ποιητέα</span> _but we at least, in my opinion_, _should adopt every means_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.35">X. A. 3.1.35</cite>. <span lang="grc">δὲ . . . γε</span> is often used when two things are compared, in order to show that one is more important than the other.

<span lang="grc"> **καὶ . . . γε** </span> sometimes means _yes_, _and_ and sometimes <span lang="grc">γέ</span> emphasizes the intervening word. Thus, <span lang="grc">κοὐδέν γε θαῦμα</span> _yes_, _and no wonder_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 1132">S. O. T. 1132</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ στίβου γε οὐδεὶς κτύπος</span> _and of footsteps there is no sound_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 29">S. Ph. 29</cite>. <span lang="grc">καὶ . . . γε</span> often emphasizes one item in a series, and especially the last item, Here <span lang="grc">καὶ . . . γέ προς</span> (<span lang="grc">καὶ πρός γε</span>) _and besides_ is common. Cp. <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 450d">P. G. 450d</cite>, 469 b.

<span lang="grc"> **μέν γε** </span> lends force to a contrast (<cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 180d">P. S. 180d</cite>); sometimes it has the force of _that is to say, for example_ (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.86">T. 6.86</cite>).

Frequent combinations are <span lang="grc">**ἀλλ᾽ οὖν . . . γε**, **μέντοι . . . γε**, **μὴν . . . γε**, **οὐκοῦν . . . γε**</span>.
