# PARTICLES: ἀτάρ

##### <a id="s2801"></a> §2801

<span lang="grc"> **ἀτάρ** </span> (prepositive; Hom. also <span lang="grc">αὐτάρ</span> from <span lang="grc">αὖτε ¨ ἄρ</span>) usually poetical, but found in Xenophon and Plato, is an adversative conjunction commonly used to introduce a strong or surprising contrast (_but_, _but yet, however_); sometimes to introduce a slight contrast (_and_, _and then_), but one stronger than that marked by <span lang="grc">δέ. ἀτάρ</span> is common as a correlative to <span lang="grc">μέν</span>. It is often found in lively questions to introduce an objection; in rapid transitions; and sometimes it serves to introduce the apodosis of a conditional sentence. <span lang="grc"> **ἀτάρ** </span> was largely displaced by the stronger <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span>.
