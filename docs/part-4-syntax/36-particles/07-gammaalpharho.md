# PARTICLES: γάρ

##### <a id="s2803"></a> §2803

<span lang="grc"> **γάρ** </span> (postpositive) _in fact, indeed_ , and _for_, a confirmatory adverb and a causal conjunction. As a conjunction, <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> usually stands after the first word in its clause; as an adverb, its position is <!-- page 638 --> freer. <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> is especially common in sentences which offer a reason for, or an explanation of, a preceding or following statement. It may be used in successive clauses.

**a.** <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> is from <span lang="grc">γέ ¨ ἄρ</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span>), <span lang="grc">γέ</span> originally giving prominence either to the word it followed or to the whole clause, while <span lang="grc">ἄρα</span> marked this prominence as due to something previously expressed or latent in the context. The compound <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> originally emphasized a thought either as the result of existing circumstances or as a patent and well known fact. In most uses of the word, however, the force of its component parts cannot be distinguished nor is it clear in many cases whether <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> is a conjunction or an adverb marking &lt;\*&gt;ssurance.

##### <a id="s2804"></a> §2804

Adverbial <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> appears in questions, answers, and wishes; and in many other cases where recourse is had to conscious or unconscious ellipse by those scholars who hold that <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> is always a conjunction. Ellipse is sometimes natural and easy, but often clumsy and artificial. Though we find in parallel use both incomplete and complete clauses with <span lang="grc">γάρ</span>, it is improbable that the Greeks were conscious of the need of any supplement to explain the thought. In many uses <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> has become formulaic, serving only to show the natural agreement with the existing situation.

##### <a id="s2805"></a> §2805

In **questions**, <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> asks for confirmation of a preceding statement, or expresses assent or dissent; asks whether an act before mentioned was not reasonable; asks a question prompted by some form of emotion; and serves to indicate transition, etc.

**a.** In questions <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> often marks surprise or indignation, and may frequently be translated by _what_, _why_, _then_, _really_, _surely_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ταυτὶ̄ λέγεις σὺ στρατηγὸν πτωχὸς ὤν; ἐγὼ γάρ εἰμι πτωχός</span>; _do you, beggar that you are, address your general thus? what! I a beggar?_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Ach. 593">Ar. Ach. 593</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἦ ζῇ γὰρ ἁ̄νήρ</span>; _is the man really alive?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 1221">S. El. 1221</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἴει γάρ σοι μαχεῖσθαι . . . τὸν ἀδελφόν</span>; _do you really think that your brother is going to fight?_ X. A. &lt;\*&gt; 7. 9. So <span lang="grc">τίς γάρ</span>; _who then, why who?_

**b.** Brief interrogative for&lt;\*&gt;ulae asking for confirmation of a preceding statement are:

<span lang="grc"> **τί γάρ** </span>; _what then, how when, how else?_ <span lang="grc">τί γάρ</span> also serves as a formula of transition (_now_, _well then, now what_ . . ., _furthermore_).

<span lang="grc"> **ἦ γάρ** </span>; _is it not so? surely this is so?_ (cp. _n'est ce pas_). Often of surprise.

<span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ γάρ** </span>; _is it not so?_ often in indignant questions; when not standing alone, _why not?_

<span lang="grc">**&lt;\*&gt;ῶς γάρ**; **πόθεν γάρ**</span>; imply that something is impossible (often of surprise). Cp. <span lang="grc">πῶς γὰρ οὔ</span>; in negative rhetorical questions.

##### <a id="s2806"></a> §2806

In answers <span lang="grc">γά&lt;\*&gt;</span> marks assent, assurance, sometimes dissent. Thus, <span lang="grc">δεινόν γε τοὐπίσαγμα τοῦ νοσἡματος. δεινὸν γὰρ οὐδὲ ῥητόν</span> _dread indeed is the burden of one disease. Aye dread indeed and beyond all words_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 755">S. Ph. 755</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁμολογεῖς οὖν περὶ ἐμὲ ἄδικος γεγενῆσθαι; ἦ γὰρ ἀνάγκη</span> _do you then confess that you have proved yourself unjust toward me? In truth I must indeed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.6.8">X. A. 1.6.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">μηδ᾽ αἱ μητέρες τὰ παιδία ἐκδειματούντων . . . μὴ γάρ, ἔφη</span> _nor let mothers frighten their children. No indeed, said he_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 381e">P. R. 381e</cite>, <span lang="grc">φῂς τάδ᾽ οὖν; ἃ μὴ φρονὼ̂ γὰρ οὐ φιλῶ λέγειν</span> _dost thou then consent to this? No_, _for I am not wont to utter words I do not mean_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 1520">S. O. T. 1520</cite>. <!-- page 639 -->

**a.** <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> is common in brief answers, as after <span lang="grc">οὐ, δεῖ, ἔοικε, εἰκός, λέγω, ὡμολόγηται</span>. So in the rhetorical questions <span lang="grc">πῶς γάρ; πῶς γὰρ οὔ</span>; used as answers.

##### <a id="s2807"></a> §2807

In **wishes**: <span lang="grc">εἰ γὰρ . . . ἐν τούτῳ εἴη</span> _would that it depended on that_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 310d">P. Pr. 310d</cite>, <span lang="grc">κακῶς γὰρ ἐξόλοιο</span> _oh that you might perish wretchedly_ E. Cyc. 261. Here <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> marks the agreement of the wish with the existing situation.

##### <a id="s2808"></a> §2808

**Explanatory** (or **prefatory**) <span lang="grc"> **γάρ** </span> has the force of _now_, _namely_, _that is_, _for example;_ but usually is not to be translated, and especially when the preceding sentence contains a _verb of saying_, _showing_, etc. It usually introduces, as an explanation, the details of that which was promised in an incomplete or general statement; sometimes, without any such statement, it introduces a new fact. Whether this <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> is an adverb or a conjunction is uncertain. Thus, <span lang="grc">δοκεῖ τοίνυν μοι χαριέστερον εἶναι μῦθον ὑ̄μῖν λέγειν. ἦν γάρ ποτε κτλ</span>. _I think it will be more interesting to tell you a myth. Once upon a time there was_, etc. <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 320c">P. Pr. 320c</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὕτω γὰρ σκοπεῖτε</span> _look at it in this light_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 19.34">L. 19.34</cite> (at the beginning of a new point in the discussion).

##### <a id="s2809"></a> §2809

Explanatory <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> often introduces a clause in apposition to a preceding demonstrative, to such expressions as <span lang="grc">τεκμήριον δέ</span> or <span lang="grc">μαρτύριον δέ</span> _now the proof is this_, <span lang="grc">δῆλον δέ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐστιν</span>) _it is clear_, <span lang="grc">τὸ δὲ μέγιστον</span> _but_, _what is of the greatest importance_, or to relative clauses ([995](../10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s995)). Thus, <span lang="grc">ὡς δ᾽ ἔτι μᾶλλον θαρρῇς, καὶ τόδε κατανόησον· οἱ μὲν γὰρ</span> (explaining <span lang="grc">τόδε</span>) <span lang="grc">πολέμιοι πολὺ μὲν ἐλά̄ττονές εἰσι νῦν ἢ πρὶν ἡττηθῆναι ὑφ᾽ ἡμῶν</span> _and that you may be still more encouraged, consider this fact too. The enemy_ (_namely_) _are much fewer now than they were before they were beaten by us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.2.36">X. C. 5.2.36</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐννοήσωμεν δὲ καὶ τῇδε, ὡς πολλὴ ἐλπίς ἐστιν ἀγαθὸν αὐτὸ εἶναι. δυοῖν γὰρ θά̄τερόν ἐστιν τὸ τεθνάναι κτλ</span>. _let us consider the matter also in this way_ and we shall see _that there is abundant reason to hope that it is a good: now death must be one of two things_, etc. <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 40c">P. A. 40c</cite>, <span lang="grc">μαρτύριον δέ· Δήλου γὰρ καθαιρομένης κτλ</span>. _and this is a proof of it: now when Delos was being purified_, etc. <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.8">T. 1.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">δ̀ δὲ πάντων σχετλιώτατον· οὓς γὰρ ὁμολογήσαιμεν ἂν πονηροτάτους εἶναι τῶν πολῑτῶν, τούτους πιστοτάτους φύλακας ἡγούμεθα τῆς πολῑτείᾱς εἶναι</span> _but the most abominable of all is this: we consider the most trustworthy guardians of the State to be those men whom we should agree were the worst citizens_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 8.53">I. 8.53</cite>.

##### <a id="s2810"></a> §2810

**Causal <span lang="grc">γάρ</span>** is a conjunction: _for_ (_nam_, _enim_). It serves to introduce a cause of, or a reason for, an action before mentioned; to justify a preceding utterance; to confirm the truth of a previous statement. Causal <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> often refers to a thought _implied_ in what has preceded. Thus, <span lang="grc">λεκτέα ἃ γιγνώσκω· ἔμπειρος γάρ</span> (causal) <span lang="grc">εἰμι καὶ τῆς χώρᾱς τῶν Παφλαγόνων καὶ τῆς δυνάμεως. ἔχει γὰρ</span> (explanatory) <span lang="grc">ἀμφότερα, καὶ πεδία κάλλιστα καὶ ὄρη ὑψηλότατα</span> _I must tell what I know_, _for I am acquainted with the country of the Paphlagonians and its resources; now the country has very fertile plains and very lofly mountains_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.6.6">X. A. 5.6.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἰού, δύστηνε· τοῦτο γάρ σ᾽ ἔχω μόνον προσειπεῖν</span> _alas_, _ill-fated one! for by this name alone can I address thee_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 1071">S. O. T. 1071</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπιστευόμην δὲ ὑπὸ τῶν Λακεδαιμονίων· οὐ γὰρ ἄν με ἔπεμπον πάλιν πρὸς ὑ̄μᾶς</span> _but I was trusted by the Lacedaemonians; for_ (otherwise, i.e. <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ ἐπίστευον</span>) _they would not have sent me back to you_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 30c">P. A. 30c</cite>.

##### <a id="s2811"></a> §2811

**Anticipatory <span lang="grc">γάρ</span>** states the cause, justifies the utterance, or gives the explanation, of something set forth in the main clause which _follows_. The main clause usually contains an inferential word, a demonstrative pointing backward, <!-- page 640 --> or <span lang="grc">καί, δέ, ἀλλά</span>; or stands without a connective. Anticipatory <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> may often be rendered by _since_, but is often omitted in translation. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔτι τοίνυν ἀκούσατε καὶ τάδε. ἐπὶ λείᾱν γὰρ ὑ̄μῶν ἐκπορεύσονταί τινες. οἶμαι νῦν βέλτιστον εἶναι κτλ</span>. _listen therefore to this proposal also. Some of you will be going out to plunder. Now it is my opinion that it is best_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.1.8">X. A. 5.1.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐσελθὼν δὲ τὴν ταχίστην, ἦν γάρ οἱ παῖς εἷς μοῦνος . . ., τοῦτον ἐκπέμπει</span> _and when he had come in straightway_, _he sent out his son, for he had one only son_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.119">Hdt. 1.119</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὦ φίλοι, οὐ γάρ τ᾽ ἴδμεν ὅπῃ ζόφος οὐδ᾽ ὅπῃ ἠώς . . . ἀλλὰ φραζώμεθα κτλ</span>. _friends_, _since we do not know where is the place of darkness nor of the dawn, let us consider_, etc. <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 10.190"><span lang="grc">κ</span> 190</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὦ φίλτατε, σπονδαὶ γάρ εἰσί σοι μόνῳ, μέτρησον εἰρήνης τί μοι</span> _my dear fellow, since you alone have got a truce, measure me out a bit of peace_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Ach. 102">Ar. Ach. 102</cite>.

**a.** In this construction <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> may be an adverb, not a conjunction. Cases of explanatory <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> ([2808](#s2808)) and of parenthetical <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> ([2812](#s2812)), especially after vocatives, may fall under 2811.

##### <a id="s2812"></a> §2812

The clause with <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> _since_ is often inserted parenthetically in the clause which it is intended to explain; as <span lang="grc">ὁ δὲ</span> (<span lang="grc">κρἱ̄νουσι γὰρ βοῇ καὶ οὐ ψήφῳ</span>) <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἔφη διαγιγνώσκειν τὴν βοὴν ποτέρᾱ μείζων</span> _but_, _since they decide by shouts and not by ballot, he said he could not decide which side shouted the louder_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.87">T. 1.87</cite>.

##### <a id="s2813"></a> §2813

<span lang="grc"> **καὶ γάρ** </span> has in general two distinct meanings according as <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> is an adverb or a conjunction. As <span lang="grc">καὶ γάρ</span> has become a formula, it is often uncertain which of the two words is the adverb, which the conjunction.

##### <a id="s2814"></a> §2814

(I) <span lang="grc"> **καὶ γάρ** </span> _and in fact, and indeed_, <span lang="grc">καί</span> being a conjunction, and <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> an adverb. Here the clause in which <span lang="grc">καὶ γάρ</span> stands is added as a new and important thought; where <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> alone would state the reason or the explanation with less independence and with slighter emphasis. The negative is <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ γάρ</span>. Thus <span lang="grc">Κῦρος δ᾽ ὁρῶν τοὺς Ἕλληνας νῑκῶντας τὸ καθ᾽ αὑτοὺς . . . ἐπεμελεῖτο ὅ τι ποιήσει βασιλεύς. καὶ γὰρ ᾔδει αὐτὸν ὅτι μέσον ἔχοι τοῦ Περσικοῦ στρατεύματος</span> _on seeing the Greeks victorious over the troops opposed to them_, _Cyrus watched to see what the king would do; and in fact he knew that he commanded the centre of the Persian force_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.21">X. A. 1.8.21</cite> (cp. [1](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s1). 1. 6, 2. 5. 5, 2. 6. 2). So often in affirmative responses: <span lang="grc">ἢ οὐκ ἀγαπήσεις τούτων τυγχάνων; ἐγὼ μὲν γὰρ ἂν ἀγαπῴην. καὶ γὰρ ἐγώ, ἔφη</span> _or will you not be content if you obtain this? For my part I shall be. And so shall I_, _he said_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 473b">P. R. 473b</cite>.

**a.** <span lang="grc"> **καὶ γὰρ καί** </span> _and even_ is <span lang="grc">καὶ γάρ</span> _and in fact_ reënforced by <span lang="grc">καί</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">καὶ γὰρ καὶ ἄδεια ἐφαίνετο αὐτοῖς</span> _and in fact it looked to them as if there was perfect safety_ in so doing <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.108">T. 4.108</cite>. The negative is <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ γὰρ οὐδέ</span> ([2938](27-omicronupsilondeltaepsilon-omicronupsilontauepsilon-muetadel.md#s2938)).

##### <a id="s2815"></a> §2815

(II) <span lang="grc"> **καὶ γάρ** </span> _for even, for also_. Here <span lang="grc">καί</span> is an adverb affecting a single word, several words, or the whole sentence, and <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> is a conjunction. The negative is <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ γάρ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">καὶ γὰρ οὗτοι</span> _for these too_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 22c">P. A. 22c</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ γὰρ ἠδικημένοι σῑγησόμεσθα</span> _for even wronged as I am I'll keep silent_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Med. 314">E. Med. 314</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ γὰρ μόνος ἡγοῖτ᾽ ἂν δύνασθαι πείθειν</span> _for_, _though quite unaided, he would think that he was able to persuade_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.2.11">X. M. 1.2.11</cite>.

**a.** <span lang="grc"> **καὶ γὰρ . . . καί** </span> _for both . . . and:_ here <span lang="grc">καί</span> is correlated with a second <span lang="grc">καί</span>; as <span lang="grc">καὶ γὰρ ὑγιαίνουσιν οἱ τὰ σώματα εὖ ἔχοντες καὶ ἰσχύ̄ουσι</span> _for those who keep their bodies in good condition are both healthy and strong_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.12.4">X. M. 3.12.4</cite>. <!-- page 641 -->

##### <a id="s2816"></a> §2816

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλὰ γάρ** </span> occurs both in conjunction and separated by one or several words, which are generally emphatic.

##### <a id="s2817"></a> §2817

**First Form** (often _but since, since however_): here there are two predicates. In prose separation is the rule. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽, οὐ γὰρ ἔπειθε, διδοῖ τὸ φᾶρος</span> _but since he could not persuade her, he gave her the mantle_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 9.109">Hdt. 9.109</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἴσως γὰρ καὶ ἄλλοι ταὐτὰ ἐνθῡμοῦνται, . . . μὴ ἀναμένωμεν ἄλλους ἐφ᾽ ἡμᾶς ἐλθεῖν κτλ</span>. _since however others too perhaps entertain the same opinion, let us not wait for others to come to us_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.24">X. A. 3.1.24</cite>. In poetry the words are generally not separated. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ γὰρ Κρέοντα λεύσσω τόνδε . . . πρὸς δόμους στείχοντα, παύσω τοὺς . . . γόους</span> _since however I see Creon yonder coming to the palace_, _I will cease my lamentations_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Phoen. 1307">E. Phoen. 1307</cite>. Here the clause coördinated by the conjunction <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> is parenthetical and gives, by anticipation, the reason for the <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> clause. Cp. <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἐπεί ε</span> 137, and Shakesp. _Sonnet_ 54: “but, for their virtue only is their show, They live unwoo'd.”—The first form is found chiefly in Homer, Pindar, Herodotus, and in the drama.

##### <a id="s2818"></a> §2818

**Second Form** (usually _but indeed, but in fact, but the truth is, but be that as it may_). Here there is a single predicate. Thus, <span lang="grc">καὶ οὐχ ὡς ἀτῑμάζων λέγω . . . ἀλλὰ γὰρ ἐμοὶ τούτων . . . οὐδὲν μέτεστι</span> _and I do not speak in disparagement; but the truth is I have nothing to do with these matters_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 19c">P. A. 19c</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ γιγνώσκω γὰρ . . . ὅτι κτλ</span>. _but indeed I know that_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.1.13">X. C. 2.1.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ εἰσορῶ γὰρ τόνδε . . . Πυλάδην δρόμῳ στείχοντα</span> _but indeed I see Pylades yonder coming at full speed_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Orest. 725">E. Or. 725</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ου᾽ γὰρ ἔστι τἀ̄μφανῆ κρύπτειν</span> _but indeed it is impossible to hide what lies open_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 755">S. O. C. 755</cite>.

**a.** In this use <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> may have preserved, or regained, its primitive adverbial (confirmatory) force. Many scholars, however, claim that there was a conscious or unconscious ellipse, after <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span>, of an idea pertinent to the situation; and thus regard this form as logically equivalent to the form in which <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> is a causal conjunction. In actual use <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ γάρ</span> was clearly a formula used without any consciousness of an omitted idea.

##### <a id="s2819"></a> §2819

<span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ γάρ</span> has a great variety of uses, most of which may be classed as follows:

**a.** In statements of direct opposition: <span lang="grc">καὶ ταῦτά σε πολλοῦ δεῖ λεληθέναι, ἀλλὰ γὰρ οἶμαι δ̀ ἄρτι οὐκ ἔφησθα ποιεῖν, τοῦτο ποιεῖς</span> _and you are far from forgetting this_, _but in fact I think you are doing that which you just denied you were doing_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Charm. 166c">P. Charm. 166c</cite>.

N. This use is post-Homeric, rare in the drama, common in the orators and Plato. It is especially frequent in putting and setting aside an objection supposed to be raised by an opponent (_hypophora_). Cp. **b.**

**b.** In real and assumed objections (cp. _at enim_): <span lang="grc">καὶ ἀληθῆ γε ἔλεγον, ὦ Σώκρατες. ἴσως. ἀλλὰ γάρ, ὦ Εὐθύφρων, καὶ ἀλλὰ πολλὰ φῂς εἶναι ὅσια</span> _yes_, _and I said what was true_, _Socrates. Perhaps_, _but in fact_, _Euthyphron_, _you say that many other things too are holy_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyph. 6d">P. Euth. 6d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ γάρ, φήσει τις, οὐ ῥᾴδιον ἀεὶ λανθάνειν κακὸν ὄντα</span> _yes_, _but some one will say that it is not easy always to conceal the fact that one is wicked_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 365c">P. R. 365c</cite>.

**c.** In transitions.—(1) At the close of the discussion of an argument, where the force of <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> is like that of _and yet_ or emphatic _but_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ γάρ, ὦ</span> <!-- page 642 --> <span lang="grc">βουλή, ταῦτα μὲν ἐνθάδε οὐκ οἶδ᾽ ὅ τι δεῖ λέγειν</span> _but_, _Senators_, _I do not know why I should discuss these matters here_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 7.42">L. 7.42</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ γὰρ ἤδη ὥρᾱ ἀπιέναι</span> _but it is already time to depart_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 42a">P. A. 42a</cite>.

(2) To restrain the expression of emotion; as <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἄναξ γάρ ἐστ᾽ ἐμός, σῑγῶ</span> _but no_, _I am silent for he is my king_ <cite data-ref="Eur. El. 1245">E. El. 1245</cite>.

(3) When the approach of a new actor is announced. Cp. [2817](#s2817), [2818](#s2818).

##### <a id="s2820"></a> §2820

**Other Combinations**.—<span lang="grc"> **γὰρ ἄρα** </span> _for sure enough_.

<span lang="grc"> **γὰρ δή** </span> _for of course, for indeed, for you must know_, as <span lang="grc">φαμὲν γὰρ δή</span> _for of course we say so_.

<span lang="grc"> **γὰρ δή που** </span> _for I presume_, _for doubtless_.

<span lang="grc"> **γὰρ οὖν** </span> often of frank assent, as <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ οὖν</span> _certainly not_, <span lang="grc">λέγω γὰρ οὖν</span> _certainly_, _I do say so;_ less often to explain (_for certainly_); <span lang="grc">καὶ γὰρ οὖν</span> (not very common) is stronger than <span lang="grc">καὶ γάρ</span>.

<span lang="grc"> **γάρ που** </span> _for I suppose_.

<span lang="grc"> **γάρ τοι** </span> _for surely, for mark you;_ sometimes <span lang="grc">καὶ γάρ τοι</span>.
