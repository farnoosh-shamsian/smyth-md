# PARTICLES: ἤ

##### <a id="s2856"></a> §2856

**Disjunctive <span lang="grc">ἤ</span>** (Epic <span lang="grc">ἠέ</span>) _or_ (_uel_, _aut_); and repeated: <span lang="grc"> **ἢ . . . ἤ** </span> _either . . . or_ (_uel . . . uel_, _aut . . . aut_) to connect the two members more closely.

<span lang="grc">ἀγαθὸν ἢ κακόν</span> _good or bad_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.9.11">X. A. 1.9.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἤ τι ἢ οὐδέν</span> _little or nothing_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 17b">P. A. 17b</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> with the subjunctive is often used when a speaker corrects himself; as <span lang="grc">νῦν δ᾽ αὖ τρίτος ἦλθέ ποθεν σωτήρ, ἢ μόρον εἴπω</span>; _and now, again_, _the third has come_, _the deliverer—or shall I call it_ a deed of _death?_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Lib. 1074">A. Ch. 1074</cite>. On <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> in questions, see [2657](../32-interrogative-sentences-questions.md#s2657), [2675](../33-indirect-dependent-questions.md#s2675).

##### <a id="s2857"></a> §2857

Between ascending numbers <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> has the force of Eng. _to_, as <span lang="grc">ἐν ἓξ ἢ ἑπτὰ ἡμέραις</span> _in six to seven days_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.3.28">X. C. 5.3.28</cite>.

##### <a id="s2858"></a> §2858

<span lang="grc"> **ἤτοι** </span> may be used instead of the first <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> when the first member, as is commonly the case, contains the more probable choice. In English the order is often inverted. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἤτοι κλύουσα παιδὸς ἢ τύχῃ πάρα</span> _she comes either by chance or because she has heard about her son_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 1182">S. Ant. 1182</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἤτοι</span> may be followed by <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> several times. <span lang="grc"> **ἤτοι . . . γε** </span> is more emphatic, as <span lang="grc">ἤτοι κρύφα γε ἢ φανερῶς</span> _either secretly or openly_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.34">T. 6.34</cite>.

##### <a id="s2859"></a> §2859

<span lang="grc">ἤ</span> often indicates that a given result will follow in case the action of <!-- page 649 --> the previous clause is not realized: _or else_ (cp. <span lang="grc">εἰ δὲ μή</span>, 2346 d). Thus, <span lang="grc">ὅπως . . . ὑ̄μεῖς ἐμὲ ἐπαινέσετε, ἐμοὶ μελήσει· ἢ μηκέτι με Κῦρον νομίζετε</span> _it shall be my concern that you commend me; or else my name is no longer Cyrus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.16">X. A. 1.4.16</cite>.

##### <a id="s2860"></a> §2860

<span lang="grc">ἤ</span> often does not introduce an alternative to a previous question, but substitutes instead another question which is more specific and intended to anticipate the answer to the first (_or rather, or precisely_). Thus, <span lang="grc">λέγε ἡμῖν πῶς με φῂς διαφθείρειν τοὺς νεωτέρους; ἢ δῆλον δὴ ὅτι . . . θεοὺς διδάσκειν μὴ νομίζειν οὓς ἡ πόλις νομίζει</span>; _tell us how you mean that I corrupt the young? Or rather clearly you mean that_ (_I corrupt them_) _by teaching them not to acknowledge the gods which the State acknowledges?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 26b">P. A. 26b</cite>.

##### <a id="s2861"></a> §2861

<span lang="grc">ἤ</span> often introduces an argument _ex contrario_ (<cite data-ref="Dem. 31.14">D. 31.14</cite>).

##### <a id="s2862"></a> §2862

<span lang="grc"> **ἢ καί** </span> is often used where <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> would suffice (cp. [2888](17-kappaalphaiota.md#s2888) a); as <span lang="grc">ἢ ξένος ἢ καί τις πο&lt;\*&gt;`ί̄της</span> _either an alien or a citizen if you will_ (or _as well_) <cite data-ref="Dem. 20.123">D. 20.123</cite>.

##### <a id="s2863"></a> §2863

**Comparative <span lang="grc">ἤ</span>** _than_ is used to mark difference. It stands after comparatives where the genitive or a preposition ([1069 f](../14-adjectives.md#s1069)f.) is not used, and after words indicating difference or diversity or having a comparative force, e.g., <span lang="grc">ἄλλος</span> or <span lang="grc">ἕτερος</span> _other_, <span lang="grc">ἄλλως</span> _otherwise_, <span lang="grc">διάφορος</span> _different_, <span lang="grc">διαφέρειν</span> _to be different_, <span lang="grc">ἐναντίος</span> _contrary_, <span lang="grc">διπλάσιος</span> _twice as much_, <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> _sooner_.

<span lang="grc">ἄλλα ἢ τὰ γενόμενα</span> _things different from what occurred_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.9">X. C. 3.1.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄλλο οὐδὲν ἢ ἐκ γῆς ἐναυμάχουν</span> <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.14">T. 4.14</cite> ([2778 a](02-alphalambdalambdaalpha.md#s2778)), <span lang="grc">τῇ ὑστεραίᾳ δεῖ με ἀποθνῄσκειν ἢ ᾗ ἂν ἔλθῃ τὸ πλοῖον</span> _I must die the day after_ (_that on which_) _the ship arrives_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 44a">P. Cr. 44a</cite> (here <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> or <span lang="grc">ᾗ</span> might be omitted), <span lang="grc">τἀ̄ναντία . . . ἢ τοὺς κύνας ποιοῦσι</span> _differently from_ the way _they treat dogs_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.8.24">X. A. 5.8.24</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸν ἥμισυν σῖτον ἢ πρόσθεν</span> _half as much corn as before_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 5.3.21">X. H. 5.3.21</cite>.

**a.** After <span lang="grc">τί</span> or a negative, <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> may be used without <span lang="grc">ἄλλος</span>, as <span lang="grc">τί ποιῶν ἢ εὐωχούμενος</span>; _doing what_ else _except feasting?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 53e">P. Cr. 53e</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἶπε μηδένα παριέναι ἢ τοὺς φίλους</span> _he said that they should let no one pass except his friends_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.5.41">X. C. 7.5.41</cite>.

**b.** Often after _verbs of willing_, _choosing_, etc.; as <span lang="grc">θάνατον μετ᾽ ἐλευθερίᾱς αἱρούμενοι ἢ βίον μετὰ δουλείᾱς</span> _preferring death with freedom rather than life with servitude_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 2.62">L. 2.62</cite>. Here we might have <span lang="grc">μᾶλλον ἤ</span>, which is usually not separated, and especially when <span lang="grc">μᾶλλον</span> belongs to the whole sentence.

**c.** If two clauses connected by <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> have the same verb it may be omitted in the clause following <span lang="grc">ἤ</span>; as <span lang="grc">ἔπρᾱττες ἀλλοῖον ἢ οἱ πολλοί</span> (<span lang="grc">πρά̄ττουσι</span>) _you behaved differently from the rest_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 20c">P. A. 20c</cite>.

**d.** On <span lang="grc">ἢ ὥστε</span> (<span lang="grc">ὡς</span>), or <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> alone, _than so as to_, see [2264](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/06-omegasigmatauepsilon-rarely-omegasigma-with-the-infinitive.md#s2264).
