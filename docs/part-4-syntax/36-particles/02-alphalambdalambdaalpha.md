# PARTICLES: ἀλλά

##### <a id="s2775"></a> §2775

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλά** </span>, a strongly adversative conjunction (stronger than <span lang="grc">δέ</span>) connects sentences and clauses, and corresponds pretty closely to _but;_ at times <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> need not or cannot be translated ([2781 b](#s2781)). In form (but with changed accent) <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> was originally the same wor&lt;\*&gt; as the accusative neuter plural <span lang="grc">ἄλλα</span> _other things_ used adverbially = _on the other hand_. <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> marks opposition, contrast, protest, dif&lt;\*&gt; ference, objection, or limitation; and is thus used both where one notion entirely excludes another and where two notions are no&lt;\*&gt; mutually exclusive. <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> is often freely repeated in successive clauses.

##### <a id="s2776"></a> §2776

**The Antecedent Statement is Negative**.—In its simplest use <span lang="grc">ἀλλ&lt;\*&gt;</span> introduces a positive statement after a negative clause. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀνδρὸς ὅρκο&lt;\*&gt; πίστις, ἀλλ᾽ ὅρκων ἀνήρ</span> _his oath is not the warrant of a man, but the man is war rant of his oath_ A. fr. 394, <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ κραυγῇ ἀλλὰ σῑγῇ ὡς ἀνυστὸν . . . προσῇσαν</span> _fo&lt;\*&gt; they came on, not with shouts, but with as little noise as possible_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.11">X. A. 1.8.11</cite>.

**a.** After a question implying a negative answer or a question to be refute&lt;\*&gt; <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> may have the force of (_nay_) _rather_, _on the contrary_. Thus, <span lang="grc">τί δεῖ σε ἰένα . . .; ἀλλὰ ἄλλους πέμψον</span> _what's the need of your going? Nay rather send oth&lt;\*&gt; ers_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.6.19">X. A. 4.6.19</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ου᾽</span> (<span lang="grc">μή</span>) has the force of _and not rather_ ([2781 b](#s2781)) as <span lang="grc">τίδεῖ ἐμβαλεῖν λόγον περὶ τούτου, ἀλλ᾽ οὐχὶ προειπεῖν ὅτι οὕτω ποιήσεις</span>; _why is i&lt;\*&gt; necessary to propose a discussion about this and not rather announce that you will have it so?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.2.19">X. C. 2.2.19</cite>.

##### <a id="s2777"></a> §2777

After a negative clause, or a question implying a negative answer <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span>, or more commonly the colloquial <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἤ</span>, may mean _except_, the combination being equivalent either to <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> or to <span lang="grc">ἤ</span>. In the preceding clause a form of <span lang="grc">ἄλλο</span> or <span lang="grc">ἕτερος</span> is often expressed. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔπαισε . . . νιν οὔτις ἀλλ᾽ ἐγώ</span> _no one smote him except myself_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 1331">S. O. T. 1331</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν ἐθέλοντες ἐπαινεῖν ἀλλ᾽ ἢ τὸν πλοῦτον</span> _wishing to praise nothing except wealth_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 330c">P. R. 330c</cite> (here <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἤ</span> is detached from <span lang="grc">οὐδέν</span>) <span lang="grc">τίνα ἄλλον ἔχουσι λόγον βοηθοῦντες ἐμοὶ ἀλλ᾽ ἢ τὸν ὀρθόν κτλ</span>.; _what other reason&lt;\*&gt; have they for supporting me except the true reason_, etc.? <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 34b">P. A. 34b</cite>.

**a.** Distinguish the use of <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἤ</span> _except_ (= <span lang="grc">εἰ μή</span>) in <span lang="grc">τὸ γοῦν σημεῖον ἕτερον φαί νεται, ἀλλ᾽ ἢ οὐ καθορῶ</span> _the device at any rate appears different, unless I can't se&lt;\*&gt;_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Kn. 953">Ar. Eq. 953</cite>.

##### <a id="s2778"></a> §2778

<span lang="grc"> **οὐδὲν ἀλλ᾽ ἤ** </span> _nothing but_ is also used elliptically, apparently by a&lt;\*&gt; original suppression of a form of <span lang="grc">ποιῶ</span> or <span lang="grc">γίγνομαι</span>; in effect, however, the phras&lt;\*&gt; has acquired a purely adverbial sense (_merely_). Thus, <span lang="grc">διεφθάρμεθα . . . ὑπ᾽ ἀνδρῶ οὐδὲν ἀλλ᾽ ἢ φενᾱκίζειν δυναμένων</span> _we have been ruined by men who are able_ (to do _nothing except deceive_ (i.e. _able merely to deceive_) <cite data-ref="Isoc. 8.36">I. 8.36</cite>. <!-- page 633 -->

**a.** With the above use compare <span lang="grc"> **οὐδὲν ἄλλο ἤ** </span> _nothing else than_, used without, and with, ellipse; as <span lang="grc">οἱ μύ̄ριοι ἱππεῖς οὐδὲν ἄλλο ἢ μύ̄ριοί εἰσιν ἄνθρωποι</span> _your ten thousand horse are nothing more_ (_else_) _than ten thousand men_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.18">X. A. 3.2.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν ἄλλο ἢ πόλιν τὴν ἑαυτοῦ ἀπόλειπων ἕκαστος</span> doing _nothing else than each abandoning his own city_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.16">T. 2.16</cite>. So also <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν ἄλλο . . . ἤ</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.27">D. 8.27</cite>. Cp. <span lang="grc"> **ἄλλο οὐδὲν ἤ** </span>, as in <span lang="grc">ἄλλο οὐδὲν ἢ ἐκ γῆς ἐναυμάχουν</span> _they_ did _nothing else than conduct_ ( = _they practically conducted_) _a sea-fight from the land_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.14">T. 4.14</cite>. Cp. [946](../09-the-predicate.md#s946), [2652](../32-interrogative-sentences-questions.md#s2652).

##### <a id="s2779"></a> §2779

The origin of <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἤ</span> is disputed, some scholars regarding <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽</span> as <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> (originally <span lang="grc">ἄλλα</span>, [2775](#s2775)), while others derive <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽</span> directly from <span lang="grc">ἄλλο</span>, which is thought to have lost its force and consequently its accent. In some passages the Mss. do not distinguish between <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽</span> and <span lang="grc">ἄλλ᾽</span>; and <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἤ</span> and <span lang="grc">ἄλλο ἤ</span> differ only slightly in meaning. In some of the above cases <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽</span> has an adjectival force, in some it hovers between an adjective and a conjunction, and in others it clearly has become a conjunction.

##### <a id="s2780"></a> §2780

After a comparative (<span lang="grc">μᾶλλον, τὸ πλέον</span>) in a negative clause <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> has the force of _as_. Thus, <span lang="grc">καὶ ἔστιν ὁ πόλεμος οὐχ ὅπλων τὸ πλέον ἀλλὰ δαπάνης</span> _and war is not so much_ (lit. _more_) _a matter of arms as_ (_but rather_) _of money_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.83">T. 1.83</cite>. Here the clause with <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> is more emphatic than if <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> had been used. Cp. “there needed no more but to advance one step”: Steele.

##### <a id="s2781"></a> §2781

**The Antecedent Statement is Affirmative**.—<span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> is sometimes found after an affirmative statement.

**a.** The antecedent clause often has a concessive force, and frequently takes <span lang="grc">μέν</span> ([2900](21-muepsilonnu.md#s2900)). Thus, <span lang="grc">τὰ μὲν καθ᾽ ἡμᾶς ἔμοιγε δοκεῖ καλῶς ἔχειν· ἀλλὰ τὰ πλάγια λῡπεὶ με</span> _the part where we are seems to me to be well disposed, but the wings cause me uneasiness_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.1.16">X. C. 7.1.16</cite>.

**b.** <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ου᾽</span> (<span lang="grc">μή</span>) after an affirmative statement often has the force of _and not_, _and not rather, instead of_ (sometimes with a touch of irony). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐκεῖθεν ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἐνθένδε ἡρπάσθη</span> _she was carried off from there and not_ (or simply _not_) _from here_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 229d">P. Phae. 229d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐμοὶ ὀργίζονται ἀλλ᾽ οὐχ αὑτοῖς</span> _they are angry with me instead of_ (or _and not rather with_) _themselves_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 23c">P. A. 23c</cite>. In such cases <span lang="grc">καὶ οὐ</span> (<span lang="grc">μή</span>) would not repudiate the opposition.

##### <a id="s2782"></a> §2782

**<span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> in Apodosis**.—After a concession or a condition expressed or implied, the apodosis may be emphatically introduced by <span lang="grc">**ἀλλά**, **ἀλλὰ . . . γε**, **ἀλλ᾽ οὖν γε**</span> _still_, _yet_, _at least_. Thus, <span lang="grc">εἰ σῶμα δοῦλον, ἀλλ᾽ ὁ νοῦς ἐλεύθερος</span> _if the body is enslaved, the mind at least is free_ A. fr. 854, <span lang="grc">εἰ δ᾽ ἐν πᾶσι τούτοις ἡττῴμεθα, ἀλλὰ τό γέ τοι πῦρ κρεῖττον καρποῦ ἐστιν</span> _but if we should be baffled in all these points_, _still_, _as they say, fire is stronger than the fruit of_ the field <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.19">X. A. 2.5.19</cite>. So also in clauses other than conditional; as <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἐπεὶ . . . πατέρα τόνδ᾽ ἐμὸν οὐκ ἀνέτλᾱτ᾽, . . . ἀλλ᾽ ἐμὲ . . . οἰκτί̄ρατε</span> _but since ye did not bear with my father_, _pity me at least_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 241">S. O. C. 241</cite>.

##### <a id="s2783"></a> §2783

**<span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> attached to Single Words**.—<span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span>, attached to a single word in an adverbial sense, may stand in the interior of the sentence (not in Hom.). Thus, <span lang="grc"> **ἀλλὰ νῦν** </span> _now at least_, as in <span lang="grc">τί δῆτ᾽ ἂν ἀλλὰ νῦν σ᾽ ἔτ᾽ ὠφελοῖμ᾽ ἐγώ</span>, _how pray_, _can I serve thee even now?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 552">S. Ant. 552</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">γέ</span>, as <span lang="grc">ἐὰ̄ν οὖν ἀλλὰ νῦν γ᾽ ἔτι . . . ἐθελήσητε</span> _if therefore you still desire even now_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 3.33">D. 3.33</cite> (and often in D.). Here <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ νῦν</span> implies <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ πρότερον. ἀλλά</span> sometimes apparently implies <span lang="grc">εἰ μή</span> <!-- page 634 --> <span lang="grc">τι ἄλλο</span> or <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ ἄλλοις</span>, etc., as <span lang="grc">λέγ᾽ ἀλλὰ τοῦτο</span> _say this at least_ (_say but this_) <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 415">S. El. 415</cite>.

##### <a id="s2784"></a> §2784

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλά** </span> **opposing Whole Sentences**.—<span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> _well_, _well but, nay but, however_ is often used, especially at the beginning of a speech, in opposition either to something said (or supposed to be meant) by another, or to a latent feeling in the mind of the writer or speaker himself. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ πρῶτον μὲν μνησθήσομαι . . . ὃ τελευταῖον κατ᾽ ἐμοῦ εἶπε</span> _well_, _I will first allude to the charge against me which he mentioned last_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.3.35">X. H. 2.3.35</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ὤφελε μὲν Κῦρος ζῆν· ἐπεὶ δὲ τετελεύτηκεν κτλ</span>. _well_, _I would that Cyrus were alive; but since he is dead_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.4">X. A. 2.1.4</cite>. Often of remonstrance or protest, as <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἀμήχανον</span> _nay_, _it is impossible_ <cite data-ref="Eur. El. 529">E. El. 529</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> is also especially common when a previous train of thought or remark is impatiently interrupted, as <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ ταῦτα μὲν τί δεῖ λέγειν</span>; _but what is the need of recounting this?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 11">S. Ph. 11</cite>. Similarly in

**a.** Replies (often in quick, abrupt, or decisive answers): <span lang="grc">ἤρετο ὅ τι εἴη τὸ σύνθημα· ὁ δ᾽ ἀπεκρἱ̄νατο· Ζεὺς σωτὴρ καὶ νἱ̄κη· ὁ δὲ Κῦρος ἀκούσᾱς Ἀλλὰ δέχομαί τε, ἔφη, καὶ τοῦτο ἔστω</span> _he asked what the watchword was; and he replied:_ “_Zeus the saviour and Victory;_” _and Cyrus_, _on hearing this, said_, “_Well_, _I accept it and so let it be_” <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.17">X. A. 1.8.17</cite>.

**b.** Assent, with an adversative sense implied (cp. _oh_, _well_): <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ει᾽ δοκεῖ, χωρῶμεν</span> _well_, _if it pleases thee, let us be going_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 645">S. Ph. 645</cite>.

**c.** Appeals, exhortations, proposals, and commands: <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἴωμεν</span> _but let us go_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 311a">P. Pr. 311a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ἐμοὶ πείθου καὶ μὴ ἄλλως ποίει</span> _nay_, _take my advice and don't refuse_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 45a">P. Cr. 45a</cite>. The tone here is often impatient.

**d.** Wishes and imprecations: <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ εὐτυχοίης</span> _well_, _my blessings on thee!_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 1478">S. O. T. 1478</cite>.

**e.** Questions, to mark surprise: <span lang="grc">πῶς εἶπας; ἀλλ᾽ ἦ καὶ σοφὸς λέληθας ὤν</span>; _what dost thou mean? can it really be that thou art subtle too and without my knowing it?_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Alc. 58">E. Alc. 58</cite>.

##### <a id="s2785"></a> §2785

<span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> is often used when a speaker introduces a supposed objection (either in his own name or in that of his opponent), and immediately answers it; as <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ νὴ τὸν Δία ἐκεῖν᾽ ἂν ἴσως εἴποι πρὸς ταῦτα κτλ</span>. _but_, _by Zeus_, _he might perhaps say in reply to this_, etc. <cite data-ref="Dem. 20.3">D. 20.3</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> may here put the supposed objection and also give the answer. Thus, <span lang="grc">τί γὰρ καὶ βουλόμενοι μετεπέμπεσθ᾽ ἂν αὐτοὺς ἐν τούτῳ τῷ καιρῷ; ἐπὶ τὴν εἰρήνην; ἀλλ᾽ ὑπῆρχεν ἅπᾱσιν· ἀλλ᾽ ἐπὶ τὸν πόλεμον; ἀλλ᾽ αὐτοὶ περὶ τῆς εἰρήνης ἐβουλεύεσθε</span> _for with what possible desire would you have been sending them at that juncture? With a view to peace? Why_ (_but_) _peace was open to all. With a view to war? Why_ (_but_) _you were yourselves deliberating about peace_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.24">D. 18.24</cite>. Cp. French _mais_ introducing a reply to a question.

**a.** So in rapid dialogue objections may take the form of questions, in which each <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> after the first may be rendered by _or_. Cp. [2654](../32-interrogative-sentences-questions.md#s2654).

##### <a id="s2786"></a> §2786

**<span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> with other Particles**.—For example:

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλὰ γάρ** </span> 2816; on <span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ γὰρ ἀλλά** </span>, see [2767](../35-negative-sentences.md#s2767).

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλὰ . . . γε** </span> _but at any rate_.

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλά γέ τοι** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **τοί γε** </span>) _yet at least, yet be sure_.

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλὰ δή** </span> _well then_. <!-- page 635 -->

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλ᾽ ἦ** </span>; _why how? can it really be that? what_, _can it be true?_ Here <span lang="grc">ἀλλά</span> marks surprise, while <span lang="grc">ἦ</span> asks the question.

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλὰ μέντοι** </span> _nay_, _but; well_, _however; yet truly_. On <span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ μέντοι ἀλλά** </span>, see [2767](../35-negative-sentences.md#s2767).

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλὰ μήν** </span> _nay_, _but; but then; but surely_. Often to introduce an objection, to reject an alternative, often merely to introduce a new idea or to resume an interrupted thought. On <span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ μὴν ἀλλά** </span>, see [2767](../35-negative-sentences.md#s2767).

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλ᾽ ὅμως** </span> _but still_. Often without a verb, to introduce the reply to an objection.

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλ᾽ οὐδέ** </span> is sometimes used elliptically, as in <span lang="grc">ὑπὲρ . . . ὧν οὗτος ἀπήγγειλε προ`ς ὑ̄μᾶς ἀλλ᾽ οὐδὲ μῑκρόν</span> _nay_, _there is not even ever so little_ (not only not a great deal _but not even a little_) _concerning which he reported to you_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.37">D. 19.37</cite>. <span lang="grc"> **ἀλλ᾽ οὐδὲ μὲν δή** </span> is often used to reject an alternative.

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλ᾽ οὖν** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **γε** </span>) _but then, well then, well at any rate;_ stronger than <span lang="grc">δ᾽ οὖν</span>.
