# PARTICLES: οὖν

##### <a id="s2955"></a> §2955

<span lang="grc"> **οὖν** </span> (Ionic, Lesbic, Doric <span lang="grc">ὦν</span>), a postpositive particle, is either confirmatory or inferential. <span lang="grc">οὖν</span> points to something already mentioned or known or to the present situation.

##### <a id="s2956"></a> §2956

**Confirmatory** <span lang="grc"> **οὖν** </span> _in fact, at all events, in truth_ belongs properly to the entire clause, but usually, for purposes of emphasis, attaches itself to some other particle, to a relative pronoun, or at times to other words (<cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 22b">P. A. 22b</cite>). On <span lang="grc">γοῦν</span>, see [2830](09-gammaomicronupsilonnu.md#s2830); on <span lang="grc">μὲν οὖν</span>, 2901; on <span lang="grc">τοιγαροῦν</span>, 2987. In some of its combinations with other particles <span lang="grc">οὖν</span> may be inferential or transitional.

##### <a id="s2957"></a> §2957

<span lang="grc"> **ἀλλ᾽ οὖν** </span> or <span lang="grc"> **ἀλλ᾽ οὖν . . . γε** </span> (stronger than <span lang="grc">δ᾽ οὖν</span>) _well_, _at all events; well_, _certainly_, _for that matter;_ as <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ οὖν πονηροί γε φαινόμενοι</span> _well_, _at all events they look like sorry fellows, that they are_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.19">X. C. 1.4.19</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ οὖν τοσοῦτόν γ᾽ ἴσθι</span> _well_, _at any rate you know this at least_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 1305">S. Ph. 1305</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ οὖν</span> may stand in the apodosis to an hypothetical proposition (<cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 91b">P. Ph. 91b</cite>).

##### <a id="s2958"></a> §2958

<span lang="grc"> **γὰρ οὖν** </span> (and <span lang="grc"> **καὶ γὰρ οὖν** </span>) _for in fact_ (_indeed_, _in any case_)_;_ as <span lang="grc">εὖ γὰρ οὖν γέγεις</span> _for indeed thou sayest well_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 1255">S. Ant. 1255</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὀνήσεσθε ἀκούοντες· μέλλω γὰρ οὖν ἄττα ὑ̄μῖν ἐρεῖν καὶ ἄλλα</span> _you will profit by listening; for I am certainly going to tell you some other things_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 30c">P. A. 30c</cite>.

Also to mark a consequence (<cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.9.11">X. A. 1.9.11</cite>), and in replies, as <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ οὖν</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 277e">P. Phae. 277e</cite>, and also when the speaker repeats an important word of his interlocutor, as <span lang="grc">φημὶ γὰρ οὖν</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 466e">P. G. 466e</cite>.

##### <a id="s2959"></a> §2959

<span lang="grc"> **δ᾽ οὖν** </span> _but certainly, at all events, anyhow_, _be that as it may_ with or without <span lang="grc">μέν</span> in the preceding clause. Here <span lang="grc">οὖν</span> shows that an unquestionable fact is to be set forth in its own clause; while the adversative <span lang="grc">δέ</span> marks opposition to what has preceded and implies that the foregoing statement is uncertain and liable to dispute: ‘be that true or not, _at any rate_ what follows is certainly true.’ <span lang="grc">δ᾽ οὖν</span> is used (**a**) to set aside conjecture, surmise, or hearsay; (**b**) to resume the main argument after long digression, and to cut short further discussion and come to the point; (**c**), with imperatives, to denote assent marked by unwillingness, impatience, or indifference. Thus, (**a**) <span lang="grc">εἰ μὲν δὴ δίκαια ποιήσω, οὐκ οἶδα· αἱρήσομαι δ᾽ οὖν ὑ̄μᾶς</span> _whether I shall do what is right_ (_or not_), _I do not know; be that as it may_, _I will choose you_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.5">X. A. 1.3.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ ἐλέγετο Κύ̄ρω δοῦναι</span> <!-- page 665 --> <span lang="grc">πολλὰ χρήματα. τῇ δ᾽ οὖν στρατιᾷ τότε ἀπέδωκε Κῦρος μισθὸν τεττάρων μηνῶν</span> _and she is said to have given Cyrus a large sum; at any rate Cyrus then gave the army four months' pay_ 1. 2. 12; (**b**) cp. <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.3">T. 1.3</cite>, 6. 15, 8. 81. Resumptive <span lang="grc">δ᾽ οὖν</span> may also set aside doubtful statements. (**c**) <span lang="grc">σὺ δ᾽ οὖν λέγε, εἴ σοι τῷ λόγῳ τις ἡδονή</span> _well speak on then, if thou hast delight in speaking_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 891">S. El. 891</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔστω δ᾽ οὖν ὅπως ὑ̄μῖν φίλον</span> _however_, _be it as you wish_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 1205">S. O. C. 1205</cite>.

<span lang="grc"> **ει᾽ δ᾽ οὖν** </span> = _but if indeed, but if in point of fact_; as <span lang="grc">εἰ δ᾽ οὖν τι κἀ̄κτρέποιτο τοῦ πρόσθεν λόγου</span> _but if he_ **should** _deviate at all from his former statement_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 851">S. O. T. 851</cite>.

##### <a id="s2960"></a> §2960

<span lang="grc"> **δὴ οὖν** </span> _certainly then;_ cp. <span lang="grc"> **οὖν δή** </span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">τί δὴ οὖν</span>; or <span lang="grc">τί οὖν δή</span>; _well then pray?_ <span lang="grc">πῶς δὴ οὖν</span>; _how then pray?_ <span lang="grc"> **οὖν δῆτα** </span> _really then_.

##### <a id="s2961"></a> §2961

<span lang="grc">**εἴτε οὖν**, **οὔτε οὖν**</span>: in alternative clauses <span lang="grc">οὖν</span> (_indeed_) is added to one or both clauses as emphasis may be desired: <span lang="grc"> **εἴτε οὖν . . . εἴτε** </span> _whether indeed . . . or_, <span lang="grc"> **εἴτε . . . εἴτε οὖν** </span> _whether . . . or indeed_, or <span lang="grc"> **εἴτε οὖν . . . εἴτε οὖν** </span> _whether indeed . . . or indeed_. So also in exclusive clauses: <span lang="grc"> **οὔτε** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **μήτε** </span>) . . . <span lang="grc"> **οὔτε** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **μήτε** </span>) <span lang="grc"> **οὖν** </span> _neither . . . nor yet_, <span lang="grc"> **οὔτε** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **μήτε** </span>) <span lang="grc"> **οὖν . . . οὔτε** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **μήτε** </span>) _neither indeed . . . nor_.

##### <a id="s2962"></a> §2962

<span lang="grc">οὖν</span> often follows interrogative pronouns and adverbs (in dialogue); as <span lang="grc"> **τίς οὖν** </span>; _who pray?_ <span lang="grc"> **τί οὖν** </span>, generally with the aorist, in impatient questions asks why that which is desired has not been done ([2197 c](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/02-purpose-clauses-final-clauses.md#s2197)).

##### <a id="s2963"></a> §2963

<span lang="grc">οὖν</span> affixed to a relative pronoun has a generalizing force and makes it indefinite ([339 e](../../part-2-inflection/06-pronouns.md#s339)). Such indefinite relative pronouns are construed like the indefinite <span lang="grc">τὶς</span> or demonstratives; and do not introduce relative clauses (unlike _whosoever_, etc., which are both indefinite and relative).

So with adverbs ([346 c](../../part-2-inflection/07-adverbs.md#s346)), as <span lang="grc">ὁπωσοῦν</span> _in any way, no matter how_ (= _utique_ not = _utcunque_). Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽ ὁπωσοῦν</span> _not even in the slightest degree_.

**a.** Simply placed _after_ relatives <span lang="grc">οὖν</span> has a strengthening force; as <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ οὖν</span> _as in fact_ (often in parentheses), <span lang="grc">οἷός περ οὖν</span> _just as in fact_.

##### <a id="s2964"></a> §2964

**Inferential <span lang="grc">οὖν</span>** _therefore_, _accordingly_ (_igitur_, _ergo_), usually classed as a conjunction, signifies that something _follows_ from what precedes. Inferential <span lang="grc">οὖν</span> marks a transition to a new thought and continues a narrative (often after <span lang="grc">ἐπεί, ἐπειδή, ὅτε</span>), resumes an interrupted narration (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.42">T. 3.42</cite>, <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.9">X. C. 3.3.9</cite>), and in general states a conclusion or inference. It stands alone or in conjunction with other particles. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀναρχίᾳ ἂν καὶ ἀταξίᾳ ἐνόμιζον ἡμᾶς ἀπολέσθαι. δεῖ οὖν πολὺ μὲν τοὺς ἄρχοντας ἐπιμελεστέρους γενέσθαι τοὺς νῦν τῶν πρόσθεν</span> _they were of the opinion that we would be overcome through our lack of leaders and discipline_. _It is imperative therefore that the leaders we have now should be much more watchful than those we had before_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.29">X. A. 3.2.29</cite>.

**a.** The inferential and transitional use is derived from the confirmative meaning, and is scarcely marked until Herodotus and the Attic poets. Cp. <span lang="grc">μὲν οὖν. ἐπεὶ οὖν</span> in Hom. is sometimes used in transitions.
