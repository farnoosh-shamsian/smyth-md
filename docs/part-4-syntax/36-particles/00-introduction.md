# PARTICLES: Introductory

<!-- page 631 -->

##### <a id="s2769"></a> §2769

Under the head of particles are included sentence adverbs ([1094](../15-adverbs.md#s1094)) and conjunctions. Many sentence adverbs remained such, some sank to mere enclitics, others became pure conjunctions, while still others fluctuated in function, being now adverbial, now conjunctional, as <span lang="grc">καί</span> _even_ and _and_, <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> _not even_ and _nor_, <span lang="grc">γάρ</span> _in fact_ and _for_, <span lang="grc">πρίν</span> _sooner_ and _until_ or _before_.

##### <a id="s2770"></a> §2770

Conjunctions are either coördinating or subordinating. The coördinating conjunctions with their several varieties are given in 2163. The subordinating conjunctions are

Causal: <span lang="grc">ὅτι, διότι, διόπερ, ἐπεί, ἐπειδή, ὅτε, ὁπότε, ὡς</span> ([2240](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/04-causal-clauses.md#s2240)).

Comparative: <span lang="grc">ὡς, ὥσπερ, καθάπερ, ὅπως, ᾗ, ὅπῃ, ᾗπερ</span> ([2463](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/13-clauses-of-comparison.md#s2463); cp. [2481](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/13-clauses-of-comparison.md#s2481)).

Concessive: <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ</span> (<span lang="grc">κει᾽</span>), <span lang="grc">καὶ ἐό̄ν</span> (<span lang="grc">κἄ̄ν</span>), <span lang="grc">εἰ καί, ἐὰ̄ν καί</span> ([2369](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/11-concessive-clauses.md#s2369)).

Conditional: <span lang="grc">εἰ, ἐά̄ν, ἤν, ἄ̄ν</span> ([2283](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/09-conditional-clauses.md#s2283)).

Consecutive: <span lang="grc">ὥστε, ὡς</span> ([2250](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/05-result-clauses-consecutive-clauses.md#s2250)).

Declarative: <span lang="grc">ὅτι, διότι, οὕνεκα, ὁθούνεκα, ὡς</span> ([2578](../31-dependent-substantive-clauses-2574-2635.md#s2578)).

Final: <span lang="grc">ἵνα, ὅπως, ὡς, μή</span>, etc. ([2193](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/02-purpose-clauses-final-clauses.md#s2193); cp. [2209](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/03-object-clauses.md#s2209), [2221](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/03-object-clauses.md#s2221)).

Local: <span lang="grc">οὗ, ὅπου, οἷ, ὅποι, ἔνθα, ὅθεν, ὁπόθεν, ᾗ, ὅπῃ</span>, etc ([2498](../30-adjective-clauses-relative-clauses-2488-2573.md#s2498)).

Temporal: <span lang="grc">ὅτε, ὁπότε, ἡνίκα, ἐπεί, ἐπειδή, ὡς, μέχρι, ἔστε, ἕως, πρίν</span>, etc ([2383](../29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/12-temporal-clauses.md#s2383)).

Some conjunctions belong to more than one class.

##### <a id="s2771"></a> §2771

Greek has an extraordinary number of sentence adverbs (or particles in the narrow sense) having a logical or emotional (rhetorical) value. Either alone or in combination these sentence adverbs give a distinctness to the relations between ideas which is foreign to other languages, and often resist translation by separate words, which in English are frequently over emphatic and cumbersome in comparison to the light and delicate nature of the Greek originals (e.g. <span lang="grc">ἄρα, γέ, τοί</span>). The force of such words is frequently best rendered by pause, stress, or alterations of pitch. To catch the subtle and elusive meaning of these often apparently insignificant elements of speech challenges the utmost vigilance and skill of the student.

##### <a id="s2772"></a> §2772

The particles show different degrees of independence as regards their position. Many are completely independent and may occupy any place in the sentence; some may occur only at the beginning (_prepositive_ particles, as <span lang="grc">ἀτάρ</span>); others find their place only after one or more words at the beginning (_postpositive_ particles, as <span lang="grc">γάρ, δέ</span>); and some are attached closely to a preceding word o&lt;\*&gt; even form compounds with that word wherever it may occur (<span lang="grc">γέ, τέ</span>).

##### <a id="s2773"></a> §2773

Some verbal forms have virtually become particles, e.g. <span lang="grc">ἄγε</span> used with the second person plural, <span lang="grc">ὁρᾷς</span> used of several persons, parenthetic <span lang="grc">οἶμαι, δῆλο&lt;\*&gt; ὅτι, εὖ οἶδ᾽ ὅτι, εὖ ἴσθ᾽ ὅτι</span> ([2585](../31-dependent-substantive-clauses-2574-2635.md#s2585)).

##### <a id="s2774"></a> §2774

As regards their _meaning_, particles may be arranged in classes, _e.g adversative_, _affirmative_, _asseverative_, _concessive_, _confirmative_, _conjunctive_, _infer_ <!-- page 632 --> _ential_, _intensive_, _interrogative_, _limitative_, _negative_, etc. These classes canno&lt;\*&gt; always be sharply distinguished: some particles fall under two or more classes Many particles, which serve to set forth the logical relation between clauses, ha&lt;\*&gt; originally only an intensive or confirmatory force that was confined to their ow&lt;\*&gt; clause. The following sections deal only with the commoner uses of the most noteworthy particles.
