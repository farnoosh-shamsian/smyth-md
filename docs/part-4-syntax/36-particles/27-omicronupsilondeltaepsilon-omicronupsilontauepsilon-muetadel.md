# PARTICLES: οὐδέ, οὔτε (μηδέ, μήτε)

##### <a id="s2930"></a> §2930

<span lang="grc"> **οὐδέ** </span> (<span lang="grc"> **μηδέ** </span>) is an adverb and a conjunction, and is to be broken up into the negative <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> (<span lang="grc">μή</span>) and <span lang="grc">δέ</span> meaning _and_, _even_, _also_ , or _but_.

## <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> (<span lang="grc">μηδέ</span>) _as an Adverb_

##### <a id="s2931"></a> §2931

Adverbial <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> (<span lang="grc">μηδέ</span>) _not even, not . . . either_, _also . . . not_, _nor yet_ (_ne . . quidem_). Cp. the use of <span lang="grc">καί</span> _even_, _also_ in affirmative sentences; as <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽ ὥς</span> _not even in that case_ (<span lang="grc">καὶ ὥς</span> _even in that case_).

<span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ οὐδὲ τούτων στερήσονται</span> _but no; even of these shall they be deprived_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.8">X. A. 1.4.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅτ᾽ οὐδ᾽ οὕτω ῥᾴδιον ἦν</span> _when besides it was not so easy_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 18.65">I. 18.65</cite> (= <span lang="grc">καὶ οὐ</span> _also not_). With <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽ ει᾽</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν</span>) _not even if_ <span lang="grc">οὐ</span> belongs with the main clause, while <span lang="grc">δέ</span> _even_ goes with the dependent clause. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽ ἂν εἰ βούλοιντο, ῥᾳδίως πονηροὶ γένοιντο</span> _even if they wished, they could not easily become wicked_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.5.86">X. C. 7.5.86</cite> (= <span lang="grc">καὶ εἰ βούλοιντο, οὐκ ἂν γένοιντο</span>). Similarly with a participle: <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ πεπονθὼς κακῶς ἐχθρὸν εἶναί μοι τοῦτον ὁμολογῶ</span> _I do not admit that this man is my enemy even though I have been ill-used_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.205">D. 21.205</cite>.

## <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> (<span lang="grc">μηδέ</span>) _as a Conjunction_

##### <a id="s2932"></a> §2932

<span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> (<span lang="grc">μηδέ</span>) as a conjunction (_and not, nor_) connects two or more whole clauses.

##### <a id="s2933"></a> §2933

In Attic prose <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> is used only to join a negative clause to another clause itself negative; as <span lang="grc">οὐδεμία ἐλπὶς ἦν τῑμωρίᾱς οὐδὲ ἄλλη σωτηρίᾱ ἐφαίνετο</span> _there was no hope of assistance nor did any chance of safety appear_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.20">T. 3.20</cite>.

**a.** A negative clause is joined to an affirmative clause by <span lang="grc">καὶ οὐ</span> (<span lang="grc">μή</span>). Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐμμενῶ τῇ ξυμμαχίᾳ . . . καὶ οὐ παραβήσομαι</span> _I will abide by the alliance and I will not violate it_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.47">T. 5.47</cite>. <span lang="grc">καὶ οὐ</span> (<span lang="grc">μή</span>) may have an adversative force (_but not_).

N.—But in poetry and Ionic prose <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> may continue an affirmative clause; as <span lang="grc">δεινὸν γὰρ οὐδὲ ῥητόν</span> _dread indeed and not to be uttered_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 756">S. Ph. 756</cite>.

##### <a id="s2934"></a> §2934

<span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> is used by the poets for _but not_, where Attic prose writers have <span lang="grc">ἀλλ᾽ ου᾽</span> or <span lang="grc">καὶ οὐ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἔνθ᾽ ἄλλοις μὲν πᾶσιν ἑήνδανεν, οὐδέ ποθ᾽ Ἥρῃ οὐδὲ Ποσει-</span> <!-- page 661 --> <span lang="grc">δάωνι</span> _then it was pleasing to all the others, but not to Hera or to Poseidon_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 24.25"><span lang="grc">Ω</span> 25</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐμαῖσι οὐδὲ σαῖσι δυσβουλίαις</span> _by my folly but not by thine_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 1269">S. Ant. 1269</cite> (cp. the _negative_ form <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἐμὸν τόδ᾽ ἀλλὰ σόν</span> _this is not my part, but thine_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 1470">S. El. 1470</cite>). Cp. <span lang="grc">σοῦ τάδε κινδῡνεύεις, ἀλλ᾽ οὐκ ἐμοῦ ἀκηκοέναι</span> _you probably heard this from yourself and not from me_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Alc. 1 113c">P. Alc. 113c</cite>.

##### <a id="s2935"></a> §2935

<span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> may stand in an apodosis corresponding to apodotic <span lang="grc">δέ</span> ([2837](10-deltaepsilon.md#s2837)). Cp. <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 590">S. O. C. 590</cite>.

##### <a id="s2936"></a> §2936

<span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> may negative a preceding word also; as <span lang="grc">αἱ Φοίνισσαι νῆες οὐδὲ ὁ Τισσαφέρνης . . . ἧκον</span> _the Phoenician ships had not arrived nor had Tissaphernes_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.99">T. 8.99</cite>. Cp. [2943](28-omicronupsilontauepsilon-muetatauepsilon.md#s2943). In such cases we usually find another negative, which goes with the verb; as <span lang="grc">ἁπλοῦν μὲν οὐδὲ δίκαιον οὐδὲν ἂν εἰπεῖν ἔχοι</span> _he could say nothing straightforward nor just_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 22.4">D. 22.4</cite>.

## <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> (<span lang="grc">μηδέ</span>) _with other Negatives_

##### <a id="s2937"></a> §2937

<span lang="grc"> **οὐδὲ . . . οὐδέ** </span> commonly means _not even . . . nor yet_ (or _no_, _nor_), the first <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> being adverbial, the second conjunctive. <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ . . . οὐδέ</span> is not correlative, like <span lang="grc">οὔτε . . . οὔτε</span>, and hence never means _neither . . . nor_. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ ἥλιον οὐδὲ σελήνην ἄρα νομίζω θεοὺς εἶναι</span>; _do I then hold that not even the sun nor yet the moon are gods?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 26c">P. A. 26c</cite>, <span lang="grc">σύ γε οὐδὲ ὁρῶν γιγνώσκεις οὐδὲ ἀκούων μέμνησαι</span> _you do not even understand though you see, nor yet do you remember though you hear_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.27">X. A. 3.1.27</cite>. <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ . . . οὐδέ</span> both copulative (_and not . . nor yet_) in <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.50">X. C. 3.3.50</cite>. <span lang="grc"> **οὐδὲ . . . οὐδὲ . . . δέ** </span> is the negative of <span lang="grc">καὶ . . . καὶ . . . δέ</span> in <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.20">X. A. 1.8.20</cite>.

**a.** So in both members of comparative sentences (cp. <span lang="grc">καί</span> [2885](17-kappaalphaiota.md#s2885)); as <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ οὐδὲ γεωργοῦ ἀ̄ργοῦ οὐδὲν ὄφελος, οὕτως οὐδὲ στρατηγοῦ ἀ̄ργοῦντος οὐδὲν ὄφελος</span> _as there is no good in an idle tiller of the soil, so there is no good in an idle general_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.18">X. C. 1.3.18</cite>.

##### <a id="s2938"></a> §2938

<span lang="grc"> **οὐδὲ γὰρ οὐδέ** </span> (negative of <span lang="grc">καὶ γὰρ καί</span>); as <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ γὰρ οὐδὲ τοῦτο ἐψεύσατο</span> _for neither did he deceive me even in this_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.2.20">X. C. 7.2.20</cite>. Here the first <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> negatives the whole sentence, the second <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> negatives <span lang="grc">τοῦτο</span>.

##### <a id="s2939"></a> §2939

<span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ . . . οὐδέ** </span>: <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> _not even_ as well as _nor_ ([2933](#s2933)) may resume a preceding <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ὕβριν γὰρ οὐ στέργουσιν οὐδὲ δαίμονες</span> lit. _not even the gods do not love insolence_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Trach. 280">S. Tr. 280</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ μέντοι ἔφη νομίζειν οὐδ᾽ ει᾽ παμπόνηρος ἦν Δέξιππος βίᾳ χρῆναι πάσχειν αὐτόν</span> _he said however that he did not think that, even if Dexippus was a downright rascal, he ought to suffer by an act of violence_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.6.25">X. A. 6.6.25</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ δεῖ δὴ τοιοῦτον . . . καιρὸν ἀφεῖναι οὐδὲ παθεῖν ταὐτὸν ὅπερ . . . πεπόνθατε</span> _we must not let such an opportunity go by nor suffer the same as you have suffered_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 1.8">D. 1.8</cite>.

<span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ μέντοι οὐδέ** </span> _not by any means however_. On <span lang="grc"> **ου᾽ μὴν οὐδέ** </span> see [2768](../35-negative-sentences.md#s2768).

##### <a id="s2940"></a> §2940

<span lang="grc"> **οὐδὲ . . . οὐ** </span>: <span lang="grc">οὐδέ</span> may be resumed by <span lang="grc">οὐ</span>; as <span lang="grc">οὐδέ γε ὁ ἰδίᾳ πονηρὸς οὐκ ἂν γένοιτο δημοσίᾳ χρηστός</span> _nor can the man who is bad in his private life prove himself good in a public capacity_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.78">Aes. 3.78</cite>.

##### <a id="s2941"></a> §2941

<span lang="grc"> **οὐδὲ . . . οὔτε** </span> is rare (<cite data-ref="Plat. Charm. 171b">P. Charm. 171b</cite>). <!-- page 662 -->
