# PREPOSITIONS

##### <a id="s1636"></a> §1636

Prepositions define the relations of a substantival notion to the predicate.

**a.** All prepositions seem to have been adverbs originally and mostly adverbs of place; as adverbs they are case-forms. Several are locatives, as <span lang="grc">περί</span>.

##### <a id="s1637"></a> §1637

The prepositions express primarily notions of space, then notions of time, and finally are used in figurative relations to denote cause, agency, means, manner, etc. Attic often differs from the Epic in using the prepositions to denote metaphorical relations. The prepositions define the character of the verbal action and set forth the relations of an oblique case to the predicate with greater precision than is possible for the cases without a preposition. Thus, <span lang="grc">μετὰ δὲ μνηστῆρσιν ἔειπε</span> _he spake among the suitors_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 17.467"><span lang="grc">ρ</span> 467</cite> specifies the meaning with greater certainty than <span lang="grc">μνηστῆρσιν ἔειπε</span>. So <span lang="grc">ὁ Ἑλλήνων φόβος</span> may mean _the fear felt by the Greeks_ or _the fear caused by the Greeks;_ but with <span lang="grc">ἐξ</span> or <span lang="grc">παρά</span> (cp. <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.18">X. A. 1.2.18</cite>, Lyc. [130](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s130)) the latter meaning is stated unequivocally. The use of a preposition often serves to show how a construction with a composite case ([1279](18-the-cases/00-introduction.md#s1279)) is to be regarded (genitive or ablative; dative, instrumental, or locative).

##### <a id="s1638"></a> §1638

**Development of the Use of Prepositions**.—

**a.** Originally the preposition was a free adverb limiting the meaning of the verb but not directly connected with it: <span lang="grc">κατ᾽ ἄρ᾽ ἕζετο</span> _down he sate him_ A 101. In this use the preposition may be called a ‘preposition-adverb.’

**b.** The preposition-adverb was also often used in sentences in which an oblique case depended directly on the verb without regard to the prepositionadverb. Here the case is independent of the preposition-adverb, as in <span lang="grc">βλεφάρων ἄπο δάκρυα πί̄πτει</span> _from her eyelids, away_, _tears fall_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 14.129"><span lang="grc">ξ</span> 129</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">βλεφάρων</span> is ablatival genitive and is not _governed_ by <span lang="grc">ἀπό</span>, which serves merely to define the relation between verb and noun.

**c.** Gradually the preposition-adverb was brought into closer connection either (1) with the verb, whence arose compounds such as <span lang="grc">ἀποπί̄πτειν</span>, or (2) with the noun, the preposition-adverb having freed itself from its adverbial relation to the verb. In this stage, which is that of Attic prose, the noun was felt to depend on the preposition. Hence arose many syntactical changes, e.g. <!-- page 366 --> the accusative of the limit of motion ([1588](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1588)) was abandoned in prose for the preposition with the accusative.

Prepositions have three uses.

##### <a id="s1639"></a> §1639

(I) Prepositions appear as adverbs defining the action of verbs.

##### <a id="s1640"></a> §1640

The preposition-adverb usually precedes the verb, from which it is often separated in Homer by nouns and other words: <span lang="grc">ἡμῖν ἀπὸ λοιγὸν ἀμῦναι</span> _to ward off destruction from_ (for) _us_ A 67, <span lang="grc">πρὸ γὰρ ἧκε θεά̄</span> _the goddess sent_ her _forth_ A 195, <span lang="grc">ἔχεν κάτα γαῖα</span> _the earth held_ him _fast_ B 699.

##### <a id="s1641"></a> §1641

So, as links connecting sentences, <span lang="grc">πρὸς δὲ καί</span> and <span lang="grc">καὶ πρός</span> _and besides_, <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ δέ</span> _and besides_, <span lang="grc">μετὰ δέ</span> _and next, thereupon_ (both in Hdt.), <span lang="grc">ἐν δέ</span> _and among the number_ (Hdt.).

##### <a id="s1642"></a> §1642

The verb (usually <span lang="grc">ἐστί</span> or <span lang="grc">εἰσί</span>, rarely <span lang="grc">εἰμί</span>) may be omitted: <span lang="grc">οὐ γάρ τις μέτα τοῖος ἀνήρ</span> _for no such man is among them_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 21.93"><span lang="grc">φ</span> 93</cite>. Cp. [944](09-the-predicate.md#s944).

##### <a id="s1643"></a> §1643

The preposition-adverb may do duty for the verb in parallel clauses: <span lang="grc">ἄνδρες ἀνέσταν, ἂν μὲν ἄρ᾽ Ἀτρεΐδης . . . ἂν δ᾽ ἄρα Μηριόνης</span> _the men rose up, rose up Atreides_, _rose up Meriones_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 23.886"><span lang="grc">Ψ</span> 886</cite>. So in Hdt.

##### <a id="s1644"></a> §1644

(II) Prepositions connect verbs and other words with the oblique cases of nouns and pronouns.

##### <a id="s1645"></a> §1645

It is often impossible to decide whether the preposition belongs to the verb or to the noun. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐκ δὲ Χρῡσηὶς νηὸς βῆ</span> A 439 may be _Chryseïs went out of the ship_ or _Chryseïs went-out-from_ (<span lang="grc">ἐξέβη</span>) _the ship_. When important words separate the prep.-adv. from the noun, the prep.-adv. is more properly regarded as belonging with the verb, which, together with the prep.-adv., governs the noun: <span lang="grc">ἀμφὶ δὲ χαῖται ὤμοις ἀ̄ΐσσονται</span> _and his mane floats-about his shoulders_ Z 509. The Mss. often vary: <span lang="grc">τοῖσιν ἐγὼ μεθ᾽ ὁμί̄λεον</span> (or <span lang="grc">μεθομί̄λεον</span>) _with these I was wont to associate_ A 269.

##### <a id="s1646"></a> §1646

(III) Prepositions unite with verbs (less frequently with nouns and other prepositions) to form compounds. Cp. [886](../part-3-formation-of-words/06-compound-words.md#s886) ff.

**a.** From this use as a prefix the name ‘preposition’ (<span lang="grc">πρόθεσις</span> _praepositio_) is derived. The original meaning of some prepositions is best seen in compounds.

##### <a id="s1647"></a> §1647

**Improper prepositions** ([1699](#s1699)) are adverbs used like prepositions, but incapable of forming compounds. The case (usually the genitive) following an improper preposition depends on the preposition alone without regard to the verb; whereas a true preposition was attached originally, as an adverb, to a case depending directly on the verb.

##### <a id="s1648"></a> §1648

The addition of a preposition (especially <span lang="grc">διά, κατά, σύν</span>) to a verbal form may mark the completion of the action of the verbal idea (perfective action). The local force of the preposition is here often lost. So <span lang="grc">διαφεύγειν</span> _succeed in escaping_, <span lang="grc">καταδιώκειν</span> _succeed in pursuing_, <span lang="grc">συντελεῖν</span> _accomplish_, _carry into effect_ (<span lang="grc">τελεῖν</span> _do_, _perform_).

##### <a id="s1649"></a> §1649

Two or more prepositions may be used with one verb, either sepa<!-- page 367 --> rately, as adverbs, or in composition with the verb. Thus, <span lang="grc">στῆ δὲ παρέξ</span> (or <span lang="grc">παρ᾽ ἐξ</span>) _he stood forth beside him_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 11.486"><span lang="grc">Λ</span> 486</cite>. When two prepositions of like meaning are used in composition, that preposition precedes which has the narrower range: <span lang="grc">συμμετέχειν</span> _take part in with_, <span lang="grc">ἀμφιπεριστέφεσθαι</span> _to be put round about as a crown_. When two prepositions are used with one noun, the noun usually depends on the second, while the first defines the second adverbially; as <span lang="grc">ἀμφὶ περὶ κρήνην</span> _round about a spring_ B 305. It is often uncertain whether or not two prepositions should be written together.

**a.** Such compound prepositions are <span lang="grc">ἀμφιπερί, παρέξ, ὑπέκ, ἀπέκ, διέκ, ἀποπρό, διαπρό, περιπρό</span>. Improper prepositions may be used with true prepositions, as <span lang="grc">μέχρι εἰς τὸ στρατόπεδον</span> _as far as_ (into) _the camp_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.4.26">X. A. 6.4.26</cite>.

##### <a id="s1650"></a> §1650

**Tmesis** (<span lang="grc">τμῆσις</span> _cutting_) denotes the separation of a preposition from its verb, and is a term of late origin, properly descriptive only of the post-epic language, in which preposition and verb normally formed an indissoluble compound. The term ‘tmesis’ is incorrectly applied to the language of Homer, since in the Epic the prep.-adv. was still in process of joining with the verb.

##### <a id="s1651"></a> §1651

In Attic poetry tmesis occurs chiefly when the preposition is separated from the verb by unimportant words (particles, enclitics), and is employed for the sake of emphasis or (in Euripides) as a mere ornament. Aristophanes uses tmesis only to parody the style of tragic choruses.

##### <a id="s1652"></a> §1652

Hdt. uses tmesis frequently in imitation of the Epic; the intervening words are <span lang="grc">ὦν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">οὖν</span>), enclitics, <span lang="grc">δέ, μὲν . . . δέ</span>, etc.

##### <a id="s1653"></a> §1653

In Attic prose tmesis occurs only in special cases: <span lang="grc">ἀντ᾽ εὖ ποιεῖν</span> (<span lang="grc">πάσχειν</span>) and <span lang="grc">σὺν εὖ</span> (<span lang="grc">κακῶς</span>) <span lang="grc">ποιεῖν</span> (<span lang="grc">πάσχειν</span>). Thus, <span lang="grc">ὅσους εὖ ποιήσαντας ἡ πόλις ἀντ᾽ εὖ πεποίηκεν</span> _all whom the city has requited with benefits for the service they rendered it_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 20.64">D. 20.64</cite>. Here <span lang="grc">εὖ πεποίηκεν</span> is almost equivalent to a single notion.

##### <a id="s1654"></a> §1654

The addition of a preposition to a verb may have no effect on the construction, as in <span lang="grc">ἐκβῆναι τῆς νεώς</span>, whereas <span lang="grc">βῆναι τῆς νεώς</span> originally, and still in poetry, can mean _go from-the-ship;_ or it may determine the construction, as in <span lang="grc">περιγενέσθαι ἐμοῦ</span> _to surpass me_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.236">D. 18.236</cite>. Prose tends to repeat the prefixed preposition: <span lang="grc">ἐκβῆναι ἐκ τῆς νεώς</span> <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.137">T. 1.137</cite>.

##### <a id="s1655"></a> §1655

A preposition usually assumes the force of an adjective when compounded with substantives which do not change their forms on entering into composition, as <span lang="grc">σύνοδος</span> _a national meeting_ (<span lang="grc">ὁδός</span>). Otherwise the compound usually gets a new termination, generally <span lang="grc">-ον, -ιον</span> neuter, or <span lang="grc">-ίς</span> feminine, as <span lang="grc">ἐνύπνιον</span> _dream_ (<span lang="grc">ὕπνος</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐπιγουνίς</span> _thigh-muscle_ (<span lang="grc">γόνυ</span>).

##### <a id="s1656"></a> §1656

The use of prepositions is, in general, more common in prose than in poetry, which retained the more primitive form of expression.

##### <a id="s1657"></a> §1657

A noun joined by a preposition to its case without the help of a verb has a verbal meaning: <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ πᾱσῶν ἀρχῶν ἐλευθερίᾱ</span> _freedom from all rule_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 698a">P. L. 698a</cite> (cp. <span lang="grc">ἐλευθεροῦν ἀπό τινος</span>).

##### <a id="s1658"></a> §1658

In general, when depending on prepositions expressing relations of place, the accusative denotes the place (or person) _toward which_ or the place _over which, along which_ motion takes place, the dative denotes _rest in_ <!-- page 368 --> or _at_, the genitive (ablative) _passing from_. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἥκω παρὰ σέ</span> _I have come to you_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.137">T. 1.137</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ παρ᾽ ἑαυτῷ βάρβαροι</span> _the barbarians in his own service_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.5">X. A. 1.1.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">παρὰ βασιλέως πολλοὶ πρὸς Κῦρον ἀπῆλθον</span> _many came over from the king to Cyrus_ 1. 9. 29. The true genitive denotes various forms of connection.

##### <a id="s1659"></a> §1659

**Constructio Praegnans.**—**a.** A verb of motion is often used with a preposition with the dative to anticipate the rest that follows the action of the verb: <span lang="grc">ἐν τῷ ποταμῷ ἔπεσον</span> _they fell_ (into and were) _in the river_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ages. 1.32">X. Ages. 1.32</cite>. This use is common with <span lang="grc">τιθέναι, ἱδρύ̄ειν, καθιστάναι</span>, etc., and with tenses of completed action which imply rest; as <span lang="grc">οἱ ἐν τῇ νήσῳ ἄνδρες διαβεβηκότες</span> _the men who had crossed to_ (and were in) _the island_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.71">T. 7.71</cite>.

**b.** A verb of rest is often followed by a preposition with the accusative to denote motion previous to or following upon the action of the verb: <span lang="grc">παρῆσαν εἰς Σάρδεις</span> (they came to Sardis and were in the city) _they arrived at Sardis_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.2">X. A. 1.2.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐς Κῠ̄ρήνην ἐσώθησαν</span> _they were saved by reaching Cyrene_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.110">T. 1.110</cite>, <span lang="grc">ᾑρέθη πρεσβευτὴς εἰς Λακεδαίμονα</span> _he was chosen ambassador_ (to go) _to Lacedaemon_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.2.17">X. H. 2.2.17</cite>. Cp. [1692](#s1692). 1. a.

##### <a id="s1660"></a> §1660

Stress is often laid on (a) the starting-point or (b) the goal of an action.

**a.** <span lang="grc">καταδήσᾱς ἀπὸ δένδρων τοὺς ἵππους</span> _tying his horses to_ (from) _trees_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.4.10">X. H. 4.4.10</cite>. By anticipation of the verbal action (attraction of the prep. with the article): <span lang="grc">τὴν ἀπὸ στρατοπέδου τάξιν ἔλιπεν</span> _he deserted his post in the army_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.159">Aes. 3.159</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ ἐκ τῆς ἀγορᾶς καταλιπόντες τὰ ὤνια ἔφυγον</span> _the market-people_ (<span lang="grc">οἱ ἐν τῇ ἀγορᾷ</span>) _left their wares and fled_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.18">X. A. 1.2.18</cite>.

**b.** With _verbs of collecting_ (<span lang="grc">ἀθροίζειν, συλλέγειν</span>) and _enrolling_ (<span lang="grc">ἐγγράφειν</span>): <span lang="grc">εἰς πεδίον ἀθροίζονται</span> _they are mustered in_(_to_) _the plain_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.2">X. A. 1.1.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰς ἄνδρας ἐγγράψαι</span> _to enrol in_(_to_) _the list of men_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.230">D. 19.230</cite>.

##### <a id="s1661"></a> §1661

So with adverbs: <span lang="grc">ὅπον ἐληλύθαμεν</span> _where_ ( = _whither_, <span lang="grc">ὅποι</span>) _we have gone_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.1.14">X. C. 6.1.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅθεν ἀπελίπομεν, ἐπανέλθωμεν</span> _let us return to the point whence_ ( = _where_, <span lang="grc">ὅπου</span>) _we left off_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 78b">P. Ph. 78b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀγνοεῖ τὸν ἐκεῖθεν πόλεμον δεῦρο ἥξοντα</span> _he does not know that the war in that region will come hither_ ( = <span lang="grc">τὸν ἐκεῖ πόλεμον ἐκεῖθεν</span>) <cite data-ref="Dem. 1.15">D. 1.15</cite>.

##### <a id="s1662"></a> §1662

Some adverbs and adverbial phrases meaning _from_ are used with reference to the point of view of the observer: <span lang="grc">ἑκατέρωθεν</span> _on either side_, <span lang="grc">ἔνθεν καὶ ἔνθεν</span> _on this side and that_, <span lang="grc">ἐκ δεξιᾶς</span> _on the right_ (_a dextra_), <span lang="grc">οἱ ἀπὸ τῆς σκηνῆς</span> _the actors_, <span lang="grc">τὸ ἐκ τοῦ ἰσθμοῦ τεῖχος, τὸ ἐς τὴν Παλλήνην τεῖχος</span> _the wall_ (seen) _from the isthmus, the wall toward_ (looking to) _Pallene_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.64">T. 1.64</cite> (of the same wall).

##### <a id="s1663"></a> §1663

**Position**.—The preposition usually precedes its noun. It may be separated from it

**a.** By particles (<span lang="grc">μέν, δέ, γέ, τέ, γάρ, οὖν</span>) and by <span lang="grc">οἶμαι</span> _I think:_ <span lang="grc">ἐν οὖν τῇ πόλει</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 456d">P. R. 456d</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰς δέ γε οἶμαι τὰ̄ς ἄλλᾱς πόλεις</span> _to the other cities I think_ 568 c.

Note that the order <span lang="grc">τὴν μὲν χώρᾱν</span> ([1155](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1155)) usually becomes, e.g. <span lang="grc">πρὸς μὲν τὴν χώρᾱν</span> or <span lang="grc">πρὸς τὴν χώρᾱν μέν</span>. Demonstrative <span lang="grc">ὁ μέν</span> and <span lang="grc">ὁ δέ</span>, when dependent on a preposition, regularly follow the preposition, and usually with order reversed ([1109](16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1109)): <span lang="grc">ἐν μὲν ἄρα τοῖς συμφωνοῦμεν, ἐν δὲ τοῖς οὔ</span> _in some things then we agree_, _but not in others_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 263b">P. Phae. 263b</cite>. <!-- page 369 -->

**b.** By attributives: <span lang="grc">εἰς Καΰστρου πεδίον</span> _to the plain of the Cayster_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.11">X. A. 1.2.11</cite>.

**c.** By the accusative in oaths and entreaties (with <span lang="grc">πρός</span>): <span lang="grc">πρός σε τῆσδε μητρός</span> _by my mother here_ I implore _thee_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Phoen. 1665">E. Phoen. 1665</cite>; cp. _per te deos oro_ and see [1599](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1599).

N.—A preposition is usually placed before a superlative and after <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> or <span lang="grc">ὅτι</span> qualifying the superlative: <span lang="grc">ὡς ἐπὶ πλεῖστον τοῦ ὁμί̄λου</span> _over the very greatest part of the throng_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.34">T. 2.34</cite>. <span lang="grc">πολύ, πάνυ, μάλα</span> may precede the preposition and its case: <span lang="grc">πολὺ ἐν πλείονι αἰτίᾳ</span> _with far better reason_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.35">T. 1.35</cite>.

##### <a id="s1664"></a> §1664

In poetry a preposition is often placed between an adjective and its substantive; very rarely in prose (<span lang="grc">τοιᾷδε ἐν τάξει</span> _in the following manner_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Criti. 115c">P. Criti. 115c</cite>).

##### <a id="s1665"></a> §1665

<span lang="grc">περί</span> is the only true preposition that may be placed after its case in Attic prose: <span lang="grc">σοφίᾱς πέρι</span> _about wisdom_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phileb. 49a">P. Phil. 49a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὧν ἐγὼ οὐδὲν οὔτε μέγα οὔτε μῑκρὸν πέρι ἐπαΐω</span> _about which I understand nothing either much or little_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 19c">P. A. 19c</cite>. When used with two substantives <span lang="grc">πέρι</span> is placed between them: <span lang="grc">τοῦ ὁσίου τε πέρι καὶ τοῦ ἀνοσίου</span> _concerning both that which is holy and that which is unholy_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyph. 4e">P. Euth. 4e</cite>. <span lang="grc">πέρι</span> occurs very often in Plato, only once in the orators and possibly twice in Xenophon. On anastrophe, see [175](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/07-accent-general-principles.md#s175).

**a.** <span lang="grc">ἕνεκα</span> and <span lang="grc">χάριν</span> (usually) and <span lang="grc">ἄνευ</span> (sometimes) are postpositive. The retention of the postpositive use of <span lang="grc">περί</span> may be due to the influence of <span lang="grc">ἕνεκα</span>. In poetry many prepositions are postpositive.

## VARIATION OF PREPOSITIONS

##### <a id="s1666"></a> §1666

The preposition in the second of two closely connected clauses may be different from that used in the first clause either (1) when the relation is essentially the same or (2) when it is different. Thus (1) <span lang="grc">ἔκ τε τῆς Κερκύ̄ρᾱς καὶ ἀπὸ τῆς ἠπείρου</span> _from Corcyra and the mainland_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.33">T. 7.33</cite>, and (2) <span lang="grc">οὔτε κατὰ γῆν οὔτε διὰ θαλάσσης</span> _neither by land nor by_ (the help of the, the medium of the) _sea_ 1. 2. Cp. [1668](#s1668).

## REPETITION AND OMISSION OF PREPOSITIONS, ETC.

##### <a id="s1667"></a> §1667

**a.** For the sake of emphasis or to mark opposition and difference, a preposition is repeated with each noun dependent on the preposition: <span lang="grc">κατά τε πόλεμον καὶ κατὰ τὴν ἄλλην δίαιταν</span> _in the pursuit of war and in the other occupations of life_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Tim. 18c">P. Tim. 18c</cite>.

**b.** A preposition is used with the first noun and omitted with the second when the two nouns (whether similar or dissimilar in meaning) unite to form a complex: <span lang="grc">περὶ τοῦ δικαίου καὶ ἀρετῆς</span> ‘_concerning the justice of our cause and the honesty of our intentions_’ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.10">T. 3.10</cite>.

**c.** In poetry a preposition may be used only with the second of two nouns dependent on it: <span lang="grc">Δελφῶν κἀ̄πὸ Δαυλίᾱς</span> _from Delphi and Daulia_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 734">S. O. T. 734</cite>.

##### <a id="s1668"></a> §1668

In contrasts or alternatives expressed by <span lang="grc">ἤ, ἢ . . . ἤ, καὶ . . . καί</span>, etc., the preposition may be repeated or omitted with the second noun: <span lang="grc">καὶ κατὰ γῆν καὶ κατὰ θάλατταν</span> _both by land and by sea_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.7">X. A. 1.1.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρὸς ἐχθρὸν ἢ φίλον</span> _to foe or friend_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.114">D. 21.114</cite>.

##### <a id="s1669"></a> §1669

When prepositions of different meaning are used with the same noun, <!-- page 370 --> the noun is repeated; thus _neither upon_ (_the earth_) _nor under the earth_ is <span lang="grc">οὔτ᾽ ἐπὶ γῆς οὔθ᾽ ὑπὸ γῆς</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Menex. 246d">P. Menex. 246d</cite>.

##### <a id="s1670"></a> §1670

In explanatory appositional clauses ([988](10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s988)) the preposition may be repeated for the sake of clearness or emphasis; as <span lang="grc">ἐκ τούτων οἱ ὀνομαστοὶ γίγνονται, ἐκ τῶν ἐπιτηδευσάντων ἕκαστα</span> _the men of mark come from those who have practised each art_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lach. 183c">P. Lach. 183c</cite>, and commonly after demonstratives. The preposition is not repeated when such an appositional clause is closely connected with what precedes: <span lang="grc">εἰκὸς μηδὲ νομίσαι περὶ ἑνὸς μόνου, δουλείᾱς ἀντ᾽ ἐλευθερίᾱς, ἀγωνίζεσθαι</span> _nor should you think that you are contending for a single issue alone:_ to avert _slavery instead of_ maintaining your _freedom_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.63">T. 2.63</cite>. A preposition is usually not repeated before descriptive appositional clauses ([987](10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s987)): <span lang="grc">περὶ χρημάτων λαλεῖς, ἀβεβαίου πρά̄γματος</span> _you are talking about wealth, an unstable thing_ Com. frag. 3. 38 (No. [128](../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/03-euphony-of-consonants.md#s128)).

##### <a id="s1671"></a> §1671

Before a relative in the same case as a noun or pronoun dependent on a preposition, the preposition is usually omitted: <span lang="grc">κατὰ ταύτην τὴν ἡλικίᾱν ἦν ἣν ἐγὼ νῦν</span> _he was at that age at which I now am_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.155">D. 21.155</cite>, <span lang="grc">φιλεῖται ὑπὸ ὧν</span> ( = <span lang="grc">τούτων ὧν</span>) <span lang="grc">φιλεῖται</span> _is loved by whom it is loved_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyph. 10c">P. Euth. 10c</cite>. But the preposition is repeated if the relative precedes: <span lang="grc">πρὸς ὅ τις πέφῡκε, πρὸς τοῦτο ἕνα πρὸς ἓν ἕκαστον ἔργον δεῖ κομίζειν</span> _it is necessary to set each individual to some one work to which he is adapted by nature_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 423d">P. R. 423d</cite>.

##### <a id="s1672"></a> §1672

In Plato a preposition is often omitted in replies: <span lang="grc">ἡττώμενοσ—ὑπὸ τίνος; φήσει. τοῦ ἀγαθοῦ, φήσομεν</span> _overcome—by what? he will say. By the good_, _we shall say_ Pr. 355 c.

##### <a id="s1673"></a> §1673

The preposition is usually omitted with the main noun or pronoun when it is used in a clause of comparison with <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> (rarely <span lang="grc">ὥσπερ</span>) _as:_ <span lang="grc">δεῖ ὡς περὶ μητρὸς καὶ τροφοῦ τῆς χώρᾱς βουλεύεσθαι</span> _they ought to take thought for their country as their mother and nurse_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 414e">P. R. 414e</cite>; so, usually, when the two members are closely united: <span lang="grc">ὡς πρὸς εἰδότ᾽ ἐμὲ σὺ τἀ̄ληθῆ λέγε</span> _speak the truth to me as to one who knows_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Lys. 993">Ar. Lys. 993</cite>. The preposition is often omitted in the clause with <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> (<span lang="grc">ὥσπερ</span>) _as_, <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> _than:_ <span lang="grc">οἳ παρ᾽ οὐδὲν οὕτως ὡς τὸ τοιαῦτα ποιεῖν ἀπολώλᾱσιν</span> _who owe their ruin to nothing so much as to such a course of action_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.263">D. 19.263</cite>, <span lang="grc">περὶ τοῦ μέλλοντος μᾶλλον βουλεύεσθαι ἢ τοῦ παρόντος</span> _to deliberate about the future rather than the present_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.44">T. 3.44</cite>.

##### <a id="s1674"></a> §1674

A preposition with its case may have the function of the subject, or the object, of a sentence; or it may represent the protasis of a condition.

Subject: <span lang="grc">ἔφυγον περὶ ὀκτακοσίους</span> _about eight hundred took to flight_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.5.10">X. H. 6.5.10</cite>; (gen. absol.) <span lang="grc">συνειλεγμένων περὶ ἑπτακοσίους, λαβὼν αὐτοὺς καταβαίνει</span> _when about seven hundred had been collected he marched down with them_ 2. 4. 5. Object: <span lang="grc">διέφθειραν ἐς ὀκτακοσίους</span> _they killed about eight hundred_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.32">T. 7.32</cite>. Protasis: <span lang="grc">ἐπεὶ διά γ᾽ ὑ̄μᾶς αὐτοὺς πάλαι ἂν ἀπωλώλειτε</span> _for had it depended on your selves you would have perished long ago_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.49">D. 18.49</cite> (cp. [2344](29-adverbial-complex-sentences-2193-2487/10-classification-of-conditional-sentences.md#s2344)).

## ORDINARY USES OF THE PREPOSITIONS

##### <a id="s1675"></a> §1675

**Use of the Prepositions in Attic Prose**.—

With the accusative only: <span lang="grc">ἀνά, εἰς</span>. <!-- page 371 -->

With the dative only: <span lang="grc">ἐν, σύν</span>.

With the genitive only: <span lang="grc">ἀντί, ἀπό, ἐξ, πρό</span>.

With the accusative and genitive: <span lang="grc">ἀμφί, διά, κατά, μετά, ὑπέρ</span>.

With accusative, genitive, and dative: <span lang="grc">ἐπί, παρά, περί, πρός, ὑπό</span>.

**a.** With the dative are also used in poetry: <span lang="grc">ἀνά, ἀμφί</span> (also in Hdt.), <span lang="grc">μετά. ἀπό</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀπύ</span>), <span lang="grc">ἐξ</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐς</span>) take the dative in Arcadian and Cyprian.

**b.** The genitive is either the genitive proper (of the goal, 1349, 1350, etc.) or the ablatival genitive.

**c.** The dative is usually the locative or the instrumental, rarely the dative proper (as with <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span> and <span lang="grc">πρός</span> of the goal).

##### <a id="s1676"></a> §1676

**Ordinary Differences in Meaning**.—

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | GENITIVE | ACCUSATIVE |
| <span lang="grc">ἀμφί, περί</span> | _concerning_ | _round about, near_ |
| <span lang="grc">διά</span> | _through_ | _owing to_ |
| <span lang="grc">κατά</span> | _against_ | _along_, _over_, _according to_ |
| <span lang="grc">μετά</span> | _with_ | _after_ |
| <span lang="grc">ὑπέρ</span> | _above_, _in behalf of_ | _over_, _beyond_ |

|  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | GENITIVE | DATIVE | ACCUSATIVE |
| <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span> | _on_ | _on_ | _to_, _toward_, _for_ |
| <span lang="grc">παρά</span> | _from_ | _with_, _near_ | _to_, _contrary to_ |
| <span lang="grc">πρός</span> | _on the side of_ | _at_, _besides_ | _to_, _toward_ |
| <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> | _by_, _under_ | _under_ | _under_ |

##### <a id="s1677"></a> §1677

Certain prepositions are parallel in many uses; e.g. <span lang="grc">ἀνά</span> and <span lang="grc">κατά, ἀντί</span> and <span lang="grc">πρό, ἀπό</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐκ, ἀμφί</span> and <span lang="grc">περί, ὑπέρ</span> and <span lang="grc">περί, ἐπί</span> and <span lang="grc">πρός, σύν</span> and <span lang="grc">μετά</span>.

##### <a id="s1678"></a> §1678

The _agent_ is expressed by different prepositions with the genitive:

<span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> of persons and things personified ([1698](#s1698). 1. N. 1): the normal usage in Attic prose.

<span lang="grc">παρά</span>: here the agent is viewed as the source. The action is viewed as starting near a person, or _on the part of_ a person.

<span lang="grc">διά</span> _through:_ the intermediate agent.

<span lang="grc">ἀπό</span>: indirect agent and source (rare) to mark the point of departure of the action. Chiefly in Thuc.

<span lang="grc">ἐξ</span>: chiefly in poetry and Hdt. In Attic prose of emanation from a source.

<span lang="grc">πρός</span>: to mark the result as due to the _presence_ (_before_) of a person; chiefly in poetry and Hdt.

##### <a id="s1679"></a> §1679

_Means_ is expressed by <span lang="grc">διά</span> with the genitive (the normal usage in Attic prose), <span lang="grc">ἀπό, ἐξ, ἐν, σύν</span>. _Motive_ is expressed by <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> (gen.), <span lang="grc">διά</span> (accus.), <span lang="grc">ἕνεκα</span>.

##### <a id="s1680"></a> §1680

Prepositions in composition (chiefly <span lang="grc">ἀπό, διά, κατά, σύν</span>) may give an idea of completion to the action denoted by the verb ([1648](#s1648)).

**a.** For the usage after compound verbs see [1382](18-the-cases/04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1382) ff., 1545 ff., 1559.

## LIST OF PREPOSITIONS

### <span lang="grc">ἀμφί</span>

##### <a id="s1681"></a> §1681

<span lang="grc"> **ἀμφί** </span> (cp. <span lang="grc">ἄμφω, ἀμφότερος</span>, Lat. _ambi-_, _amb-_, _am-_) originally <!-- page 372 --> _on both sides_ (either externally only, or inside and outside), hence _about_. Cp. the use of <span lang="grc">περί</span> ([1693](#s1693)) throughout. Chiefly poetic, Ionic, and Xenophontic. In Attic prose chiefly with the accusative.

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **ἀμφί** </span> _with the Genitive_

Local (very rare and doubtful): <span lang="grc">οἱ ἀμφὶ ταύτης οἰκέοντες τῆς πόλιος</span> _dwellers round about this city_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 8.104">Hdt. 8.104</cite> (only here). Cause: _about_, _concerning:_ <span lang="grc">ἀμφὶ σῆς λέγω παιδός</span> _I speak about thy child_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hec. 580">E. Hec. 580</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀμφὶ ὧν εἶχον διαφερόμενοι</span> _quarrelling about what they had_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.5.17">X. A. 4.5.17</cite>.

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **ἀμφί** </span> _with the Dative_

Local: <span lang="grc">ἀμφ᾽ ὤμοισιν ἔχει σάκος</span> _he has a shield about his shoulders_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 11.527"><span lang="grc">Λ</span> 527</cite>. Cause: <span lang="grc">φοβηθεὶς ἀμφὶ τῇ γυναικί</span> _afraid on account of his wife_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 6.62">Hdt. 6.62</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀμφὶ φόβῳ</span> _by reason of_ (encompassed by) _terror_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Orest. 825">E. Or. 825</cite>; Means: <span lang="grc">ἀμφὶ σοφίᾳ</span> ‘_with the environment of poetic art_’ Pind. P. 1. 12. Often in Pindar.

#### 3. <span lang="grc"> **ἀμφί** </span> _with the Accusative_

Local: <span lang="grc">ἀμφὶ Μί̄λητον</span> _about Miletus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.3">X. A. 1.2.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔδραμον ἀμφ᾽ Ἀχιλῆα</span> _they ran around Achilles_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 18.30"><span lang="grc">Σ</span> 30</cite>; temporal: <span lang="grc">ἀμφὶ δείλην</span> _towards evening_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.2.14">X. A. 2.2.14</cite>. Number: <span lang="grc">ἀμφὶ τούς δισχῑλίους</span> _about two thousand_ 1. 2. 9; of occupation with an object: <span lang="grc">ἀμφὶ δεῖπνον εἶχεν</span> _he was busy about dinner_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.5.44">X. C. 5.5.44</cite>.

**a.** <span lang="grc">οἱ ἀμφί τινα</span> the attendants, followers of a person, or the person himself with his attendants, etc.: <span lang="grc">ἀνὴρ τῶν ἀμφὶ Κῦρον πιστῶν</span> _one of the trusty adherents of Cyrus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.1">X. A. 1.8.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ ἀμφὶ Χειρίσοφον</span> _Chirisophus and his men_ 4. 3. 21, <span lang="grc">οἱ ἀμφὶ Πρωταγόρᾱν</span> _the school of Protagoras_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 170c">P. Th. 170c</cite>. This last phrase contains the only use of <span lang="grc">ἀμφί</span> in Attic prose outside of Xenophon.

#### 4. <span lang="grc"> **ἀμφί** </span> _in Composition_

_Around_, _about_' <span lang="grc">ἀμφιβάλλειν</span> _throw around_ (_on both sides_), <span lang="grc">ἀμφιλέγειν</span> _dispute_ (_speak on both sides_).

### <span lang="grc">ἀνά</span>

##### <a id="s1682"></a> §1682

<span lang="grc"> **ἀνά** </span> (Lesb. <span lang="grc">ὀν</span>, Lat. _an-_ in _anhelare_, Eng. _on_): originally _up to_, _up_ (opposed to <span lang="grc">κατά</span>). Cp. <span lang="grc">ἄνω</span>.

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **ἀνά** </span> _with the Dative_

Local only (Epic, Lyric, and in tragic choruses): <span lang="grc">ἀνὰ σκήπτρῳ</span> _upon a staff_ A 15.

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **ἀνά** </span> _with the Accusative_

_Up along; over_, _through_, _among_ (of horizontal motion). Usually avoided by Attic prose writers except Xenophon (three times in the orators).

**a.** Local: To a higher point: <span lang="grc">ἀνὰ τὸν ποταμόν</span> _up stream_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.194">Hdt. 1.194</cite> (cp. <span lang="grc">κατὰ τὸν ποταμόν</span>). Extension: <span lang="grc">ἀνὰ στρατόν</span> _through the camp_ A 10, <span lang="grc">ἀνὰ πᾶσαν τὴν γῆν</span> _over the whole earth_ X. Ag. 11. 16, <span lang="grc">βασιλῆας ἀνὰ στόμ᾽ ἔχων</span> _having kings in thy mouth_ B 250 (cp. <span lang="grc">διὰ στόματος ἔχειν</span>).

**b.** Extension in Time: <span lang="grc">ἀνὰ νύκτα</span> _through the night_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 14.80"><span lang="grc">Ξ</span> 80</cite>. See c.

**c.** Other relations: Distributively: <span lang="grc">ἀνὰ ἑκατὸν ἄνδρας</span> _by hundreds_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.4.21">X. A. 3.4.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀνὰ πᾶσαν ἡμέρᾱν</span> _daily_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.2.8">X. C. 1.2.8</cite>. Manner: <span lang="grc">ἀνὰ κράτος</span> _with all their might_ (up to their strength) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.10.15">X. A. 1.10.15</cite> (better Attic <span lang="grc">κατὰ κράτος</span>), <span lang="grc">ἀνὰ λόγον</span> _proportionately_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 110d">P. Ph. 110d</cite>.

#### 3. <span lang="grc"> **ἀνά** </span> _in Composition_

<!-- page 373 -->

_Up_ (<span lang="grc">ἀνίστασθαι</span> _stand up_, <span lang="grc">ἀναστρέφειν</span> _turn upside down_), _back_ (<span lang="grc">ἀναχωρεῖν</span> _go back_, <span lang="grc">ἀναμιμνῄσκειν</span> _remind_), _again_ (<span lang="grc">ἀναπνεῖν</span> _breathe again_, <span lang="grc">ἀναπειρᾶσθαι</span> _practise constantly_), often with a reversing force force (<span lang="grc">ἀναλύ̄ειν</span> _unlcose_).

### <span lang="grc">ἀντί</span>

##### <a id="s1683"></a> §1683

<span lang="grc"> **ἀντί** </span>: originally _in the face of, opposite to;_ cp. <span lang="grc">ἄντα, ἐναντίος</span>, Lat. _ante_ (with meaning influenced by _post_), Germ. Antwort, ‘reply.’

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **ἀντί** </span> _with the Genitive only_

Local: <span lang="grc">ἀνθ᾽ ὧν ἑστηκότες</span> _standing opposite to_ (from the point of view of the speaker, i.e. _behind_) _which_ (pine-trees) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.7.6">X. A. 4.7.6</cite>. In other meanings: _Instead of, for_, _as an equivalent to:_ <span lang="grc">ἀντὶ πολέμου εἰρήνη</span> _peace instead of war_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.20">T. 4.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ παρ᾽ ἐμοὶ ἑλέσθαι ἀντὶ τῶν οἴκοι</span> _to prefer what I have to offer you here instead of what you have left at home_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.4">X. A. 1.7.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὴν τελευτὴν ἀντὶ τῆς τῶν ζώντων σωτηρίᾱς ἠλλάξαντο</span> _they exchanged death for the safety of the living_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Menex. 237a">P. Menex. 237a</cite>; _in return for_, hence <span lang="grc">ἀνθ᾽ ὅτου</span> _wherefore_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 585">S. El. 585</cite>; for <span lang="grc">πρός</span> in entreaty: <span lang="grc">σ᾽ ἀντὶ παίδων τῶνδε ἱκετεύομεν</span> _we entreat thee by these children here_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 1326">S. O. C. 1326</cite>.

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **ἀντί** </span> _in Composition_

_Instead_, _in return_ (<span lang="grc">ἀντιδιδόναι</span> _give in return_), _against_, _in opposition to_ (<span lang="grc">ἀντιλέγειν</span> _speak against_).

### <span lang="grc">ἀπό</span>

##### <a id="s1684"></a> §1684

<span lang="grc"> **ἀπό** </span> (Lesb. etc. <span lang="grc">ἀπύ</span>) _from_, _off_, _away from;_ originally of separation and departure. Cp. Lat. _ab_, Eng. _off_, _of_.

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **ἀπό** </span> _with the Genitive only_

**a.** Local: <span lang="grc">καταπηδήσᾱς ἀπὸ τοῦ ἵππου</span> _leaping down from his horse_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.28">X. A. 1.8.28</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐθήρευεν ἀπὸ ἵππου</span> _he used to hunt_ (from a horse) _on horseback_ 1. 2. 7, <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ θαλάσσης</span> _at a distance from the sea_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.7">T. 1.7</cite>. Figuratively: <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ θεῶν ἀρχόμενοι</span> _beginning with the gods_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.3.18">X. A. 6.3.18</cite>.

**b.** Temporal: <span lang="grc">ἀφ᾽ ἑσπέρᾱς</span> _after evening began_ (after sundown) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.3.23">X. A. 6.3.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ τοῦ αὐτοῦ σημείου</span> _on the same signal_ 2. 5. 32, <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ τῶν σί̄των</span> _after meals_ X. R. <cite data-ref="Lys. 5.8">L. 5.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀφ᾽ οὗ</span> _since_.

**c.** Other relations: (1) Origin, Source: in prose of more remote ancestry: <span lang="grc">τοὺς μὲν ἀπὸ θεῶν, τοὺς δ᾽ ἐξ αὐτῶν τῶν θεῶν γεγονότας</span> _some descended_ (remotely) _from gods, others begotten_ (directly) _of the gods themselves_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 12.81">I. 12.81</cite>. (This distinction is not always observed.) Various other relations may be explained as source.

(2) Author: as agent with passives and intransitives, when an action is done indirectly, through the influence of the agent (<span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> of the direct action of the agent himself). Not common, except in Thuc. (chiefly with <span lang="grc">πρἀ̄ττεσθαι, λέγεσθαι</span>, and verbs of like meaning): <span lang="grc">ἐπρά̄χθη ἀπ᾽ αὐτῶν οὐδὲν ἔργον</span> _nothing was done under their rule_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.17">T. 1.17</cite>. The startingpoint of an action is often emphasized rather than the agent: <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ πολλῶν καὶ πρὸς πολλοὺς λόγοι γιγνόμενοι</span> _speeches made by many and to many_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.93">T. 8.93</cite>. <!-- page 374 -->

(3) Cause (remote): <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ τούτου τοῦ τολμήματος ἐπῃνέθη</span> _he was praised in consequence of this bold deed_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.25">T. 2.25</cite>, <span lang="grc">ταῦτα οὐκ ἀπὸ τύχης ἐγίγνετο, ἀλλ᾽ ἀπὸ παρασκευῆς τῆς ἐμῆς</span> _this happened not from chance but by reason of the preparations I made_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 21.10">L. 21.10</cite>.

(4) Means, Instrument: <span lang="grc">στράτευμα συνέλεξεν ἀπὸ χρημάτων</span> _he raised an army by means of money_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.9">X. A. 1.1.9</cite>; rarely of persons: <span lang="grc">ἀπ᾽ αὐτῶν βλάψαι</span> _to do injury by means of them_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.29">T. 7.29</cite>.

(5) Manner: <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ τοῦ προφανοῦς</span> _openly_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.66">T. 1.66</cite>.

(6) Conformity: <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ τοῦ ἴσου</span> _on a basis of equality_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.10">T. 3.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ ξυμμαχίᾱς αὐτόνομοι</span> _independent by virtue of_ (_according to_) _an alliance_ 7. 57.

N.—<span lang="grc">ἀπό</span> with gen. is sometimes preferred to the simple gen., often for emphasis: <span lang="grc">οἱ λόγοι ἀφ᾽ ὑ̄μῶν</span> _the words that proceed from you_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.40">T. 6.40</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὀλίγοι ἀπὸ πολλῶν</span> _a few of the many_ 1. 110 (cp. [1317](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1317) a). Thuc. has many free uses of <span lang="grc">ἀπό</span>.

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **ἀπό** </span> _in Composition_

_From_, _away_, _off_ (<span lang="grc">ἀπιέναι</span> _go away_, <span lang="grc">ἀποτειχίζειν</span> _wall off_), _in return, back_ (<span lang="grc">ἀποδιδόναι</span> _give back_ what is due, <span lang="grc">ἀπαιτεῖν</span> _demand what is one's right_). Separation involves completion (hence <span lang="grc">ἀπανᾱλίσκειν</span> _utterly consume_, <span lang="grc">ἀποθύ̄ειν</span> _pay off a vow_), or privation and negation (<span lang="grc">ἀπαγορεύειν</span> _forbid_, <span lang="grc">ἀποτυγχάνειν</span> _miss_). Often almost equivalent to an intensive (<span lang="grc">ἀποφάναι</span> _speak out_, <span lang="grc">ἀποδεικνύναι</span> _point out_, <span lang="grc">ἀποτολμᾶν</span> _dare without reserve_).

### <span lang="grc">διά</span>

##### <a id="s1685"></a> §1685

<span lang="grc"> **διά** </span> (Lesb. <span lang="grc">ζά</span>) _through_, originally _through and out of_, and _apart_ (separation by cleavage), a force seen in comp. (cp. Lat. _dis-_, Germ. _zwi-schen_).

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **διά** </span> _with the Genitive_

**a.** Local: _through and out of_ (cp. Hom. <span lang="grc">διέκ, διαπρό</span>), as <span lang="grc">δι᾽ ὤμου ἔγχος ἦλθεν</span> _the spear went clear through his shoulder_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 4.481"><span lang="grc">Δ</span> 481</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀκοῦσαι διὰ τέλους</span> _to listen from beginning to end_ Lyc. 16. _Through_, but not _out of:_ <span lang="grc">διὰ πολεμίᾱς</span> (<span lang="grc">γῆς</span>) <span lang="grc">πορεύεσθαι</span> _to march through the enemy's country_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hiero 2.8">X. Hi. 2.8</cite> and often in figurative expressions: <span lang="grc">διὰ χειρὸς ἔχειν</span> _to control_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.13">T. 2.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">διὰ στόματος ἔχειν</span> _to have in one's mouth_ (_be always talking of_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.25">X. C. 1.4.25</cite> (also <span lang="grc">ἀνὰ στόμα</span>).

**b.** Temporal: of uninterrupted duration, as <span lang="grc">διὰ νυκτός</span> _through the night_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.6.22">X. A. 4.6.22</cite>, <span lang="grc">διὰ παντός</span> _constantly_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.49">T. 2.49</cite>.

**c.** Intervals of Space or Time: <span lang="grc">διὰ δέκα ἐπάλξεων</span> _at intervals of ten battlements_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.21">T. 3.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">διὰ χρόνου</span> _after an interval_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 1.12">L. 1.12</cite>, _intermittently_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.220">Aes. 3.220</cite>, <span lang="grc">διὰ πολλοῦ</span> _at a long distance_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.94">T. 3.94</cite>.

**d.** Other relations: Means, Mediation (_per_): <span lang="grc">αὐτὸς δι᾽ ἑαυτοῦ</span> _ipse per se_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 48.15">D. 48.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">διὰ τούτου γράμματα πέμψᾱς</span> _sending a letter by this man_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.162">Aes. 3.162</cite>. State or feeling: with <span lang="grc">εἶναι, γίγνεσθαι, ἔχειν</span>, of a property or quality: <span lang="grc">διὰ φόβου εἰσί</span> _they are afraid_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.34">T. 6.34</cite>, <span lang="grc">δι᾽ ἡσυχίᾱς εἶχεν</span> _he kept in quiet_ 2. 22, <span lang="grc">ἐλθεῖν ἡμῖν διὰ μάχης</span> _to meet us in battle_ 2. 11, <span lang="grc">αὐτοῖς διὰ φιλίᾱς ἰέναι</span> _to enter into friendship with them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.8">X. A. 3.2.8</cite>. Manner: <span lang="grc">διὰ ταχέων</span> _quickly_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.8">T. 4.8</cite>.

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **διά** </span> _with the Accusative_

**a.** Local: of space traversed, _through_, _over_ (Epic, Lyric, tragic choruses): <span lang="grc">διὰ δώματα</span> _through the halls_ A 600; <span lang="grc">διὰ νύκτα Θ</span> 510 is quasi-temporal. <!-- page 375 -->

**b.** Cause: _owing to, thanks to, on account of, in consequence of_ (cp. _propter_, _ob_): <span lang="grc">διὰ τοὺς θεοὺς ἐσῳζόμην</span> _I was saved thanks to the gods_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.249">D. 18.249</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῑμώμενος μὴ δι᾽ ἑαυτόν, ὰλλὰ διὰ δόξαν προγόνων</span> _honoured_, _not for himself, but on account of the renown of his ancestors_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Menex. 247b">P. Menex. 247b</cite>. So in <span lang="grc">εἰ μὴ διά τινα</span> (<span lang="grc">τι</span>) _had it not been for_ in statements of an (unsurmounted) obstacle: <span lang="grc">φαίνονται κρατήσαντες ἂν τῶν βασιλέως πρᾱγμάτων, εἰ μὴ διὰ Κῦρον</span> _it seems they would have got the better of the power of the king, had it not been for Cyrus_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 5.92">I. 5.92</cite>.

**c.** <span lang="grc">διά</span> is rarely used (in place of <span lang="grc">ἕνεκα</span>) to denote a purpose or object: <span lang="grc">διὰ τὴν σφετέρᾱν δόξαν</span> _for the sake of their honour_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.89">T. 2.89</cite>, <span lang="grc">δι᾽ ἐπήρειαν</span> _for spite_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 39.32">D. 39.32</cite> (cp. <span lang="grc">διὰ νόσον ἕνεκα ὑγιείᾱς</span> _on account of disease in order to gain health_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lysis 218e">P. Lys. 218e</cite>).

**d.** <span lang="grc">διά</span> with gen. is used of direct, <span lang="grc">διά</span> with accus. of indirect, agency (fault, merit, of a person, thing, or situation). <span lang="grc">διά</span> with gen. is used of an agent employed to bring about an intended result; <span lang="grc">διά</span> with accus. is used of a person, thing, or state beyond our control (accidental agency). (1) Persons: <span lang="grc">ἔπρᾱξαν ταῦτα δι᾽ Εὐρυμάχου</span> _they effected this by the mediation of Eurymachus_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.2">T. 2.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ διὰ τούτους ἀπολωλότα</span> _what has been lost by_ (_the fault of_) _these men_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 6.34">D. 6.34</cite>. The accus. marks a person as an agent not as an instrument. (2) Things: <span lang="grc">νόμοι, δι᾽ ὧν ἐλευθέριος ὁ βίος παρασκευασθήσεται</span> _laws_, _by means of which a life of freedom will be provided_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.52">X. C. 3.3.52</cite>, <span lang="grc">διὰ τοὺς νόμους βελτί̄ους γιγνόμενοι ἄνθρωποι</span> _men become better thanks to the laws_ 8. 1. 22. Sometimes there is little difference between the two cases: <span lang="grc">δι᾽ ὧν ἅπαντ᾽ ἀπώλετο</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.33">D. 18.33</cite>, <span lang="grc">δι᾽ οὓς ἅπαντ᾽ ἀπώλετο</span> 18. 35.

N.—<span lang="grc">διά</span> with gen. ( = _through_) is distinguished from the simple dative ( = _by_): <span lang="grc">δι᾽ οὗ ὁρῶμεν καὶ ᾧ ἀκούομεν</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 184c">P. Th. 184c</cite>.

**e.** For <span lang="grc">διά</span> with accus. to express the reason for an action, the dative is sometimes used ([1517](18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1517)): <span lang="grc">τοῖς πεπρᾱγμένοις φοβούμενος τοὺς Ἀθηναίους</span> _fearing the Athenians by reason of what had happened_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.98">T. 3.98</cite>. The dative specifies the reason less definitely than <span lang="grc">διά</span> with the accusative.

**f.** When used in the same sentence, the dative may express the immediate, <span lang="grc">διά</span> with the accus. the remoter, cause: <span lang="grc">ἀσθενείᾳ σωμάτων διὰ τὴν σῑτοδείᾱν ὑπεχώρουν</span> _they gave ground from the fact that they were weak through lack of food_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.36">T. 4.36</cite>.

**g.** <span lang="grc">διά</span> with accus. contrasted with <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> with gen.: <span lang="grc">φήσομεν αὐτὸ δι᾽ ἐκεῖνα ὑπὸ τῆς αὑτοῦ κακίᾱς ἀπολωλέναι</span> _we shall say that it_ (the body) _is destroyed on account of those_ (remoter) _causes_ (as badness of food) _by its own evil_ (immediately) <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 609e">P. R. 609e</cite>.

#### 3. <span lang="grc"> **διά** </span> _in Composition_

_Through_, _across_, _over_ (<span lang="grc">διαβαίνειν</span> _cross_), _apart_, _asunder_ (<span lang="grc">διακόπτειν</span> _cut in two_, <span lang="grc">διακρί̄νειν</span> _discernere_, <span lang="grc">διαφέρειν</span> _differ_, <span lang="grc">διαζυγνύναι</span> _disjoin_), _severally_ (<span lang="grc">διαδιδόναι</span> _distribute_).

<span lang="grc">δια-</span> often denotes intensity, continuance, or fulfilment (<span lang="grc">διαμένειν</span> _remain to the end_, <span lang="grc">διαφθείρειν</span> _destroy completely_). <span lang="grc">δια-</span> is common in the reciprocal middle ([1726](20-the-verb-voices/03-middle-voice.md#s1726)), as in <span lang="grc">διαλέγεσθαι</span> _converse;_ often of rivalry (<span lang="grc">οἱ διαπολῑτευόμενοι</span> _rival statesmen_, <span lang="grc">διακοντίζεσθαι</span> _contend in throwing the javelin_).

### <span lang="grc">εἰς, ἐς</span>

<!-- page 376 -->

##### <a id="s1686"></a> §1686

<span lang="grc">**εἰς**, **ἐς**</span> _into_, _to_, opposed to <span lang="grc">ἐξ</span>; from <span lang="grc">ἐν ¨ ς</span> (cp. Lat. _abs_ from _ab_ + _s_). See on <span lang="grc">ἐν</span>. On <span lang="grc">εἰς</span> with the genitive by ellipsis, see [1302](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1302).

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **εἰς** </span> _with the Accusative only_

In the Old Attic alphabet (2 a), generally used in Attica in the fifth century, <span lang="grc">ΕΣ</span> was written, and this may be either <span lang="grc">εἰς</span> or <span lang="grc">ἐς</span>. In the fourth century <span lang="grc">ΕΙΣ</span> was generally written. In Thuc. <span lang="grc">ἐς</span> is printed, but its correctness may be doubted; other Attic prose writers use <span lang="grc">εἰς</span>, the poets <span lang="grc">εἰς</span> or (less frequently) <span lang="grc">ἐς</span>. It is not true that in poetry <span lang="grc">ἐς</span> is used only before consonants, <span lang="grc">εἰς</span> only before vowels.

**a.** Local: of the goal: <span lang="grc">Σικελοὶ ἐξ Ἰταλίᾱς διέβησαν ἐς Σικελίᾱν</span> _the Sicels crossed over out of Italy into Sicily_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.2">T. 6.2</cite>; with a personal object: <span lang="grc">ἦλθεν ἐκ τῆς Ἀσίᾱς ἐς ἀνθρώπους ἀπόρους</span> _he came from Asia to_ (a land of) _poor men_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.9">T. 1.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐσπέμπει γράμματα ἐς</span> (_v. l._ <span lang="grc">πρὸς</span>) <span lang="grc">βασιλέᾱ</span> _he dispatches a letter to_ (the palace of) _the king_ 1. 137 (of sending, etc., to individuals <span lang="grc">ὡς</span> or <span lang="grc">πρός</span> is used); _against:_ <span lang="grc">ἐστράτευσαν ἐς τὴν Ἀττικήν</span> _they invaded Attica_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.1">T. 3.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">πόλεμος τοῖς Κορινθίοις ἐς τοὺς Ἀθηναίους</span> _war between the Corinthians and the Athenians_ 1. 55; with verbs of rest, 1659 b. The idea of motion holds where Eng. uses _in_ or _at:_ <span lang="grc">τελευτᾶν εἴς τι</span> _to end in_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.51">T. 2.51</cite>. Extension: <span lang="grc">Πελοποννησίους διαβαλεῖν ἐς τοὺς Ἕλληνας</span> _to raise a prejudice against the Peloponnesians among the Greeks_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.109">T. 3.109</cite>; _in the presence of_ (_coram_): <span lang="grc">ἐς τὸ κοινὸν λέγειν</span> _to speak before the assembly_ 4. 58.

**b.** Temporal: of the goal: _up to, until:_ <span lang="grc">ἐς ἐμέ</span> _up to my time_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.52">Hdt. 1.52</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐς τέλος</span> _finally_ 3. 40; _at_ (_by_) _such a time_ (of a fixed or expected time): <span lang="grc">προεῖπε εἰς τρίτην ἡμέρᾱν παρεῖναι</span> _commanded_ them _to be present on the third day_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.42">X. C. 3.1.42</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἥκετε εἰς τριᾱκοστὴν ἡμέρᾱν</span> _come on the thirtieth day_ 5. 3. 6. Limit of time attained: <span lang="grc">εἰς τοιοῦτον καιρὸν ἀφῑγμένοι</span> _arriving at such a time_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 16.5">L. 16.5</cite>. Extension (over future time): <span lang="grc">εἰς τὸν λοιπὸν χρόνον</span> _in all future time_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 16.2">L. 16.2</cite>.

**c.** Measure and Limit with numerals: <span lang="grc">εἰς χῑλίους</span> _to the number of_ (up to) _a thousand_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.5">X. A. 1.8.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰς δύο</span> _two abreast_ 2. 4. 26, <span lang="grc">ἐς δραχμήν</span> _to the amount of a drachma_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.29">T. 8.29</cite>.

**d.** Other relations: Goal, Purpose, Intention: <span lang="grc">ἡ σὴ πατρὶς εἰς σὲ ἀποβλέπει</span> _your country looks for help to you_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.1.8">X. H. 6.1.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">χρῆσθαι εἰς τὰ̄ς σφενδόνᾱς</span> _to use for the slings_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.4.17">X. A. 3.4.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">παιδεύειν εἰς ἀρετήν</span> _to train with a view to virtue_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 519e">P. G. 519e</cite>. Relation to: <span lang="grc">καλὸν εἰς στρατιά̄ν</span> _excellent for the army_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.6">X. C. 3.3.6</cite>, often in Thuc. ( = <span lang="grc">πρός</span> with accus.). Manner: <span lang="grc">εἰς καιρόν</span> _in season_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.8">X. C. 3.1.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰς δύναμιν</span> _to the extent of one's powers_ 4. 5. 52.

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **εἰς** </span> _in Composition_

_Into_, _in_, _to_ (<span lang="grc">εἰσβαίνειν</span> _enter_, <span lang="grc">εἰσπρά̄ττειν</span> _get in_, exact a debt).

### <span lang="grc">ἐν</span>

##### <a id="s1687"></a> §1687

<span lang="grc"> **ἐν** </span> _in_ (poetic <span lang="grc">ἐνί, εἰν, εἰνί</span>), Lat. _in_ with the abl., _en-_; opposed to <span lang="grc">εἰς</span> _into_, <span lang="grc">ἐξ</span> _out of_. On <span lang="grc">ἐν</span> with the genitive by ellipsis, see [1302](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1302).

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **ἐν** </span> _with the Dative_ (_Locative_) _only_

**a.** Local: _in_, _at_, _near_, _by_, _on_, _among:_ <span lang="grc">ἐν Σπάρτῃ</span> _in Sparta_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.128">T. 1.128</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ ἐν Κορίνθῳ μάχη</span> _the battle at Corinth_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ages. 7.5">X. Ages. 7.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">πόλις οἰκουμένη ἐν τῷ Εὐξείνῳ πόντῳ</span> _a city built on the Euxine_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.8.22">X. A. 4.8.22</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν τῇ κλί̄νῃ ἑστηκώς</span> <!-- page 377 --> _standing upon the bed_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 1.24">L. 1.24</cite> (<span lang="grc">ἐν</span> of superposition is rare), <span lang="grc">νόμοι ἐν πᾶσιν εὐδόκιμοι τοῖς Ἕλλησιν</span> _laws famous among all the Greeks_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 631b">P. L. 631b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν ὑ̄μῖν ἐδημηγόρησεν</span> _he made an harangue before_ (_coram_) _you_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.74">D. 8.74</cite>. With verbs of motion, see [1659 a](#s1659). Of circumstance, occupation, as <span lang="grc">οἱ ἐν τοῖς πρά̄γμασιν</span> _the men at the head of affairs_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.56">D. 9.56</cite> (so <span lang="grc">ἐν εἰρήνῃ, ἔργῳ, ὠφελείᾳ, φιλοσοφίᾳ, φόβῳ εἶναι; ἐν αἰτίᾳ ἔχειν</span> _to blame_, <span lang="grc">ἐν ὀργῇ ἔχειν</span> _to be angry with_); _in the power of:_ <span lang="grc">ἐν τῷ θεῷ τὸ τέλος ἦν, οὐκ ἐμοί</span> _the issue rested with God_, _not with me_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.193">D. 18.193</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν ἑαυτῷ ἐγένετο</span> _he came to himself_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.17">X. A. 1.5.17</cite>.

**b.** Temporal: _in_, _within_, _during_ (cp. [1542](18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1542)): <span lang="grc">ἐν πέντε ἔτεσιν</span> _in five years_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 19.29">L. 19.29</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν σπονδαῖς</span> _during a truce_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.55">T. 1.55</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν ᾧ</span> _while_.

**c.** Instrument, Means, Cause, Manner (originally local): <span lang="grc">ἐν ὀφθαλμοῖσιν ἴδωμαι</span> _see with the eyes_ A 587, <span lang="grc">ἐν ἑνὶ κινδῡνεύεσθαι</span> _to be endangered by_ (i.e. _to depend on_) _a single person_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.35">T. 2.35</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν τούτοις ἢ λῡπούμενοι ἢ χαίροντες</span> _either grieving or rejoicing at this_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 603c">P. R. 603c</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν τούτῳ δηλῶσαι</span> _to make clear by this_ 392 e, <span lang="grc">ἐν τῷ φανερῷ</span> _openly_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.21">X. A. 1.3.21</cite>. Conformity: <span lang="grc">ἐν τοῖς ὁμοίοις νόμοις ποιήσαντες τὰ̄ς κρίσεις</span> _deciding according to equal laws_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.77">T. 1.77</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν ἐμοί</span> _in my opinion_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hipp. 1320">E. Hipp. 1320</cite>.

N.—In many dialects, e.g. those north of the Corinthian Gulf (rarely in Pindar), <span lang="grc">ἐν</span> retains its original meaning of _in_ (with dat.) and _into_ (with accus.). The latter use appears in <span lang="grc">ἐνδέξια</span> _towards the right_.

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **ἐν** </span> _in Composition_

_In_, _at_, _on_, _among_ (<span lang="grc">ἐμπί̄πτειν</span> _fall in_ or _on_, <span lang="grc">ἐντυγχάνειν</span> _fall in with_, <span lang="grc">ἐγγελᾶν</span> _laugh at_, <span lang="grc">ἐνάπτειν</span> _bind on_).

### <span lang="grc">ἐξ, ἐκ</span>

##### <a id="s1688"></a> §1688

<span lang="grc">**ἐξ**, **ἐκ**</span> _out_, _out of, from_, _from within_, opposed to <span lang="grc">ἐν, εἰς</span>; cp. Lat. _ex_, _e._ As contrasted with <span lang="grc">ἀπό</span> _away from_, <span lang="grc">ἐξ</span> denotes _from within_.

#### 1. <span lang="grc">**ἐξ**, **ἐκ**</span> _with the_ (_Ablatival_) _Genitive only_

In Arcadian and Cyprian <span lang="grc">ἐς</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἐξ</span>) takes the dative.

**a.** Local: <span lang="grc">ἐκ Φοινί̄κης ἐλαύνων</span> _marching out of Phoenicia_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.12">X. A. 1.7.12</cite>; of transition: <span lang="grc">ἐκ πλείονος ἔφευγον</span> _they fled when at_ (from) _a greater distance_ 1. 10. 11. On <span lang="grc">ἐξ</span> in the _constructio praegnans_, see [1660 a](#s1660).

**b.** Temporal: <span lang="grc">ἐκ τοῦ ἀ̄ρίστου</span> _after breakfast_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.6.21">X. A. 4.6.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐκ παίδων</span> _from boyhood_ 4. 6. 14.

**c.** Other relations: immediate succession or transition: <span lang="grc">ἄλλην ἐξ ἄλλης πόλεως ἀμειβόμενος</span> _exchanging one city for another_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 37d">P. A. 37d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐκ πολέμου ποιούμενος εἰρήνην</span> _making peace after_ (a state of) _war_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.133">D. 19.133</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐκ πτωχῶν πλούσιοι γίγνονται</span> _from beggars they become rich_ 8. 66. Origin: immediate origin (whereas <span lang="grc">ἀπό</span> is used of remote origin, 1684. 1. c): <span lang="grc">ἀγαθοὶ καὶ ἐξ ἀγαθῶν</span> _noble and of noble breed_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 246a">P. Phae. 246a</cite>. Agent, regarded as the source: with pass. and intr. verbs instead of <span lang="grc">ὑπδ</span> (chiefly poetic and in Hdt.): <span lang="grc">πόλεις ἐκ βασιλέως δεδομέναι</span> _cities a gift_ (having been given) _of_ (by) _the king_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.6">X. A. 1.1.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡμολογεῖτο ἐκ πάντων</span> _it was agreed by all_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.49">T. 2.49</cite>; but <span lang="grc">ἐκ</span> is often used with a different force, as <span lang="grc">ἐκ τῶν τυχόντων ἀνθρώπων συνοικισθῆναι</span> _to have been settled by the vulgar_ (as constituent parts of a whole) Lyc. 62. Consequence: <span lang="grc">ἐξ αὐτοῦ τοῦ ἔργου</span> _in consequence of the fact itself_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.75">T. 1.75</cite>. Cause or ground of judgment (where the dat, is more usual with inanimate <!-- page 378 --> objects): <span lang="grc">ἐξ οὗ διέβαλλεν αὐτόν</span> _for which reason he accused him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.6.11">X. A. 6.6.11</cite>. Material: <span lang="grc">τὸ ἄγκιστρον ἐξ ἀδάμαντος</span> _the hook of adamant_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 616c">P. R. 616c</cite>. Instrument and means: <span lang="grc">ἐκ τῶν πόνων τὰ̄ς ἀρετὰ̄ς κτᾶσθαι</span> _to acquire by labour the fruits of virtue_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.123">T. 1.123</cite>. Conformity: <span lang="grc">ἐκ τῶν νόμων</span> _in accordance with the laws_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 24.28">D. 24.28</cite>. Manner (rare): <span lang="grc">ἐκ τοῦ ἴσου</span> _on equal terms_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.3">T. 2.3</cite>. Partitive (cp. [1317](18-the-cases/03-genitive.md#s1317) a): <span lang="grc">ἐκ τῶν δυναμένων εἰσί</span> _they belong to the class that has power_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 525e">P. G. 525e</cite>.

#### 2. <span lang="grc">**ἐξ**, **ἐκ**</span> _in Composition_

_Out_, _from_, _off_, _away_ (cp. <span lang="grc">ἐξελαύνειν</span> _drive out_ and _away_); often with an implication of fulfilment, completion, thoroughness, resolution (<span lang="grc">ἐκπέρθειν</span> _sack utterly_, <span lang="grc">ἐκδιδάσκειν</span> _teach thoroughly_). Cp. [1648](#s1648).

### <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span>

##### <a id="s1689"></a> §1689

<span lang="grc"> **ἐπί** </span> (cp. Lat. _ob_) _upon_, _on_, _on the surface of;_ opposed to <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> _under_, and to <span lang="grc">ὑπέρ</span> when <span lang="grc">ὑπέρ</span> means _above the surface of_.

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **ἐπί** </span> _with the Genitive_

**a.** Local: _upon:_ <span lang="grc">οὔτ᾽ ἐπὶ γῆς οὔθ᾽ ὑπὸ γῆς</span> _neither upon the earth nor under the earth_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Menex. 246d">P. Menex. 246d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ θρόνου ἐκαθέζετο</span> _he seated himself on a throne_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.1.6">X. C. 6.1.6</cite>; of the vehicle (lit. or figur.) _upon which:_ <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ τῶν ἵππων ὀχεῖσθαι</span> _to ride on horseback_ 4. 5. 58 (never <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span> with dat.), <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ τῆς ἐμῆς νεώς</span> _on my ship_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 21.6">L. 21.6</cite>; _in the direction of:_ <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ Σάρδεων ἔφευγε</span> _he fled toward Sardis_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.2.1">X. C. 7.2.1</cite>; _in the presence of_ (cp. <span lang="grc">παρά</span> with dat.): <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ μαρτύρων</span> _before witnesses_ Ant. 2. <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 3.8"><span lang="grc">γ</span>. 8</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span> is rarely used of mere proximity in poetry or standard prose.

N.—In expressions of simple superposition <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span> with the gen. denotes familiar relations and natural position; whereas <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span> with the dat. gives clear and emphatic outlines to statements of the definite place of an object or action, is used in detailed pictures, and marks the object in the dative as distinct from the subject of the verbal action. <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span> with the gen. is colourless and phraseological, and often makes, with the verb or the subject, a compound picture. Even in contrasting two objects <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span> with gen. is used since no special point is made of position. With (unemphatic) pronouns of reference (<span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span> with gen. is much more frequent than <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span> with dat. The distinction between the two cases is often the result of feeling; and certain phrases become stereotyped, now with the gen., now with the dat.

**b.** Temporal, usually with personal gen.: _in the time of:_ <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ τῶν προγόνων</span> _in the time of our ancestors_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.178">Aes. 3.178</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπ᾽ ἐμοῦ</span> _in my time_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.86">T. 7.86</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ τοῦ Δεκελεικου᾽ πολέμου</span> _in the Decelean war_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 22.15">D. 22.15</cite>.

**c.** Other relations: <span lang="grc">μενεῖν ἐπὶ τῆς ἀνοίᾱς τῆς αὐτῆς</span> _to persist in the same folly_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.14">D. 8.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἃ ἐπὶ τῶν ἄλλων ὁρᾶτε, ταῦτ᾽ ἐφ᾽ ὑ̄μῶν αὐτῶν ἀγνοεῖτε</span> _what you see in the case of others, that you ignore in your own case_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 8.114">I. 8.114</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐφ᾽ ἑαυτῶν ἐχώρουν</span> _they proceeded by themselves_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.4.10">X. A. 2.4.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ τεττάρων</span> _four deep_ 1. 2. 15, <span lang="grc">οἱ ἐπὶ τῶν πρᾱγμάτων</span> _the men in power_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.247">D. 18.247</cite>.

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **ἐπί** </span> _with the Dative_

**a.** Local: _on_, _by:_ <span lang="grc">οἰκοῦσιν ἐπὶ τῷ ἰσθμῷ</span> _they dwell on the isthmus_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.56">T. 1.56</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ ἐπὶ θαλάσσῃ τεῖχος</span> _the wall by the sea_ 7. 4. The dat. with <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span> denotes proxim<!-- page 379 --> ity much more frequently than the gen. with <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span>; but denotes superposition less often than the gen. with <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span>.

**b.** Temporal (rare in prose): <span lang="grc">ἦν ἥλιος ἐπὶ δυσμαῖς</span> _the sun was near setting_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.3.34">X. A. 7.3.34</cite>.

**c.** Other relations: Succession, Addition: <span lang="grc">τὸ ἐπὶ τούτῳ γ᾽ ἀπόκρῑναι</span> _answer the next question_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 27b">P. A. 27b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀνέστη ἐπ᾽ αὐτῷ</span> _he rose up after him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.3.7">X. C. 2.3.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ τῷ σί̄τῳ ὄψον</span> _relish with bread_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.14.2">X. M. 3.14.2</cite>. Supervision: <span lang="grc">ἄρχων ἐπὶ τούτοις ἦν</span> _there was a commander over them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.3.56">X. C. 5.3.56</cite>. Dependence: <span lang="grc">καθ᾽ ὅσον ἐστὶν ἐπ᾽ ἐμοί</span> _as far as is in my power_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 6.8">I. 6.8</cite>. Condition: <span lang="grc">ἐφ᾽ οἷς τὴν εἰρήνην ἐποιησάμεθα</span> _on what terms we made the peace_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.5">D. 8.5</cite>. Reason, motive, end, as with verbs of emotion (instead of the simple dative, [1517](18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1517)): <span lang="grc">πάντα ταῦτα θαυμάζω ἐπὶ τῷ κάλλει</span> _I am astonished at all these_ trees _because of their beauty_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 4.21">X. O. 4.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἐπὶ τέχνῃ ἔμαθες ἀλλ᾽ ἐπὶ παιδείᾳ</span> _you learned_ this _not to make it a profession but to gain general culture_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 312b">P. Pr. 312b</cite>. Hostility (less common in prose than in poetry; usually with accus.): <span lang="grc">ἡ ἐπὶ τῷ Μήδῳ ξυμμαχίᾱ</span> _the alliance against the Medes_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.63">T. 3.63</cite>. Price: <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ πόσῳ</span>; _for how much?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 41a">P. A. 41a</cite>.

#### 3. <span lang="grc"> **ἐπί** </span> _with the Accusative_

**a.** Local: of the goal: <span lang="grc">ἐξελαύνει ἐπὶ τὸν ποταμόν</span> _he marches to the river_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.4.11">X. A. 1.4.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀφί̄κοντο ἐπὶ τὸν ποταμόν</span> _they arrived at the river_ 4. 7. 18 (rarely the gen. with verbs of _arrival_), <span lang="grc">ἀνέβαινεν ἐπὶ τὸν ἵππον</span> _he mounted his horse_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.1.1">X. C. 7.1.1</cite>. Extension: <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ πᾶσαν Ἀσίᾱν ἐλλόγιμοι</span> _famous over all Asia_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Criti. 112e">P. Criti. 112e</cite>.

**b.** Temporal: extension: <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ πολλὰ̄ς ἡμέρᾱς</span> _for many days_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.41">D. 21.41</cite>.

**c.** Quantity, measure: <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ μῑκρόν</span> _a little_, <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ πλέον</span> _still more_, <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ πᾶν</span> _in general_, <span lang="grc">πλάτος ἔχων πλεῖον η῍ ἐπὶ δύο στάδια</span> _wider than_ (up to) _two stades_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.5.8">X. C. 7.5.8</cite>.

**d.** Other relations: Purpose, object in view: <span lang="grc">πέμπειν ἐπὶ κατασκοπήν</span> _to send for the purpose of reconnoitering_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.2.9">X. C. 6.2.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπέστειλαν ἐπὶ χρήματα</span> _they sent for money_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.74">T. 6.74</cite>. Hostility: <span lang="grc">ἔπλεον ἐπὶ τοὺς Ἀθηναίους</span> _they sailed against the Athenians_ 2. 90. Reference: <span lang="grc">τὸ ἐπ᾽ ἐμέ</span> (with or without <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span>) _as far as I am concerned_ (more commonly <span lang="grc">ἐπ᾽ ἐμοί</span>); <span lang="grc">τό γε ἐπ᾽ ἐκεῖνον εἶναι</span> <cite data-ref="Lys. 13.58">L. 13.58</cite>.

N.—To express purpose <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span> with accus. is generally used when the purpose involves actual or implied motion to an object; <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span> with dat. is used when the purpose may be attained by mental activity.

#### 4. <span lang="grc"> **ἐπί** </span> _in Composition_

_Upon_ (<span lang="grc">ἐπιγράφειν</span> _write upon_), _over_ (<span lang="grc">ἐπιπλεῖν</span> _sail over_), _at_, of cause (<span lang="grc">ἐπιχαίρειν</span> _rejoice over or at_), _to_, _toward_ (<span lang="grc">ἐπιβοηθεῖν</span> _send assistance to_), _in addition_ (<span lang="grc">ἐπιδιδόναι</span> _give in addition_), _against_ (<span lang="grc">ἐπιβουλεύειν</span> _plot against_), _after_ (<span lang="grc">ἐπιγίγνεσθαι</span> _be born after_, <span lang="grc">ἐπισκευάζειν</span> _repair_); causative (<span lang="grc">ἐπαληθεύειν</span> _verify_); intensity (<span lang="grc">ἐπικρύπτειν</span> _hide;_ <span lang="grc">ἐπιβουλεύεσθαι</span> _further deliberate_ = _reflect_); reciprocity (<span lang="grc">ἐπιμείγνυσθαι ἀλλήλοις</span> _exchange friendly dealings_).

### <span lang="grc">κατά</span>

##### <a id="s1690"></a> §1690

<span lang="grc"> **κατά** </span> _down_ (cp. <span lang="grc">κάτω</span>), opposed to <span lang="grc">ἀνά</span>. With the genitive (the genitive proper (of the goal) and the ablatival genitive) and the <!-- page 380 --> accusative. With the genitive, the motion is perpendicular; with the accusative, horizontal.

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **κατά** </span> _with the Genitive_

**a.** Local: _down from, down toward, under:_ <span lang="grc">ἁλάμενοι κατὰ τῆς πέτρᾱς</span> _having leapt down from the rock_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.2.17">X. A. 4.2.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">κατ᾽ ἄκρᾱς</span> _utterly_, _completely_ (down from the summit) <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 909b">P. L. 909b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ψῡχὴ κατὰ χθονὸς ᾤχετο</span> _his soul went down under the earth_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 23.100"><span lang="grc">Ψ</span> 100</cite>, <span lang="grc">μύρον κατὰ τῆς κεφαλῆς καταχέαντες</span> _having poured myrrh_ (_down_) _over their heads_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 398a">P. R. 398a</cite>; rarely of rest: <span lang="grc">ὁ κατὰ γῆς</span> _the man under the earth_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.6.5">X. C. 4.6.5</cite>.

**b.** Temporal (very rare): <span lang="grc">κατὰ παντὸς τοῦ αἰῶνος</span> _for all eternity_ Lyc. 7.

**c.** Other relations: _against_, as <span lang="grc">κατ᾽ ἐμαυτοῦ ἐρεῖν</span> _to speak against myself_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 37b">P. A. 37b</cite>; rarely in a favourable or neutral sense, as <span lang="grc">οἱ κατὰ Δημοσθένους ἔπαινοι</span> _the eulogies on Demosthenes_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.50">Aes. 3.50</cite>, <span lang="grc">κατὰ πάντων λέγειν</span> _to speak with regard to all_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.2.16">X. C. 1.2.16</cite>; _by_ (with verbs of swearing), as <span lang="grc">ὀμνύντων τὸν ὅρκον κατὰ ἱερῶν τελείων</span> _let them swear the oath by_ (lit. down over) _fullgrown victims_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.47">T. 5.47</cite>.

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **κατά** </span> _with the Accusative_

**a.** Local: <span lang="grc">ἔπλεον κατὰ ποταμόν</span> _they sailed down-stream_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 4.44">Hdt. 4.44</cite>, <span lang="grc">κατὰ τὰ̄ς εἰσόδους ἐφεπόμενοι</span> _following to the entrances_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.64">X. C. 3.3.64</cite>. Extension: <span lang="grc">καθ᾽ ὅλην τὴν πόλιν</span> _throughout the entire city_ Lyc. 40, <span lang="grc">κατὰ γῆν</span> _by land_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 2.32">L. 2.32</cite>, <span lang="grc">διώκοντες τοὺς καθ᾽ αὑτούς</span> _pursuing those stationed opposite themselves_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.10.4">X. A. 1.10.4</cite>.

**b.** Temporal (post-Homeric): <span lang="grc">κατὰ πλοῦν</span> _during the voyage_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.32">T. 3.32</cite>, <span lang="grc">κατ᾽ ἐκεῖνον τὸν χρόνον</span> _at that time_ 1. 139, <span lang="grc">οἱ καθ᾽ ἑαυτόν</span> _his contemporaries_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 20.73">D. 20.73</cite>.

**c.** Other relations: Purpose: <span lang="grc">κατὰ θέᾱν ἧκεν</span> _came for the purpose of seeing_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.31">T. 6.31</cite>. Conformity: <span lang="grc">κατὰ τούτους ῥήτωρ</span> _an orator after their style_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 17b">P. A. 17b</cite>, <span lang="grc">κατὰ τοὺς νόμους</span> _according to the laws_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.2">D. 8.2</cite>. Ground on which an act is based: <span lang="grc">κατὰ φιλίᾱν</span> _owing to friendship_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.60">T. 1.60</cite>. Comparisons: <span lang="grc">μείζω ἢ κατὰ δάκρυα πεπονθότες</span> _having endured sufferings too great for_ (than according to) _tears_ 7. 75 (cp. _maior quam pro_). Manner: <span lang="grc">καθ᾽ ἡσυχίᾱν</span> _quietly_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.64">T. 6.64</cite>. Distribution: <span lang="grc">κατ᾽ ἔθνη</span> _nation by nation_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.122">T. 1.122</cite>, <span lang="grc">δέκα δραχμαὶ κατ᾽ ἄνδρα</span> _ten drachmae the man_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.187">Aes. 3.187</cite>, <span lang="grc">κατὰ σφᾶς αὐτούς</span> _per se_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.79">T. 1.79</cite>. Approximate numbers: <span lang="grc">κατὰ πεντήκοντα</span> _about fifty_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 6.79">Hdt. 6.79</cite>.

#### 3. <span lang="grc"> **κατά** </span> _in Composition_

_Down from above_ (<span lang="grc">καταπί̄πτειν</span> _fall down_), _back_ (<span lang="grc">καταλείπειν</span> _leave behind_), _against_, _adversely_ (<span lang="grc">καταγιγνώσκειν</span> _condemn_, _decide against_, <span lang="grc">καταφρονεῖν</span> _despise_), _completely_ (<span lang="grc">καταπετροῦν</span> _stone to death_, <span lang="grc">κατεσθίειν</span> _eat up_), often with an intensive force that cannot be translated. An intransitive verb when compounded with <span lang="grc">κατά</span> may become transitive ([1559](18-the-cases/07-accusative.md#s1559)).

### <span lang="grc">μετά</span>

##### <a id="s1691"></a> §1691

<span lang="grc"> **μετά** </span>: original meaning _amid_, _among_ (cp. Germ. _mit_, Eng. _mid_ in _midwife_). Hence properly only with plurals or collectives (so in Hom. with gen. and dat.). <span lang="grc"> **μετά** </span> denotes participation, community of action. <span lang="grc"> **πεδά** </span> (Lesb. and other dialects) agrees in meaning with <span lang="grc"> **μετά** </span>, but is of different origin.

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **μετά** </span> _with the Genitive_

<!-- page 381 -->

Usually of persons and abstract nouns.

Local: _among_, _together with_, as <span lang="grc">καθήμενος μετὰ τῶν ἄλλων</span> _sitting among the rest_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 359e">P. R. 359e</cite>, <span lang="grc">θῦσαι μετ᾽ ἐκείνων</span> _to sacrifice in company with them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.3.1">X. C. 8.3.1</cite>; _on the side of_, as <span lang="grc">οἱ μετὰ Κύ̄ρου βάρβαροι</span> _the barbarians in the army of Cyrus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.10">X. A. 1.7.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">μετὰ τῶν ἠδικημένων πολεμεῖν</span> _to wage war on the side of the wronged_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.24">D. 9.24</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ μετὰ τοῦ πλήθους</span> _without the consent of the people_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.66">T. 3.66</cite>; _besides:_ <span lang="grc">γενόμενος μετὰ τοῦ ξυνετοῦ καὶ δυνατός</span> _showing himself powerful as well as sagacious_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.15">T. 2.15</cite>. Accompanying circumstances (concurrent act or state): <span lang="grc">μετὰκινδύ̄νων κτησάμενοι</span> (<span lang="grc">τὴν τάξιν</span>) _having acquired their position amid dangers_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 3.36">D. 3.36</cite>, <span lang="grc">λύ̄πη μετὰ φόβου</span> _grief and terror_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.75">T. 7.75</cite>. Joint efficient cause: <span lang="grc">μετὰ πόνων ἐλευθέρᾱν ἐποίησαν τὴν Ἑλλάδα</span> _by_ (_amid_) _struggles they freed Greece_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 2.55">L. 2.55</cite>. Conformity: <span lang="grc">μετὰ τῶν νόμων</span> _in accordance with the laws_ 3. 82.

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **μετά** </span> _with the Dative_ (_Locative_)

Chiefly Epic (usually with the plural or with the collective singular of persons or things personified, or of the parts of living objects): <span lang="grc">μετὰ μνηστῆρσιν ἔειπεν</span> _he spake amid the suitors_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 17.467"><span lang="grc">ρ</span> 467</cite>, <span lang="grc">μετὰ φρεσί</span> _in their hearts_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 4.245"><span lang="grc">Δ</span> 245</cite>.

#### 3. <span lang="grc"> **μετά** </span> _with the Accusative_

Local: _into the midst of:_ <span lang="grc">νεκροὺς ἔρυσαν μετὰ λᾱὸν Ἀχαιῶν</span> _they dragged the dead into the midst of the host of the Achaeans_ E 573; with an idea of purpose: <span lang="grc">ἰέναι μετὰ Νέστορα</span> _to go after_ (in quest of) _Nestor_ K 73. Extension over the midst of: <span lang="grc">μετὰ πληθύ̄ν</span> _throughout the multitude_ B 143. Phrase: <span lang="grc">μετὰ χεῖρας ἔχειν</span> _to have in hand_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.138">T. 1.138</cite>.

N.—From the use in <span lang="grc">μετ᾽ ἴχνια βαῖνε θεοῖο</span> _he went after the steps of the goddess_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 3.30"><span lang="grc">γ</span> 30</cite> is derived the prose use: _after_ (of _time_ or _rank_), as <span lang="grc">μετὰ τὰ Τρωϊκά</span> _after the Trojan war_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.68">T. 2.68</cite>, <span lang="grc">μετὰ θεοὺς ψῡχὴ θειότατον</span> _after the gods the soul is most divine_ P. L. 726. The range of <span lang="grc">μετά</span> with acc. in Attic prose is not wide.

#### 4. <span lang="grc"> **μετά** </span> _in Composition_

_Among_ (<span lang="grc">μεταδιδόναι</span> _give a share_), _after_, _in quest of_ (<span lang="grc">μεταπέμπεσθαι</span> _send for_). When one thing is _among_ other things, it may be said to _come after_ another, to succeed or alternate with it; hence of succession (<span lang="grc">μεθημερινός</span> _diurnus;_ cp. <span lang="grc">μεθ᾽ ἡμέρᾱν</span> _after daybreak_), alteration or change (<span lang="grc">μεταγράφειν</span> _rewrite_, <span lang="grc">μεταμέλειν</span> _repent_ i.e. _care for_ something else).

When contrasted with <span lang="grc">σύν, μετά</span> often denotes participation: <span lang="grc">ὁ μέτοχος</span> _the partner_, <span lang="grc">ὁ συνών</span> _the companion_. <span lang="grc">σύν</span> often denotes something added. But <span lang="grc">μετά</span> is usually the prose preposition for <span lang="grc">σύν</span>, though it does not mean _inclusive of_.

### <span lang="grc">παρά</span>

##### <a id="s1692"></a> §1692

<span lang="grc"> **παρά** </span> (Hom. <span lang="grc">παραί</span>, Lat. _por-_ in _porrigere_) _alongside_, _by_, _near_. Except with the accusative <span lang="grc">παρά</span> is commonly used of persons and personified things.

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **παρά** </span> _with the_ (_Ablatival_) _Genitive_

Usually coming or proceeding _from_ a person, in Hom. also of things; cp. _de chez_. <!-- page 382 -->

**a.** Local: <span lang="grc">οἱ αὐτομολοῦντες παρὰ βασιλέως</span> _the deserters from the king_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.6">X. A. 2.1.6</cite>. In poetry, where we might expect the dat. ([1659 a](#s1659)): <span lang="grc">ἔγρετο παρ᾽ Ἥρης</span> lit. _he awoke from the side of Hera_ O 5. In standard Attic prose <span lang="grc">παρά</span> with the gen. of a thing is excessively rare. When so used, the thing is personified, or the thing implies a person (as <span lang="grc">πόλις, ἀρχή, θέᾱτρον</span>).

**b.** Author, Source (cp. [1410](18-the-cases/05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1410)): with _verbs of receiving_, _taking_, _asking_, _learning_, _sending_, etc.: <span lang="grc">παρὰ Μήδων τὴν ἀρχὴν ἐλάμβανον Πέρσαι</span> _the Persians wrested the empire from the Medes_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.4.8">X. A. 3.4.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">παρὰ σοῦ ἐμάθομεν</span> _we learned from you_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.2.6">X. C. 2.2.6</cite>; <span lang="grc">ἡ παρὰ τῶν θεῶν εὔνοια</span> _the good-will on the part of the gods_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 2.1">D. 2.1</cite> (less commonly <span lang="grc">ἀπό</span>); with passives and intransitives (instead of <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> with the gen. of the agent): <span lang="grc">τὰ παρὰ τῆς τύχης δωρηθέντα</span> _the gifts of Fortune_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.26">I. 4.26</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦτο παρὰ πάντων ὁμολογεῖται</span> _this is acknowledged on all sides_ (_on the part of all_) <cite data-ref="Lys. 30.12">L. 30.12</cite>.

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **παρά** </span> _with the Dative_

Almost always of persons in standard Attic prose; cp. _chez_.

**a.** Local: <span lang="grc">οὐ παρὰ μητρὶ σῑτοῦνται οι᾽ παῖδες, ἀλλὰ παρὰ τῷ διδασκάλῳ</span> _the boys do not eat with their mothers, but with their teachers_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.2.8">X. C. 1.2.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">παρ᾽ ἐμοί σκηνοῦν</span> _to mess with me_ (as _chez moi_) 6. 1. 49; of things: <span lang="grc">τὰ παρὰ θαλάττη χωρία</span> _the places along the sea_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.2.25">X. A. 7.2.25</cite>.

**b.** Other relations: Possessor: <span lang="grc">τὸ μὲν χρῡσίον παρὰ τούτῳ, οἱ δὲ κίνδῡνοι παρ᾽ ὑ̄μῖν</span> _this man has the gold, you the dangers_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.240">Aes. 3.240</cite>; of the superior in command: <span lang="grc">οἱ παρὰ βασιλεῖ ὄντες</span> _those under the king_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.16">X. A. 1.5.16</cite>; of the person judging: <span lang="grc">ἀναίτιος παρὰ τοῖς στρατιώταις</span> _blameless in the opinion of the troops_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.6.10">X. C. 1.6.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁμολογεῖται παρὰ τῷ δήμῳ</span> _it is agreed in the opinion of the people_ Lyc. 54 (here <span lang="grc">παρά</span> denotes the sphere of judgment); with the gen. after a passive ([1692](#s1692). 1. b) it denotes the source.

#### 3. <span lang="grc"> **παρά** </span> _with the Accusative_

**a.** Local: of motion _to_, in prose only of persons: <span lang="grc">ἧκε παρ᾽ ἐμέ</span> _come to me_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.5.25">X. C. 4.5.25</cite>; motion _along_, _by_, _past_ (a place): <span lang="grc">παρὰ γῆν πλεῖν</span> _sail along shore_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.13">T. 6.13</cite>; of parallel extent (_along_, _alongside_, _beside_) with verbs of motion and of rest (often the dat.), and often when no verb is used: <span lang="grc">ἥνπερ ἔλαβον ναῦν, ἀνέθεσαν παρὰ τὸ τροπαῖον</span> _the ship they captured they set up alongside of the trophy_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.92">T. 2.92</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἶπεν αὐτῷ μένειν παρ᾽ ἑαυτόν</span> _he told him to remain close by him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.18">X. C. 1.4.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ πεδίον τὸ παρὰ τὸν ποταμόν</span> _the plain extending along the river_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.3.1">X. A. 4.3.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἦν παρὰ τὴν ὁδὸν κρήνη</span> _there was a spring by the road_ 1. 2. 13. _Contrary to:_ <span lang="grc">παρὰ τοὺς νόμους ἢ κατ᾽ αὐτούς</span> _contrary to_ (i.e. going past) _the laws or in accordance with them_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.20">D. 23.20</cite>; _in addition to_ (along beside): <span lang="grc">ἔχω παρὰ ταῦτα ἄλλο τι λέγειν</span> _besides this I have to say something else_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 107a">P. Ph. 107a</cite>. Phrase: <span lang="grc">παρ᾽ ὀλίγον ἐποιοῦντο Κλέανδρον</span> _they treated Cleander as of no account_ (cp. ‘next to nothing’) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.6.11">X. A. 6.6.11</cite>.

**b.** Temporal: (duration) <span lang="grc">παρὰ πάντα τὸν χρόνον</span> _throughout the whole time_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 5.2">D. 5.2</cite>, (momentary) <span lang="grc">παρὰ τὰ δεινά</span> _in the hour of danger_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.170">Aes. 3.170</cite>, <span lang="grc">παρ᾽ αὐτὰ τἀ̄δικήματα</span> _at the time of_ (i.e. _immediately after_) _the offences themselves_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.13">D. 18.13</cite>.

**c.** Other relations: Cause = <span lang="grc">διά</span>: <span lang="grc">παρὰ τὴν ἡμετέρᾱν ἀμέλειαν</span> _in consequence of our negligence_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.11">D. 4.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ παρὰ τὸ προαισθέσθαι κεκώλῡται</span> _if it was prevented by being perceived in advance_ 19. 42. Dependence: <span lang="grc">παρὰ τοῦτο γέγονε τὰ</span> <!-- page 383 --> <span lang="grc">τῶν Ἑλλήνων</span> _the fortunes of the Greeks depend on this_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.232">D. 18.232</cite>. Measure: <span lang="grc">παρὰ μῑκρὸν ἤλθομεν ἐξανδραποδισθῆναι</span> _we had a narrow escape_ (came by a little) _from being enslaved_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 7.6">I. 7.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">παρὰ πολύ</span> _by far_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.8">T. 2.8</cite>. Comparison: <span lang="grc">ἐξέτασον παρ᾽ ἄλληλα</span> _contrast with each other_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.265">D. 18.265</cite>, <span lang="grc">χειμὼν μείζων παρὰ τὴν καθεστηκυῖαν ὥρᾱν</span> _stormy weather more severe than was to be expected at the season_ then _present_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.6">T. 4.6</cite>.

#### 4. <span lang="grc"> **παρά** </span> _in Composition_

_Alongside_, _by_, _beside_ (<span lang="grc">παριέναι</span> _go alongside_), _beyond_, _past_ (<span lang="grc">παρελαύνειν</span> _drive past_), _over_ (<span lang="grc">παρορᾶν</span> _overlook_), _aside_, _amiss_ (<span lang="grc">παρακούειν</span> _misunderstand_).

### <span lang="grc">περί</span>

##### <a id="s1693"></a> §1693

<span lang="grc"> **περί** </span> _around_ (on all sides), _about;_ cp. <span lang="grc">πέριξ</span> _round about_. Lat. _per_ in _permagnus_. <span lang="grc">περί</span> is wider than <span lang="grc">ἀμφί</span>: cp. <cite data-ref="Xen. Ways 1.7">X. Vect. 1.7</cite> <span lang="grc">οὐ πεοίρρυτος οὖσα ὥσπερ νῆσος . . . ἀμφιθάλαττος γάρ ἐστι</span> _it_ (Attica) _is not_, _like an island, surrounded by the sea . . . for it has the sea on two sides_. On <span lang="grc">περί</span> post-positive, see [1665](#s1665).

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **περί** </span> _with the Genitive_

**a.** Local (poetic): <span lang="grc">περὶ τρόπιος βεβαώς</span> _riding on_ (astride) _the keel_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 5.130"><span lang="grc">ε</span> 130</cite>.

**b.** Other relations: _about_, _concerning_ (Lat. _de_), the subject _about which_ an act or thought centres: <span lang="grc">περὶ πατρίδος μαχούμενοι</span> _fighting for their country_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.69">T. 6.69</cite> (cp. <span lang="grc">ὑπέρ</span>), <span lang="grc">δείσᾱς περὶ τοῦ υἱοῦ</span> _fearing for his son_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.4.22">X. C. 1.4.22</cite>, <span lang="grc">λέγειν περὶ τῆς εἰρήνης</span> _to speak about peace_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.55">T. 5.55</cite>; <span lang="grc">τὰ περί τινος</span> instead of <span lang="grc">τὰ περί τινα</span> is used in the neighbourhood of a verb of saying or thinking (which takes <span lang="grc">περί</span> with gen.): <span lang="grc">τὰ περὶ τῆς ἀρετῆς</span> _the relations of virtue_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 360e">P. Pr. 360e</cite>. Superiority (cp. [1402](18-the-cases/05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1402)): <span lang="grc">περίεσσι γυναικῶν εἶδος</span> _thou dost surpass women in beauty_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 18.248"><span lang="grc">ς</span> 248</cite>, <span lang="grc">περὶ παντὸς ποιούμενοι</span> _regarding as_ (more than everything) _all-important_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.11">T. 2.11</cite> (cp. [1373](18-the-cases/04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1373)).

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **περί** </span> _with the Dative_

**a.** Local: _about:_ of arms, dress, etc., in prose: <span lang="grc">στρεπτοὶ περὶ τοῖς τραχήλοις</span> _collars about their necks_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.8">X. A. 1.5.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἃ περὶ τοῖς σώμασιν ἔχουσιν</span> _the clothes about their persons_ I. ep. 9. 10 (only case in the orators), <span lang="grc">περὶ δουρί</span> A 303.

**b.** Other relations (usually poetic): External cause: <span lang="grc">δείσαντες περὶ ταῖς ναυσίν</span> _afraid for their ships_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.53">T. 7.53</cite> (with _verbs of fearing_, <span lang="grc">περί</span> with the gen. is _fear of_ or _fear for_). Inner impulse: <span lang="grc">περὶ τάρβει</span> _from fear_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Pers. 694">A. Pers. 694</cite>.

#### 3. <span lang="grc"> **περί** </span> _with the Accusative_

**a.** Local: of position: <span lang="grc">ἀπέστειλαν ναῦς περὶ Πελοπόννησον</span> _they despatched ships round about Peloponnese_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.23">T. 2.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">ᾤκουν περὶ πᾶσαν τὴν Σικελίᾱν</span> _they settled all round Sicily_ 6. 2; of persons: <span lang="grc">οἱ περὶ Ἡρἁ̄κλειτον</span> _the followers of Heraclitus_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crat. 440c">P. Crat. 440c</cite>.

**b.** Indefinite statement of time and number: <span lang="grc">περὶ ὄρθρον</span> _about dawn_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.101">T. 6.101</cite>, <span lang="grc">περὶ ἑβδομήκοντα</span> _about seventy_ 1. 54.

**c.** Other relations: Occupation: <span lang="grc">οἱ περὶ τὴν μουσικὴν ὄντες</span> _those who are engaged in liberal pursuits_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 9.4">I. 9.4</cite>; _connected with_, of general relation (_with reference to_): <span lang="grc">οἱ νόμοι οἱ περὶ τοὺς γάμους</span> _the laws about marriage_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 50d">P. Cr. 50d</cite>, <span lang="grc">περὶ θεοὺς ἀσεβέστατοι</span> _most impious in regard to the gods_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.3.53">X. H. 2.3.53</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ περὶ τὰ̄ς ναῦς</span> _naval affairs_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.13">T. 1.13</cite>. Verbs of action (except verbs of <!-- page 384 --> _striving_) prefer <span lang="grc">περί</span> with accus., verbs of perception, emotion, knowing, prefer <span lang="grc">περί</span> with gen. But the cases often shift.

#### 4. <span lang="grc"> **περί** </span> _in Composition_

_Around_, _about_ (<span lang="grc">περιέχειν</span> _surround_), _beyond_, _over_ (<span lang="grc">περιεῖναι</span> _excel;_ and <span lang="grc">περιορᾶ&lt;\*&gt;</span> _look beyond, overlook_, _suffer_), (remaining) _over_ (<span lang="grc">περιγίγνεσθαι</span> _remain over_, _result_, and _excel_), _exceedingly_ (<span lang="grc">περιχαρής</span> _very glad_).

### <span lang="grc">πρό</span>

##### <a id="s1694"></a> §1694

<span lang="grc"> **πρό** </span> (Lat. _pro_, for) _before_. Cp. <span lang="grc">ἀντί</span>, which is narrower in meaning.

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **πρό** </span> _with the Genitive only_

**a.** Local: <span lang="grc">πρὸ τῶν ἁμαξῶν</span> _in front of the wagons_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.2.36">X. C. 6.2.36</cite>.

**b.** Temporal: <span lang="grc">πρὸ τῆς μάχης</span> _before the battle_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.13">X. A. 1.7.13</cite>.

**c.** Other relations: Defence or care (cp. <span lang="grc">ὑπέρ</span>): <span lang="grc">διακινδῡνευειν πρὸ βασιλέως</span> _to incur danger in defence of_ (prop. _in front of_) _the king_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.8.4">X. C. 8.8.4</cite>. Preference (cp. <span lang="grc">ἀντί</span>): <span lang="grc">οἱ ἐπαινοῦντες πρὸ δικαιοσύνης ἀδικίᾱν</span> _th&lt;\*&gt;se who laud injustice in preference to justice_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 361e">P. R. 361e</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρὸ πολλοῦ ποιεῖσθαι</span> _to esteem highly_ (in preference to much) <cite data-ref="Isoc. 5.138">I. 5.138</cite>, <span lang="grc">φωνεῖν πρὸ τῶνδε</span> _to speak for them_ (_as their spokesman_) <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 10">S. O. T. 10</cite> (<span lang="grc">ἀντὶ τῶνδε</span> = _as their deputy_, <span lang="grc">ὑπὲρ τῶνδε</span> _as their champion_).

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **πρό** </span> _in Composition_

_Before_, _forward_, _forth_ (<span lang="grc">προβάλλειν</span> _put forward_), _for_, _in behalf of, in defence of_, _in public_ (<span lang="grc">προαγορεύειν</span> _give public notice_), _beforehand_ (<span lang="grc">πρόδηλος</span> _manifest beforehand_), _in preference_ (<span lang="grc">προαιρεῖσθαι</span> _choose in preference_).

### <span lang="grc">πρός</span>

##### <a id="s1695"></a> §1695

<span lang="grc"> **πρός** </span> (Hom. also <span lang="grc">προτί</span>), _at_, _by_ (_fronting_). Of like meaning, but of different origin, is Hom. <span lang="grc">ποτί</span>.

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **πρός** </span> _with the Genitive_

**a.** Local (not common in prose): <span lang="grc">τὸ πρὸς ἑσπέρᾱς τεῖχος</span> _the wall facing the west_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.4.18">X. H. 4.4.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ ὑποζύγια ἔχοντες πρὸς τοῦ ποταμοῦ</span> _having the pack-animals on the side toward the river_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.2.4">X. A. 2.2.4</cite>.

**b.** Other relations: Descent: <span lang="grc">πρὸς πατρός</span> _on the father's side_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.169">Aes. 3.169</cite>. Characteristic: <span lang="grc">οὐ γὰρ ἦν πρὸς τοῦ Κύ̄ρου τρόπου</span> _for it was not the way of Cyrus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.11">X. A. 1.2.11</cite>. Point of view of a person: <span lang="grc">πρὸς ἀνθρώπων αἰσχρός</span> _base in the eyes of men_ 2. 5. 20. Agent as the source, with passive verbs (instead of <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span>): <span lang="grc">ὁμολογεῖται πρὸς πάντων</span> _it is agreed by all_ 1. 9. 20; _to the advantage of;_ <span lang="grc">σπονδὰ̄ς ποιησάμενος πρὸς Θηβαίων μᾶλλον ἢ πρὸς ἑαυτ ῶν</span> _making a truce more to the advantage of the Thebans than of his own party_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 7.1.17">X. H. 7.1.17</cite>; in oaths and entreaties: <span lang="grc">πρὸς θεῶν</span> _by the gods_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.4.21">X. H. 2.4.21</cite>.

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **πρός** </span> _with the Dative_

In a local sense, denoting proximity (generally, in prose, of towns or buildings, not of persons): <span lang="grc">πρὸς τῇ πόλει τὴν μάχην ποιεῖσθαι</span> _to fight near the city_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.49">T. 6.49</cite>; sometimes like <span lang="grc">ἐν</span>, as <span lang="grc">πρὸς ἱεροῖς τοῖς κοινοῖς ἀνατεθῆναι</span> _to be dedicated in the common shrines_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.57">T. 3.57</cite>. Occupation: <span lang="grc">ἦν ὅλος πρὸς τῷ λήμματι</span> _he was wholly intent upon his gain_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.127">D. 19.127</cite>. _In addition to:_ <span lang="grc">πρὸς αὐτοῖς</span> _besides these_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.57">T. 7.57</cite>. _In the presence of:_ <span lang="grc">πρὸς τῷ διαιτητῆ λέγειν</span> _to speak before the arbitrator_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 39.22">D. 39.22</cite>. <!-- page 385 -->

#### 3. <span lang="grc"> **πρός** </span> _with the Accusative_

**a.** Local (direction toward or to, strictly _fronting_, _facing_): <span lang="grc">ὑ̄μᾶς ἄξομεν πρὸς αὐτούς</span> _we will lead you to them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.6.6">X. A. 7.6.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρὸς νότον</span> (_toward the_) _south_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.6">T. 3.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἰέναι πρὸς τοὺς πολεμίους</span> _to go against the enemy_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.6.10">X. A. 2.6.10</cite>.

**b.** Temporal (rare): <span lang="grc">πρὸς ἡμέρᾱν</span> _toward daybreak_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.4.6">X. H. 2.4.6</cite>.

**c.** Other relations: friendly or hostile relation: <span lang="grc">πρὸς ἐμὲ λέγετε</span> _speak to me_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.4.19">X. C. 6.4.19</cite>, <span lang="grc">φιλίᾱ πρὸς ὑ̄μᾶς</span> _friendship with you_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 5.32">I. 5.32</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔχθρᾱ πρὸς τοὺς Ἀργείους</span> _enmity to the Argives_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.68">T. 2.68</cite>, but <span lang="grc">ἡ πρὸς ἡμᾶς ἔχθρᾱ</span> _our enmity_ 6. 80, <span lang="grc">ἡ ἀπέχθεια πρὸς τοὺς Θηβαίους</span> _our enmity to the Thebans_ and _the enmity of the Thebans to us_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.36">D. 18.36</cite>. With words of _hating_, _accusing_, and their opposites, <span lang="grc">πρός</span> is used either of the subject or of the object or of both parties involved. With words denoting _warfare_ <span lang="grc">πρός</span> indicates a double relation, and the context must determine which party is the aggressor or assailant: <span lang="grc">ναυμαχίᾱ Κορινθίων πρὸς Κερκῡραίους</span> _a sea-fight between the Corinthians and the Corcyreans_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.13">T. 1.13</cite> (here <span lang="grc">καί</span> often suffices, as <span lang="grc">ὁ Λακεδαιμονίων καὶ Ἠλείων πόλεμος</span> <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 3.2.31">X. H. 3.2.31</cite>). Relation in general: <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν αὐτῷ πρὸς τὴν πόλιν ἐστίν</span> _he has nothing to do with the city_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.44">D. 21.44</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρὸς τοὺς θεοὺς εὐσεβῶς ἔχειν</span> _to be pious toward the gods_ Lyc. 15. Purpose: <span lang="grc">πρὸς τί</span>; _to what end?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.3.20">X. C. 6.3.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρὸς χάριν λέγειν</span> _to speak in order to court favour_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.51">D. 4.51</cite>; _with a view to_ (often nearly = <span lang="grc">διά</span>): <span lang="grc">πρὸς ταῦτα βουλεύεσθε εὖ</span> _wherefore be well advised_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.87">T. 4.87</cite>, <span lang="grc">πρὸς τὰ παρόντα</span> _in consequence of the present circumstances_ 6. 41. Conformity: <span lang="grc">πρὸς τὴν ἀξίᾱν</span> _according to merit_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.4.29">X. C. 8.4.29</cite>. Standard of judgment: <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ πρὸς ἀργύριον τὴν εὐδαιμονίᾱν ἔκρῑνον</span> _nor did they estimate happiness by the money-standard_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.76">I. 4.76</cite>, <span lang="grc">χώρᾱ ὡς πρὸς τὸ πλῆθος τῶν πολῑτῶν ἐλαχίστη</span> _a territory very small in proportion to the number of its citizens_ 4. 107; and hence of comparison: <span lang="grc">οί φαυλότεροι τῶν ἀνθρώπων πρὸς τοὺς ξυνετωτέρους . . . ἄμεινον οἰκοῦσι τὰ̄ς πόλεις</span> _the simpler class of men, in comparison with the more astute, manage their public affairs better_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.37">T. 3.37</cite>. Exchange: <span lang="grc">ἡδονὰ̄ς πρὸς ἡδονὰ̄ς καταλλάττεσθαι</span> _to exchange pleasures for pleasures_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 69a">P. Ph. 69a</cite>.

#### 4. <span lang="grc"> **πρός** </span> _in Composition_

_To_, _toward_ (<span lang="grc">προσελαύνειν</span> _drive to_, <span lang="grc">προστρέπειν</span> _turn toward_), _in addition_ (<span lang="grc">προσλαμβάνειν</span> _take in addition_), _against_ (<span lang="grc">προσκρούειν</span> _strike against, be angry with_). Often in the general sense of _additionally_, qualifying the whole sentence rather than the verb.

### <span lang="grc">σύν</span>

##### <a id="s1696"></a> §1696

<span lang="grc"> **σύν** </span> (Older Attic <span lang="grc">ξύν</span>; cp. Ion. <span lang="grc">ξῡνός</span> from <span lang="grc">κονιος ῀ κοινός</span>, Lat. _cum_) _with_.

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **σύν** </span> _with the Instrumental Dative only_.

**a.** In standard (i.e. not Xenophontic) prose <span lang="grc">σύν</span> has been almost driven out of use by <span lang="grc">μετά</span>. It is used (1) in old formulas, as <span lang="grc">σὺν</span> (<span lang="grc">τοῖς</span>) <span lang="grc">θεοῖς</span> _with the help of the gods_, <span lang="grc">σὺν</span> (<span lang="grc">τοῖς</span>) <span lang="grc">ὅπλοις</span> _in arms_, etc. (of things attached to a person), <span lang="grc">σὺν νῷ</span> _intelligently;_ (2) of sum totals (_along with, including_), as <!-- page 386 --> <span lang="grc">σὺν τοῖς ἔργοις πλέον ἢ δέκα τάλαντα ἔχει</span> _he has more than ten talents interest included_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 28.13">D. 28.13</cite>.

**b.** <span lang="grc">σύν</span> is usually poetic (rare in comedy) and Xenophontic; it is often used in the formulas of **a** (1) and of persons and things personified. Its older and poetic meaning is _along with_ (of something secondary or added to the action) and _with the help of_. So in Xen.: _together with, along with:_ <span lang="grc">σὺν τῆ γυναικὶ δειπνεῖν</span> _to sup with your wife_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.1.49">X. C. 6.1.49</cite>; to reinforce the simple dative: <span lang="grc">ἀκολουθεῖν σύν τινι, πορεύεσθαι σύν τινι</span> ([1524](18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1524)); with the collateral notion of help: _with the aid of_, as <span lang="grc">σὺν ἐκείνῳ μάχεσθαι</span> _to fight with his help_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.3.5">X. C. 5.3.5</cite>.

**c.** Means and Instrument (regarded as accompaniments of an action: the comitative instrumental): <span lang="grc">ἡ κτῆσις αὐτῶν ἔστιν οὐδαμῶς σὺν τῇ βίᾳ, ἀλλὰ μᾶλλον σὺν τῇ εὐεργεσίᾳ</span> _they_ (friends) _are acquired, not by forcible means, but by kindness_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.7.13">X. C. 8.7.13</cite>.

**d.** Manner: <span lang="grc">σὺν γέλωτι ἦλθον</span> _they went laughing_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.18">X. A. 1.2.18</cite>. _In conformity with_ (opp. to <span lang="grc">παρά</span>): <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἐπέτρεψε τῷ δήμῳ παρὰ τοὺς νόμους ψηφίσασθαι, ἀλλὰ σὺν τοῖς νόμοις ἠναντιώθη κτλ</span>. _he did not permit the people to vote contrary to the laws, but_, _in conformity with them, opposed himself_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.4.2">X. M. 4.4.2</cite>.

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **σύν** </span> _in Composition_

_Together with_ (<span lang="grc">συμβιοῦν</span> _live with_, <span lang="grc">συμπορεύεσθαι</span> _march in company with_), _together_ (<span lang="grc">συμβάλλειν</span> _conicere_), _completely_ (<span lang="grc">συμπληροῦν</span> _fill up_), contraction in size (<span lang="grc">συντέμνειν</span> _cut short_), and generally of union or connection. Standard prose uses <span lang="grc">συν-</span> freely.

### <span lang="grc">ὑπέρ</span>

##### <a id="s1697"></a> §1697

<span lang="grc"> **ὑπέρ** </span> (Hom. also <span lang="grc">ὑπείρ</span>) _over_, Lat. _super_. For the contrast with <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span>, see [1689](#s1689).

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **ὑπέρ** </span> _with the Genitive_

**a.** Local: _from over:_ <span lang="grc">ὑπὲρ τῶν ἄκρων κατέβαινον</span> _they came down over the heights_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.25">T. 4.25</cite>; _over_, _abover:_ <span lang="grc">ὑπὲρ τῆς κώμης γήλοφος ἦν</span> _above the village was a hill_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.10.12">X. A. 1.10.12</cite>.

**b.** Other relations: _in defence of, on behalf of:_ <span lang="grc">μαχόμενος ὑπὲρ ὑ̄μῶν</span> _fighting for you_ (standing _over_ to protect) <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 642c">P. L. 642c</cite>; _in place of, in the name of:_ <span lang="grc">ἐγὼ λέξω καὶ ὑπὲρ σοῦ καὶ ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν</span> _I will speak both for you and for ourselves_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.14">X. C. 3.3.14</cite>. Purpose: <span lang="grc">ὑπὲρ τοῦ ταῦτα λαβεῖν</span> _in order to get this_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.44">D. 8.44</cite>; _concerning_, _about_ (often = <span lang="grc">περί</span> in Demos. and the later orators; in inscr. after 300 B. C.): <span lang="grc">φόβος ὑπὲρ τοῦ μέλλοντος</span> _fear for the future_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.71">T. 7.71</cite>, <span lang="grc">μὴ περὶ τῶν δικαίων μηδ᾽ ὑπὲρ τῶν ἔξω πρᾱγμάτων</span> _not about your just claims nor about your foreign interests_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 6.35">D. 6.35</cite>.

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **ὑπέρ** </span> _with the Accusative_

**a.** Local: <span lang="grc">ὑπὲρ οὐδὸν ἐβήσετο</span> _he passed over the threshold_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 13.63"><span lang="grc">ν</span> 63</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ ὑπὲρ Ἑλλήσποντον οἰκοῦντες</span> _those who dwell beyond the Hellespont_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.9">X. A. 1.1.9</cite>.

**b.** Temporal ( = <span lang="grc">πρό</span>) rare: <span lang="grc">ὑπὲρ τὰ Μηδικά</span> _before the Persian wars_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.41">T. 1.41</cite>.

**c.** Measure: <span lang="grc">ὑπὲρ ἥμισυ</span> _more than half_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.47">X. C. 3.3.47</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπὲρ ἄνθρωπον</span> _beyond the power of man_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 839d">P. L. 839d</cite>.

#### 3. <span lang="grc"> **ὑπέρ** </span> _in Composition_

<!-- page 387 -->

_Over_, _above_ (<span lang="grc">ὑπερβάλλειν</span> _cross over_, <span lang="grc">ὑπερέχειν</span> trans. _hold over_, intr. _be above_), _in behalf of, for_ (<span lang="grc">ὑπερμαχεῖν</span> poet. _fight for_), _exceedingly_ (<span lang="grc">ὑπερφρονεῖν</span> _be over-proud_).

### <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span>

##### <a id="s1698"></a> §1698

<span lang="grc"> **ὑπό** </span> (Hom. also <span lang="grc">ὑπαί</span>, Lesbian <span lang="grc">ὐπα-</span>), _under_, _by_, Lat. _sub._

#### 1. <span lang="grc"> **ὑπό** </span> _with the Genitive_

**a.** Local (rare in Attic prose): _out from under_ (poet., cp. <span lang="grc">ὑπέκ</span>): <span lang="grc">ῥέει κρήνη ὑπὸ σπείους</span> _a spring flows out from a cave_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 9.140"><span lang="grc">ι</span> 140</cite>, <span lang="grc">λαβὼν βοῦν ὑπὸ ἁμάξης</span> _taking an ox from a wagon_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.4.25">X. A. 6.4.25</cite>_; under_ (of rest): <span lang="grc">τὰ ὑπὸ γῆς</span> (a fixed phrase) <span lang="grc">ἅπαντα</span> _all things under the earth_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 18b">P. A. 18b</cite>.

**b.** Other relations (metaphorically _under the agency of_): Direct agent (with passives and with verbs having a passive force); contrast <span lang="grc">διά</span>, 1685. 2. d: <span lang="grc">σωθέντες ὑπὸ σοῦ</span> _saved by you_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.14">X. A. 2.5.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">αἰσθόμενος ὑπ᾽ αὐτομόλων</span> _informed by deserters_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.2">T. 5.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">εὖ ἀκούειν ὑπὸ ἀνθρώπων</span> _to be well spoken of by men_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.7.23">X. A. 7.7.23</cite>. With passive nouns: <span lang="grc">ἡ ὑπὸ Μελήτου γραφή</span> _the indictment brought by Meletus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.4.4">X. M. 4.4.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">κλῆσις ὑπὸ τῆς βουλῆς</span> _invitation by the Senate_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.32">D. 19.32</cite>. External cause: <span lang="grc">ἀπώλετο ὑπὸ λῑμοῦ</span> _perished of hunger_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.5">X. A. 1.5.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἐπὶ πολὺ ὑπὸ τῶν ἰππέων ἐξιόντες</span> _not going out far because of the cavalry_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.37">T. 6.37</cite>. Internal cause: <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ τῶν μεγίστων νῑκηθέντες, τῑμῆς καὶ δέους καὶ ὠφελίᾱς</span> _constrained by the strongest motives, honour and fear and profit_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.76">T. 1.76</cite>. External accompaniment, as pressure, in <span lang="grc">ἐτόξευον ὑπὸ μαστί̄γων</span> _they shot under the lash_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.4.25">X. A. 3.4.25</cite>; sound, in <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ αὐλητῶν</span> _to the accompaniment of flute-players_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.70">T. 5.70</cite>; light, in <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ φᾱνοῦ πορεύεσθαι</span> _to go with a torch_ X. R. <cite data-ref="Lys. 5.7">L. 5.7</cite>. Manner: <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ σπουδῆς</span> _hastily_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.33">T. 3.33</cite>.

N. 1.—<span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> with the genitive of a thing personifies the thing. The things so personified are (1) words implying a person, as <span lang="grc">λόγοι</span>, (2) external circumstances, as <span lang="grc">συμφορά̄, κίνδῡνος, νόμος</span>, (3) natural phenomena, as <span lang="grc">χειμών</span>, (4) emotions, as <span lang="grc">φθόνος</span>. The dative may also be employed. See [1493](18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1493), [1494](18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1494).

N. 2.—On <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> to express the personal agent with the perf. pass. see [1493](18-the-cases/06-dative.md#s1493).

#### 2. <span lang="grc"> **ὑπό** </span> _with the Dative_

**a.** Local: _under_ (of rest): <span lang="grc">ἑστάναι ὑπό τινι δένδρῳ</span> _to stand under a tree_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phileb. 38c">P. Phil. 38c</cite>. <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> of place is more common with the dative than with the genitive.

**b.** Other relations: Agent (poetic, except with verbs signifying _to educate_): <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ παιδοτρίβῃ ἀγαθῷ πεπαιδευμένος</span> _educated under_ (the guidance of) _a good master_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lach. 184e">P. Lach. 184e</cite>. Coöperative cause (poet.): <span lang="grc">βῆ ὑπ᾽ ἀμύ̄μονι πομπῇ</span> _he went under a blameless convoy_ Z 171. Subjection: <span lang="grc">οί ὑπὸ βασιλεῖ ὄντες</span> _the subjects of_ (i.e. those under) _the king_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.1.6">X. C. 8.1.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑφ᾽ αὑτῷ ποιήσασθαι</span> _to bring under his own power_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.40">D. 18.40</cite>.

#### 3. <span lang="grc"> **ὑπό** </span> _with the Accusative_

**a.** Local: Motion under: <span lang="grc">ὑπ᾽ αὐτὸν</span> (<span lang="grc">τὸν λόφον</span>) <span lang="grc">στήσᾱς τὸ στράτευμα</span> _halting the army under the hill_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.10.14">X. A. 1.10.14</cite>. Motion down under (poet.): <span lang="grc">εἶμ᾽ ὑπὸ γαῖαν</span> _I shall go down under the earth_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 18.333"><span lang="grc">Σ</span> 333</cite>. Extension or position: <span lang="grc">αἱ</span> <!-- page 388 --> <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ τὸ ὄρος κῶμαι</span> _the villages at the foot of the mountain_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.4.5">X. A. 7.4.5</cite>. Proximity: <span lang="grc">ὑποκειμένη ἡ Εὔβοια ὑπὸ τὴν Ἀττικήν</span> _Euboea lying close by_ (under) _Attica_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.108">I. 4.108</cite>.

**b.** Temporal (of time impending or in progress): <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ νύκτα</span> _at the approach of night_ (_sub noctem_) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.92">T. 2.92</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ νύκτα</span> _during the night_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 9.58">Hdt. 9.58</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ τὴν εἰρήνην</span> _at the time of the peace_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.177">I. 4.177</cite>.

**c.** Other relations. Subjection: <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ σφᾶς ποιεῖσθαι</span> _to bring under their own sway_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.60">T. 4.60</cite>.

#### 4. <span lang="grc"> **ὑπό** </span> _in Composition_

_Under_ (<span lang="grc">ὑποτιθέναι</span> _place under_), _behind_ (<span lang="grc">ὑπολείπειν</span> _leave behind_), _secretly_ (cp. _underhand;_ <span lang="grc">ὑποπέμπειν</span> _send as a spy_), _gradually_ (<span lang="grc">ὑποκαταβαίνειν</span> _descend by degrees_), _slightly_ (<span lang="grc">ὑποφαίνειν</span> _shine a little_); of accompaniment (<span lang="grc">ὑπᾴδειν</span> _accompany with the voice_); of an action performed by another (<span lang="grc">ὑποκηρύ̄ττεσθαι</span> _have oneself proclaimed by the herald_).

## IMPROPER PREPOSITIONS

##### <a id="s1699"></a> §1699

Improper prepositions do not form compounds ([1647](#s1647)).

##### <a id="s1700"></a> §1700

**With the Genitive**.

The list below contains some of the adverbial words used as prepositions.

##### <a id="s1701"></a> §1701

##### <a id="s1702"></a> §1702

**With the Accusative**.

<span lang="grc"> **ὡς** </span> _to_, of persons only, used after verbs expressing or implying motion. Probably used especially in the language of the people.
