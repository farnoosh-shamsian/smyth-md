# THE CASES: VOCATIVE

##### <a id="s1283"></a> §1283

The vocative is used in exclamations and in direct address: <span lang="grc">ὦ Ζεῦ καὶ θεοί</span> _oh Zeus and ye gods_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 310d">P. Pr. 310d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄνθρωπε</span> _my good fellow_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.2.7">X. C. 2.2.7</cite>. The vocative forms an incomplete sentence ([904 d](../01-definitions.md#s904)).

**a.** The vocative is never followed immediately by <span lang="grc">δέ</span> or <span lang="grc">γάρ</span>.

##### <a id="s1284"></a> §1284

In ordinary conversation and public speeches, the polite <span lang="grc">ὦ</span> is usually added. Without <span lang="grc">ὦ</span> the vocative may express astonishment, joy, contempt, a threat, or a warning, etc. Thus <span lang="grc">ἀκούεις Αἰσχίνη</span>; _d'ye hear_, _Aeschines?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.121">D. 18.121</cite>. But this distinction is not always observed, though in general <span lang="grc">ὦ</span> has a familiar tone which was unsuited to elevated poetry.

##### <a id="s1285"></a> §1285

The vocative is usually found in the interior of a sentence. At the beginning it is emphatic. In prose <span lang="grc">ἔφη</span>, in poetry <span lang="grc">ὦ</span>, may stand between the vocative and an attributive or between an attributive and the vocative; in poetry <span lang="grc">ὦ</span> may be repeated for emphasis. <!-- page 313 -->

##### <a id="s1286"></a> §1286

In late poetry a predicate adjective may be attracted into the vocative: <span lang="grc">ὄλβιε κῶρε γένοιο</span> _blessed_, _oh boy, mayest thou be_ Theocr. 17. 66. Cp. _Matutine pater seu Iane libentius audis_ Hor. S. 2. 6. 20.

##### <a id="s1287"></a> §1287

By the omission of <span lang="grc">σύ</span> or <span lang="grc">ὑ̄μεῖς</span> the nominative with the article may stand in apposition to a vocative: <span lang="grc">ὦ ἄνδοες οἱ παρόντες</span> _you_, _gentlemen_, _who are present_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 337c">P. Pr. 337c</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὦ Κῦρε καὶ οἱ ἄλλοι Πέρσαι</span> _Cyrus and the rest of you Persians_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.3.20">X. C. 3.3.20</cite>; and in apposition to the pronoun in the verb: <span lang="grc">ὁ παῖς, ἀκολούθει</span> _boy_, _attend me_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Frogs 521">Ar. Ran. 521</cite>.

##### <a id="s1288"></a> §1288

The nominative may be used in exclamations as a predicate with the subject unexpressed: <span lang="grc">ὦ πικρὸς θεοῖς</span> _oh loathed of heaven_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 254">S. Ph. 254</cite>, <span lang="grc">φίλος ὦ Μενέλᾱε</span> _ah dear Menelaus_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 4.189"><span lang="grc">Δ</span> 189</cite>; and connected with the vocative by _and:_ <span lang="grc">ὦ πόλις καὶ δῆμε</span> _oh city and people_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Kn. 273">Ar. Eq. 273</cite>. In exclamations about a person: <span lang="grc">ὢ γενναῖος</span> _oh the noble man_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 227c">P. Phae. 227c</cite>.

**a.** <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span> is regular in address: <span lang="grc">οὗτος, τί πάσχεις, ὦ Ξανθίᾱ</span>; _ho there_, _I say_, _Xanthias_, _what is the matter with you?_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Wasps 1">Ar. Vesp. 1</cite>; <span lang="grc">ὦ οὗτος, Αἴᾱς</span> _ho there_, _I say_, _Ajax_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 89">S. Aj. 89</cite>.
