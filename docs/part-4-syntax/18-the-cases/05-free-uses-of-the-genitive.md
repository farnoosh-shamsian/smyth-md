# THE CASES: FREE USES OF THE GENITIVE

##### <a id="s1388"></a> §1388

Many verbs ordinarily construed with the accusative are also followed by a genitive of a person, apparently dependent on the verb but in reality governed by an accusative, generally a neuter pronoun or a dependent clause. Thus, <span lang="grc">τάδ᾽ αὐτοῦ ἅγαμαι</span> _I admire this in him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ages. 2.7">X. Ages. 2.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦτο ἐπαινῶ Ἀ̄γησιλά̄ου</span> _I praise this in Agesilaus_ 8. 4, <span lang="grc">αὐτῶν ἓν ἐθαύμασα</span> _I was astonished at one thing in them_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 17a">P. A. 17a</cite>. <span lang="grc">Ἀθηναῖοι σφῶν ταῦτα οὐκ ἀποδέξονται</span> _the Athenians will not be satisfied with them in this_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.48">T. 7.48</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὂ μέμφονται μάλιστα ἡμῶν</span> _which they most censure in us_ 1. 84, <span lang="grc">εἰ ἄγασαι τοῦ πατρὸς ὅσα πέπρᾱχε</span> _if you admire in my father what he has done_ (the actions of my father) <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.15">X. C. 3.1.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">διαθεώμενος αὐτῶν ὅσην χώρᾱν ἔχοιεν</span> _contemplating how large a country they possess_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.19">X. A. 3.1.19</cite>, <span lang="grc">θαυμάζω τῶν στρατηγῶν ὅτι οὐ πειρῶνται ἡμῖν ἐκπορίζειν σῑτηρέσιον</span> _I wonder that the generals do not try to supply us with money for provisions_ 6. 2. 4, <span lang="grc">ἐνενόησε δὲ αὐτῶν καὶ ὡς ἐπηρώτων ἀλλήλους</span> _he took note also how they asked each other questions_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.2.18">X. C. 5.2.18</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">θεωρεῖν</span> _observe_, <span lang="grc">ὑπονοεῖν</span> _feel suspicious of_, <span lang="grc">ἐνθῡμεῖσθαι</span> _consider_, etc.

##### <a id="s1389"></a> §1389

From such constructions arose the use of the genitive in actual dependence on the verb without an accusative word or clause: <span lang="grc">ἄγασαι αὐτοῦ</span> _you admire him_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.6.33">X. M. 2.6.33</cite>, <span lang="grc">θαυμάζω τῶν ὑπὲρ τῆς ἰδίᾱς δόξης ἀποθνῄσκειν ἐθελόντων</span> _I wonder at those who are willing to die in defence of their personal opinions_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 6.93">I. 6.93</cite>. The use in 1389 recalls that with <span lang="grc">αἰσθάνεσθαι</span> ([1367](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1367)). On <span lang="grc">ἄγασθαι, θαυμάζειν</span> with the genitive of cause, see [1405](#s1405).

##### <a id="s1390"></a> §1390

A form of the genitive of possession appears in poetry with verbal adjectives and passive participles to denote the personal origin of an action (cp. [1298](03-genitive.md#s1298)): <span lang="grc">κείνης διδακτά</span> _taught of her_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 344">S. El. 344</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐκδιδαχθεὶς τῶν κατ᾽ οἶκον</span> _informed by those in the house_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Trach. 934">S. Tr. 934</cite>, <span lang="grc">πληγεὶς θυγατρός</span> _struck by a daughter_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Orest. 497">E. Or. 497</cite>. Cp. <span lang="grc">διόσδοτος</span> _given of God;_ and “beloved of the Lord.”

On the genitive absolute, see [2070](../22-the-participle.md#s2070).

## THE ABLATIVAL GENITIVE WITH VERBS

##### <a id="s1391"></a> §1391

The same verb may govern both a true genitive and an ablatival genitive. So <span lang="grc">ἄρχεσθαι</span> _to begin_ ([1348 a](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1348)) and _to start from_, <span lang="grc">ἔχεσθαι</span> _to hold to_ ([1345](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1345)) and _to keep oneself from_. In many cases it is difficult to decide whether the genitive in question was originally the true genitive or the ablatival genitive, or whether the two have been combined; e.g. in <span lang="grc">κυνέη ῥῑνοῦ ποιητή</span> _a cap made of hide_ K 262, <span lang="grc">κύπελλον ἐδέξατο ἧς ἀλόχοιο</span> _he received a goblet from his wife_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 24.305"><span lang="grc">Ω</span> 305</cite>. So with verbs _to hear from, know of_ ([1364](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1364), [1411](#s1411)), and verbs of emotion ([1405](#s1405)), the partitive idea, cause, and source are hard to distinguish. Other cases open to doubt are _verbs of missing_ ([1352](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1352)), _being deceived_ ([1392](#s1392)), and the exclamatory genitive ([1407](#s1407)).

### GENITIVE OF SEPARATION

##### <a id="s1392"></a> §1392

With verbs signifying _to cease, release_, _remove_, _restrain_, _give up, fail_, _be distant from_, etc., the genitive denotes separation. <!-- page 329 -->

<span lang="grc">λήγειν τῶν πόνων</span> _to cease from toil_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.14">I. 1.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπιστήμη χωριζομένη δικαιοσύνης</span> _knowledge divorced from justice_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Menex. 246e">P. Menex. 246e</cite>, <span lang="grc">μεταστὰ̄ς τῆς Ἀθηναίων ξυμμαχίᾱς</span> _withdrawing from the alliance with the Athenians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.67">T. 2.67</cite>, <span lang="grc">παύσαντες αὐτὸν τῆς στρατηγίᾱς</span> _removing him from his office of general_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 6.2.13">X. H. 6.2.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἴργεσθαι τῆς ἀγορᾶς</span> _to be excluded from the forum_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 6.24">L. 6.24</cite>, <span lang="grc">σῶσαι κακοῦ</span> _to save from evil_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 919">S. Ph. 919</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐκώλῡον τῆς πορείᾱς αὐτόν</span> _they prevented him from passing_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ages. 2.2">X. Ages. 2.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">πᾶς ἀσκὸς δύο ἄνδρας ἕξει τοῦ μὴ καταδῦναι</span> _each skin will keep two men from sinking_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.5.11">X. A. 3.5.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">λόγου τελευτᾶν</span> _to end a speech_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.59">T. 3.59</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῆς ἐλευθερίᾱς παραχωρῆσαι Φιλίππῳ</span> _to surrender their freedom to Philip_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.68">D. 18.68</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐ πόνων ὑφί̄ετο, οὐ κινδύ̄νων ἀφί̄στατο, οὐ χρημάτων ἐφείδετο</span> _he did not relax his toil_, _stand aloof from dangers, or spare his money_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ages. 7.1">X. Ages. 7.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">ψευσθέντες τῶν ἐλπίδων</span> _disappointed of their expectations_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.58">I. 4.58</cite> (but cp. [1352](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1352)), <span lang="grc">ἡ νῆσος οὐ πολὺ διέχουσα τῆς ἠπείρου</span> _the island being not far distant from the mainland_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.51">T. 3.51</cite>.

##### <a id="s1393"></a> §1393

Several verbs of separation, such as <span lang="grc">ἐλευθεροῦν</span> (especially with a personal subject), may take <span lang="grc">ἀπό</span> or <span lang="grc">ἐξ</span> when the local idea is prominent. Many take also the accusative.

##### <a id="s1394"></a> §1394

The genitive, instead of the accusative ([1628](07-accusative.md#s1628)), may be used with _verbs of depriving:_ <span lang="grc">ἀποστερεῖ με τῶν χρημάτων</span> _he deprives me of my property_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 17.35">I. 17.35</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῶν ἄλλων ἀφαιρούμενοι χρήματα</span> _taking away property from others_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.5.3">X. M. 1.5.3</cite>.

##### <a id="s1395"></a> §1395

The genitive of the place _whence_ is employed in poetry where a compound verb would be used in prose: <span lang="grc">βάθρων ἵστασθε</span> _rise from the steps_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 142">S. O. T. 142</cite> (cp. <span lang="grc">ὑπανίστανται θά̄κων</span> _they rise from their seats_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 4">X. S. 4</cite>. [31](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s31)), <span lang="grc">χθονὸς ἀείρᾱς</span> _raising from the ground_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 417">S. Ant. 417</cite>.

##### <a id="s1396"></a> §1396

The genitive with verbs signifying _to want, lack_, _empty_, etc. may be classed with the genitive of separation.

<span lang="grc">τῶν ἐπιτηδείων οὐκ ἀπορήσομεν</span> _we shall not want provisions_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.2.11">X. A. 2.2.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπαίνου οὔποτε σπανίζετε</span> _you never lack praise_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hiero 1.14">X. Hi. 1.14</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀνδρῶν τά̄νδε πόλιν κενῶσαι</span> _to empty this city of its men_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Supp. 660">A. Supp. 660</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">ἐλλείπειν</span> and <span lang="grc">στέρεσθαι</span> _lack_, <span lang="grc">ἐρημοῦν</span> _deliver from_.

##### <a id="s1397"></a> §1397

<span lang="grc">δέω</span> _I lack_ (the personal construction) usually takes the genitive of quantity: <span lang="grc">πολλοῦ γε δέω</span> _nothing of the sort_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 228a">P. Phae. 228a</cite>, <span lang="grc">μῑκροῦ ἔδεον ἐν χερσὶ τῶν ὁπλῑτῶν εἶναι</span> _they were nearly at close quarters with the hoplites_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.6.11">X. H. 4.6.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοσούτου δέω ζηλοῦν</span> _I am so far from admiring_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.70">D. 8.70</cite> (also <span lang="grc">τοσοῦτον δέω</span>).

##### <a id="s1398"></a> §1398

<span lang="grc">δέομαι</span> _I want_, _request_ may take the genitive, or the accusative (regularly of neuter pronouns and adjectives), of the thing wanted; and the genitive of the person: <span lang="grc">ἐρωτώμενος ὅτου δέοιτο, Ἀσκῶν, ἔφη, δισχῑλίων δεήσομαι</span> _being asked what he needed, he said_ ‘_I shall have need of two thousand skins_’ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.5.9">X. A. 3.5.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦτο ὑ̄μῶν δέομαι</span> _I ask this of you_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 17c">P. A. 17c</cite>. The genitive of the thing and of the person is unusual: <span lang="grc">δεόμενοι Κύ̄ρου ἄλλης ἄλλης πρά̄ξεως</span> _petitioning Cyrus about different matters_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.3.19">X. C. 8.3.19</cite>.

##### <a id="s1399"></a> §1399

<span lang="grc">δεῖ</span> (impersonal) is frequently used with genitives of quantity: <span lang="grc">πολλοῦ δεῖ οὕτως ἔχειν</span> _far from that being the case_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 35d">P. A. 35d</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ πολλοῦ δεῖ</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.42">D. 8.42</cite> (only in D.) and <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽ ὀλίγου δεῖ</span> _no_, _far from it_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.184">D. 19.184</cite>. <span lang="grc">δεῖν</span> may be omitted (but not with <span lang="grc">πολλοῦ</span>), leaving <span lang="grc">ὀλίγου</span> and <span lang="grc">μῑκροῦ</span> in the sense of _almost_, _all but;_ <!-- page 330 --> <span lang="grc">&lt;\*&gt;λίγου πάντες</span> _almost all_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 552d">P. R. 552d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὀλίγου εἷλον τὴν πόλιν</span> _they all but. took the city_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.35">T. 8.35</cite>. On <span lang="grc">δεῖν</span> used absolutely, see [2012](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s2012) d; on <span lang="grc">δέων</span> with numerals, 350 c.

##### <a id="s1400"></a> §1400

<span lang="grc">δεῖ μοί τινος</span> means _I have need of something_. In place of the dative ([1467](06-dative.md#s1467)) an accusative of the person is rarely allowed in poetry on the analogy of <span lang="grc">δεῖ</span> with the infinitive ([1985](../21-verbal-nouns.md#s1985)): <span lang="grc">οὐ πόνου πολλοῦ με δεῖ</span> _I have need of no great toil_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hipp. 23">E. Hipp. 23</cite> (often in E.). The thing needed is rarely put in the accusative: <span lang="grc">εἴ τι δέοι τῷ χορῷ</span> _if the chorus need anything_ <cite data-ref="Antiph. 6.12">Ant. 6.12</cite> (here some regard <span lang="grc">τὶ</span> as nominative). Cp. [1562](07-accusative.md#s1562).

### GENITIVE OF DISTINCTION AND OF COMPARISON

##### <a id="s1401"></a> §1401

The genitive is used with _verbs of differing_.

<span lang="grc">ἄρχων ἀγαθὸς οὐδὲν διαφέρει πατρὸς ἀγαθοῦ</span> _a good ruler differs in no respect from a good father_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.1.1">X. C. 8.1.1</cite>.

##### <a id="s1402"></a> §1402

With verbs signifying _to surpass, be inferior to_, the genitive denotes that with which anything is compared.

<span lang="grc">τῑμαῖς τούτων ἐπλεονεκτεῖτε</span> _you had the advantage over them in honours_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.37">X. A. 3.1.37</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡττῶντο τοῦ ὕδατος</span> _they were overpowered by the water_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 5.2.5">X. H. 5.2.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑστερεῖν τῶν ἔργων</span> _to be too late for operations_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.38">D. 4.38</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡμῶν λειφθέντες</span> _inferior to us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.7.31">X. A. 7.7.31</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">πρεσβεύειν</span> _hold the first place_, <span lang="grc">ἀριστεύειν</span> _be best_ (poet.), <span lang="grc">μειοῦσθαι</span> _fall short of_, <span lang="grc">μειονεκτεῖν</span> _be worse off_, <span lang="grc">ἐλαττοῦσθαι</span> _be at a disadvantage_. <span lang="grc">νικᾶσθαί τινος</span> is chiefly poetic. <span lang="grc">ἡττᾶσθαι</span> often takes <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span>. Akin to this genitive is that with _verbs of ruling_ ([1370](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1370)), which are often derived from a substantive signifying _ruler_.

##### <a id="s1403"></a> §1403

Many verbs compounded with <span lang="grc">πρό, περί, ὑπέρ</span> denoting superiority take the genitive, which may depend on the preposition ([1384](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1384)): <span lang="grc">τάχει περιεγένου αὐτοῦ</span> _you excelled him in speed_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.19">X. C. 3.1.19</cite>, <span lang="grc">γνώμῃ προέχειν τῶν ἐναντίων</span> _to excel the enemy in spirit_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.62">T. 2.62</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῖς ὅπλοις αὐτῶν ὑπερφέρομεν</span> _we surpass them in our infantry_ 1. 81. So with <span lang="grc">περιεῖναι, ὑπερέχειν. προτῑμᾶν, προκρί̄νειν</span>, and <span lang="grc">προαιρεῖσθαι</span> _prefer_, <span lang="grc">προεστηκέναι</span> _be at the head of_ certainly take the genitive by reason of the preposition. <span lang="grc">ὑπερβάλλειν</span> and <span lang="grc">ὑπερβαίνειν</span> _surpass_ take the accusative.

##### <a id="s1404"></a> §1404

The object compared may be expressed by <span lang="grc">πρό, ἀντί</span> with the genitive, or by <span lang="grc">παρά, πρός</span> with the accusative. See under Prepositions. That _in which_ one thing is superior or inferior to another usually stands in the dative ([1513](06-dative.md#s1513), [1515](06-dative.md#s1515)).

### GENITIVE OF CAUSE

##### <a id="s1405"></a> §1405

With verbs of emotion the genitive denotes the cause. Such verbs are _to wonder at, admire_, _envy_, _praise_, _blame. hate_, _pity_, _grieve for_, _be angry at, take vengeance on_, and the like.

<span lang="grc">ἐθαύμασα τῆς τόλμης τῶν λεγόντων</span> _I wondered at the hardihood of the speakers_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.41">L. 12.41</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦτον ἀγασθεὶς τῆς πρᾳότητος</span> _admiring him for his mildness_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.3.21">X. C. 2.3.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">ζηλῶ σε τοῦ νοῦ, τῆς δὲ δειλίᾱς στυγῶ</span> _I envy thee for thy prudence_, _I hate thee for thy cowardice_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 1027">S. El. 1027</cite>, <span lang="grc">σὲ ηὐδαιμόνισα τοῦ τρόπου</span> _I thought you happy_ <!-- page 331 --> _because of your disposition_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 43b">P. Cr. 43b</cite>, <span lang="grc">συγχαίρω τῶν γεγενημένων</span> _I share the joy at what has happened_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 15.15">D. 15.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀνέχεσθαι τῶν οἰκείων ἀμελουμένων</span> _to put up with the neglect of my household affairs_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 31b">P. A. 31b</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸν ξένον δίκαιον αἰνέσαι προθῡμίᾱς</span> _it is right to praise the stranger for his zeal_ <cite data-ref="Eur. IA 1371">E. I. A. 1371</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὔποτ᾽ ἀνδρὶ τῷδε κηρῡκευμάτων μέμψῃ</span> _never wilt thou blame me for my tidings_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Seven 651">A. Sept. 651</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦ πάθους ᾤκτῑρεν αὐτόν</span> _he pitied him for his misery_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.4.32">X. C. 5.4.32</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδ᾽ εἰκὸς χαλεπῶς φέρειν αὐτών</span> _nor is it reasonable to grieve about them_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.62">T. 2.62</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκέτι ὧν οὗτοι κλέπτουσιν ὀργίζεσθε, ἀλλ᾽ ὧν αὐτοὶ λαμβάνετε χάριν ἴστε</span> _you are no longer angry at their thefts_, _but you are grateful for what you get yourselves_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 27.11">L. 27.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῑμωρήσασθαι αὐτοὺς τῆς ἐπιθέσεως</span> _to take revenge on them for their attack_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.4.23">X. A. 7.4.23</cite>. Here belongs, by analogy, <span lang="grc">συγγιγνώσκειν αὐτοῖς χρὴ τῆς ἐπιθῡμίᾱς</span> _it is necessary to forgive them for their desire_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyd. 306c">P. Eu. 306c</cite> (usually <span lang="grc">συγγιγνώσκειν τὴν ἐπιθῡμίᾱν τινί</span> or <span lang="grc">τῇ ἐπιθῡμίᾳ τινός</span>).

**a.** The genitive of cause is partly a true genitive, partly ablatival.

##### <a id="s1406"></a> §1406

With the above verbs the person stands in the accusative or dative. Some of these verbs take the dative or <span lang="grc">ἐπί</span> and the dative (e.g. <span lang="grc">ἀλγεῖν, στένειν, ἄχθεσθαι, φθονεῖν</span>) to express the cause of the emotion. See the Lexicon.

##### <a id="s1407"></a> §1407

The genitive of cause is used in exclamations and is often preceded by an interjection: <span lang="grc">φεῦ τοῦ ἀνδρός</span> _alas for the man!_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.39">X. C. 3.1.39</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῆς τύχης</span> _my ill luck!_ 2. 2. 3. In tragedy, the genitive of a pronoun or adjective after <span lang="grc">οἴμοι</span> or <span lang="grc">ὤμοι</span> refers to the second or third person. For the first person the nominative is used (<span lang="grc">οἴμοι τάλαινα</span> _ah me, miserable!_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 554">S. Ant. 554</cite>).

##### <a id="s1408"></a> §1408

Allied to the genitive of cause is the genitive of purpose in <span lang="grc">τοῦ</span> with the infinitive (esp. with <span lang="grc">μή</span>, 2032 e), and in expressions where <span lang="grc">ἕνεκα</span> is usually employed, as <span lang="grc">ἡ πᾶσ᾽ ἀπάτη συνεσκευάσθη τοῦ περὶ Φωκέᾱς ὀλέθρου</span> _the whole fraud was contrived for the purpose of ruining the Phocians_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.76">D. 19.76</cite>.

##### <a id="s1409"></a> §1409

Closely connected with the genitive of cause is the genitive with _verbs of disputing:_ <span lang="grc">οὐ βασιλεῖ ἀντιποιούμεθα τῆς ἀρχῆς</span> _we have no dispute with the king about his empire_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.23">X. A. 2.1.23</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἠμφισβήτησεν Ἐρεχθεῖ τῆς πόλεως</span> _he disputed the possession of the city with Erechtheus_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 12.193">I. 12.193</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἆρ᾽ οὖν μὴ ἡμῖν ἐναντιώσεται τῆς ἀπαγωγῆς</span>; _well then he will not oppose us about the removal_ (of the army), _will he?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.6.5">X. A. 7.6.5</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἀντιποιεῖσθαι</span> _claim_ may follow 1349 (<span lang="grc">τῆς πόλεως ἀντεποιοῦντο</span> _they laid claim to the city_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.122">T. 4.122</cite>). _Verbs of disputing_ are sometimes referred to 1343 or 1349.

### GENITIVE OF SOURCE

##### <a id="s1410"></a> §1410

The genitive may denote the source.

<span lang="grc">πίθων ἠφύσσετο οἶνος</span> _wine was broached from the casks_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 23.305"><span lang="grc">ψ</span> 305</cite>, <span lang="grc">Δᾱρείου καὶ Παρυσάτιδος γίγνονται παῖδες δύο</span> _of Darius and Parysatis are born two sons_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.1">X. A. 1.1.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">ταῦτα δέ σου τυχόντες</span> _obtaining this of you_ 6. 6. 32, <span lang="grc">μάθε μου καὶ τάδε</span> _learn this also from me_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.6.44">X. C. 1.6.44</cite>.

##### <a id="s1411"></a> §1411

With _verbs of hearing from_ and the like the genitive is probably ablatival rather than partitive ([1364](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1364)): <span lang="grc">ἐμοῦ ἀκούσεσθε πᾶσαν τὴν ἀλήθειαν</span> _from me you shall hear the whole truth_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 17b">P. A. 17b</cite>, <span lang="grc">τούτων πυνθάνομαι ὅτι οὐκ ἄβατόν ἐστι τὸ ὄρος</span> _I learn from these men that the mountain is not impassable_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.6.17">X. A. 4.6.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοιαῦτά</span> <!-- page 332 --> <span lang="grc">του παρόντος ἔκλυον</span> _such a tale I heard from some one who was present_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 424">S. El. 424</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰδέναι δέ σου χρῄζω</span> _I desire to know of thee_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 668">S. El. 668</cite>.

**a.** Usually (except with <span lang="grc">πυνθάνεσθαι</span>) we have <span lang="grc">παρά</span> (<span lang="grc">ἀπό</span> rarely), <span lang="grc">ἐξ</span> or <span lang="grc">πρός</span> (in poetry and Hdt.) with _verbs of hearing from_.

**b.** The genitive with <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span> in <span lang="grc">πατρὸς δ᾽ εἴμ᾽ ἀγαθοῖο</span> _I am of a good father_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 21.109"><span lang="grc">Φ</span> 109</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοιούτων μέν ἐστε προγόνων</span> _of such ancestors are you_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.13">X. A. 3.2.13</cite> is often regarded as a genitive of source, but is probably possessive.

### GENITIVE WITH ADJECTIVES

##### <a id="s1412"></a> §1412

The genitive is used with many adjectives corresponding in derivation or meaning to verbs taking the genitive.

##### <a id="s1413"></a> §1413

The adjective often borrows the construction with the genitive from that of the corresponding verb; but when the verb takes another case (especially the accusative), or when there is no verb corresponding to the adjective, the adjective may govern the genitive to express possession, connection more or less close, or by analogy. Many of the genitives in question may be classed as objective as well as partitive or ablatival. Rigid distinction between the undermentioned classes must not be insisted on.

##### <a id="s1414"></a> §1414

**Possession and Belonging** ([1297](03-genitive.md#s1297)).—<span lang="grc">ὁ ἔρως κοινὸς πάντων ἀνθρώπων</span> _love common to all men_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 205a">P. S. 205a</cite> (cp. <span lang="grc">κοινωνεῖν</span> [1343](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1343)), <span lang="grc">ἱερὸς τοῦ αὐτοῦ θεοῦ</span> _sacred to the same god_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 85b">P. Ph. 85b</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ κίνδῡνοι τῶν ἐφεστηκότων ἴδιοι</span> _the dangers belong to the commanders_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 2.28">D. 2.28</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">οἰκεῖος</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐπιχώριος</span> _peculiar to_. <span lang="grc">κοινός</span> (usually), <span lang="grc">οἰκεῖος</span> _inclined to, appropriate to_, and <span lang="grc">ἴδιος</span> also take the dative ([1499](06-dative.md#s1499)).

##### <a id="s1415"></a> §1415

**Sharing** ([1343](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1343)).—<span lang="grc">σοφίᾱς μέτοχος</span> _partaking in wisdom_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 689d">P. L. 689d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἰσόμοιροι πάντων</span> _having an equal share in everything_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.1.31">X. C. 2.1.31</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὕβρεως ἄμοιρος</span> _having no part in wantonness_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 181c">P. S. 181c</cite>. So <span lang="grc">&lt;\*&gt;κληρος</span> _without lot in_, <span lang="grc">ἀμέτοχος</span> _not sharing in_.

##### <a id="s1416"></a> §1416

**Touching**, **Desiring**, **Attaining**, **Tasting** ([1345](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1345), [1350](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1350), [1355](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1355)).—<span lang="grc">ἄψαυστος ἔγχους</span> _not touching a spear_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 969">S. O. T. 969</cite>, <span lang="grc">χάρις ὧν πρόθῡμοι γεγενήμεθα</span> _gratitude for the objects of our zeal_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.67">T. 3.67</cite>, <span lang="grc">παιδείᾱς ἐπήβολοι</span> _having attained to_ (possessed of) _culture_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 724b">P. L. 724b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐλευθερίᾱς ἄγευστος</span> _not tasting freedom_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 576a">P. R. 576a</cite>. So <span lang="grc">δύσερως</span> _passionately desirous of_.

##### <a id="s1417"></a> §1417

**Connection**.—<span lang="grc">ἀκόλουθα ἀλλήλων</span> _dependent on one another_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 11.12">X. O. 11.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ τούτων ἀδελφά</span> _what is akin to this_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hiero 1.22">X. Hi. 1.22</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῶν προειρημένων ἑπόμεναι ἀποδείξεις</span> _expositions agreeing with what had preceded_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 504b">P. R. 504b</cite>, <span lang="grc">φέγγος ὕπνου διάδοχον</span> _light succeeding sleep_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Phil. 867">S. Ph. 867</cite>. All these adjectives take also the dative; as does <span lang="grc">συγγενής</span> _akin_, which has become a substantive.

##### <a id="s1418"></a> §1418

**Capacity and Fitness**.—Adjectives in <span lang="grc">-ικός</span> from active verbs, and some others: <span lang="grc">παρασκευαστικὸν τῶν εἰς τὸν πόλεμον τὸν στρατηγὸν εἶναι χρὴ καὶ ποριστικὸν τῶν ἐπιτηδείων τοῖς στρατιώταις</span> _the general must be able to provide what is needed in war and to supply provisions for his men_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.1.6">X. M. 3.1.6</cite>. So <span lang="grc">διδασκαλικός</span> _able to instruct_, <span lang="grc">πρᾱκτικός</span> _able to effect_. Here may belong <span lang="grc">γάμου ὡραίᾱ</span> _ripe for marriage_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.6.9">X. C. 4.6.9</cite>.

##### <a id="s1419"></a> §1419

**Experience** ([1345](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1345)).—<span lang="grc">ὁδῶν ἔμπειρος</span> _acquainted with the roads_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.3.35">X. C. 5.3.35</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῆς θαλάσσης ἐπιστήμων</span> _acquainted with the sea_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.142">T. 1.142</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἰδιώτης τούτου</span> <!-- page 333 --> <span lang="grc">τοῦ ἔργου</span> _unskilled in this business_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 3.9">X. O. 3.9</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">τρίβων</span> _skilled in_, <span lang="grc">τυφλός</span> _blind_, <span lang="grc">ἄπειρος</span> _unacquainted_, <span lang="grc">ἀγύμναστος</span> _unpractised_, <span lang="grc">ἀπαίδευτος</span> _uneducated_, <span lang="grc">ἀήθης</span> _unaccustomed_, <span lang="grc">ὀψιμαθής</span> _late in learning_, <span lang="grc">φιλομαθής</span> _fond of learning_.

##### <a id="s1420"></a> §1420

**Remembering**, **Caring For** ([1356](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1356)).—<span lang="grc">κακῶν μνήμονες</span> _mindful of crime_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Eum. 382">A. Eum. 382</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπιμελὴς τῶν φίλων</span> _attentive to friends_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.6.35">X. M. 2.6.35</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀμνήμων τ ῶν κινδύ̄νων</span> _unmindful of dangers_ Ant. 2. <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 1.7"><span lang="grc">α</span>. 7</cite>; and, by analogy, <span lang="grc">συγγνώμων τῶν ἀνθρωπίνων ἁμαρτημάτων</span> _forgiving of human errors_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.1.37">X. C. 6.1.37</cite>. So <span lang="grc">ἀμελής</span> _careless of_, <span lang="grc">ἐπιλήσμων</span> _forgetful of_.

##### <a id="s1421"></a> §1421

**Perception** ([1361](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1361)).—Compounds in <span lang="grc">-ήκοος</span> from <span lang="grc">ἀκούω</span>: <span lang="grc">λόγων καλῶν ἐπήκοοι</span> _hearers of noble words_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 499a">P. R. 499a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπήκοοι Θεσσαλῶν</span> _subjects of the Thessalians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.78">T. 4.78</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑπήκοος τῶν γονέων</span> _obedient to parents_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 463d">P. R. 463d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀνήκοοι παιδείᾱς</span> _ignorant of culture_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 1.141">Aes. 1.141</cite>. So <span lang="grc">συνήκοος</span> _hearing together_, <span lang="grc">κατήκοος</span> _obeying_. <span lang="grc">ἐπήκοος, κατήκοος</span>, and <span lang="grc">ὑπήκοος</span> also take the dative.

##### <a id="s1422"></a> §1422

**Fulness** ([1369](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1369)).—<span lang="grc">χαρᾶς ἡ πόλις ἦν μεστή</span> _the city was full of rejoicing_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.217">D. 18.217</cite>, <span lang="grc">παράδεισος ἀγρίων θηρίων πλήρης</span> _a park full of wild beasts_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.7">X. A. 1.2.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">πλουσιώτερος φρονήσεως</span> _richer in good sense_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Stat. 261e">P. Pol. 261e</cite>, <span lang="grc">φιλόδωρος εὐμεϝείᾱς</span> _generous of good-will_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 197d">P. S. 197d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄπληστος χρημάτων</span> _greedy of money_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.2.20">X. C. 8.2.20</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">ἔμπλεως, σύμπλεως. πλήρης</span> may take the dative.

##### <a id="s1423"></a> §1423

**Ruling** ([1370](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1370)).—<span lang="grc">ταύτης κύ̄ριος τῆς χώρᾱς</span> _master of this country_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 3.16">D. 3.16</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀκρατὴς ὀργῆς</span> _unrestrained in passion_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.84">T. 3.84</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">ἐγκρατής</span> _master of_, <span lang="grc">αὐτοκράτωρ</span> _complete master of_, <span lang="grc">ἀκράτωρ</span> _intemperate in_.

##### <a id="s1424"></a> §1424

**Value** ([1372](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1372)).—<span lang="grc">τάπις ἀξίᾱ δέκα μνῶν</span> _a rug worth ten minae_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.3.27">X. A. 7.3.27</cite>, <span lang="grc">δόξα χρημάτων οὐκ ὠνητή</span> _reputation is not to be bought for money_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 2.32">I. 2.32</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">ἀντάξιος</span> _worth_, <span lang="grc">ἰσόρροπος</span> _in equal poise with_ (<cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.42">T. 2.42</cite>), <span lang="grc">ἀξιόχρεως</span> _sufficient_, <span lang="grc">ἀνάξιος</span> _unworthy_. <span lang="grc">ἄξιόν τινι</span> with the infinitive denotes _it is meet for a person to do something_ or the like.

##### <a id="s1425"></a> §1425

**Accountability** ([1375](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1375)).—<span lang="grc">αἴτιος τούτων</span> _accountable for this_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 447a">P. G. 447a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔνοχος λιποταξίου</span> _liable to a charge of desertion_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 14.5">L. 14.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀσεβείᾱς ὑπόδικος</span> _subject to a trial for impiety_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 907e">P. L. 907e</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑποτελὴς φόρου</span> _subject to tribute_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.19">T. 1.19</cite>, <span lang="grc">τούτων ὑπεύθῡνος ὑ̄μῖν</span> _responsible to you for this_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.69">D. 8.69</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀθῷοι τῶν ἀδικημάτων</span> _unpunished for offences_ Lyc. 79. <span lang="grc">ἔνοχος</span> usually takes the dative, and so <span lang="grc">ὑπεύθῡνος</span> meaning _dependent on_ or _exposed to_. The above compounds of <span lang="grc">ὑπό</span> take the genitive by virtue of the substantive contained in them.

##### <a id="s1426"></a> §1426

**Place**.—<span lang="grc">ἐναντίος</span> _opposite_ and a few other adjectives denoting nearness or approach ([1353](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1353)) may take the genitive, chiefly in poetry: <span lang="grc">ἐναντίοι ἔσταν Ἀχαιῶν</span> _they stood opposite the Achaeans_ P 343. Cp. <span lang="grc">τοῦ Πόντου ἐπικάρσιαι</span> _at an angle with the Pontus_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 7.36">Hdt. 7.36</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἐναντίος</span> usually takes the dative.

##### <a id="s1427"></a> §1427

**Separation** ([1392](#s1392)).—<span lang="grc">φίλων ἀγαθῶν ἔρημοι</span> _deprived of good friends_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.4.24">X. M. 4.4.24</cite>, <span lang="grc">ψῡχὴ ψῑλὴ σώματος</span> _the soul separated from the body_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 899a">P. L. 899a</cite>, <span lang="grc">φειδωλοὶ χρημάτων</span> _sparing of money_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 548b">P. R. 548b</cite> (or perhaps under [1356](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1356)), <span lang="grc">ὕ̄λης καθαρόν</span> _clear of undergrowth_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 16.13">X. O. 16.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄπαυστος γόων</span> _never ceasing lamentations_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Supp. 82">E. Supp. 82</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">ἐλεύθερος</span> _free from_, <span lang="grc">ἁγνός</span> _pure from, innocent of_, <span lang="grc">ὀρφανός</span> _bereft of_, <span lang="grc">γυμνός</span> _stripped of_, <span lang="grc">μόνος</span> _alone_.

##### <a id="s1428"></a> §1428

**Compounds of alpha privative**.—In addition to the adjectives with _alpha privative_ which take the genitive by reason of the notion expressed in the <!-- page 334 --> verb, or by analogy, there are many others, some of which take the genitive because of the idea of separation, especially when the genitive is of kindred meaning and an attributive adjective is added for the purpose of more exact definition. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἄτῑμος</span> _deprived of_, <span lang="grc">ἀπαθής</span> _not suffering_, <span lang="grc">ἀτελής</span> _free from_ ([1392](#s1392)): as <span lang="grc">τῑμῆς ἄτῑμος</span> _deprived of honour_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 774b">P. L. 774b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄπαις ἀρρένων παίδων</span> _without male children_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 12.126">I. 12.126</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦ ἡδίστου θεά̄ματος ἀθέᾱτος</span> _not seeing the most pleasant sight_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.1.31">X. M. 2.1.31</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄφωνος τῆσδε τῆς ἀρᾶς</span> _without uttering this curse_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 865">S. O. C. 865</cite>. This is more frequent in poetry than prose.

**a.** So when the adjectives are passive: <span lang="grc">φίλων ἄκλαυτος</span> _unwept by friends_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 847">S. Ant. 847</cite>, cp. <span lang="grc">κακῶν δυσάλωτος οὐδείς</span> _no one is hard for evil fortune to capture_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 1722">S. O. C. 1722</cite>. The genitive with adjectives in _alpha privative_ is sometimes called the _genitive of relation_.

##### <a id="s1429"></a> §1429

**Want** ([1396](#s1396)).—<span lang="grc">ἅρματα κενὰ ἡνιόχων</span> _chariots deprived of their drivers_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.8.20">X. A. 1.8.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐνδεὴς ἀρετῆς</span> _lacking virtue_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 381c">P. R. 381c</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">πένης</span> _poor_, <span lang="grc">ἐλλιπής</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐπιδεής</span> _lacking_.

##### <a id="s1430"></a> §1430

**Distinction** ([1401](#s1401)).—<span lang="grc">διάφορος τῶν ἄλλων</span> _different from the rest_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Parm. 160d">P. Par. 160d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἕτερον τὸ ἡδὺ τοῦ ἀγαθοῦ</span> _pleasure is different from what is good_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 500d">P. So with <span lang="grc">ἀλλοῖος</span> and <span lang="grc">ἀλλότριος</span> _alien from_ (also with dat. _unfavourable to, disinclined to_). <span lang="grc">διάφορος</span> with dative means _at variance with_.

##### <a id="s1431"></a> §1431

**Comparison** ([1402](#s1402)).—Adjectives of the comparative degree or implying comparison take the genitive. The genitive denotes the standard or point of departure from which the comparison is made, and often expresses a condensed comparison when actions are compared. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἤττων ἀμαθὴς σοφοῦ, δειλὸς ἀνδρείου</span> _an ignorant man is inferior to a wise man, a coward to a brave man_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 239a">P. Phae. 239a</cite>, <span lang="grc">κρεῖττόν ἐστι λόγου τὸ κάλλος τῆς γυναικός</span> _the beauty of the woman is too great for description_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.11.1">X. M. 3.11.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">Ἐπύαξα προτέρᾱ Κύ̄ρου πέντε ἡμέραις ἀφί̄κετο</span> _Epyaxa arrived five days before Cyrus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.25">X. A. 1.2.25</cite>, <span lang="grc">καταδεεστέρᾱν τὴν δόξαν τῆς ἐλπίδος ἔλαβεν</span> _the reputation he acquired fell short of his expectation_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 2.7">I. 2.7</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">δεύτερος, ὑστεραῖος, περιττός</span>. Comparatives with <span lang="grc">ἤ</span>, 1069.

##### <a id="s1432"></a> §1432

So with _multiplicatives_ in <span lang="grc">-πλοῦς</span> and <span lang="grc">-πλάσιος</span>: <span lang="grc">διπλάσια ἀπέδωκεν ὧν ἔλαβεν</span> _it returned double what it received_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.3.38">X. C. 8.3.38</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">πολλοστός</span>.

##### <a id="s1433"></a> §1433

The genitive with the comparative often takes the place of <span lang="grc">ἤ</span> with another construction: <span lang="grc">ἀ̄θλιώτερόν ἐστι μὴ ὑγιοῦς σώματος</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἢ μὴ ὑγιεῖ σώματι</span>) <span lang="grc">μὴ ὑγιεῖ ψῡχῇ συνοικεῖν</span> _it is more wretched to dwell with a diseased soul than a diseased body_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 479b">P. G. 479b</cite>, <span lang="grc">πλείοσι ναυσὶ τῶν Ἀθηναίων</span> ( = <span lang="grc">ἢ οἱ Ἀθηναῖοι</span>) <span lang="grc">παρῆσαν</span> _they came with more ships than the Athenians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.52">T. 8.52</cite>.

##### <a id="s1434"></a> §1434

The superlative with the genitive is both partitive and ablatival; the latter, when a thing is compared with many things taken singly. Thus, <span lang="grc">σοφώτατος ἀνθρώπων</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 22c">P. A. 22c</cite> means _wisest among men_ (part.) and _wiser than any other single man_. The partitive idea is the stronger. The comparative and the superlative idea are both expressed in <span lang="grc">ἀνὴρ ἐπιεικὴς υἱὸν ἀπολέσᾱς οἴσει ῥᾷστα τῶν ἄλλων</span> _a reasonable man will bear the loss of a son more easily than other men_ (and _most easily of all men_) <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 603e">P. R. 603e</cite>, <span lang="grc">στρατείᾱ μεγίστη τῶν πρὸ αὐτῆς</span> _an expedition greater than any preceding it_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.10">T. 1.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῶν ἄλλων ὕστατοι</span> _the last among nations_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.72">D. 8.72</cite>. Cp. <span lang="grc">μόνος τῶν ἄλλων</span> = _alone of all_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.223">D. 21.223</cite>. <!-- page 335 -->

##### <a id="s1435"></a> §1435

**Cause** ([1405](#s1405)).—<span lang="grc">εὐδαίμων τοῦ τρόπου</span> _happy because of his disposition_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 58e">P. Ph. 58e</cite>, <span lang="grc">δείλαιος τῆς συμφορᾶς</span> _wretched because of thy lot_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 1347">S. O. T. 1347</cite>, <span lang="grc">βάλανοι θαυμάσιαι τοῦ μεγέθους</span> _dates wonderful for their size_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.3.15">X. A. 2.3.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">περίφοβος τοῦ καταφρονηθῆναι</span> _fearful of becoming an object of contempt_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 239b">P. Phae. 239b</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">τάλᾱς</span> and <span lang="grc">τλήμων</span> _wretched_.

##### <a id="s1436"></a> §1436

**Free Use.—a.** Compound adjectives formed of a preposition and substantive may take a genitive dependent on the substantive: <span lang="grc">σκηνῆς ὕπαυλος</span> _under the shelter of the tent_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 796">S. Aj. 796</cite> ( = <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ αὐλῇ</span>). Frequent in poetry.

**b.** Some adjectives are freely used with the genitive in poetry, as <span lang="grc">λάμοι Πάριδος ὀλέθριοι φίλων</span> _the marriage of Paris bringing ruin on his friends_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Ag. 1156">A. Ag. 1156</cite>. This is rare in prose: <span lang="grc">τὸ πῦρ ἐπίκουρον ψύ̄χους</span> _fire that protects against cold_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.3.7">X. M. 4.3.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">κακοῦργος μὲν τῶν ἄλλων, ἑαυτοῦ δὲ κακουργότερος</span> _doing evil to the others but more to himself_ 1. 5. 3, <span lang="grc">ὁ τῆς Ἑλλάδος ἀλιτήριος</span> _the curse and destroyer of Greece_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.157">Aes. 3.157</cite>. These adjectives are practically equivalent to substantives. Cp. _amans patriae_.

[1349](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1349)), <span lang="grc">εὐθὺ Λυκείου</span> _straight for the Lyceum_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lysis 203b">P. ##### <a id="s1438"></a> §1438

An adverb with <span lang="grc">ἔχειν</span> or <span lang="grc">διακεῖσθαι</span> is often used as a periphrasis for an adjective with <span lang="grc">εἶναι</span> or for a verb.

##### <a id="s1439"></a> §1439

The genitive is used with many adverbs (a) of place, (b) of time, (c) of quantity.

**a.** <span lang="grc">ἐμβαλεῖν που τῆς ἐκείνων χώρᾱς</span> _to make an attack at some point of their country_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 6.1.42">X. C. 6.1.42</cite>, <span lang="grc">αἰσθόμενος οὗ ἦν κακοῦ</span> _perceiving what a plight he was in_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 23.156">D. 23.156</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἷ προελήλυθ᾽ ἀσελγείᾱς</span> _to what a pitch of wanton arrogance he has come_ 4. 9, <span lang="grc">ἐνταῦθα τῆς πολῑτείᾱς</span> _at that point of the administration_ 18. 62, <span lang="grc">εἰδέναι ὅπου γῆς ἐστιν</span> _to know where in the world he is_ <cite data-ref="Plat. **b.** <span lang="grc">πηνίκ᾽ ἐστὶν ἄρα τῆς ἡμέρᾱς</span>; _at what time of day?_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Birds 1498">Ar. Av. 1498</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῆς ἡμέρᾱς ὀψέ</span> _late in the day_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.1.23">X. H. 2.1.23</cite>.

**c.** <span lang="grc">τῶν τοιούτων ἅδην</span> _enough of such matters_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Charm. 153d">P. Charm. 153d</cite>, <span lang="grc">τούτων ἅλι&lt;\*&gt;</span> _enough of this_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.7.25">X. C. 8.7.25</cite>.

##### <a id="s1440"></a> §1440

Most of the genitives in 1439 are partitive. Some of the adverbs falling under 1437 take also the dative (<span lang="grc">ἄγχι, ἐγγύς, πλησίον</span> in the poets, <span lang="grc">ἑξῆς, ἐφεξῆς</span>).

##### <a id="s1441"></a> §1441

The genitive is used with adverbs of manner, especially with the intransitive <span lang="grc">ἔχω, ἥκω</span> (Hdt.). The genitive usually has no article: <span lang="grc">ὡς τάχους ἕκαστος εἶχεν</span> _as fast as each could_ (with what measure of speed he had) <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.5.15">X. H. 4.5.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡς ποδῶν εἶχον</span> _as fast as my legs could carry me_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 6.116">Hdt. 6.116</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔχοντες εὖ φρενῶν</span> _being in their right minds_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hipp. 462">E. Hipp. 462</cite>, <span lang="grc">εὖ σώματος ἕξειν</span> _to be in good bodily condition_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 404d">P. R. 404d</cite> (cp. ### GENITIVE OF TIME AND PLACE

##### <a id="s1444"></a> §1444

**Time**.—The genitive denotes the time _within which_, or at a certain point _of which_, an action takes place. As contrasted with the accusative of time ([1582](07-accusative.md#s1582)), the genitive denotes a portion of time. Hence the genitive of time is partitive. Cp. <span lang="grc">τὸν μὲν χειμῶνα ὕ̄ει ὁ θεός, τοῦ δὲ θέρεος χρηίσκονται τῷ ὕδατι</span> _during the_ (entire) _winter the goo&lt;\*&gt;rains_, _but in_ (a part of) _summer they need the water_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 3.117">Hdt. 3.117</cite>.

<span lang="grc">ἡμέρᾱς</span> _by day_, <span lang="grc">νυκτός</span> _at_ or _by night_, <span lang="grc">μεσημβρίᾱς</span> _at midday_, <span lang="grc">δείλης</span> _in the afternoon_, <span lang="grc">ἑσπέρᾱς</span> _in the evening_, <span lang="grc">θέρους</span> _in summer_, <span lang="grc">χειμῶνος</span> _in winter_, <span lang="grc">ἦρος</span> _in spring_, <span lang="grc">ὀπώρᾱς</span> _in autumn_, <span lang="grc">τοῦ λοιποῦ</span> _in the future_. The addition of article or attributive usually defines the time more exactly. Thus, <span lang="grc">οὐκοῦν ἡδὺ μὲν θέρους ψῡχεινὴν ἔχειν, ἡδὺ δὲ χειμῶνος ἀλεεινήν</span>; _is it not pleasant to have_ (_a house_) _cool in summer, and warm in winter?_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 3.8.9">X. M. 3.8.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">ᾤχετο τῆς νυκτός</span> _he departed during the night_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.2.17">X. A. 7.2.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">καὶ ἡμέρᾱς καὶ νυκτὸς ἄγων ἐπὶ τοὺς πολεμίους</span> _both by day and by night leading against the enemy_ 2. 6. 7, <span lang="grc">ἔλεγον τοῦ λοιποῦ μηκέτι</span> <!-- page 337 --> <span lang="grc">ἐξεῖναι ἀνομίᾱς ἄρξαι</span> _they said that for the future_ (at any time in the future) _it should no longer be permitted to set an example of lawlessness_ 5. 7. 34. (Distinguish <span lang="grc">τὸ λοιπόν</span> _for the_ (entire) _future_ 3. 2. 8.) <span lang="grc">ἐντός</span> _within_ is sometimes added to the genitive.

##### <a id="s1445"></a> §1445

The addition of the article may have a distributive sense: <span lang="grc">δραχμὴν ἐλάμβανε τῆς ἡμέρᾱς</span> _he received a drachm a day_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.17">T. 3.17</cite>.

##### <a id="s1446"></a> §1446

The genitive may denote the time _since_ an action has happened or the time _until_ an action will happen: <span lang="grc">οὐδείς μέ πω ἠρώτηκε καινὸν οὐδὲν πολλῶν ἐτῶν</span> _for many years nobody has put a new question to me_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 448a">P. G. 448a</cite>, <span lang="grc">βασιλεὺς οὐ μαχεῖται δέκα ἡμερῶν</span> _the king will not fight for ten days_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.18">X. A. 1.7.18</cite>.

##### <a id="s1447"></a> §1447

The genitive may or may not denote a definite part of the time during which anything takes place; the dative fixes the time explicitly either by specifying a definite point in a given period or by contracting the whole period to a definite point; the accusative expresses the whole extent of time from beginning to end: cp. <span lang="grc">τῇ δὲ ὑστεραίᾳ οἱ μὲν Ἀθηναῖοι τό τε προάστειον εἷλον καὶ τὴν ἡμέρᾱν ἅπᾱσαν ἐδῄουν τὴν γῆν, οἵ τε τριᾱκόσιοι τῶν Σκιωναίων τῆς ἐπιούσης νυκτὸς ἀπεχώρησαν</span> _on the next day the Athenians captured the suburb and laid waste the land for that entire day, while the three hundred Scionaeans departed in the course of the following night_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.130">T. 4.130</cite>; <span lang="grc">ἡμέρᾳ δὲ ἀρξάμενοι τρίτῃ ὡς οἴκοθεν ὥρμησαν, ταύτην τε εἰργάζοντο καὶ τὴν τετάρτην καὶ τῆς πέμπτης μέχρι ἀ̄ρίστου</span> _beginning on the third day after their departure, they continued their work_ (all) _this day and the fourth_, _and on the fifth until the mid-day meal_ 4. 90.

**a.** The genitive of time is less common than the dative of time ([1539](06-dative.md#s1539)) with ordinals, or with <span lang="grc">ὅδε, οὗτος, ἐκεῖνος</span>; as <span lang="grc">ταύτης τῆς νυκτός</span> <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.97">T. 6.97</cite>, <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 44a">P. Cr. 44a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐκείνου τοῦ μηνός</span> _in the course of that month_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.8.2">X. M. 4.8.2</cite>. For <span lang="grc">θέρους</span> we find <span lang="grc">ἐν θέρει</span> rarely and, in poetry, <span lang="grc">θέρει</span>. <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.133">T. 4.133</cite> has both <span lang="grc">τοῦ αὐτοῦ θέρους</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐν τῷ αὐτῷ θέρει</span> _in the course of the same summer;_ cp. <span lang="grc">ἶσος ῥέει ἔν τε θέρεϊ καὶ χειμῶνι ὁ Ἴστρος</span> <cite data-ref="Hdt. 4.50">Hdt. 4.50</cite> and <span lang="grc">Ἴστρος ἶσος ῥέει θέρεος καὶ χειμῶνος</span> 4. 48 (_the Ister flows with the same volume in summer and winter_).

##### <a id="s1448"></a> §1448

**Place**.—The genitive denotes the place _within which_ or _at which_ an action happens. This is more frequent in poetry than in prose.

<span lang="grc">πεδίοιο διωκέμεν</span> _to chase over the plain_ E 222, <span lang="grc">ἷζεν τοίχου τοῦ ἑτέροιο</span> _he was sitting by the other wall_ (lit. in a place of the wall) I 219, <span lang="grc">λελουμένος Ὠκεανοῖο</span> _having bathed in Oceanus_ E 6, <span lang="grc">οὔτε Πύλου ἱερῆς οὔτ᾽ Ἄργεος οὔτε Μυκήνης</span> _neither in sacred Pylos nor in Argos nor in Mycenae_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 21.108"><span lang="grc">φ</span> 108</cite>, <span lang="grc">τόνδ᾽ εἰσεδέξω τειχέων</span> _thou didst admit this man within the walls_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Phoen. 451">E. Phoen. 451</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἰέναι τοῦ πρόσω</span> _to go forward_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.3.1">X. A. 1.3.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπετάχῡνον τῆς ὁδοῦ τοὺς σχολαίτερον προσιόντας</span> _they hastened on their way those who came up more slowly_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.47">T. 4.47</cite>; <span lang="grc">λαιᾶς χειρὸς οἰκοῦσι</span> _they dwell on the left hand_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. PB 714">A. Pr. 714</cite> (possibly ablatival).

##### <a id="s1449"></a> §1449

Many adverbs of place are genitives in form (<span lang="grc">αὐτοῦ</span> _there_, <span lang="grc">ποῦ</span> _where?_ <span lang="grc">οὐδαμοῦ</span> _nowhere_). Cp. [341](../../part-2-inflection/07-adverbs.md#s341).
