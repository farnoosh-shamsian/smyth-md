# THE CASES: GENITIVE

##### <a id="s1289"></a> §1289

The genitive most commonly limits the meaning of substantives, adjectives, and adverbs, less commonly that of verbs.

Since the genitive has absorbed the ablative it includes (1) the genitive proper, denoting the class to which a person or thing belongs, and (2) the ablatival genitive.

**a.** The name _genitive_ is derived from _casus genitivus, the case of origin_, the inadequate Latin translation of <span lang="grc">γενικὴ πτῶσις</span> _case denoting the class_.

## THE GENITIVE PROPER WITH NOUNS: (ADNOMINAL GENITIVE)

##### <a id="s1290"></a> §1290

A substantive in the genitive limits the meaning of a substantive on which it depends.

##### <a id="s1291"></a> §1291

The genitive limits for the time being the scope of the substantive on which it depends by referring it to a particular class or description, or by regarding it as a part of a whole. The genitive is akin in meaning to the adjective and may often be translated by an epithet. Cp. <span lang="grc">στέφανος χρῡσίου</span> with <span lang="grc">χρῡσοῦς στέφανος, φόβος πολεμίου</span> with <span lang="grc">πολέμιος φόβος, τὸ εὖρος πλέθρου</span> with <span lang="grc">τὸ εὖρος πλεθριαῖον</span> ([1035](../14-adjectives.md#s1035)). But the use of the adjective is not everywhere parallel to that of the genitive.

##### <a id="s1292"></a> §1292

In poetry a genitive is often used with <span lang="grc">βίᾱ, μένος, σθένος</span> _might_, etc., instead of the corresponding adjective: <span lang="grc">βίη Διομήδεος</span> _mighty Diomede_ E 781.

##### <a id="s1293"></a> §1293

In poetry <span lang="grc">δέμας</span> _form_, <span lang="grc">κάρᾱ</span> and <span lang="grc">κεφαλή</span> _head_, etc., are used with a genitive to express majestic or loved persons or objects: <span lang="grc">Ἰσμήνης κάρᾱ</span> <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 1">S. Ant. 1</cite>.

##### <a id="s1294"></a> §1294

<span lang="grc">χρῆμα</span> _thing_ is used in prose with a genitive to express size, strength, etc.: <span lang="grc">σφενδονητῶν πάμπολύ τι χρῆμα</span> _a very large mass of slingers_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.1.5">X. C. 2.1.5</cite>. Cp. [1322](#s1322). <!-- page 314 -->

##### <a id="s1295"></a> §1295

The genitive with substantives denotes in general a connection or dependence between two words. This connection must often be determined (1) by the meaning of the words, (2) by the context, (3) by the facts presupposed as known ([1301](#s1301)). The same construction may often be placed under more than one of the different classes mentioned below; and the connection between the two substantives is often so loose that it is difficult to include with precision all cases under specific grammatical classes.

**a.** The two substantives may be so closely connected as to be equivalent to a single compound idea: <span lang="grc">τελευτὴ τοῦ βίου</span> ‘life-end’ (cp. _life-time_) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.1.1">X. A. 1.1.1</cite>. Cp. [1146](../16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1146).

**b.** The genitive with substantives has either the attributive ([1154](../16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1154)), or, in the case of the genitive of the divided whole ([1306](#s1306)), and of personal pronouns ([1185](../16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1185)), the predicate, position ([1168](../16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1168)).

##### <a id="s1296"></a> §1296

Words denoting number, especially numerals or substantives with numerals, often agree in case with the limited word instead of standing in the genitive: <span lang="grc">φόρος τέσσαρα τάλαντα</span> _a tribute of four talents_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.57">T. 4.57</cite> (cp. [1323](#s1323)), <span lang="grc">ἐς τὰ̄ς ναῦς, αἳ ἐφρούρουν δύο, καταφυγόντες</span> _fleeing to the ships, two of which were keeping guard_ 4. 113. So with <span lang="grc">οἱ μέν, οἱ δέ</span> in apposition to the subject ([981](../10-concord-of-subject-and-predicate.md#s981)).

### GENITIVE OF POSSESSION OR BELONGING

##### <a id="s1297"></a> §1297

The genitive denotes ownership, possession, or belonging: <span lang="grc">ἡ οἰκίᾱ ἡ Σίμωνος</span> _the house of Simon_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 3.32">L. 3.32</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὁ Κύ̄ρου στόλος</span> _the expedition of Cyrus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.5">X. A. 1.2.5</cite>. Cp. the dative of possession ([1476](06-dative.md#s1476)).

##### <a id="s1298"></a> §1298

Here may be classed the genitive of origin: <span lang="grc">οἱ Σόλωνος νόμοι</span> _the laws of Solon_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 20.103">D. 20.103</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ ἐπιστολὴ τοῦ Φιλίππου</span> _the letter of Philip_ 18. 37, <span lang="grc">κύ̄ματα παντοίων ἀνέμων</span> _waves caused by all kinds of winds_ B 396.

##### <a id="s1299"></a> §1299

The possessive genitive is used with the neuter article (singular or plural) denoting affairs, conditions, power, and the like: <span lang="grc">τὸ τῶν ἐφόρων</span> _the power of the ephors_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 712d">P. L. 712d</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ τῆς τέχνης</span> _the function of the art_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 450c">P. G. 450c</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ τοῦ Σόλωνος</span> _the maxim of Solon_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Lach. 188b">P. Lach. 188b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄδηλα τὰ τῶν πολέμων</span> _the chances of war are uncertain_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.11">T. 2.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ τῆς πόλεως</span> _the interests of the State_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 36c">P. A. 36c</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ τοῦ δήμου φρονεῖ</span> _is on the side of the people_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Kn. 1216">Ar. Eq. 1216</cite>. Sometimes this is almost a mere periphrasis for the thing itself: <span lang="grc">τὸ τῆς τύχης</span> _chance_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 4.12">D. 4.12</cite> <span lang="grc">τὰ τῆς σωτηρίᾱς</span> _safety_ 23. 163, <span lang="grc">τὸ τῆς ὁσίᾱς, ὁτιδήποτ᾽ ἐστί</span> _the quality of holiness, whatever it is_ 21. 126, <span lang="grc">τὸ τῶν πρεσβυτέρων ἡμῶν</span> _we elders_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 657d">P. L. 657d</cite>. So <span lang="grc">τὸ τούτου</span> <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 124">S. Aj. 124</cite> is almost = <span lang="grc">οὗτος</span>, as <span lang="grc">τοὐμόν</span> is = <span lang="grc">ἐγώ</span> or <span lang="grc">ἐμέ</span>. Cp. <cite data-ref="Lys. 8.19">L. 8.19</cite>.

##### <a id="s1300"></a> §1300

The genitive of possession may be used after a demonstrative or relative pronoun: <span lang="grc">τοῦτό μου διαβάλλει</span> _he attacks this action of mine_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.28">D. 18.28</cite>.

##### <a id="s1301"></a> §1301

With persons the genitive may denote the relation of child to parent, wife to husband, and of inferior to superior: <span lang="grc">Θουκῡδίδης ὁ Ὀλόρου</span> _Thucydides_, _the son of Olorus_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.104">T. 4.104</cite> (and so <span lang="grc">υιός</span> is regularly omitted in Attic official documents), <span lang="grc">Διὸς Ἄρτεμις</span> _Artemis_, _daughter of Zeus_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 172">S. Aj. 172</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ Σμῑκυθίωνος Μελιστίχη</span> _Melistiche wife of Smicythion_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Eccl. 46">Ar. Eccl. 46</cite>, <span lang="grc">Αῡδὸς ὁ Φερεκλέους</span> _Lydus_, _the slave of Pherecles_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.17">And. 1.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ Μένωνος</span> _the troops of Menon_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.5.13">X. A. 1.5.13</cite> (<span lang="grc">οἱ τοῦ Μένωνος στρατιῶται</span> 1. 5. [11](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s11)). <!-- page 315 -->

**a.** In poetry we may have an attributive adjective: <span lang="grc">Τελαμώνιος Αἴᾱς</span> ( = <span lang="grc">Αἴᾱς ὁ Τελαμῶνος</span>) B 528. Cp. [846 f](../../part-3-formation-of-words/02-formation-of-substantives.md#s846).

##### <a id="s1302"></a> §1302

The word on which the possessive genitive depends may be represented by the article: <span lang="grc">ἀπὸ τῆς ἑαυτῶν</span> _from their own_ country (<span lang="grc">γῆς</span>) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.15">T. 1.15</cite> (cp. [1027](../14-adjectives.md#s1027) b). A word for _dwelling_ (<span lang="grc">οἰκίᾱ, δόμος</span>, and also <span lang="grc">ἱερόν</span>) is perhaps omitted after <span lang="grc">ἐν, εἰς</span>, and sometimes after <span lang="grc">ἐξ</span>. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἐν Ἀρίφρονος</span> _at Ariphron's_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 320a">P. Pr. 320a</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν Διονύ̄σου</span> (_scil_. <span lang="grc">ἱερῷ</span>) _at the shrine of Dionysus_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 5.7">D. 5.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰς διδασκάλου φοιτᾶν</span> _to go to school_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.3.9">X. C. 2.3.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐκ Πατροκλέους ἔρχομαι</span> _I come from Patroclus's_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Pl. 84">Ar. Plut. 84</cite>. So, in Homer, <span lang="grc">εἰν</span>(<span lang="grc">εἰς</span>) <span lang="grc">Ἀίδᾱο</span>.

##### <a id="s1303"></a> §1303

**Predicate Use**.—The genitive may be connected with the noun it limits by means of a verb.

<span lang="grc">Ἱπποκράτης ἐστὶ οἰκίᾱς μεγάλης</span> _Hippocrates is of an influential house_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 316b">P. Pr. 316b</cite>, <span lang="grc">Βοιωτῶν ἡ πόλις ἔσται</span> _the city will belong to the Boeotians_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.58">L. 12.58</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ Ζέλειά ἐστι τῆς Ἀσίᾱς</span> _Zelea is in Asia_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 9.43">D. 9.43</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδὲ τῆς αὐτῆς Θρᾴκης ἐγένοντο</span> _nor did they belong to the same Thrace_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.29">T. 2.29</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἃ διώκει τοῦ ψηφίσματος, ταῦτ᾽ ἐστίν</span> _the clauses in the bill which he attacks, are these_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.56">D. 18.56</cite>.

##### <a id="s1304"></a> §1304

The genitive with <span lang="grc">εἰμί</span> may denote the person whose _nature_, _duty_, _custom_, etc., it is to do that set forth in an infinitive subject of the verb: <span lang="grc">πενίᾱν φέρειν οὐ παντός, ἀλλ᾽ ἀνδρὸς σοφοῦ</span> '_tis the sage, not every one, who can bear poverty_ Men. Sent. 463, <span lang="grc">δοκεῖ δικαίου τοῦτ᾽ εἶναι πολί̄του</span> _this seems to be the duty of a just citizen_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.72">D. 8.72</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῶν νῑκώντων ἐστὶ καὶ τὰ ἑαυτῶν σῴζειν καὶ τὰ τῶν ἡττωμένων λαμβάνειν</span> _it is the custom of conquerors to keep what is their own and to take the possessions of the defeated_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.39">X. A. 3.2.39</cite>.

##### <a id="s1305"></a> §1305

With verbs signifying to refer or attribute, by thought, word, or action, anything to a person or class. Such verbs are _to think, regard_, _make_, _name_, _choose_, _appoint_, etc.

<span lang="grc">λογίζου . . . τὰ δ᾽ ἄλλα τῆς τύχης</span> _deem that the rest belongs to chance_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Alc. 789">E. Alc. 789</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῶν ἐλευθερωτάτων οἴκων νομισθεῖσα</span> _deemed a daughter of a house most free_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Andr. 12">E. And. 12</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐμὲ γράφε τῶν ἱππεύειν ὑπερεπιθῡμούντων</span> _put me down as one of those who desire exceedingly to serve on horseback_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 4.3.21">X. C. 4.3.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῆς πρώτης τάξεως τεταγμένος</span> _assigned to the first class_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 14.11">L. 14.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῆς ἀγαθῆς τύχης τῆς πόλεως εἶναι τίθημι</span> _I reckon as belonging to the good fortune of the State_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.254">D. 18.254</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ δέ τινες τὴν Ἀσίᾱν ἑαυτῶν ποιοῦνται</span> _but if some are claiming Asia as their own_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ages. 1.33">X. Ages. 1.33</cite>, <span lang="grc">νομίζει ὑ̄μᾶς ἑαυτοῦ εἶναι</span> _he thinks that you are in his power_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.11">X. A. 2.1.11</cite>.

### GENITIVE OF THE DIVIDED WHOLE (PARTITIVE GENITIVE)

##### <a id="s1306"></a> §1306

The genitive may denote a whole, a part of which is denoted by the noun it limits. The genitive of the divided whole may be used with any word that expresses or implies a part.

##### <a id="s1307"></a> §1307

**Position**.—The genitive of the whole stands before or after the word denoting the part: <span lang="grc">τῶν Θρᾳκῶν πελτασταί</span> _targeteers of the Thracians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.27">T. 7.27</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ ἄποροι τῶν πολῑτῶν</span> _the needy among the citizens_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.104">D. 18.104</cite>; rarely between the limited noun and its article: <span lang="grc">οἱ τῶν ἀδίκων ἀφικνούμενοι</span> _those of the unrighteous who come here_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 525c">P. G. 525c</cite>. Cp. [1161](../16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1161) N. 1. <!-- page 316 -->

##### <a id="s1308"></a> §1308

When all are included there is no partition: so in <span lang="grc">οὗτοι πάντες</span> _all of these_, _all these_, <span lang="grc">τέτταρες ἡμεῖς ἦμεν</span> _there were four of us_, <span lang="grc">τὸ πᾶν πλῆθος τῶν ὁπλῑτῶν</span> _the entire body of the hoplites_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 8.93">T. 8.93</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅσοι ἐστὲ τῶν ὁμοίων</span> _as many of you as belong to the_ ‘_peers_’ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.6.14">X. A. 4.6.14</cite>.

##### <a id="s1309"></a> §1309

The idea of division is often not explicitly stated. See third example in 1310.

##### <a id="s1310"></a> §1310

(I) The genitive of the divided whole is used with substantives.

<span lang="grc">μέρος τι τῶν βαρβάρων</span> _some part of the barbarians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.1">T. 1.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ Δωριῆς ἡμῶν</span> _those of us who are Dorians_ 4. 61. The governing word may be omitted: <span lang="grc">Ἀρχίᾱς τῶν Ἡρᾱκλειδῶν</span> _Archias_ (one) _of the Heraclidae_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.3">T. 6.3</cite>. To an indefinite substantive without the article may be added a genitive denoting the special sort: <span lang="grc">Φεραύλᾱς Πέρσης τῶν δημοτῶν</span> _Pheraulas_, _a Persian_, _one of the common people_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 2.3.7">X. C. 2.3.7</cite>.

##### <a id="s1311"></a> §1311

**Chorographic Genitive**.—<span lang="grc">τῆς Ἀττικῆς ἐς Οἰνόην</span> _to Oenoë in Attica_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.18">T. 2.18</cite> (or <span lang="grc">ἐς Οἰνόην τῆς Ἀττικῆς</span>, _not_ <span lang="grc">ἐς τῆς Ἀττικῆς Οἰνόην</span>), <span lang="grc">τῆς Ἰταλίᾱς Αοκροί</span> _the Locrians in Italy_ 3. 86. The article, which is always used with the genitive of the country (as a place well known), is rarely added to the governing substantive (<span lang="grc">τὸ Κήναιον τῆς Εὐβοίᾱς</span> _Cenaeum in Euboea_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.93">T. 3.93</cite>).

##### <a id="s1312"></a> §1312

(II) With substantive adjectives and participles.

<span lang="grc">οἱ ἄδικοι τῶν ἀνθρώπων</span> _the unjust among men_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 27.68">D. 27.68</cite> (but always <span lang="grc">οἱ θνητοὶ ἄνθρωποι</span>), <span lang="grc">μόνος τῶν πρυτάνεων</span> _alone of the prytans_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 32b">P. A. 32b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὀλίγοι αὐτῶν</span> _few of them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.3">X. A. 3.1.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῶν ἄλλων Ἑλλήνων ὁ βουλόμενος</span> _whoever of the rest of the Greeks so desires_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.92">T. 3.92</cite>. So <span lang="grc">τὸ καταντικρὺ αὐτῶν τοῦ σπηλαίου</span> _the part of the cavern facing them_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 515a">P. R. 515a</cite>. For _nihil novi_ the Greek says <span lang="grc">οὐδὲν καινόν</span>.

##### <a id="s1313"></a> §1313

Adjectives denoting magnitude, and some others, may conform in gender to the genitive, instead of appearing in the neuter: <span lang="grc">ἔτεμον τῆς γῆς τὴν πολλήν</span> _they ravaged most of the land_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.56">T. 2.56</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῆς γῆς ἡ ἀρίστη</span> _the best of the land_ 1. 2. This construction occurs more frequently in prose than in poetry.

##### <a id="s1314"></a> §1314

But such adjectives, especially when singular, may be used in the neuter: <span lang="grc">τῶν Ἀργείων λογάδων τὸ πολύ</span> _the greater part of the picked Argives_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.73">T. 5.73</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ πολὺ τῆς χώρᾱς</span> _over a great part of the land_ 4. 3.

##### <a id="s1315"></a> §1315

(III) With comparatives and superlatives.

<span lang="grc">ἡμῶν ὁ γεραίτερος</span> _the elder of us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.1.6">X. C. 5.1.6</cite> ([1066 b](../14-adjectives.md#s1066)), <span lang="grc">οἱ πρεσβύτατοι τῶν στρατηγῶν</span> _the oldest of the generals_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.3.11">X. A. 3.3.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">σί̄τῳ πάντων ἀνθρώπων πλείστῳ χρώμεθ᾽ ἐπεισάκτῳ</span> _we make use of imported grain more than all other people_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.87">D. 18.87</cite>. So with a superlative adverb: <span lang="grc">ἡ ναῦς ἄριστά μοι ἔπλει παντὸς τοῦ στρατοπέδου</span> _my ship was the best sailer of the whole squadron_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 21.6">L. 21.6</cite>.

##### <a id="s1316"></a> §1316

In poetry this use is extended to positive adjectives: <span lang="grc">ἀριδείκετος ἀνδρῶν</span> _conspicuous among men_ A 248, <span lang="grc">ὦ φίλᾱ γυναικῶν</span> _oh dear among women_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Alc. 460">E. Alc. 460</cite>. In tragedy an adjective may be emphasized by the addition of the same adjective in the genitive: <span lang="grc">ἄρρητ᾽ ἀρρήτων</span> _horrors unspeakable_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OT 465">S. O. T. 465</cite>. Cp. [1064](../14-adjectives.md#s1064).

##### <a id="s1317"></a> §1317

(IV) With substantive pronouns and numerals. <!-- page 317 -->

<span lang="grc">οἱ μὲν αὐτῶν, οἱ δ᾽ οὔ</span> _some of them and not others_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 24e">P. A. 24e</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἳ ὕστερον ἐλήφθησαν τῶν πολεμίων</span> _those of the enemy who were taken later_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.7.13">X. A. 1.7.13</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς ἀνθρώπων</span> _no one in the world_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 220a">P. S. 220a</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὶ τοῦ τείχους</span> _a part of the wall_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.4">T. 7.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὶς θεῶν</span> _one of the gods_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hec. 164">E. Hec. 164</cite> (<span lang="grc">τὶς θεός</span> _a god_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 5.2.12">X. C. 5.2.12</cite>), <span lang="grc">ἓν τῶν πολλῶν</span> _one of the many things_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 17a">P. A. 17a</cite>; rarely after demonstrative pronouns: <span lang="grc">τούτοις τῶν ἀνθρώπων</span> _to these_ (_of_) _men_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.71">T. 1.71</cite>.

**a.** With <span lang="grc">ὀλίγοι</span> and with numerals <span lang="grc">ἀπό</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐξ</span> are rarely added: <span lang="grc">ἐκ τριῶν ἕν</span> _one of three_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Trach. 734">S. Tr. 734</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἐξ</span> with superlatives is also rare. See also [1688](../19-prepositions.md#s1688). 1 c.

##### <a id="s1318"></a> §1318

The genitive of the divided whole may do duty as the subject of a finite verb ([928 b](../06-the-subject.md#s928)) or of the infinitive: (<span lang="grc">ἔφασαν</span>) <span lang="grc">ἐπιμειγνύναι σφῶν πρὸς ἐκείνους</span> _they said that_ some _of their number associated with them_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.5.16">X. A. 3.5.16</cite>.

##### <a id="s1319"></a> §1319

**Predicate Use**.—<span lang="grc">ἦν δ᾽ αὐτῶν Φαλῖνος</span> _and among them was Phalinus_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.1.7">X. A. 2.1.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">Σόλων τῶν ἑπτὰ σοφιστῶν ἐκλήθη</span> _Solon was called_ one _of the Seven Sages_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 15.235">I. 15.235</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῶν ἀτοπωτάτων ἂν εἴη</span> _it would be very strange_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 1.26">D. 1.26</cite>; and often with verbs signifying _to be, become_, _think_, _say_, _name_, _choose_. With some of these verbs <span lang="grc">εἷς</span> with the genitive may be used instead of the genitive alone.

### GENITIVE OF QUALITY

##### <a id="s1320"></a> §1320

The genitive to denote quality occurs chiefly as a predicate.

<span lang="grc">ἐὼν τρόπου ἡσυχίου</span> _being of a peaceful disposition_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.107">Hdt. 1.107</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ δέ τινες τῆς αὐτῆς γνώμης ὀλίγοι κατέφυγον</span> _but some few of the same opinion fled_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.70">T. 3.70</cite>, <span lang="grc">ταῦτα παμπόλλων ἐστὶ λόγων</span> _this calls for a thorough discussion_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 642a">P. L. 642a</cite>, <span lang="grc">θεωρήσατ᾽ αὐτόν, μὴ ὁποτέρου τοῦ λόγου, ἀλλ᾽ ὁποτέρου τοῦ βίου ἐστίν</span> _consider_, _not the manner of his speech, but the manner of his life_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 3.168">Aes. 3.168</cite>, <span lang="grc">εἰ δοκεῖ ταῦτα καὶ δαπάνης μεγάλης καὶ πόνων πολλῶν καὶ πρᾱγματείᾱς εἶναι</span> _if these matters seem to involve great expense and much toil and trouble_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.48">D. 8.48</cite>.

**a.** The attributive use occurs in poetry: <span lang="grc">χόρτων εὐδένδρων Εὐρώπᾱς</span> _Europe with its pastures amid fair trees_ <cite data-ref="Eur. IT 134">E. I. T. 134</cite>, <span lang="grc">λευκῆς χιόνος πτέρυξ</span> _a wing white as snow_ (of white snow) <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 114">S. Ant. 114</cite>.

##### <a id="s1321"></a> §1321

The use of the genitive to express quality, corresponding to the Latin genitive, occurs in the non-predicate position, only when _age_ or _size_ is exactly expressed by the addition of a numeral (genitive of measure, [1325](#s1325)). The Latin genitive of quality in _mulier mirae pulchritudinis_ is expressed by <span lang="grc">γυνὴ θαυμασίᾱ κάλλος</span> (or <span lang="grc">τοῦ κάλλους</span>), <span lang="grc">γυνὴ θαυμασίᾱ ἰδεῖν, γυνὴ ἔχουσα θαυμάσιον σχῆμα</span>, etc.

### GENITIVE OF EXPLANATION (APPOSITIVE GENITIVE)

##### <a id="s1322"></a> §1322

The genitive of an explicit word may explain the meaning of a more general word.

<span lang="grc">Ἰ̄λίου πόλις</span> E 642, as _urbs Romae_, <span lang="grc">ἄελλαι παντοίων ἀνέμων</span> _blasts formed of winds of every sort_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 5.292"><span lang="grc">ε</span> 292</cite>. This construction is chiefly poetic, but in prose we find <span lang="grc">ὑὸς μέγα χρῆμα</span> _a monster_ (great affair, [1294](#s1294)) _of a boar_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 1.36">Hdt. 1.36</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ ὄρος τῆς Ἰστώνης</span> _Mt. Istone_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.46">T. 4.46</cite> (very rare, 1142 c). An articular infinitive in the genitive often defines the application of a substantive: <span lang="grc">ἀμαθίᾱ ἡ τοῦ οἴεσθαι εἰδέναι α·̀ οὐκ οἶδεν</span> _the ignorance of thinking one knows what one does not know_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 29b">P. A. 29b</cite>. <!-- page 318 -->

**a.** But with <span lang="grc">ὄνομα</span> the person or thing named is usually in apposition to <span lang="grc">ὄνομα</span>: <span lang="grc">τῷ δὲ νεωτάτῳ ἐθέμην ὄνομα Καλλίστρατον</span> _I gave the youngest the name Callistratus_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 43.74">D. 43.74</cite>.

### GENITIVE OF MATERIAL OR CONTENTS

##### <a id="s1323"></a> §1323

The genitive expresses material or contents.

<span lang="grc">ἕρκος ὀδόντων</span> _the fence_ (consisting) _of the teeth_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 4.350"><span lang="grc">Δ</span> 350</cite>, <span lang="grc">κρήνη ἡδέος ὕδατος</span> _a spring of sweet water_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 6.4.4">X. A. 6.4.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">σωροὶ σί̄του, ξύλων, λίθων</span> _heaps of corn_, _wood_, _stones_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.4.12">X. H. 4.4.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἑξακόσια τάλαντα φόρου</span> _six hundred talents in taxes_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.13">T. 2.13</cite> (cp. [1296](#s1296)).

##### <a id="s1324"></a> §1324

**Predicate Use**: <span lang="grc">στεφάνους ῥόδων ὄντας, ἀλλ᾽ ου᾽ χρῡσίου</span> _crowns that were of roses, not of gold_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 22.70">D. 22.70</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐστρωμένη ἐστὶ ὁδὸς λίθου</span> _a road was paved with stone_ <cite data-ref="Hdt. 2.138">Hdt. 2.138</cite>, and often with _verbs of making_, which admit also the instrumental dative. Hdt. has <span lang="grc">ποιεῖσθαι ἀπό</span> and <span lang="grc">ἔκ τινος</span>.

### GENITIVE OF MEASURE

##### <a id="s1325"></a> §1325

The genitive denotes measure of space, time, or degree.

<span lang="grc">ὀκτὼ σταδίων τεῖχος</span> _a wall eight stades long_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.2">T. 7.2</cite>, <span lang="grc">πέντε ἡμερῶν σῑτία</span> _provisions for five days_ 7. 43 (cp. _fossa pedum quindecim, exilium decem annorum_ Less commonly with a neuter adjective or pronoun: <span lang="grc">ἐπὶ μέγα ἐχώρησαν δυνά&lt;\*&gt;</span> _they advanced to a great pitch of power_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.118">T. 1.118</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὶ δόξης</span> _some honour_ (_aliq&lt;\*&gt; famae_) 1. 5, <span lang="grc">ἀμήχανον εὐδαιμονίᾱς</span> (_something infinite in the way of happiness_) _infinite happiness_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 41c">P. A. 41c</cite> (with emphasis on the adj.). But the phrases <span lang="grc">εἰς τοῦτο, εἰς τοσοῦτο ἀφικέσθαι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἤκειν, ἐλθεῖν, προσβαίνειν</span>, usually with a personal subject) followed by the genitive of abstracts are common: <span lang="grc">εἰς τοῦτο θράσους ἀφί̄κετο</span> _he reached such a pitch of boldness_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.194">D. 21.194</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν παντὶ ἀθῡμίᾱς</span> _in utter despondency_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.55">T. 7.55</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐν τούτῳ παρασκευῆς</span> _in this stage of preparation_ 2. 17, <span lang="grc">κατὰ τοῦτο καιροῦ</span> _at that critical moment_ 7. 2. The article with this genitive is unusual in classical Greek: <span lang="grc">εἰς τοῦτο τῆς ἡλικίᾱς</span> _to this stage of life_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 5.3">L. 5.3</cite>. Some of these genitives may also be explained by 1306.

##### <a id="s1326"></a> §1326

Under the head of _measure_ belongs _amount:_ <span lang="grc">δυοῖν μναῖν πρόσοδος</span> _an income of two minae_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ways 3.10">X. Vect. 3.10</cite>. Cp. [1296](#s1296), [1323](#s1323).

##### <a id="s1327"></a> §1327

**Predicate Use**.—<span lang="grc">ἐπειδὰν ἐτῶν ᾖ τις τριά̄κοντα</span> _when a man is thirty years old_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 721a">P. L. 721a</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὰ τείχη ἦν σταδίων ὀκτώ</span> _the walls were eight stades long_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.66">T. 4.66</cite>.

### SUBJECTIVE AND OBJECTIVE GENITIVE

##### <a id="s1328"></a> §1328

With a verbal noun the genitive may denote the subject or object of the action expressed in the noun.

**a.** Many of these genitives derive their construction from that of the kindred verbs: <span lang="grc">τοῦ ὕδατος ἐπιθῡμίᾱ</span> _desire for water_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.52">T. 2.52</cite> ([1349](04-genitive-with-verbs.md#s1349)), <span lang="grc">χόλος υἱός</span> _anger because of his son_ O 138 ([1405](05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1405)). But the verbal idea sometimes requires the accusative, or (less commonly) the dative.

##### <a id="s1329"></a> §1329

In poetry an adjective may take the place of the genitive: <span lang="grc">νόστος ὁ βασίλειος</span> _the return of the king_ <cite data-ref="Aesch. Pers. 8">A. Pers. 8</cite>. Cp. [1291](#s1291). <!-- page 319 -->

##### <a id="s1330"></a> §1330

The **Subjective Genitive** is active in sense: <span lang="grc">τῶν βαρβάρων φόβος</span> _the fear of the barbarians_ (which they feel: <span lang="grc">οἱ βάρβαροι φοβοῦνται</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.2.17">X. A. 1.2.17</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ βασιλέως ἐπιορκίᾱ</span> _the perjury of the king_ (<span lang="grc">βασιλεὺς ἐπιορκεῖ</span>) 3. 2. 4, <span lang="grc">τὸ ὀργιζόμενον τῆς γνώμης</span> _their angry feelings_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.59">T. 2.59</cite> (such genitives with substantive participles are common in Thucydides; cp. [1153 b](../16-the-article-origin-and-development.md#s1153), N. 2).

##### <a id="s1331"></a> §1331

The **Objective Genitive** is passive in sense, and is very common with substantives denoting a frame of mind or an emotion: <span lang="grc">φόβος τῶν Εἱλώτων</span> _the fear of the Helots_ (felt towards them: <span lang="grc">φοβοῦνται τοὺς Εἵλωτας</span>) <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.54">T. 3.54</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ τῶν Ἑλλήνων εὔνοια</span> _good-will towards the Greeks_ (<span lang="grc">εὐνοεῖ τοῖς Ἕλλησι</span>) <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.7.20">X. A. 4.7.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ τῶν καλῶν συνουσίᾱ</span> _intercourse with the good_ (<span lang="grc">σύνεισι τοῖς καλοῖς</span>) <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 838a">P. L. 838a</cite>.

**a.** The objective genitive often precedes another genitive on which it depends: <span lang="grc">μετὰ τῆς ξυμμαχίᾱς τῆς αἰτήσεως</span> _with the request for an alliance_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.32">T. 1.32</cite>.

##### <a id="s1332"></a> §1332

Various prepositions are used in translating the objective genitive: <span lang="grc">ὁ θεῶν πόλεμος</span> _war with the gods_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 2.5.7">X. A. 2.5.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅρκοι θεῶν</span> _oaths by the gods_ <cite data-ref="Eur. Hipp. 657">E. Hipp. 657</cite>, <span lang="grc">θεῶν εὐχαί</span> _prayers to the gods_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 244e">P. Phae. 244e</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀδικημάτων ὀργή</span> _anger at injustice_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 12.20">L. 12.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐγκράτεια ἡδονῆς</span> _moderation in pleasure_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.21">I. 1.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ τῶν ἡδονῶν νί̄κη</span> _victory over pleasures_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 840c">P. L. 840c</cite>, <span lang="grc">τρόπαια βαρβάρων</span> _memorials of victory over barbarians_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 7.6.36">X. A. 7.6.36</cite>, <span lang="grc">παραινέσεις τῶν ξυναλλαγῶν</span> _exhortations to reconciliation_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.59">T. 4.59</cite>, <span lang="grc">μῦθος φίλων</span> _tidings about friends_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Ant. 11">S. Ant. 11</cite>, <span lang="grc">σοῦ μῦθος</span> _speech with thee_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 1161">S. O. C. 1161</cite>. In <span lang="grc">θανάτου λύσις</span> _release from death_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 9.421"><span lang="grc">ι</span> 421</cite>, <span lang="grc">μεταπαυσωλὴ πολέμοιο</span> _respite from war_ T 201, it is uncertain whether the genitive is objective or ablatival ([1392](05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1392)).

##### <a id="s1333"></a> §1333

The objective genitive is often used when a prepositional expression, giving greater precision, is more usual: <span lang="grc">τὸ Μεγαρέων ψήφισμα</span> _the decree relating to_ (<span lang="grc">περί</span>) _the Megarians_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.140">T. 1.140</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀπόβασις τῆς γῆς</span> _a descent upon the land_ (<span lang="grc">ἐς τὴν γῆν</span>) 1. 108, <span lang="grc">ἀπόστασις τῶν Ἀθηναίων</span> _revolt from the Athenians_ (<span lang="grc">ἀπὸ τῶν Ἀθηναίων</span>) 8. 5.

##### <a id="s1334"></a> §1334

For the objective genitive a possessive pronoun is sometimes used: <span lang="grc">σὴν χάριν</span> _for thy sake_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Soph. 242a">P. Soph. 242a</cite>, <span lang="grc">διαβολὴ ἡ ἐμή</span> _calumniation of me_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 20e">P. A. 20e</cite>. <span lang="grc">ὁ ἐμὸς φόβος</span> is usually objective: _the fear which I inspire_. (But <span lang="grc">σοῦ μῦθος</span> _speech with thee_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 1161">S. O. C. 1161</cite>.)

##### <a id="s1335"></a> §1335

**Predicate Use**.—<span lang="grc">οὐ τῶν κακούργων οἶκτος, ἀλλὰ τῆς δίκης</span> _compassion is not for wrong-doers_, _but for justice_ E. fr. 270.

### GENITIVE OF VALUE

##### <a id="s1336"></a> §1336

The genitive expresses value.

<span lang="grc">ἱερὰ τριῶν ταλάντων</span> _offerings worth three talents_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 30.20">L. 30.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">χῑλίων δραχμῶν δίκην φεύγω</span> _I am defendant in an action involving a thousand drachmas_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 55.25">D. 55.25</cite>.

##### <a id="s1337"></a> §1337

**Predicate Use**: <span lang="grc">τοὺς αἰχμαλώτους τοσούτων χρημάτων λύ̄εσθαι</span> _to ransom the captives at so high a price_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.222">D. 19.222</cite>, <span lang="grc">τριῶν δραχμῶν πονηρὸς ὤν</span> _a threepenny rogue_ 19. 200.

### TWO GENITIVES WITH ONE NOUN

##### <a id="s1338"></a> §1338

Two genitives expressing different relations may be used with one noun. <!-- page 320 -->

<span lang="grc">οἱ ἄνθρωποι διὰ τὸ αὑτῶν δέος τοῦ θανάτου καταψεύδονται</span> _by reason of their fear of death men tell lies_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 85a">P. Ph. 85a</cite>, <span lang="grc">Διονύ̄σου πρεσβῡτῶν χορός</span> _a chorus of old men in honour of Dionysus_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 665b">P. L. 665b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ τοῦ Αάχητος τῶν νεῶν ἀρχή</span> _Laches' command of the fleet_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.115">T. 3.115</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ Φαιά̄κων προενοίκησις τῆς Κερκύ̄ρᾱς</span> _the former occupation of Corcyra by the Phaeacians_ 1.25.
