# THE CASES: GENITIVE WITH VERBS

##### <a id="s1339"></a> §1339

The genitive may serve as the immediate complement of a verb, or it may appear, as a secondary definition, along with an accusative which is the immediate object of the verb ([920](../02-syntax-of-the-simple-sentence.md#s920), [1392](05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1392), [1405](05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1405)).

##### <a id="s1340"></a> §1340

The subject of an active verb governing the genitive may become the subject of the passive construction: <span lang="grc">Νῑκήρατος ἐρῶν τῆς γυναικὸς ἀντερᾶται</span> _Niceratus_, _who is in love with his wife, is loved in return_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 8">X. S. 8</cite>. 3. Cp. [1745 a](../20-the-verb-voices/04-passive-voice.md#s1745).

## THE GENITIVE PROPER WITH VERBS

### THE PARTITIVE GENITIVE

##### <a id="s1341"></a> §1341

A verb may be followed by the partitive genitive if the action affects the object only in part. If the _entire_ object is affected, the verb in question takes the accusative.

<span lang="grc">Ἀδρήστοιο δ᾽ ἔγημε θυγατρῶν</span> _he married_ one _of Adrastus' daughters_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 14.121"><span lang="grc">Ξ</span> 121</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῶν πώλων λαμβάνει</span> _he takes_ some _of the colts_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.5.35">X. A. 4.5.35</cite>, <span lang="grc">λαβόντες τοῦ βαρβαρικοῦ στρατοῦ</span> _taking_ part _of the barbarian force_ 1. 5. 7, <span lang="grc">κλέπτοντες τοῦ ὄρους</span> _seizing_ part of _the mountain secretly_ 4. 6. 15 (cp. <span lang="grc">τοῦ ὄρους κλέψαι τι</span> 4. 6. [11](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/01-the-letters.md#s11)), <span lang="grc">τῆς γῆς ἔτεμον</span> _they ravaged_ part of _the land_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.56">T. 2.56</cite> (cp. <span lang="grc">τὴν γῆν πᾶσαν ἔτεμον</span> 2. 57 and <span lang="grc">ἔτεμον τῆς γῆς τὴν πολλήν</span> 2. [56](../../part-1-letters-sounds-syllables-accent/02-euphony-of-vowels.md#s56)), <span lang="grc">κατεά̄γη τῆς κεφαλῆς</span> _he had a hole knocked_ somewhere _in his head_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Wasps 1428">Ar. Vesp. 1428</cite> (<span lang="grc">τὴν κεφαλὴν κατεᾱγέναι</span> _to have one's head broken_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 54.35">D. 54.35</cite>).

##### <a id="s1342"></a> §1342

With impersonals a partitive genitive does duty as the subject: <span lang="grc">πολέμου οὐ μετῆν αὐτῇ</span> _she had no share in war_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.2.28">X. C. 7.2.28</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐμοὶ οὐδαμόθεν προσήκει τούτου τοῦ πρά̄γματος</span> _I have no part whatever in this affair_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 4.34">And. 4.34</cite>. Cp. [1318](03-genitive.md#s1318).

##### <a id="s1343"></a> §1343

The genitive is used with _verbs of sharing_.

<span lang="grc">πάντες μετεῖχον τῆς ἑορτῆς</span> _all took part in the festival_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.3.9">X. A. 5.3.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">μετεδίδοσαν ἀλλήλοις ὧν</span> (= <span lang="grc">τούτων ἃ</span>) <span lang="grc">εἶχον ἕκαστοι</span> _they shared with each other what each had_ 4. 5. 6, <span lang="grc">τὸ ἀνθρώπινον γένος μετείληφεν ἀθανασίᾱς</span> _the human race has received a portion of immortality_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 721b">P. L. 721b</cite>, <span lang="grc">σί̄του κοινωνεῖν</span> _to take a share of food_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 2.6.22">X. M. 2.6.22</cite>, <span lang="grc">δικαιοσύνης οὐδὲν ὑ̄μῖν προσήκει</span> _you have no concern in righteous dealing_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.4.40">X. H. 2.4.40</cite>, <span lang="grc">πολῑτείᾱ, ἐν ᾗ πένησιν οὐ μέτεστιν ἀρχῆς</span> _a form of government in which the poor have no part in the management of affairs_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 550c">P. R. 550c</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">μεταλαγχάνειν</span> _get a share_ (along with somebody else), <span lang="grc">συναίρεσθαι</span> and <span lang="grc">κοινοῦσθαι</span> _take part in_, <span lang="grc">μεταιτεῖν</span> and <span lang="grc">μεταποιεῖσθαι</span> _demand a share in_.

##### <a id="s1344"></a> §1344

The part received or taken, if expressed, stands in the accusative. <span lang="grc">οἱ τύραννοι τῶν μεγίστων ἀγαθῶν ἐλάχιστα μετέχουσι</span> _tyrants have the smallest por-_ <!-- page 321 --> _tion in the greatest blessings_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hiero 2.6">X. Hi. 2.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">τούτων μεταιτεῑ τὸ μέρος</span> _he demands his share of this_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Wasps 972">Ar. Vesp. 972</cite>.

**a.** With <span lang="grc">μέτεστι</span> the part may be added in the nominative: <span lang="grc">μέτεστι χὐ̄μῖν τῶν πεπρᾱγμένων μέρος</span> _ye too have had a share in these doings_ <cite data-ref="Eur. IT 1299">E. I. T. 1299</cite>.

##### <a id="s1345"></a> §1345

The genitive is used with verbs signifying _to touch, take hold of, make trial of_.

(<span lang="grc">ἡ νόσος</span>) <span lang="grc">ἥψατο τῶν ἀνθρώπων</span> _the plague laid hold of the men_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.48">T. 2.48</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῆς γνώμης τῆς αὐτῆς ἔχομαι</span> _I hold to the same opinion_ 1. 140, <span lang="grc">ἐν τῇ ἐχομένῃ ἐμοῦ κλί̄νῃ</span> _on the couch next to me_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 217d">P. S. 217d</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀντιλάβεσθε τῶν πρᾱγμάτων</span> _take our public policy in hand_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 1.20">D. 1.20</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅπως πειρῷντο τοῦ τείχους</span> _to make an attempt on_ (a part of) _the wall_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.81">T. 2.81</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">ψαύειν</span> _touch_ (rare in prose), <span lang="grc">ἀντέχεσθαι</span> _cling to_, <span lang="grc">ἐπιλαμβάνεσθαι</span> and <span lang="grc">συλλαμβάνεσθαι</span> _lay hold of_.

##### <a id="s1346"></a> §1346

The genitive of the _part_, with the accusative of the _person_ (the whole) who has been touched, is chiefly poetical: <span lang="grc">τὸν δὲ πεσόντα ποδῶν ἔλαβε</span> _but him as he fell, he seized by his feet_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 4.463"><span lang="grc">Δ</span> 463</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἔλαβον τῆς ζώνης τὸν Ὀρόντᾱν</span> _they took hold of Orontas by the girdle_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.6.10">X. A. 1.6.10</cite> (but <span lang="grc">μοῦ λαβόμενος τῆς χειρός</span> _taking me by the hand_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Charm. 153b">P. Charm. 153b</cite>), <span lang="grc">ἄγειν τῆς ἡνίᾱς τὸν ἵππον</span> _to lead the horse by the bridle_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Horse. 6.9">X. Eq. 6.9</cite> (cp. <span lang="grc">βοῦν δ᾽ ἀγέτην κεράων</span> _they led the cow by the horns_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 3.439"><span lang="grc">γ</span> 439</cite>).

##### <a id="s1347"></a> §1347

_Verbs of beseeching_ take the genitive by analogy to _verbs of touching:_ <span lang="grc">ἐμὲ λισσέσκετο γούνων</span> _she besought me by_ (_clasping_) _my knees_ I 451 (cp. <span lang="grc">γενείου ἁψάμενος λίσσεσθαι</span> _beseech by touching his chin_ K [454](../../part-2-inflection/09-verbs/06-reduplication.md#s454)).

##### <a id="s1348"></a> §1348

The genitive is used with _verbs of beginning_.

**a. Partitive**: <span lang="grc">ἔφη Κῦρον ἄρχειν τοῦ λόγου ὧδε</span> _he said that Cyrus began the discussion as follows_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 1.6.5">X. A. 1.6.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦ λόγου ἤρχετο ὧδε</span> _he began his speech as follows_ 3. 2. 7. On <span lang="grc">ἄρχειν</span> as distinguished from <span lang="grc">ἄρχεσθαι</span> see [1734](../20-the-verb-voices/03-middle-voice.md#s1734). 5.

**b. Ablatival** ([1391](05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1391)) denoting the point of departure: <span lang="grc">σέο δ᾽ ἄρξομαι</span> _I will make a beginning with thee_ I 97. In this sense <span lang="grc">ἀπό</span> or <span lang="grc">ἐξ</span> is usually added: <span lang="grc">ἀρξάμενοι ἀπὸ σοῦ</span> <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.297">D. 18.297</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἄρξομαι ἀπὸ τῆς ἰ̄ᾱτρικῆς λέγων</span> _I will make a beginning by speaking of medicine_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 186b">P. S. 186b</cite>.

##### <a id="s1349"></a> §1349

The genitive is used with verbs signifying _to aim at, strive after_, _desire_ (genitive of the end desired).

<span lang="grc">ἀνθρώπων στοχάζεσθαι</span> _to aim at men_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.6.29">X. C. 1.6.29</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐφῑέμενοι τῶν κερδῶν</span> _desiring gain_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.8">T. 1.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">πάντες τῶν ἀγαθῶν ἐπιθῡμοῦσιν</span> _all men desire what is good_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 438a">P. R. 438a</cite>, <span lang="grc">τὸ ἐρᾶν τῶν καλῶν</span> _the passionate love of what is noble_ <cite data-ref="Aeschin. 1.137">Aes. 1.137</cite>, <span lang="grc">πεινῶσι χρημάτων</span> _they are hungry for wealth_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Sym. 4">X. S. 4</cite>. 36, <span lang="grc">πόλις ἐλευθερίᾱς διψήσᾱσα</span> _a state thirsting for freedom_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 562c">P. R. 562c</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">ὀϊστεύειν</span> _shoot at_ (poet.), <span lang="grc">λιλαίεσθαι</span> _desire_ (poet.), <span lang="grc">γλίχεσθαι</span> _desire_. <span lang="grc">φιλεῖν</span> _love_, <span lang="grc">ποθεῖν</span> _long for_ take the accusative.

##### <a id="s1350"></a> §1350

The genitive is used with verbs signifying _to reach, obtain_ (genitive of the end attained).

<span lang="grc">τῆς ἀρετῆς ἐφικέσθαι</span> _to attain to virtue_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.5">I. 1.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ ἀκοντισταὶ βραχύτερα ἠκόντιζον ἢ ὡς ἐξικνεῖσθαι τῶν σφενδονητῶν</span> _the javelin-throwers did not hurl far enough to reach the slingers_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.3.7">X. A. 3.3.7</cite>, <span lang="grc">σπονδῶν ἔτυχε</span> _he obtained a truce_ 3. 1. 28. <!-- page 322 --> So with <span lang="grc">κυρεῖν</span> _obtain_ (poet.), <span lang="grc">κληρονομεῖν</span> _inherit_, <span lang="grc">ἀποτυγχάνειν</span> _fail to hit_. <span lang="grc">τυγχάνειν</span>, when compounded with <span lang="grc">ἐν, ἐπί, παρά, περί</span>, and <span lang="grc">σύν</span>, takes the dative. <span lang="grc">λαγχάνειν</span> _obtain by lot_ usually takes the accusative.

**a.** This genitive and that of 1349 form the _genitive of the goal_.

##### <a id="s1351"></a> §1351

The genitive of the thing obtained may be joined with an ablatival genitive ([1410](05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1410)) of the person: <span lang="grc">οὗ δὲ δὴ πάντων οἰόμεθα τεύξεσθαι ἐπαίνου</span> _in a case where we expect to win praise from all men_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.7.33">X. A. 5.7.33</cite>. But where the thing obtained is expressed by a neuter pronoun, the accusative is employed.

##### <a id="s1352"></a> §1352

It is uncertain whether verbs signifying _to miss_ take a partitive or an ablatival genitive: <span lang="grc">οὐδεὶς ἡμάρτανεν ἀνδρός</span> _no one missed his man_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.4.15">X. A. 3.4.15</cite>, <span lang="grc">σφαλέντες τῆς δόξης</span> _disappointed in expectations_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.85">T. 4.85</cite>.

##### <a id="s1353"></a> §1353

_Verbs of approaching_ and _meeting_ take the genitive according to 1343 or 1349. These verbs are poetical. Thus, <span lang="grc">ἀντιόων ταύρων</span> _for the purpose of obtaining_ (his share of) _bulls_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 1.25"><span lang="grc">α</span> 25</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀντήσω τοῦδ᾽ ἀνέρος</span> _I will encounter this man_ II 423, <span lang="grc">πελάσαι νεῶν</span> _to approach the ships_ <cite data-ref="Soph. Aj. 709">S. Aj. 709</cite>. In the meaning _draw near to_ _verbs of approaching_ take the dative ([1463](06-dative.md#s1463)).

##### <a id="s1354"></a> §1354

The genitive is used with _verbs of smelling_.

<span lang="grc">ὄζω μύρου</span> _I smell of perfume_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Eccl. 524">Ar. Eccl. 524</cite>. So <span lang="grc">πνεῖν μύρου</span> _to breathe_ (smell of) _perfume_ S. fr. 140.

##### <a id="s1355"></a> §1355

The genitive is used with verbs signifying _to enjoy, taste_, _eat_, _drink_.

<span lang="grc">ἀπολαύομεν πάντων τῶν ἀγαθῶν</span> _we enjoy all the good things_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 4.3.11">X. M. 4.3.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">εὐωχοῦ τοῦ λόγου</span> _enjoy the discourse_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 352b">P. R. 352b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὀλίγοι σί̄του ἐγεύσαντο</span> _few tasted food_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.1.3">X. A. 3.1.3</cite>. So (rarely) with <span lang="grc">ἥδεσθαι</span> _take pleasure in_.

**a.** Here belong <span lang="grc">ἐσθίειν, πί̄νειν</span> when they do not signify _to eat up_ or _drink up:_ <span lang="grc">ὠμῶν ἐσθίειν αὐτῶν</span> _to eat them alive_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 3.3.6">X. H. 3.3.6</cite>, <span lang="grc">πί̄νειν οἴνοιο</span> _drink some wine_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 22.11"><span lang="grc">χ</span> 11</cite>, as _boire du vin_ (but <span lang="grc">πί̄νειν οἶνον</span> _drink wine_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Il. 14.5"><span lang="grc">Ξ</span> 5</cite>, as _boire le vin_). Words denoting _food_ and _drink_ are placed in the accusative when they are regarded as kinds of _nourishment_.

##### <a id="s1356"></a> §1356

The genitive is used with verbs signifying _to remember, remind_, _forget_, _care for_ , and _neglect_.

<span lang="grc">τῶν ἀπόντων φίλων μέμνησο</span> _remember your absent friends_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.26">I. 1.26</cite>, <span lang="grc">βούλομαι δ᾽ ὑ̄μᾶς ἀναμνῆσαι τῶν ἐμοὶ πεπρᾱγμένων</span> _I desire to remind you of my past actions_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 4.41">And. 4.41</cite>, <span lang="grc">δέδοικα μὴ ἐπιλαθώμεθα τῆς οἴκαδε ὁδοῦ</span> _I fear lest we may forget the way home_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 3.2.25">X. A. 3.2.25</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπιμελόμενοι οἱ μὲν ὑποζυγίων, οἱ δὲ σκευῶν</span> _some taking care of the pack animals, others of the baggage_ 4. 3. 30, <span lang="grc">τῆς τῶν πολλῶν δόξης δεῖ ἡμᾶς φροντίζειν</span> _we must pay heed to the world's opinion_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Crito 48a">P. Cr. 48a</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί ἡμῖν τῆς τῶν πολλῶν δόξης μέλει</span>; _what do we care for the world's opinion?_ 44 c, <span lang="grc">τοῖς σπουδαίοις οὐχ οἷόν τε τῆς ἀρετῆς ἀμελεῖν</span> _the serious cannot disregard virtue_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.48">I. 1.48</cite>, <span lang="grc">μηδενὸς ὀλιγωρεῖτε μηδὲ καταφρονεῖτε</span> (cp. [1385](#s1385)) <span lang="grc">τῶν προστεταγμένων</span> _neither neglect nor despise any command laid on you_ 3. 48.

##### <a id="s1357"></a> §1357

So with <span lang="grc">μνημονεύειν</span> _remember_ (but usually with the accus., especially of things), <span lang="grc">ἀμνημονεῖν</span> _not to speak of_, <span lang="grc">κήδεσθαι</span> _care for_, <span lang="grc">ἐντρέπεσθαι</span> _give heed to_, <!-- page 323 --> <span lang="grc">ἐνθῡμεῖσθαι</span> _think deeply of_, <span lang="grc">προορᾶν</span> _make provision for_ (in Hdt.), <span lang="grc">μεταμέλει μοι</span> _it repents me_, <span lang="grc">καταμελεῖν</span> _neglect_.

##### <a id="s1358"></a> §1358

Many of these verbs also take the accusative. With the accus. <span lang="grc">μεμνῆσθαι</span> means _to remember something as a whole_, with the gen. _to remember something about a thing, bethink oneself_. The accus. is usually found with _verbs of remembering_ and _forgetting_ when they mean _to hold_ or _not to hold in memory_, and when the object is a thing. Neuter pronouns must stand in the accus. <span lang="grc">ἐπιλανθάνεσθαι</span> _forget_ takes either the genitive or the accusative, <span lang="grc">λανθάνεσθαι</span> (usually poetical) always takes the genitive. <span lang="grc">μέλει</span> _it is a care_, <span lang="grc">ἐπιμέλεσθαι</span> _care for_, <span lang="grc">μεμνῆσθαι</span> _think about_ may take <span lang="grc">περί</span> with the genitive. <span lang="grc">οἶδα</span> generally means _I remember_ when it has a person as the object (in the accusative).

##### <a id="s1359"></a> §1359

_Verbs of reminding_ may take two accusatives: <span lang="grc">ταῦθ᾽ ὑπέμνησ᾽ ὑ̄μᾶς</span> _I have reminded you of this_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.25">D. 19.25</cite> ([1628](07-accusative.md#s1628)).

##### <a id="s1360"></a> §1360

With <span lang="grc">μέλει</span>, the subject, if a neuter pronoun, may sometimes stand in the nominative (the personal construction): <span lang="grc">ταῦτα θεῷ μελήσει</span> _God will care for this_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 238d">P. Phae. 238d</cite>. Except in poetry the subject in the nominative is very rare with other words than neuter pronouns: <span lang="grc">χοροὶ πᾶσι μέλουσι</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 835e">P. L. 835e</cite>.

##### <a id="s1361"></a> §1361

The genitive is used with verbs signifying _to hear_ and _perceive:_ <span lang="grc">ἀκούειν, κλύειν</span> (poet.) _hear_, <span lang="grc">ἀκροᾶσθαι</span> _listen to_, <span lang="grc">αἰσθάνεσθαι</span> _perceive_, <span lang="grc">πυνθάνεσθαι</span> _hear_, _learn of_, <span lang="grc">συνῑέναι</span> _understand_, <span lang="grc">ὀσφραίνεσθαι</span> _scent_. The person or thing, whose words, sound, etc. are perceived by the senses, stands in the genitive; the words, sound, etc. generally stand in the accusative.

<span lang="grc">τινὸς ἤκουσ᾽ εἰπόντος</span> _I heard somebody say_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.4">D. 8.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀκούσαντες τῆς σάλπιγγος</span> _hearing the sound of the trumpet_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 4.2.8">X. A. 4.2.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀκούσαντες τὸν θόρυβον</span> _hearing the noise_ 4. 4. 21, <span lang="grc">ἀκροώμενοι τοῦ ᾁδοντος</span> _listening to the singer_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.3.10">X. C. 1.3.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὅσοι ἀλλήλων ξυνί̄εσαν</span> _all who understood each other_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.3">T. 1.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐπειδὰν συνῑῇ τις τὰ λεγόμενα</span> _when one understands what is said_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 325c">P. Pr. 325c</cite> (_verbs of understanding_, <span lang="grc">συνῑέναι</span> and <span lang="grc">ἐπίστασθαι</span>, usually take the accus.), <span lang="grc">κρομμύων ὀσφραίνομαι</span> _I smell onions_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Frogs 654">Ar. Ran. 654</cite>.

**a.** A supplementary participle is often used in agreement with the genitive of the person from whom something is heard: <span lang="grc">λέγοντος ἐμοῦ ἀκροά̄σονται οἱ νέοι</span> _the young men will listen when I speak_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 37d">P. A. 37d</cite>.

**b.** The accusative is almost always used when the thing heard is expressed by a substantivized neuter adjective or participle, but the genitive plural in the case of <span lang="grc">οὗτος, ὅδε, αὐτός</span>, and <span lang="grc">ὅς</span> is frequent.

##### <a id="s1362"></a> §1362

A double genitive, of the person and of the thing, is rare with <span lang="grc">ἀκούειν</span>: <span lang="grc">τῶν ὐπὲρ τῆς γραφῆς δικαίων ἀκούειν μου</span> _to listen to my just pleas as regards the indictment_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.9">D. 18.9</cite>.

##### <a id="s1363"></a> §1363

<span lang="grc">ἀκούειν, αἰσθάνεσθαι, πυνθάνεσθαι</span>, meaning _to become aware of, learn_, take the accusative (with a participle in indirect discourse, 2112 b) of a personal or impersonal object: <span lang="grc">οἱ δέ Πλαταιῆς, ὡς ᾔσθοντο ἔνδον τε ὄντας τοὺς Θηβαίους και κατειλημμένην τὴν πόλιν</span> _but the Plataeans_, _when they became aware that the Thebans were inside and that the city had been captured_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.3">T. 2.3</cite>, <span lang="grc">πυθόμενοι Ἀρταξέρξην τεθνηκότα</span> _having learned that Artaxerxes was dead_ 4. 50. <!-- page 324 -->

**a.** _To hear a thing_ is usually <span lang="grc">ἀκούειν τι</span> when the thing heard is something definite and when the meaning is simply _hear_, not _listen to_.

##### <a id="s1364"></a> §1364

<span lang="grc">ἀκούειν, ἀκροᾶσθαι, πυνθἁνεσθαι</span>, meaning _to hear from, learn from_, take the genitive of the actual source ([1411](05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1411)).

##### <a id="s1365"></a> §1365

<span lang="grc">ἀκούειν, κλύειν, πυνθάνεσθαίτινος</span> may mean _to hear about, hear of:_ <span lang="grc">εἰ δέ κε τεθνηῶτος ἀκούσῃς</span> _but if you hear that he is dead_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 1.289"><span lang="grc">α</span> 289</cite>, <span lang="grc">κλύων σοῦ</span> _hearing about thee_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 307">S. O. C. 307</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡς ἐπύθοντο τῆς Πύλου κατειλημμένης</span> _when they heard of the capture of Pylos_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.6">T. 4.6</cite>. For the participle (not in indirect discourse) see [2112 a](../22-the-participle.md#s2112). <span lang="grc">περί</span> is often used with the genitive without the participle.

##### <a id="s1366"></a> §1366

In the meaning _heed_, _hearken_, _obey_, _verbs of hearing_ generally take the genitive: <span lang="grc">ἄκουε πάντων, ἐκλέγου δ᾽ ἃ συμφέρει</span> _listen to everything_, _but choose that which is profitable_ Men. Sent. 566, <span lang="grc">τῶν πολεμίων ἀκούειν</span> _to submit to enemies_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 8.1.4">X. C. 8.1.4</cite>. <span lang="grc">πείθεσθαι</span> takes the genitive, instead of the dative, by analogy to this use (<cite data-ref="Hdt. 6.12">Hdt. 6.12</cite>, <cite data-ref="Thuc. 7.73">T. 7.73</cite>). (On the dative with <span lang="grc">ἀκούειν</span> _obey_ see [1465](06-dative.md#s1465).)

##### <a id="s1367"></a> §1367

<span lang="grc">αἰσθάνεσθαι</span> takes the genitive, or (less frequently) the accusative, of the thing immediately perceived by the senses: <span lang="grc">τῆς κραυγῆς ᾔσθοντο</span> _they heard the noise_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 4.4.4">X. H. 4.4.4</cite>, <span lang="grc">ᾔσθετο τὰ γιγνόμενα</span> _he perceived what was happening_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.1.4">X. C. 3.1.4</cite>. The genitive is less common than the accusative when the perception is intellectual: <span lang="grc">ὡς ᾔσθοντο τειχιζόντων</span> _when they heard that they were progressing with their fortification_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 5.83">T. 5.83</cite>. Cp. [1363](#s1363).

##### <a id="s1368"></a> §1368

Some verbs, ordinarily construed with the accusative, take the genitive by the analogy of <span lang="grc">αἰσθάνεσθαι</span>, etc.: <span lang="grc">ἔγνω ἄτοπα ἐμοῦ ποιοῦντος</span> _he knew that I was acting absurdly_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 7.2.18">X. C. 7.2.18</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀγνοοῦντες ἀλλήλων ὅ τι λέγομεν</span> _each of us mistaking what the other says_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Gorg. 517c">P. G. 517c</cite>. This construction of _verbs of knowing_ (and _showing_) occurs in Attic only when a participle accompanies the genitive.

##### <a id="s1369"></a> §1369

The genitive is used with verbs signifying _to fill, to be full of_. The thing filled is put in the accusative.

<span lang="grc">οὐκ ἐμπλήσετε τὴν θάλατταν τριήρων</span> _; will you not cover the sea with your triremes?_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.74">D. 8.74</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀναπλῆσαι αἰτιῶν</span> _to implicate in guilt_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 32c">P. A. 32c</cite>, <span lang="grc">τροφῆς εὐπορεῖν</span> _to have plenty of provisions_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ways 6.1">X. Vect. 6.1</cite>, <span lang="grc">τριήρης σεσαγμένη ἀνθρώπων</span> _a trireme stowed with men_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 8.8">X. O. 8.8</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὕβρεως μεστοῦσθαι</span> _to be filled with pride_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 713c">P. L. 713c</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">πλήθειν, πληροῦν, γέμειν, πλουτεῖν, βρί̄θειν</span> (poet.), <span lang="grc">βρύειν</span> (poet.).

**a.** Here belong also <span lang="grc">χεὶρ στάζει θυηλῆς Ἄρεος</span> _his hand drips with sacrifice to Ares_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 1423">S. El. 1423</cite>, <span lang="grc">μεθυσθεὶς τοῦ νέκταρος</span> _intoxicated with nectar_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 203b">P. S. 203b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡ πηγὴ ῥεῖ ψῡχροῦ ὕο̂ατος</span> _the spring flows with cold water_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedrus 230b">P. Phae. 230b</cite>. The instrumental dative is sometimes used.

##### <a id="s1370"></a> §1370

The genitive is used with verbs signifying _to rule, command_, _lead_.

<span lang="grc">θεῖον τὸ ἐθελόντων ἄρχειν</span> _it is divine to rule over willing subjects_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 21.12">X. O. 21.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῆς θαλάττης ἐκράτει</span> _he was master of the sea_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Menex. 239e">P. Menex. 239e</cite>, <span lang="grc">Ἔρως τῶν θεῶν βασιλεύει</span> _Love is king of the gods_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Sym. 195c">P. S. 195c</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἡγεῖτο τῆς ἐξόδου</span> _he led the expedition_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 2.10">T. 2.10</cite>, <span lang="grc">στρατηγεῖν τῶν ξένων</span> _to be general of the mercenaries_ X. A. <!-- page 325 --> 2. 6. 28. So with <span lang="grc">τυραννεῖν</span> _be absolute master of_, <span lang="grc">ἀνἀσσειν</span> _be lord of_ (poet.), <span lang="grc">ἡγεμονεύειν</span> _be commander of_. This genitive is connected with that of 1402.

##### <a id="s1371"></a> §1371

Several _verbs of ruling_ take the accusative when they mean _to conquer_, _overcome_ (so <span lang="grc">κρατεῖν</span>), or when they express the domain over which the rule extends; as <span lang="grc">τὴν Πελοπόννησον πειρᾶσθε μὴ ἐλά̄σσω ἐξηγεῖσθαι</span> _try not to lessen your dominion over the Peloponnese_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 1.71">T. 1.71</cite>. <span lang="grc">ἡγεῖσθαί τινι</span> means _to be a guide to any one, show any one the way_. Cp. [1537](06-dative.md#s1537).

### GENITIVE OF PRICE AND VALUE

##### <a id="s1372"></a> §1372

The genitive is used with verbs signifying _to buy, sell, cost, value_, _exchange_. The price for which one gives or does anything stands in the genitive.

<span lang="grc">ἀργυρίου πρίασθαι ἢ ἀποδόσθαι ἵππον</span> _to buy or sell a horse for money_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 333b">P. R. 333b</cite>, <span lang="grc">Θεμιστοκλέᾱ τῶν μεγίστων δωρεῶν ἠξίωσαν</span> _they deemed Themistocles worthy of the greatest gifts_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.154">I. 4.154</cite>, <span lang="grc">οὐκ ἀνταλλακτέον μοι τὴν φιλοτῑμίᾱν οὐδενὸς κέρδους</span> _I must not barter my public spirit for any price_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 19.223">D. 19.223</cite>. So with <span lang="grc">τάττειν</span> _rate_, <span lang="grc">μισθοῦν</span> _let_, <span lang="grc">μισθοῦσθαι</span> _hire_, <span lang="grc">ἐργάζεσθαι</span> _work_, and with any verb of doing anything for a wage, as <span lang="grc">οἱ τῆς παρ᾽ ἡμέρᾱν χάριτος τὰ μέγιστα τῆς πόλεως ἀπολωλεκότες</span> _those who have ruined the highest interests of the State to purchase ephemeral popularity_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 8.70">D. 8.70</cite>, <span lang="grc">πόσου διδάσκει; πέντε μνῶν</span> _for how much does he teach? for five minae_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 20b">P. A. 20b</cite>, <span lang="grc">οἱ Χαλδαῖοι μισθοῦ στρατεύονται</span> _the Chaldaeans serve for pay_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 3.2.7">X. C. 3.2.7</cite>.

**a.** The instrumental dative is also used. With _verbs of exchanging_, <span lang="grc">ἀντί</span> is usual ([1683](../19-prepositions.md#s1683)).

##### <a id="s1373"></a> §1373

_To value highly_ and _lightly_ is <span lang="grc">περὶ πολλοῦ</span> (<span lang="grc">πλείονος, πλείστου</span>) and <span lang="grc">περὶ ὀλίγου</span> (<span lang="grc">ἐλά̄ττονος, ἐλαχίστου</span>) <span lang="grc">τῑμᾶσθαι</span> or <span lang="grc">ποιεῖσθαι</span>: <span lang="grc">τὰ πλείστου ἄξια περὶ ἐλαχίστου ποιεῖται, τὰ δὲ φαυλότερα περὶ πλείονος</span> _he makes least account of what is most important, and sets higher what is less estimable_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 30a">P. A. 30a</cite>. The genitive of value, without <span lang="grc">περί</span>, is rare: <span lang="grc">πολλοῦ ποιοῦμαι ἀκηκοέναι ἃ ἀκήκοα Πρωταγόρου</span> _I esteem it greatly to have heard what I did from Protagoras_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Prot. 328d">P. Pr. 328d</cite>.

**a.** The genitive of cause is rarely used to express the thing bought or that for which pay is demanded: <span lang="grc">οὐδένα τῆς συνουσίᾱς ἀργύριον πρά̄ττει</span> _you charge nobody anything for your teaching_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Mem. 1.6.11">X. M. 1.6.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">τρεῖς μναῖ διφρίσκου</span> _three minae for a small chariot_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Cl. 31">Ar. Nub. 31</cite>.

##### <a id="s1374"></a> §1374

In legal language <span lang="grc">τῑμᾶν τινι θανάτου</span> is _to fix the penalty at death_ (said of the jury, which is not interested in the result), <span lang="grc">τῑμᾶσθαί τινι θανάτου</span> _to propose death as the penalty_ (said of the accuser, who is interested), and <span lang="grc">τιμᾶσθαί τινος</span> _to propose a penalty against oneself_ (said of the accused). Cp. <span lang="grc">τῑμᾶταί μοι ὁ ἀνὴρ θανάτου</span> _the man proposes death as my penalty_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 36b">P. A. 36b</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀλλὰ δὴ φυγῆς τῑμήσωμαι*;* ἴσως γὰρ ἄν μοι τούτου τῑμήσαιτε</span> _but shall I propose exile as my penalty? for perhaps you_ (the jury) _might fix it at this_ 37 c. So <span lang="grc">θανάτου</span> with <span lang="grc">κρί̄νειν, διώκειν, ὑπάγειν</span>. Cp. [1379](#s1379).

### GENITIVE OF CRIME AND ACCOUNTABILITY

##### <a id="s1375"></a> §1375

With verbs of judicial action the genitive denotes the crime, the accusative denotes the person accused. <!-- page 326 -->

<span lang="grc">αἰτιᾶσθαι ἀλλήλους τοῦ γεγενημένου</span> _to accuse one another of what had happened_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Ages. 1.33">X. Ages. 1.33</cite>, <span lang="grc">διώκω μὲν κακηγορίᾱς, τῇ δ᾽ αὐτῇ ψήφῳ φόνου φεύγω</span> _I bring an accusation for defamation and at the same trial am prosecuted for murder_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 11.12">L. 11.12</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἐμὲ ὁ Μέλητος ἀσεβείᾱς ἐγράψατο</span> _Meletus prosecuted me for impiety_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Euthyph. 5c">P. Euth. 5c</cite>, <span lang="grc">δώρων ἐκρίθησαν</span> _they were tried for bribery_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 27.3">L. 27.3</cite>. On _verbs of accusing_ and _condemning_ compounded with <span lang="grc">κατά</span>, see [1385](#s1385).

##### <a id="s1376"></a> §1376

So with <span lang="grc">ἀμύ̄νεσθαι</span> and <span lang="grc">κολάζειν</span> _punish_, <span lang="grc">εἰσάγειν</span> and <span lang="grc">προσκαλεῖσθαι</span> _summon into court_, <span lang="grc">αἱρεῖν</span> _convict_, <span lang="grc">τῑμωρεῖσθαι</span> _take vengeance on_. With <span lang="grc">τῑμωρεῖν</span> _avenge_ and <span lang="grc">λαγχάνειν</span> _obtain leave to bring a suit_, the person avenged and the person against whom the suit is brought are put in the dative. So with <span lang="grc">δικάζεσθαί τινί τινος</span> _to go to law with a man about something_.

##### <a id="s1377"></a> §1377

Verbs of judicial action may take a cognate accusative (<span lang="grc">δίκην, γραφήν</span>), on which the genitive of the crime depends: <span lang="grc">γραφὴν ὕβρεως καὶ δίκην κακηγορίᾱς φεύξεται</span> _he will be brought to trial on an indictment for outrage and on a civil action for slander_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.32">D. 21.32</cite>. From this adnominal use arose the construction of the genitive with this class of verbs.

##### <a id="s1378"></a> §1378

<span lang="grc">ἁλίσκεσθαι</span> (<span lang="grc">ἁλῶναι</span>) _be convicted_, <span lang="grc">ὀφλισκάνειν</span> _lose a suit_, <span lang="grc">φεύγειν</span> _be prosecuted_ are equivalent to passives: <span lang="grc">ἐά̄ν τις ἁλῷ κλοπῆς . . . κἂ̄ν ἀστρατείᾱς τις ὄφλῃ</span> _if any one be condemned for theft . . . and if any one be convicted of desertion_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 24.103">D. 24.103</cite>, <span lang="grc">ἀσεβείᾱς φεύγοντα ὑπὸ Μελήτου</span> _being tried for impiety on the indictment of Meletus_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 35d">P. A. 35d</cite>. <span lang="grc">ὀφλισκάνειν</span> may take <span lang="grc">δίκην</span> as a cognate accus. (<span lang="grc">ὠφληκέναι δίκην</span> _to be cast in a suit_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Birds 1457">Ar. Av. 1457</cite>); the crime or the penalty may stand in the genitive (with or without <span lang="grc">δίκην</span>), or in the accusative: <span lang="grc">ὁπόσοι κλοπῆς ἢ δώρων ὄφλοιεν</span> _all who had been convicted of embezzlement or bribery_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 1.74">And. 1.74</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὑφ᾽ ὑ̄μῶν θανάτου δίκην ὀφλών</span> _having incurred through your verdict the penalty of death_, <span lang="grc">ὑπὸ τῆς ἀληθείᾱς ὠφληκότες μοχθηρίᾱν</span> _condemned by the truth to suffer the penalty of wickedness_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Apol. 39b">P. A. 39b</cite>.

##### <a id="s1379"></a> §1379

With verbs of judicial action the genitive of the penalty may be regarded as a genitive of value: <span lang="grc">θανάτου κρί̄νουσι</span> _they judge in matters of life and death_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Cyrop. 1.2.14">X. C. 1.2.14</cite>. So <span lang="grc">ὑπάγειν τινὰ θανάτου</span> _to impeach a man on a capital charge_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Hell. 2.3.12">X. H. 2.3.12</cite>; cp. <span lang="grc">τῑμᾶν θανάτου</span> 1374.

**a.** With many verbs of judicial action <span lang="grc">περί</span> is used.

### GENITIVE OF CONNECTION

##### <a id="s1380"></a> §1380

The genitive may express a more or less close connection or relation, where <span lang="grc">περί</span> is sometimes added.

With _verbs of saying_ or _thinking:_ <span lang="grc">τί δὲ ἵππων οἴει</span>; _but what do you think of horses?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 459b">P. R. 459b</cite>. Often in poetry: <span lang="grc">εἰπὲ δέ μοι πατρός</span> _but tell me about my father_ <cite data-ref="Hom. Od. 11.174"><span lang="grc">λ</span> 174</cite>, <span lang="grc">τοῦ κασιγνήτου τί φῄς</span>; _what dost thou say of thy brother?_ <cite data-ref="Soph. El. 317">S. El. 317</cite>.

##### <a id="s1381"></a> §1381

The genitive is often used loosely, especially at the beginning of a construction, to state the subject of a remark: <span lang="grc">ἵππος ἢν κακουργῇ, τὸν ἱππέᾱ κακίζομεν· τῆς δὲ γυναικός, εἰ κακοποιεῖ κτλ</span>. _if a horse is vicious, we lay the fault to the groom; but as regards a wife, if she conducts herself ill_, etc. <cite data-ref="Xen. Ec. 3.11">X. O. 3.11</cite>, <span lang="grc">ὡσαύτως δὲ καὶ τῶν ἄλλων τεχνῶν</span> _and so in the case of the other arts too_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Charm. 165d">P. Charm. 165d</cite>, <span lang="grc">τί δὲ τῶν πολλῶν καλῶν</span>; _what about the many beautiful things?_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Phaedo 78d">P. Ph. 78d</cite>.

### GENITIVE WITH COMPOUND VERBS

<!-- page 327 -->

##### <a id="s1382"></a> §1382

The genitive depends on the meaning of a compound verb as a whole (1) if the simple verb takes the genitive without a preposition, as <span lang="grc">ὑπείκειν</span> _withdraw_, <span lang="grc">παραλύ̄ειν</span> _release_, <span lang="grc">παραχωρεῖν</span> _surrender_ ([1392](05-free-uses-of-the-genitive.md#s1392)), <span lang="grc">ἐφί̄εσθαι</span> _desire_ ([1349](#s1349)); or (2) if the compound has acquired through the preposition a signification different from that of the simple verb with the preposition: thus <span lang="grc">ἀπογνόντες τῆς ἐλευθερίᾱς</span> _despairing of freedom_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 2.46">L. 2.46</cite> cannot be expressed by <span lang="grc">γνόντες ἀπὸ τῆς ἐλευθερίᾱς</span>. But it is often difficult to determine whether the genitive depends on the compound verb as a whole or on the preposition contained in it.

##### <a id="s1383"></a> §1383

A verb compounded with a preposition taking the dative or accusative may take the genitive by analogy of another compound verb whose preposition requires the genitive: so <span lang="grc">ἐμβαίνειν ὅρων</span> _to set foot on the boundaries_ <cite data-ref="Soph. OC 400">S. O. C. 400</cite> by analogy to <span lang="grc">ἐπιβαίνειν τῶν ὅρων</span> <cite data-ref="Plat. Laws 778e">P. L. 778e</cite>.

##### <a id="s1384"></a> §1384

Many verbs compounded with <span lang="grc">ἀπό, πρό, ὑπέρ, ἐπί</span>, and <span lang="grc">κατά</span> take the genitive when the compound may be resolved into the simple verb and the preposition without change in the sense: <span lang="grc">τοὺς συμμάχους ἀποτρέψαντες τῆς γνώμης</span> _dissuading the allies from their purpose_ <cite data-ref="Andoc. 3.21">And. 3.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">προαπεστάλησαν τῆς ἀποστάσεως</span> _they were despatched before the revolt_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 3.5">T. 3.5</cite>, <span lang="grc">πολλοῖς ἡ γλῶττα προτρέχει τῆς διανοίᾱς</span> _in many people the tongue outruns the thought_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 1.41">I. 1.41</cite>, (<span lang="grc">οἱ πολέμιοι</span>) <span lang="grc">ὑπερκάθηνται ἡμῶν</span> _the enemy are stationed above us_ <cite data-ref="Xen. Anab. 5.1.9">X. A. 5.1.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">τῷ ἐπιβάντι πρώτῳ τοῦ τείχους</span> _to the first one setting foot on the wall_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 4.116">T. 4.116</cite>. This use is most frequent when the prepositions are used in their proper signification. Many compounds of <span lang="grc">ὑπέρ</span> take the accusative.

**a.** This use is especially common with <span lang="grc">κατά</span> _against_ or _at:_ <span lang="grc">μή μου κατείπῃς</span> _don't speak against me_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Theaet. 149a">P. Th. 149a</cite>, <span lang="grc">κατεψεύσατό μου</span> _he spoke falsely against me_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 18.9">D. 18.9</cite>, <span lang="grc">ψευδῆ κατεγλώττιζέ μου</span> _he mouthed lies at me_ <cite data-ref="Aristoph. Ach. 380">Ar. Ach. 380</cite>. The construction in 1384 is post-Homeric.

##### <a id="s1385"></a> §1385

The _verbs of accusing_ and _condemning_ (cp. [1375](#s1375)) containing <span lang="grc">κατά</span> in composition (<span lang="grc">καταγιγνώσκειν</span> _decide against_, <span lang="grc">καταδικάζειν</span> _adjudge against_, <span lang="grc">καταψηφίζεσθαι</span> _vote against_, <span lang="grc">κατακρί̄νειν</span> _give sentence against_) take a genitive of the _person_, and an accusative of the _penalty_. <span lang="grc">κατηγορεῖν</span> _accuse_, <span lang="grc">καταγιγνώσκειν</span> and <span lang="grc">καταψηφίζεσθαι</span> take a genitive of the _person_, an accusative of the _crime:_ <span lang="grc">καταγνῶναι δωροδοκίᾱν ἐμοῦ</span> _to pronounce me guilty of bribery_ <cite data-ref="Lys. 21.21">L. 21.21</cite>, <span lang="grc">τούτου δειλίᾱν καταψηφίζεσθαι</span> _to vote him guilty of cowardice_ 14. 11, <span lang="grc">τῶν διαφυγόντων θάνατον καταγνόντες</span> _having condemned the fugitives to death_ <cite data-ref="Thuc. 6.60">T. 6.60</cite>; _person_, _crime_, and _penalty:_ <span lang="grc">πολλῶν οἱ πατέρες μηδισμοῦ θάνατον κατέγνωσαν</span> _our fathers passed sentence of death against many for favouring the Persians_ <cite data-ref="Isoc. 4.157">I. 4.157</cite>. The genitive is rarely used to express the _crime_ or the _penalty:_ <span lang="grc">παρανόμων αὐτοῦ κατηγορεῖν</span> _to accuse him of proposing unconstitutional measures_ <cite data-ref="Dem. 21.5">D. 21.5</cite>; cp. <span lang="grc">ἀνθρώπων καταψηφισθέντων θάνατον</span> _men who have been condemned to death_ <cite data-ref="Plat. Rep. 558a">P. R. 558a</cite>.

##### <a id="s1386"></a> §1386

In general, prose, as distinguished from poetry, repeats the preposition contained in the compound; but <span lang="grc">κατά</span> is not repeated.
